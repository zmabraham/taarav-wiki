# Chabad Chronology: 1521–2001

Comprehensive timeline of Chabad history drawn from all 14 sources in this wiki corpus. Hebrew and Gregorian dates given where known. Entries include wikilinks to relevant entity, event, and concept pages.

---

## Pre-History: Before the Founding

| Year (CE)  | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1521       | —             | Town of **Lubavitch** first appears in historical documentation — five centuries before becoming Chabad's world center. Town name derives from founder "Lubiyetzky" (Slavic: lover/love). See [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]]. |
| ~1698–1700 | ~5458–60      | Birth of **Rabbi Yisrael Baal Shem Tov** (Besht) — founder of the broader Chassidic movement from which Chabad emerged                                                                                                                  |
| ~1745      | ~5505         | Birth of **Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi** ([[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]), future founder of Chabad, in Liozna, Vitebsk Governorate                                                                                     |
| ~1760      | ~5520         | Alter Rebbe travels to study under the **Maggid of Mezeritch** — the Besht's main disciple and the organizer of the first generation of Chassidic rebbes                                                                                |
| 1772       | 5532          | **Death of the Maggid** of Mezeritch; Alter Rebbe emerges as independent Chabad teacher. Chabad ("Chochma-Bina-Daat" — Wisdom-Understanding-Knowledge) begins as a distinct school of Chassidism                                        |
| 1777       | 5537          | **First Chassidic aliyah to Eretz Yisrael**: Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk leads ~300 Chassidim to the Land of Israel; settle initially in Safed (Tzfat). See [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Eretz Yisrael]]                       |
| 1782       | 5542          | Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk relocates from Safed to **Tiberias** (Teveria); establishes Chassidic community there                                                                                                                  |
| 1788       | 5548          | Alter Rebbe recognized as independent Rebbe; founds his court in **Liadi**                                                                                                                                                              |
| 1796       | 5556          | Alter Rebbe begins writing **Tanya** (_Likkutei Amarim_) — the foundational text of Chabad philosophy; publishes first edition                                                                                                          |

## 18th Century — Founding Era

| Year (CE) | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1798      | 5558          | **First arrest of the Alter Rebbe** by Czarist authorities in St. Petersburg on **19 Kislev** — charged with sending Palestine funds that might aid Ottoman enemies of Russia, and with running an unauthorized religious organization. Liberated same year → liberation date becomes _Chag HaGeulah_ (Yud-Tes Kislev). See [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] |
| 1798      | 5559          | **Vilna Gaon's ban (_cherem_)**: On Hoshana Raba 5559, the Gaon of Vilna issues his most severe ban against Chassidism, with particular force in Lithuania — explaining why Chabad's strongholds remained in Belarus/Russia rather than Lithuania. See [[entities/places/poland-lithuania|Poland, Lithuania, Latvia]]                                                   |
| 1800      | 5560          | **Second arrest** of the Alter Rebbe; liberated again. Second liberation reinforces the Yud-Tes Kislev celebration                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

## Early 19th Century — Consolidation

| Year (CE)                        | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1812                             | 5572          | **Napoleon invades Russia**; Alter Rebbe — a Russian patriot — flees east with his community, refusing to allow Napoleon's liberalism to lure Jews from Torah life                                                                                                   |
| 24 Tevet 5573 (Jan. 1813)        | 5573          | **Alter Rebbe dies** in Piena village while fleeing Napoleon's retreating army. [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (Rabbi DovBer Schneuri) succeeds                                                                                                  |
| 1813                             | 5573          | **Mitteler Rebbe establishes headquarters in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]]** — a town in the Smolensk region of Russia. Chabad's world center remains here for the next 102 years (1813–1915)                                                             |
| ~1820s                           | ~5580s        | **First Chabad community established in Hebron** (Land of Israel), supported by the Kolel Chabad fund. See [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Eretz Yisrael]]                                                                                                          |
| 1823                             | 5583          | **Hebron Chabad community formally established** with institutional structure — synagogues, scholars, _chalukah_ fund distribution. See [[events/chabad-eretz-yisrael|Chabad in Eretz Yisrael]]                                                                     |
| June–July 1825                   | 5585          | **Three denunciations** filed against the Mitteler Rebbe by informant Simcha Kissin of Vitebsk — accusing Chabad of seditious Palestine fund collection, unauthorized religious organization, and suspicious foreign correspondence                                  |
| October 1, 1825 (1 Tishrei 5586) | 5586          | **Mitteler Rebbe arrested** by order of Governor Khavanski; taken for questioning. Charges: see above. First interrogation October 7; written defense submitted October 9; second interrogation October 15; third October 19                                         |
| Late 1825                        | 5586          | Mitteler Rebbe's case transferred to **Mogilev courts**; attempted escalation to St. Petersburg                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **9-10 Kislev 5587** (1826)      | 5587          | **[[events/arrest-mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe liberated]]** — his birthday and the date that becomes his yahrzeit → celebrated as _Yud-Tet/Yud Kislev_, a major Chabad holiday. International pressure and the Rebbe's composed legal defense secured his release |
| **9 Kislev 5588** (1827)         | 5588          | **Mitteler Rebbe dies** — exactly one year after his liberation date. [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn) succeeds as third Rebbe                                                                                 |

## Mid-19th Century — Tzemach Tzedek Era

| Year (CE)                 | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1834                      | 5594          | **Hebron pogrom** (_pra'ot_): Arab attacks devastate the Chabad community in Hebron; significant suffering and disruption to the community. See [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Eretz Yisrael]]                                                                                                                               |
| 1840s–60s                 | 5600s–20s     | Tzemach Tzedek resists the **Haskalah** (Enlightenment) and **Cantonist** military conscription decrees — Czarist policy requiring Jewish communities to supply child conscripts for 25-year military service. His legal and communal resistance is documented in [[summaries/toldot-chabad-russia|Chabad in Czarist Russia]] |
| 1843                      | 5603          | **Tzemach Tzedek attends government-sponsored Rabbinical Assembly** in St. Petersburg — convened by the Czarist government to discuss "reforming" Jewish life. The Tzemach Tzedek represented traditional Jewry against the Haskalah faction.                                                                                  |
| **2 Iyar 5594** (1834)    | 5594          | Birth of **[[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn (Maharash)]]** — fourth Rebbe — in Lubavitch, youngest son of the Tzemach Tzedek                                                                                                                                                                         |
| 1845                      | 5605          | **Rebbetzin Menucha Rochel Slonim** (daughter of the Mitteler Rebbe) immigrates to Hebron, Israel — the most prominent Chabad figure yet to settle in Eretz Yisrael; her presence anchors the Hebron community                                                                                                                 |
| **13 Nissan 5626** (1866) | 5626          | **Tzemach Tzedek dies**; [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Maharash]] (Rabbi Shmuel) succeeds as fourth Rebbe                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| 1866–1882                 |               | **Maharash leads Chabad**: travels repeatedly to Berlin, Vienna, Paris to intercede with European governments on behalf of Russian Jewry; establishes precedent of Rebbe-as-diplomat. Famous aphorism: _"L'chatchila ariber"_ ("from the outset, go over it")                                                                  |

## Late 19th Century — Rashab Era Begins

| Year (CE)                               | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **20 Cheshvan 5621** (1860)             | 5621          | Birth of **[[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn (Rashab)]]** — fifth Rebbe — in Lubavitch                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **12 Tammuz 5640** (1880)               | 5640          | Birth of **[[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (Rayatz)]]** — sixth Rebbe — in Lubavitch, son of the Rashab                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **13 Tishrei 5643** (1882)              | 5643          | **Maharash dies** in Lubavitch; [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] succeeds as fifth Rebbe (age 22)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **5645** (1885)                         | 5645          | **Rashab visits Berlin** — calls on Rabbi **Azriel Hildesheimer** (founder of Adass Jisroel congregation and Berlin Rabbinical Seminary). Hildesheimer receives him with great honor. First contact between Chabad and German Neo-Orthodox leadership. See [[events/chabad-germany-history|Chabad and German Jewry]]                                                                                                    |
| 1889                                    | 5649          | **Rashab visits Berlin Aquarium** specifically to observe a creature he believes might be the _chilazon_ (source of biblical sky-blue _techelet_ dye). He sees a small creature secreting black liquid but does not study it closely, not recognizing it. Reports this to the Radzyner Rebbe 20 years later in correspondence. See [[entities/places/germany|Germany]]                                                  |
| 1895                                    | 5655          | **The Akedah** — in his private chambers in Lubavitch, the Rashab calls his son Yosef Yitzchak (age 15) and performs "the akedah" (_binding of Isaac_): tells him that G-d has demanded he be "bound" as a sacrifice for the generation, and designates him as the next Rebbe. Both father and son weep. See [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]                                                                          |
| 1895                                    | 5655          | Rayatz (age 15) begins serving as his father's **private secretary**; represents Rashab at rabbinical conference in **Kovno**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 1896                                    | 5656          | Rayatz represents Rashab at conference in **Vilna**; manages correspondence with Jewish leaders across Russia; makes trips to St. Petersburg to lobby against anti-Semitic decrees                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 1896                                    | 5656          | Rayatz becomes **engaged** to Nechama Dina Schneersohn (second cousin); Rashab bears all wedding expenses                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **13 Elul 5657** (September 10, 1897)   | 5657          | **Wedding of the Rayatz** to Nechama Dina in Lubavitch. The Rayatz makes his famous request: "Open a yeshiva combining Talmud and Chassidic philosophy — as one of my wedding gifts."                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **15 Elul 5657** (September 12, 1897)   | 5657          | **Tomchei Temimim founded**: Rashab convenes 50 distinguished guests the day after the wedding celebration and announces the yeshiva despite widespread skepticism. Opens with 18 students in Lubavitch. See [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]]                                                                                                                                                              |
| Simchat Torah 5658 (1898)               | 5658          | **Rashab names the yeshiva** _Tomchei Temimim_ ("Supporters of the Sincere") — from the verse recited during the seventh hakafah. Students are called _temimim_                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **24 Elul 5659** (September 11, 1898)   | 5659          | **Rayatz becomes director of Tomchei Temimim** (age 18) — responsible for fundraising, student selection, faculty hiring, and expansion across Russia                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| 1899–1903                               | 5659–63       | Tomchei Temimim grows from **18 students** (budget 3,120 rubles, 1899) to **200 students** (budget 20,000+ rubles, 1903). Branches open in Zembin, Doksycy, Koblitz, Vitebsk                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ~1900                                   | ~5660         | **First Chabad settlers arrive in the United States** — a small, weak community that the Rayatz later described as inadequate to the movement's needs                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| 1905                                    | 5665          | Russian Revolution of 1905; widespread pogroms against Jews across Russia                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **5666** (1905–1908)                    | 5666          | Rashab begins dictating **Hemshech Samech-Vav** — the most sustained and philosophically deep Chassidic discourse series in Chabad history                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **22 March 1906**                       | 5666          | **Poalei Tzion Confrontation**: When a Tomchei Temimim student defects to the Marxist-Zionist Poalei Tzion group, armed confrontation follows. The Rayatz refuses to submit to armed demands: "Get out of here! I'm not afraid of your guns." A mob of 200 subsequently shoots at the yeshiva; yeshiva students drive them off. The Rayatz carries a gun during this period and narrowly escapes a train-station ambush. |
| **Cheshvan 5667** (November 1906)       | 5667          | **Rashab arrives in Würzburg, Bavaria** for a year-long medical stay — his longest time outside Russia. He accesses Kaiser Wilhelm's writing room (via a bribed guard) and writes a Chassidic maamar on the Kaiser's stationery with the Kaiser's pen. See [[entities/places/germany|Germany]]                                                                                                                          |
| **Kislev 5667** (December 1906)         | 5667          | **Rayatz joins Rashab in Würzburg**; the Rashab composes Hemshech Samech-Vav during this period and collaborates with Rabbis Shlomo Zalman Breuer and Yaakov Rosenheim on the "Histaadus HaYereim" plan to strengthen German Orthodoxy                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Yud-Tes Kislev 5667** (December 1906) | 5667          | Rashab leads **farbrengen in Würzburg** on the Chabad liberation holiday; German Jews deeply impressed by his two-hour prayer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| After **Shavuot 5667** (1907)           | 5667          | Rashab returns from Würzburg to Lubavitch                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| 1908                                    | 5668          | **Yeshiya Berlin** — Lubavitch philanthropist and primary funder of Tomchei Temimim facilities — dies, creating funding crisis for the yeshiva                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **1911**                                | 5671          | **Toras Emes founded in Hebron**: Rashab purchases Beit Romano (a large complex) in Hebron and sends Rabbi Zalman Havlin to establish a branch yeshiva — the **first Chassidic yeshiva in the Land of Israel**. Named _Toras Emes_. See [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Eretz Yisrael]]                                                                                                                                 |

## WWI and Revolution (1914–1920)

| Year (CE)                      | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1914–18                        | 5674–78       | **World War I**: Front approaches Lubavitch from the German-Russian front. Russian Jews face mass displacement                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **5675** (1915)                | 5675          | **Rashab moves Chabad headquarters** from [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] to **Rostov-on-Don** — the first time the Chabad center had shifted since the Mitteler Rebbe established it in 1813. The town of Lubavitch is **burned** in the war, ending its 102-year role as world center |
| 1915–18                        |               | WWI forces Toras Emes students (Russians) to return from Hebron; yeshiva temporarily closes                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **1917**                       | 5677          | **Russian Revolution**: Bolsheviks seize power. Soviet state established. The Yevsektsiya (Jewish Section of the Communist Party) begins systematic war on Jewish religious life                                                                                                                 |
| **1918**                       | 5678          | Tomchei Temimim goes underground. The Rebbe's court complex in Lubavitch burned — likely by Bolshevik forces — ending the visible institutional presence                                                                                                                                         |
| **2 Nissan 5680** (April 1920) | 5680          | **[[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab dies]]** in Rostov-on-Don, having witnessed the Revolution but not its full horrors. His son [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] inherits leadership under dramatically more difficult conditions                                                        |

## Soviet Underground (1920–1927)

| Year (CE)                    | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1920–27                      | 5680–87       | **Rayatz organizes underground yeshivos** throughout Soviet territory — clandestine Tomchei Temimim network across dozens of cities. Coded correspondence; secret maamad funds; constant movement to evade GPU. See [[events/soviet-persecution|Soviet Persecution of Chabad]]                                                                |
| **1921**                     | 5681          | The **Rebbe's court complex in Lubavitch burned** by revolutionary forces — the last physical institutional presence in Lubavitch destroyed                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **1922**                     | 5682          | **Toras Emes reconstituted in Jerusalem** by Rabbi Zalman Havlin after WWI forced evacuation from Hebron. The Jerusalem yeshiva becomes a major Chabad institution in Eretz Yisrael                                                                                                                                                            |
| **1926**                     | 5686          | Rayatz records in diary the **Rashab's prophecy** (given 3 weeks before death): "There will be difficult times; you will need to have _mesirat nefesh bepoel_ (actual self-sacrifice). But eventually, the Communist government will disintegrate." Fulfilled 1990.                                                                            |
| **1926**                     | 5686          | **R' Yisrael Jacobson** emigrates to America; helps found Agudas HaTmimim (organization of Tomchei Temimim graduates) and Agudas Chassidei Chabad in New York. See [[summaries/zikaron-lebnei-yisrael|Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael]]                                                                                                                |
| **June 1927**                | 5687          | **Rayatz arrested** by GPU (Soviet secret police) in Leningrad. He tells his interrogators: "I recognize only one authority — the Almighty." Sentenced to **death** → commuted to **10 years in Solovki** labor camp → commuted to **3 years exile** in Kostroma. International pressure from religious Jews and world leaders secures release |
| **12-13 Tammuz 5687** (1927) | 5687          | **[[events/soviet-persecution|Rayatz liberated]]** and permitted to leave the Soviet Union. This date is celebrated as _Yud-Beis Tammuz_ — a Chabad holiday parallel in significance to 19 Kislev (Alter Rebbe) and 9-10 Kislev (Mitteler Rebbe)                                                                                              |

## Exile and Rebuilding: Riga and Warsaw (1927–1939)

| Year (CE)                              | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **25 Tishrei 5688** (October 21, 1927) | 5688          | **Rayatz arrives in Riga, Latvia** — thousands come to the train station to greet him. He describes the overnight ride as "eighteen hours with anguish in the heart, pain in the soul." The word "temporary" appears on his Riga stationery: "I fled the bad, but I have not yet found the good." See [[entities/places/poland-lithuania|Poland, Lithuania, Latvia]] |
| **Within first month in Riga** (1927)  | 5688          | Rayatz launches **70 letters to Russian Chassidim**; founds **Vaad Magen UMekhaseh** (Committee for Protection of Russian Jewry) with the Rogotchover Gaon and Berlin's Chief Rabbi Hildesheimer                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **1927–33**                            | 5688–93       | Rayatz diagnosed with **multiple sclerosis** during Riga years; makes extended trips to Berlin, Vienna, Paris, and Marienbad health spas for treatment                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **5688–89** (1928)                     | 5688          | **Wedding of Chaya Mushka** (Rayatz's daughter) to **Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson** (the Ramash, future seventh Rebbe) in Warsaw. The future succession is cemented. See [[summaries/rayatz-biography|Undaunted]] Ch. 6                                                                                                                                          |
| **1928–1931**                          | 5688–91       | **Future seventh Rebbe studies in Berlin**: Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson studies at the **University of Berlin**, living at 7 Hanse-Ufer Street. He later continues at the Sorbonne in Paris before assuming Chabad leadership in 1951. See [[entities/places/germany|Germany]]                                                                                  |
| **Passover 5689** (1929)               | 5689          | Rayatz organizes the **1929 Matzah Campaign** — international diplomatic and fundraising operation to ship matzah into starving Soviet Russia. Coordinates with Latvian MP Mordechai Dubin, Germany's Jewish community in Berlin, and chief rabbis across Europe                                                                                                      |
| **1929**                               | 5689          | **Hebron riots**: Arab attacks on the Jewish community force evacuation of Chabad families from Hebron to Jerusalem. Yeshivat Toras Emes (later Torat Emes) in Jerusalem absorbs the displaced community                                                                                                                                                              |
| **1929–30**                            | 5690          | Rayatz makes **extended trip to Eretz Yisrael and America** — his first encounter with American Jewry. He observes the small, weak Chabad community in the US and takes detailed notes. This trip prepares him for permanent immigration a decade later                                                                                                               |
| **1933**                               | 5693          | **Hitler comes to power** in Germany (January 30). Chabad leadership engages German Orthodox rabbis to advocate for German Jewry; Rayatz makes diplomatic contacts                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **1933**                               | 5693          | **Rayatz relocates from Riga to Warsaw/Poland** to be closer to the Tomchei Temimim Polish branches. He sets up an expanded network of yeshivos across Poland and Lithuania                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **1934–1935**                          | 5694–95       | Rayatz based in **Warsaw**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| **1936–1939**                          | 5696–99       | Rayatz based in **Otwock** (suburb of Warsaw)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **September 1, 1939**                  | 5699          | **Germany invades Poland**: World War II begins. Rayatz is in Warsaw. American diplomatic intervention (including Senator James Byrnes) coordinates his evacuation through Riga to the United States                                                                                                                                                                  |

## WWII, Holocaust, and Rescue (1939–1945)

| Year (CE)                            | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **September–November 1939**          | 5700          | Rayatz evacuated from war-torn Warsaw through Riga; reaches New York                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **9 Adar II 5700** (March 19, 1940)  | 5700          | **[[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz arrives in America]]** — docks at Pier 97 in Manhattan on the SS _Drottningholm_, in a wheelchair. When a reporter asks about his plans, he rises and declares: **"America iz nisht anderesh — America is no different. I came to make America a center of Torah."** See [[summaries/rayatz-biography|Undaunted]] Ch. 10                                                            |
| **First full day in America** (1940) | 5700          | Rayatz insists: "If you do not promise me that you will open the yeshivah, I will not go to bed." Tomchei Temimim USA begins **one week later** in Congregation Oneg Shabbat, East Flatbush, Brooklyn                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **12 Av 5700** (August 16, 1940)     | 5700          | **770 Eastern Parkway purchased** — a three-story brownstone in Crown Heights, Brooklyn. The Chasidim had searched for a suburban location; the Rayatz insisted on visible Crown Heights. 770 becomes the iconic Chabad world headquarters; replicas built in 30+ locations worldwide                                                                                                                                   |
| **1940–1941**                        | 5700–01       | **Shanghai escape route** opens: Dutch consul **Jan Zwartendyk** stamps "no visa required to Curacao"; Japanese consul **Chiune Sugihara** issues thousands of transit visas. Route: Vilna → Moscow → trans-Siberian railway → Vladivostok → Kobe, Japan → Shanghai. Chabad students among the first groups to use this route. Rayatz coordinates US visa negotiations and raises $500/student for Russian transit fees |
| **1941**                             | 5701          | **Rabbi Chaim Gutnick** sent to **Australia** by the Rayatz to prepare for incoming refugee chassidim — one of 6 Chabad families eventually sent to Melbourne and surroundings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **June 1941**                        | 5701          | Germany invades Soviet Union. Chabad chassidim flee eastward to **Samarkand** (Uzbekistan) and other Central Asian cities. See [[entities/places/bukhara|Bukhara / Central Asia]]                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **November 1941**                    | 5702          | **Nazis murder all 483 remaining Jews of Lubavitch** — the town that had been Chabad's world center for 102 years. No survivors. See [[summaries/lubavitch|ליובאוויטש]] and [[events/holocaust-chabad|Holocaust]]                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **1941–1945**                        | 5701–05       | ~**40 Chabad students** survive the war years in **Shanghai** under Rabbi Meir Ashkenazi (a Chabad chassid, Ashkenazi chief rabbi of Shanghai) — funded and supported by the Rayatz from Brooklyn                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **1941–1945**                        | 5701–05       | **Holocaust devastates Chabad communities**: Warsaw, Vilna, Lublin, and hundreds of smaller towns annihilated. Rayatz's daughter **Sheina** and her husband killed. Rayatz weeps uncontrollably over photographs of fallen chassidim — Rabbis Yechezkel Feigin and Shlomo Horowitz among those murdered                                                                                                                 |

## America and Post-War Reconstruction (1945–1951)

| Year (CE)                             | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1945**                              | 5705          | World War II ends; mass emigration of Soviet-era Chabad survivors begins. The Rayatz now orchestrates global resettlement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **1946**                              | 5706          | **Operation Great Escape begins**: Chabad chassidim in Soviet Uzbekistan (where they fled the Nazi invasion) acquire Polish papers on the black market. Skilled counterfeiters modify names; women serve as messengers (less conspicuous to authorities). A **secret rabbinical court of 23 judges** in Lvov sets guidelines and pools resources                                                                                                                         |
| **December 2, 1946**                  | 5707          | **The Az Yashir moment**: During a key Great Escape border crossing, the Rayatz is found by his nurse staring into space, swaying and reciting _Az Yashir_ (the Song of the Sea) as if walking across the border himself. He suddenly says "They got through, they got through" — **moments before a telegram arrives** confirming 232 people crossed the Polish border safely. See [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]                                                   |
| **1946–1947**                         | 5706–07       | **Great Escape completed**: Approximately **1,000 Chabad chassidim** escape the Soviet Union using forged Polish passports via the Lvov → Polish border → Western Europe route. The Rayatz provides $7,000 in bribes to free arrested chassidim. Operation coordinated by **Binyamin Gorodetsky** (Rayatz's European representative)                                                                                                                                     |
| **1946–1948**                         | 5707–08       | **Global dispersion**: The Rayatz choreographs resettlement of refugee chassidim — **France** (becomes nucleus of largest Jewish community in Europe), **Australia** (6 families to Melbourne), **Canada**, **UK**, **South Africa**, **Cuba**, **Sweden**, and **South America**. Each placement understood not as finding haven but as a mission: "The reason that divine providence has brought you here is to warm up the tepid [country] with reverence of Heaven." |
| **1946**                              | 5706          | **R' Yisrael Yehuda Levi** escapes the Soviet Union with forged papers in the name "Naftali Bat" (a Polish Jewish identity). Eventually reaches Eretz Yisrael; settles in Kfar Chabad. See [[summaries/zichronosai|Zichronosai]]                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **5708** (1948)                       | 5708          | **State of Israel proclaimed** (5 Iyar 5708 / May 14, 1948)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

## Contemporary Milestones (1951–2001)

| Year (CE)                | Year (Hebrew) | Event                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1969**                 | 5729          | Seventh Rebbe begins **global shlichus campaign** in earnest; first campus Chabad House at **UCLA** — fulfilling and vastly expanding the Rayatz's 1949 Brandeis initiative. Chabad Houses proliferate at universities worldwide                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **1989**                 | 5749          | **Ohel of the Tzemach Tzedek rebuilt** in Lubavitch after decades of Soviet-era neglect; renewed pilgrimage to the graves of the Rebbes becomes possible                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **1989**                 | 5749          | Seventh Rebbe, speaking to a Berlin Jewish activist about the Wall, says: **"Es vet nisht blaybn af lang" — "It won't last long."** The Berlin Wall falls weeks later                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **1990**                 | 5750          | **Soviet Union disintegrates** — fulfilling the Rashab's prophecy (c. 1920) to the Rayatz: "But eventually, the Communist government will disintegrate." Chabad institutions reopen throughout the former Soviet Union                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **1994**                 | 5754          | Seventh Rebbe **Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson dies** (3 Tammuz 5754 / June 12, 1994) in New York. By this time, Chabad has established institutions in over 100 countries                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **1996**                 | 5756          | **Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel** arrives in Berlin to establish Chabad presence — beginning the movement's extraordinary post-Holocaust return to Germany. Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin eventually houses a synagogue (250 seats), school (former Nazi officers' club), restaurant, mikveh, library, and youth center                                                                                                                      |
| **2001**                 | 5761          | **Lubavitch Museum opens** — commemorating the history of the town and the 483 Jews murdered in 1941. See [[summaries/lubavitch|ליובאוויטש]]                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **3 Kislev 5762** (2001) | 5762          | **Berlin memorial plaque** installed by the Berlin city government at 7 Hanse-Ufer Street — the building where the future seventh Rebbe lived while studying at the University of Berlin (1928–1931). First time Berlin officially honored a Jewish personality with such a plaque. Mayor Joachim Zeller: "In those days, antisemitic signs were placed in Berlin. Today we place a memorial sign for the great Jewish leader." |
