# אדמו"רי חב"ד ויהדות אוסטריה

## Chabad Rebbes and Austrian Jewry

**Series:** מסביב לחסידות (Mesibbv L'Chassidus), Vol. 12 (Vol. 16 in the extended series)  
**Publisher:** Otzar HaChassidim, 770 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn  
**Editor:** R' Shalom DovBer Levin  
**Source type:** Regional Jewish history and Chabad-community relations

---

## Overview

This volume documents the relationship between the Chabad Rebbes and the Jewish community of the Austro-Hungarian Empire and its successor state, Austria. The Austrian Jewish community, centered in Vienna, was one of Europe's most culturally significant and rapidly modernizing Jewish communities in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries — in many ways the opposite of the traditional Eastern European world from which Chabad came.

The Chabad-Austrian connection developed primarily through the Rebbes' medical journeys to Austrian health resorts and through specific missions on behalf of Russian Jewish interests that required representation in Vienna.

---

## Structure of the Volume

**The Rashab Period (Rebbe Rashab, 1882–1920)**  
The Rashab made several trips to the Austrian Empire for medical treatment. Vienna was a center of advanced European medicine, and the Rashab — who suffered from respiratory problems — sought treatment from leading Viennese physicians. These medical visits created sustained contacts with Austrian Jewish communities and rabbis.

The Rashab's visits to **Marienbad** and other Austro-Hungarian health resorts are documented in both this volume and the Germany volume (Book 13), as these spa towns attracted the Chabad leadership during the era when Russian Jews could travel abroad relatively freely (pre-WWI).

**The Rayatz Period (1920–1950)**  
After WWI dissolved the Austro-Hungarian Empire, Austria remained a transit point and location for Chabad activities. The Rayatz maintained correspondence and relations with Austrian Jewish figures.

**The Contemporary Rebbe Period**  
The Rebbe (R' Menachem Mendel Schneerson) and his engagement with Austrian Jewry in the post-WWII period, when Austrian Jews were rebuilding under very different circumstances.

---

## The Medical-Political Context

The Rashab's Austrian visits were not purely medical. The Austro-Hungarian Empire, while hardly free of antisemitism, offered Russian Jewish leaders relatively more freedom of movement and public activity than the Czarist system. The Rashab used his time in Austria and German-speaking regions to engage in political and communal activities: meeting with rabbinic and lay leaders, discussing the situation of Russian Jews, and representing Chabad's interests.

The contrast between Chabad's home territory (Czarist Russia with its severe restrictions on Jews) and the more modern Central European environment created interesting encounters between traditional Chassidic leadership and more acculturated Western Jewish communities.

---

## Holocaust Impact

Austrian Jewry was devastated in the Holocaust. Vienna's Jewish community of approximately 185,000 in 1938 was almost entirely murdered or expelled. The Anschluss (1938) and subsequent deportations eliminated the community with shocking speed. Any Chabad-Austrian connections that had developed over the previous decades were severed.
