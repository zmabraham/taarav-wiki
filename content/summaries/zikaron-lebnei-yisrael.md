# זכרון לבני ישראל

## Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael (A Memorial for the Children of Israel)

**Series:** מסביב לחסידות (Mesibbv L'Chassidus), Vol. 6  
**Publisher:** Otzar HaChassidim, 770 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn  
**Editor:** R' Shalom DovBer Levin  
**Author:** R' Yisrael Dzeikaobson (Jacobson)  
**Written:** 1958 (at instruction of the Rebbe); published serially in Di Yiddishe Heim quarterly until author's death 17 Sivan 1975  
**Length:** 2,448 lines

---

## Overview

These are the memoirs of R' Yisrael Jacobson, written in 1958 at the explicit instruction of the Rebbe (R' Menachem Mendel Schneerson) during a private audience (yechidus). The Rebbe directed Jacobson to record his memories for posterity, creating a firsthand account of Chabad life spanning from Tsarist Russia to American Chabad's founding years.

The memoirs were serialized in Di Yiddishe Heim, the Chabad women's periodical, making them unusual: a man's memoirs published in a women's magazine, reflecting their broad value as history rather than gender-specific content.

---

## Yeshiva Years (1908–1918)

Jacobson's account of his time in Tomchei Tmimim yeshiva provides a rare ground-level view of the flagship Chabad institution:

- **Lubavitch (the main yeshiva):** Life at the center of the Chabad world, in proximity to the Rebbe
- **Shchedrin:** A branch yeshiva
- **Kremenchug:** Another city of study
- **Kherson:** The southernmost location

His descriptions of student life, the relationship with the Rebbe Rashab, the curriculum, and the atmosphere of intense religious seriousness in the final years before the Soviet revolution provide irreplaceable documentation.

---

## In the Rebbes' Presence

A central theme of the memoirs is direct experience with the Rebbes — the sacred encounter that defined chassidic life. Jacobson describes time near the Rebbe Rashab and later the Rayatz, conveying the texture of Chabad court life in its Russian period.

---

## Homel (Gomel) Years (1920–1925)

After the Revolution, Jacobson was based in **Homel** (Gomel, Belarus), which had a significant Jewish community and remained active despite growing Soviet pressures. His account of Jewish life in early Soviet Homel captures the anxious transitional period when it was still possible to maintain some religious life but the signs of what was coming were visible.

---

## New York from 1926

Jacobson emigrated to America in 1926, arriving before the main wave of Soviet-era Chabad immigrants. In New York he became a key institutional figure:

**Founding Agudas HaTmimim:** He helped establish this organization of Tomchei Tmimim graduates in America, creating the organizational infrastructure that would later support broader Chabad activities.

**Managing Agudas Chassidei Chabad:** He played a central role in running the umbrella Chabad organization in America during its formative years.

**The Rayatz's American Visit (1929–1930):** When the Rayatz visited America, Jacobson was at the center of organizing and facilitating this historic visit. The Rayatz came to raise funds and support for Chabad institutions under Soviet persecution, and ultimately settled in America after his escape from Poland in 1940. Jacobson's account of the 1929–1930 visit provides inside documentation of how American Chabad began.

---

## The Rebbe's Instruction

The circumstance of composition is itself significant: a Rebbe directing a chassid to write memoirs for publication represents the Chabad approach to history as sacred preservation. The Rebbe understood that Jacobson's memories were irreplaceable documentation of a world that no longer existed.
