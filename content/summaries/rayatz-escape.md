# The Rayatz's Escape from Europe

**Subject**: The dramatic escape of [[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn]] from Nazi-occupied Europe
**Period**: September 1939 – March 1940
**Route**: Warsaw → Riga → Stockholm → New York

---

## Overview

The escape of the Rayatz from war-torn Europe is one of the most dramatic episodes in Chabad history. When Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939, the Rayatz was in [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]] — directly in the path of the Nazi advance. Over the following six months, a combination of diplomatic pressure, political connections, and miraculous timing brought him safely to [[entities/places/crown-heights|America]].

The escape was orchestrated through the combined efforts of Chabad chassidim, American Jewish organizations, and sympathetic government officials in multiple countries. It became a defining narrative of Chabad resilience and divine providence.

---

## Bombing of Warsaw (September 1939)

When the Luftwaffe began bombing Warsaw on September 1, 1939, the Rayatz was staying at his home in the city. The building was hit and partially destroyed. The Rayatz, who suffered from multiple health conditions, was buried under rubble before being rescued by his followers.

Despite the chaos, the Rayatz refused to leave Warsaw without ensuring that his manuscript collections and sacred texts were preserved. His followers worked under bombardment to salvage what they could of the Lubavitch library.

---

## Diplomatic Efforts

The rescue operation involved multiple actors:

- **American Jewish organizations** lobbied the U.S. State Department to intervene
- **Senator [[entities/people/sen-byrnes|James F. Byrnes]]** assisted with visa arrangements
- **[[entities/people/mordechai-dubin|Mordechai Dubin]]**, the Latvian Jewish leader, used his diplomatic connections
- **[[entities/people/jan-zwartendijk|Jan Zwartendijk]]** (Dutch Consul in Kovno) provided crucial transit documentation
- **[[entities/people/chiune-sugihara|Chiune Sugihara]]** (Japanese Consul in Kovno) issued transit visas through Japan

The combined pressure from these sources eventually secured safe passage for the Rayatz through the Soviet Union and the Baltic states.

---

## The Route to Freedom

The Rayatz's journey out of occupied Europe passed through several stages:

1. **Warsaw to Riga** (late 1939) — Smuggled out of occupied Poland through the assistance of chassidim and diplomats
2. **[[entities/places/riga|Riga]], Latvia** — Brief stay where the Rayatz continued his work and met with chassidim who had also escaped
3. **Stockholm, Sweden** — Transit point where the Rayatz's health was stabilized
4. **[[events/america-arrival-1940|Arrival in America]]** (March 1940) — The Rayatz landed in New York on 9 Adar II 5700 (March 1940)

---

## Significance

The Rayatz's escape had enormous consequences for Chabad and world Jewry:

- **Rebuilding in America**: The Rayatz established Chabad's new headquarters at [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]] in [[entities/places/crown-heights|Crown Heights]], Brooklyn
- **Institutional founding**: He created [[events/founding-merkos|Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch]], [[events/founding-machne-israel|Machne Israel]], and [[events/founding-kehot|Kehot Publication Society]]
- **Continuity**: His survival ensured the unbroken chain of Chabad leadership, leading to the eventual transition to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]
- **Spiritual narrative**: The escape became a powerful story of divine providence that reinforced Chabad's message of faith under persecution
