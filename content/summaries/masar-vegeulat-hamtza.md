# מאסר וגאולת אדמו"ר האמצעי

## Imprisonment and Liberation of the Mitteler Rebbe

**Series:** מסביב לחסידות (Mesibbv L'Chassidus), Vol. 1  
**Publisher:** Otzar HaChassidim, 770 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn  
**Editor:** R' Shalom DovBer Levin  
**Source type:** Documentary collection — translated Russian state documents, police records, and Rebbe's letters

---

## Overview

This volume is a primary-source documentary collection, not a narrative history. It reproduces actual Russian imperial court and police records from 1825–1828 pertaining to the arrest, investigation, and three-stage liberation of Rabbi DovBer Schneersohn, the Mitteler Rebbe (second Chabad Rebbe), alongside the Rebbe's own letters written during the ordeal.

The documentary approach gives the book unusual historical weight: readers encounter the bureaucratic machinery of the Czarist state directly, including the language and categories through which Russian authorities processed a Chassidic leader.

---

## The Arrest

The Mitteler Rebbe was arrested in 1825 (5586) on charges initiated by an informant named **Simcha Kissin of Vitebsk**, who accused the Rebbe of various offenses including collecting money for the Land of Israel (which could be construed as seditious activity) and other charges. The documents reveal Governor **Nikolai Khavanski** as the key administrative figure overseeing the case.

In Russian imperial documents, the Rebbe is referred to by his Russian name **"Berkeh Shniur"** — a bureaucratic transliteration of his Yiddish name and father's name (Ber, son of Shneur).

---

## Three Phases of Liberation

What makes this case historically remarkable is that full legal exoneration came only after the Rebbe's death, in three distinct phases:

1. **10 Kislev 5586 (1825):** Local release — the Rebbe is freed from immediate custody in Vitebsk/the local district pending further proceedings. This date became the first day of Chabad's liberation celebration.

2. **11 Kislev 5587 (1826):** The Mogilev regional court formally acquits the Rebbe of all charges. This represented official judicial vindication at the gubernia (province) level.

3. **21 Kislev 5588 (1827):** The Russian Imperial Senate in Petersburg issues its final confirmation of acquittal — but this arrives approximately twelve days after the Rebbe's death on **9 Kislev 5588**. The full legal exoneration was thus posthumous.

The 10th of Kislev became the primary holiday of liberation celebrated in Chabad communities, commemorating the first phase when the Rebbe was physically freed.

---

## The Documents

The book reproduces a wide range of document types:

- **Police investigation records** detailing the charges and witness statements
- **Court proceedings** from multiple levels of the Russian judicial hierarchy
- **The Rebbe's own letters** written during the period of the investigation, which demonstrate his continued spiritual leadership even while under scrutiny
- **Gubernia (provincial) court rulings**
- **Senate decrees** from Petersburg

The contrast between the dry bureaucratic Russian prose and the Rebbe's Hebrew/Yiddish letters illuminates the double life Chabad leadership navigated: subject to imperial law while simultaneously directing a major religious movement.

---

## Historical Significance

The arrest and liberation of the Mitteler Rebbe is the foundational trauma-and-triumph narrative of early Chabad history, analogous in communal memory to the Alter Rebbe's earlier arrest in 1798. It established the pattern of Chabad Rebbes facing state persecution — later repeated with the Rayatz in Soviet times.

The Rebbe continued leading the Chabad community despite the legal cloud hanging over him, maintaining correspondence and spiritual guidance throughout. The posthumous nature of the final exoneration became a source of both poignancy and theological reflection in Chabad tradition.
