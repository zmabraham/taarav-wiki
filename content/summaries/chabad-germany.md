# אדמו"רי חב"ד ויהדות גרמניה

## Chabad Rebbes and German Jewry

**Series:** מסביב לחסידות (Mesibbv L'Chassidus), Vol. 13 (Vol. 17 in the extended series)  
**Publisher:** Otzar HaChassidim, 770 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn  
**Editor:** R' Shalom DovBer Levin  
**Source type:** Regional Jewish history, Rebbe correspondence, and memoir  
**Structure:** Five parts; approximately 3,000 lines total across parts

---

## Overview

This multi-part volume is the most substantial of the "Chabad Rebbes and [community]" books, reflecting the depth and complexity of the relationship between Chabad leadership and German Jewry. The book covers the Rashab's extensive sojourns in Germany, the Rayatz's Berlin years (1928–1933), the contemporary Rebbe's studies in Berlin (1928–1931), and the post-WWII Chabad renewal in Germany — all documented through actual letters (many reproduced in full), diaries, and first-person accounts.

---

## Part One: The Rebbes and Germany (Rashab, Rayatz, Contemporary Rebbe)

### The Rashab in Germany (1880s–1914)

The Rashab (R' Shalom DovBer Schneersohn) made multiple extended visits to Germany for medical treatment and communal purposes:

**The Berlin Visit of 1885 (תרמ"ה/5645):**  
At age 24, the Rashab visited Berlin and called on **Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer** — the founder of the Neo-Orthodox "Adass Jisroel" congregation and the Berlin Rabbinical Seminary. Hildesheimer received him with great respect, honoring the Maharash (the Rashab's father) through his son. This inaugural contact established the Rashab's relationship with German Orthodox Jewry.

**The Berlin Aquarium Visit (1889):**  
The Rashab, while traveling through Berlin, visited the aquarium specifically to observe what he believed might be the chilazon — the sea creature from which the biblical sky-blue dye (techelet) was extracted. He saw a small creature that secreted black liquid; not realizing it might be the chilazon, he did not study it closely. He reported this observation twenty years later in correspondence with the Radzyner Rebbe who was researching techelet.

**Würzburg Year (1906–1907/תרס"ז):**  
The Rashab spent an entire year in **Würzburg, Bavaria** — one of his longest sojourns outside Russia. He arrived in Cheshvan 5667 (November 1906) and returned to Lubavitch after Shavuot 5667 (1907). The Rayatz joined him in Kislev (December 1906).

The year in Würzburg was extraordinarily productive:

- The Rashab visited the Kaiser Wilhelm's writing room (through the Rayatz's bribery of a guard) and wrote a Chassidic discourse (maamar) on the Kaiser's personal stationery using the Kaiser's pen — a remarkable act of appropriating imperial space for spiritual purposes
- He composed the famous "Hemshech Samech Vov" (המשך תרס"ו) — one of the most profound and lengthy Chassidic philosophical discourses in the Chabad canon, completed in Würzburg after being forced to leave Lubavitch when Zionist workers attacked the Rebbe's household
- He engaged in extensive communal work, including drafting a detailed plan ("התאגדות היראים") with Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Breuer and Rabbi Yaakov Rosenheim for strengthening Orthodox Judaism in Germany
- On Yud-Tes Kislev (the Chabad liberation holiday) in Würzburg, he held a farbrengen (Chassidic gathering) attended by German Jews who were deeply impressed by the Rashab's prayer style — which lasted over two hours
- His disdain for Kaiser Wilhelm II was intense: he called him "Amalek" and "vill-he-lem" (a Yiddish pun meaning "wants to destroy the world"), prophesying Germany's ultimate defeat

**Correspondence with German Rabbis:**  
Part Four documents the Rashab's sustained correspondence with several German rabbinical figures:

- **Rabbi Michael HaKohen of Fulda** — warmly personal letters, including a birthday blessing letter rich with Torah content; the Rashab prayed at the Rebbes' ohalim on behalf of Rabbi Michael's health
- **Rabbi Pinchas HaKohen of Ansbach** — correspondence about supporting Russian Jewry through Agudas Yisrael
- **Rabbi Yaakov Rosenheim** — Agudas Yisrael president; worked with the Rashab on the "Histaadus HaYereim" organization
- **The Breuer family of Frankfurt** — ongoing relationship with the Orthodox community
- **Rabbi Meir Hildesheimer of Berlin** — son of Azriel Hildesheimer; the Rashab praised him as the first to genuinely engage with the situation of Russian Jews

### German Jewish Intervention to Free the Rayatz (1927)

Before the Rayatz settled in Berlin, German Jewry played a decisive role in securing his release from Soviet imprisonment. When the Rayatz was arrested and sentenced to death in June 1927, an extraordinary cross-denominational coalition intervened:

- **Rabbi Dr. Meir Hildesheimer** (head of the Orthodox Berlin Rabbinical Seminary) and **Dr. Leo Baeck** (leader of Berlin Liberal Jewry) — leaders from opposite religious poles — **jointly petitioned** Dr. Oskar Cohn to use his political connections
- **Dr. Oskar Cohn** (a Jewish socialist Reichstag member) met with Foreign Minister **Stresemann** and Vice-Reichskanzler **Weissmann**, who in turn pressured Soviet Ambassador Krestinsky
- Cohn also contacted Soviet Foreign Commissar **Chicherin** — his personal friend — directly
- The **Frankfurt Orthodox community** sent a formal rabbinical appointment letter to provide a face-saving rationale for Soviet release
- The Rayatz was freed on 12–13 Tammuz 5687 (July 1927)

After liberation, the Rayatz's first letter from Riga specifically thanked Meir Hildesheimer. When the Rayatz arrived in Berlin in Kislev 5688 (December 1927), he personally visited Vice-Reichskanzler Weissmann to express gratitude.

### The Rayatz in Berlin (1928–1933)

After escaping Soviet Russia in 1927, the Rayatz settled in **Berlin** from 1928 to 1933. The book documents this crucial period when Chabad's center temporarily relocated to Germany's capital — perhaps the most culturally and politically charged location for a Jewish religious leader in the late Weimar Republic.

Key documented episodes from the Berlin years include:

- **Kislev 5688 (December 1927)**: Rayatz arrived in Berlin for JDC budget meetings to arrange support for Russian Jews; attended by Hildesheimer, Dr. Razin, Dr. Cohn, and Reb Shmaryahu Gurary
- **Shvat 5688 (early 1928)**: Meeting with Rabbi Meir Munk (son of Rabbi Azriel Munk) in Berlin
- **Adar II 5689 (1929)**: Berlin conference for Passover matzah campaign for Russian Jews — delegates from London, Basel, Vienna, Frankfurt, Hamburg, Warsaw
- **Tammuz 5689 (1929)**: The Rayatz journeyed to Eretz Yisrael via Berlin — the future Rebbe and Chaya Mushka met him at the Berlin station (documented in his diary)
- **Tammuz 5690 (1930)**: Rayatz returned from America to Berlin; the future Rebbe and Chaya Mushka again met at the station
- **Tisha B'Av 5690**: The future Rebbe kept a detailed diary entry about Tisha B'Av spent with the Rayatz in Berlin
- **Tevet 5693 (winter 1932–33)**: Rayatz spent 2+ months in a Berlin sanatorium; "only my middle son-in-law [the future Rebbe] came to me daily at a fixed time"

### The Contemporary Rebbe in Berlin (1928–1931)

R' Menachem Mendel Schneerson — the future seventh Rebbe — studied in Berlin from 1928 to 1931. He lived at **7 Hanse-Ufer Street** (later confirmed through archival research). The Rebbe studied at the University of Berlin before continuing studies in Paris. His Berlin years preceded his assumption of Chabad leadership in 1951.

A memorial plaque was installed by the Berlin city government on 3 Kislev 5762 (2001) at the site of the Rebbe's Berlin residence — the first time Berlin officially honored a Jewish personality with such a plaque. Mayor Joachim Zeller noted at the ceremony that the plaque represented a new era: "In those days, antisemitic signs were placed in Berlin. Today we place a memorial sign for the great Jewish leader."

## Part Five: The Rashab and World War One

The Rashab's attitude toward Germany and Kaiser Wilhelm during WWI is documented through diary entries and letters:

- He watched with satisfaction as the English ultimatum weakened German overconfidence
- He called Wilhelm II "Amalek" and an atheist — more dangerous than Russia despite Jews' suffering under the Czar
- He composed a Pidyon Nefesh (redemptive petition) praying for Russian victory, sending daily minyanim of yeshiva students to the Rebbes' ohalim in Lubavitch with this prayer
- He told his son the Rayatz: "Der Deitsh vet einnemen Rusland, er vet ihr shlingen, un mit ihr vet er zikh dervergn" — "The German will conquer Russia, he will swallow it, and it will choke him"

## Post-Holocaust Chabad in Berlin

The book concludes with the contemporary Chabad renewal in Berlin, led by **Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel** (who arrived in Berlin in 1996) and Rabbi Shmuel Segel. Against all historical expectations, Chabad established:

- A major Chabad center ("Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin") in central Berlin, funded by local Jewish donors
- A synagogue seating 250
- A Jewish school ("Yudishe Traditsyone Shule Ohr Avner") in a building that had served as a Nazi officers' club
- A Jewish restaurant, mikveh, library, and youth center
- Regular Shabbat minyanim that grew from 20 people to hundreds

Chancelllor Angela Merkel acknowledged the significance of a Jewish community self-funding its own institutions as evidence of genuine Jewish confidence in Germany's future.

The book frames this renewal as the fulfillment of the Rebbe's vision (expressed to a Berlin Jewish activist in 1989): "Es vet nisht blaybn af lang" — "It won't last long" (referring to the Berlin Wall), and his instruction that Jewish activity must continue in both East and West Germany.

---

## Historical Significance

The Germany volume reveals Chabad's engagement with the most consequential tensions of modern Jewish history: the relationship between traditional Eastern European Jewry and modern Western Jewish communities, the catastrophe of the Holocaust, and the astonishing post-Holocaust renewal. The Rashab's deep hostility to Germany stands in stark irony alongside his grandson's (the future Rebbe's) studies there and the ultimate Chabad re-establishment in Berlin.
