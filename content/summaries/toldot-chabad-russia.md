# תולדות חב״ד ברוסיה הצארית

**Chabad History in Czarist Russia — Selected Chapters**

**Editor**: Rabbi Shalom DovBer Levin  
**Publisher**: Otzar HaChassidim, 770 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn NY  
**Year**: 5770 (2010)  
**Series**: מסביב לחסידות (Around Chassidism)  
**Coverage**: 5530–5680 (1770–1920), six Rebbe generations

---

## Overview

This volume, the fourth in the _Toldot Chabad_ series, presents selected chapters from Chabad history in Czarist Russia spanning the movement's entire pre-Soviet era — from the founding generation of the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] through the early years of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] leadership. Unlike the other volumes (which cover the Holy Land, America, and Soviet Russia), this volume covers the original Russian heartland of the movement — the region that gave Chabad its character and identity.

Rabbi Shalom DovBer Levin, the compiler, drew on decades of work editing Chabad's epistolary literature (_Igrot Kodesh_ of the Rebbes). His articles, previously published in journals including _Yagdil Torah_, _Kfar Chabad_, and _Pardess Chabad_, were gathered, revised, and organized chronologically by Rebbe generation.

The volume documents Chabad's relationship with Russian imperial authorities — at times hostile, at times grudgingly tolerant — and the movement's internal social history: its communities, institutions, financial structures, and extraordinary figures.

## Structure: Six Rebbe Generations

### Generation 1 — [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] (Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi)

Founding generation (c.1770–1812). Covers the Alter Rebbe's two imprisonments in St. Petersburg (1798, 1800), his release on 19 Kislev — celebrated annually as "Yud-Tet Kislev" — and the flight from Liadi during Napoleon's invasion of Russia (1812), during which the Alter Rebbe died.

### Generation 2 — [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (Rabbi DovBer of Lubavitch)

1812–1827. Covers the establishment of Lubavitch as the movement's headquarters, the organization of Chabad communities in White Russia and Ukraine, and critically, the 1826 arrest and liberation (see [[summaries/masar-vegeulat-hamtza|separate volume]]).

### Generation 3 — [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn)

1827–1866. The longest-tenured Rebbe. Covers his opposition to the Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment), his battles against Russian government decrees affecting Jewish education and conscription, and his enormous literary output in halachah and Chassidus.

### Generation 4 — [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Maharash]] (Rabbi Shmuel of Lubavitch)

1866–1882. A period of relative calm. Covers the Maharash's diplomatic efforts on behalf of Russian Jewry, his travels to Western Europe, and the consolidation of Chabad's educational structure.

### Generation 5 — [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] (Rabbi Shalom DovBer of Lubavitch)

1882–1920. A period of growing persecution and crisis. Covers the founding of the Tomchei Temimim yeshiva (1897) in Lubavitch, the Rashab's opposition to Zionism, World War I and the displacement of the movement, and his death in Rostov-on-Don in 1920.

### Generation 6 — [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn)

Early years of leadership (1920–1927, until his exile). Covers the Soviet takeover, the Yevsektsiya's campaign against religious Jews, and the heroic underground continuation of Chabad activities in the new Soviet state.

## Key Themes

**Chabad and Russian Imperial Authorities**: The movement navigated complex relationships with successive tsars — from Catherine II's suspicion of Chassidism to the reforms of Alexander II. The Rebbes often served as _shtadlanim_ (intercessors) for broader Russian Jewry.

**Internal Chabad Community Life**: The book documents the rise of _anash_ (Chabad followers) communities in towns across White Russia, Ukraine, and Lithuania — their communal structures, _kollelim_ (stipend-supported scholars), and institutions.

**The Role of Lubavitch Town**: The town of [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] itself was the nerve center — from the Mitteler Rebbe's arrival (~1813) through the Rashab's departure in 1915. See also [[summaries/lubavitch|ליובאוויטש]].

**Education and Yeshivos**: The founding of Tomchei Temimim in 1897 was a transformational moment — the first formal Chabad yeshiva, designed to train a new generation of _temimim_ (sincere students) in both Chassidus and Talmud.
