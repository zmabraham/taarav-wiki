# Bereishit — In the Beginning

**Hebrew:** בְּרֵאשִׁית | **Book:** Bereishit (Genesis)

## Summary
The first parsha covers the creation of the world (six days), the story of Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden, the sin and exile, and the lineage from Adam through Noah.

## Chabad Chassidic Teachings

## The Purpose of Creation: *Dirah BeTachtonim*
The Alter Rebbe teaches that the Torah begins with *bereishit* — "in the beginning" — to signal the *purpose* of creation: God desired a dwelling (*dirah*) in the lowest world. The physical world, despite appearing as the furthest point from Divinity, is precisely where God's deepest desire lies. See [[concepts/dirah-betachtonim|Dirah BeTachtonim]].

## *Beit* — Why Torah Begins with the Second Letter
The Midrash asks why creation begins with *beit* (ב) rather than *alef* (א). Chabad explains: *alef* represents *alufo shel olam* — the Master of the universe in His boundless infinitude. The *beit* — with its opening facing forward (east) — represents the world of Divine *chesed* (kindness) that faces creation. The world was created through and for chesed.

## The Inner Dimension of Adam and Eve
The Baal Shem Tov and Chabad masters explain the Garden of Eden narrative as an inner spiritual map:
- **The Garden** = the state of innocent Divine consciousness
- **The Tree of Knowledge** = the world of self-awareness (*yeshut*) that creates the sense of separation from God
- **The Sin** = the entry of the ego-consciousness (*da'at hatev vhara* — knowledge of good and evil as separate realities) into human experience
- **The Exile** = the galut of consciousness that every soul now experiences

## *Bereishit* and the Beinoni
The Torah begins with *beit*, which the Sages say alludes to *berakhah* (blessing). Chabad teaches that the act of creation is itself a continuous blessing — at every moment God is *actively* choosing to give existence to all beings. This makes every moment of life an ongoing act of divine generosity.
