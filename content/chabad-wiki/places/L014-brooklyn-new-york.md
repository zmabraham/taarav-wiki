# Brooklyn, New York

> Final headquarters of Chabad-Lubavitch and center of global shluchim network

## Overview

Brooklyn, specifically the Crown Heights neighborhood, became the permanent headquarters of Chabad-Lubavitch in 1940. From here, the Rayatz and later the Ramash built a global movement that transformed Jewish life worldwide.

## Significance in Chabad History

### Arrival in America (March 1940)

- **Date:** March 1940
- **Location:** Initially settled in Brooklyn
- **Challenge:** "America iz nisht anderesh" (America is not different) - combating assimilation
- **Mission:** Rebuild Jewish life on new continent

### 770 Eastern Parkway

- **Acquisition:** Rayatz established headquarters at 770 Eastern Parkway
- **Significance:** Became the most famous address in Chabad world
- **Expansion:** Building expanded multiple times
- **Symbol:** 770 replicated worldwide as Chabad centers

### Post-WWII Rebuilding

- **Refugees:** Helped Holocaust survivors rebuild lives
- **Publications:** Established Kehot Publication Society
- **Education:** Founded network of Jewish day schools
- **Outreach:** Pioneered Jewish outreach movement

### Leadership Transition (1950-1951)

- **1950:** Passing of Rayatz in Brooklyn
- **1951:** Ramash (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson) accepts leadership
- **Growth:** Explosive expansion of Chabad activities
- **Shluchim:** Global emissary network launched from Brooklyn

## Crown Heights Community

- **Jewish Community:** Vibrant Chabad community
- **Institutions:** Yeshivas, schools, synagogues, mikvah
- **Population:** Thousands of Chabad families
- **Pilgrimage:** Jews worldwide visit 770

## Global Impact

From Brooklyn, Chabad launched:
- **3,500+ shluchim** (emissary families) worldwide
- **Jewish institutions** in 100+ countries
- **Educational programs** reaching millions
- **Publication of Torah** literature in multiple languages

## Legacy

Brooklyn transformed from a refuge for a displaced Rebbe into the command center for a global Jewish renaissance. The decision to make Brooklyn home proved pivotal for Jewish history.
