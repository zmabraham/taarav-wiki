# Lubavitch (Лубавичи)

> The Chabad capital for 102 years (1813-1915) and spiritual heart of the movement

## Summary

Lubavitch was a small village in White Russia (now in Belarus) that served as the headquarters of the Chabad-Lubavitch movement for over a century. The town's name means "city of brotherly love" in Russian, reflecting the warmth and unity that characterized the Chasidic community there.

The village was nestled in rolling hills and forests, with a population of approximately 2,000 people. It was transformed during market fair days when the population would double as farmers, craftsmen, and vendors from surrounding areas converged for the two-day **yarmarka** (market fair).

## Significance

**Chabad Capital (1813-1915):**
- Rabbi Dovber (the Mitteler Rebbe) settled in Lubavitch in 1813
- Served as residence for four consecutive Rebbes:
  - Rabbi Dovber (Mitteler Rebbe, 1813-1827)
  - Rabbi Menachem Mendel (Tzemach Tzedek, 1827-1866)
  - Rabbi Shmuel (Maharash, 1866-1882)
  - Rabbi Shalom Dovber (Rashab, 1882-1915)

**Spiritual Oasis:**
- Known as an "oasis of sanctity" where people could set aside the difficulties of austere Czarist Russia
- Home to the renowned **Yeshivas Tomchei Temimim**, founded in 1897
- Vibrant community life with numerous charitable institutions
- Center of Torah learning and Chasidic teachings

**Community Life:**
- The Malbish Arumim society provided clothes for the poor
- Free loan funds (gemachim) supported merchants and craftsmen
- Active marketplace and commercial center
- Population swelled during market fairs, particularly the summer yarmarka on June 29-30

## Related Events

- **1813:** Rabbi Dovber settles in Lubavitch, establishing it as Chabad headquarters
- **1827:** Tzemach Tzedek becomes Rebbe in Lubavitch
- **1866:** Rabbi Shmuel (Maharash) becomes fourth Rebbe
- **1880:** Birth of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak (Rayatz) in Lubavitch on 12 Tamuz
- **1891:** Young Yosef Yitzchak's first arrest at age 11 during yarmarka
- **1897:** Founding of Yeshivas Tomchei Temimim
- **1915:** Rashab flees to Rostov-on-Don due to World War I

## Physical Description

- Population: ~2,000 residents
- Architecture: Traditional wooden houses and stone buildings
- Economy: Agriculture, trade, craftsmanship
- Setting: Rolling hills and forests of White Russia
- Atmosphere: Festive during market days, spiritual year-round
