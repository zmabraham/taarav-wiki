# Warsaw, Poland

> Pre-WWII refuge and base for rebuilding Chabad in Europe

## Overview

Warsaw (Warszawa) was the capital of Poland and served as the Rayatz's European headquarters from 1934 to 1939. It became a center for rebuilding Chabad institutions after leaving the Soviet Union.

## Significance in Chabad History

### Refuge from Soviet Union (1934)

- **Arrival:** Rayatz settled in Warsaw after leaving Soviet Union
- **Freedom:** Relative religious freedom compared to USSR
- **Center:** Established new headquarters for international Chabad work
- **Communication:** Could correspond with followers worldwide

### Rebuilding Activities

- **Publications:** Printed and disseminated Chabad teachings
- **Correspondence:** Thousands of letters to chassidim globally
- **Organizations:** Coordinated Jewish education and support networks
- **Visitors:** Hosted chassidim and Jewish leaders from around the world

### Pre-WWII Growth

- **Yeshivas:** Supported Tomchei Temimim branches in Poland
- **Institutions:** Established schools, synagogues, and community organizations
- **Leadership:** Provided guidance to Jewish communities facing rising antisemitism
- **Outreach:** Maintained connections with Chabad centers worldwide

### WWII Crisis (1939)

- **September 1939:** Nazi invasion of Poland
- **Destruction:** Warsaw's Jewish community was devastated
- **Escape:** Rayatz fled Warsaw with tremendous difficulty
- **Loss:** Most of Polish Jewry, including many chassidim, perished in the Holocaust

## City Context

- **Interwar Period:** Vibrant Jewish cultural and religious center
- **Jewish Population:** Approximately 350,000 Jews (one-third of city)
- **Religious Life:** Hundreds of synagogues, yeshivas, and Jewish schools
- **Destruction:** Warsaw Ghetto and uprising; nearly complete destruction of Jewish life

## Legacy

Warsaw represented a brief but important period of rebuilding and freedom between Soviet oppression and Nazi destruction. The work done from Warsaw laid groundwork for post-war reconstruction.
