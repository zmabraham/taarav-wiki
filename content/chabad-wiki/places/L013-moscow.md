# Moscow (Москва)

> Capital of Soviet Union and center of anti-religious campaigns against Judaism

## Summary

Moscow served as the capital of the Soviet Union and was the seat of power for the communist regime's anti-religious policies. It housed the Kremlin, central government offices, and the headquarters of the G.P.U. secret police that orchestrated the persecution of Jewish religious leaders, including the arrest of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn in 1927.

## Significance

**Political Center:**
- Capital of Soviet Union (1918-1991)
- Seat of communist government
- Headquarters of secret police (Cheka → G.P.U. → NKVD → KGB)
- Central planning for anti-religious campaigns

**Anti-Religious Campaigns:**
- **Yevsektzia** headquartered in Moscow
- Centralized planning for persecution of religion
- Coordination of arrests and trials
- Policy formulation for suppressing Judaism

**Jewish Community:**
- Historic but smaller Jewish population
- **Choral Synagogue** - Main synagogue (under constant surveillance)
- Underground religious activities
- Center for Jewish communist activities

## Related Events

- **1918:** Bolshevik government moves capital to Moscow
- **1918-1920s:** Yevkom and Yevsektzia activities
- **1920s:** Coordinated anti-religious campaigns
- **1927:** G.P.U. orders arrest of Rayatz in Leningrad
- **1930s-1940s:** Great Terror and continued persecutions
