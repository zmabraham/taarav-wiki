# Rostov-on-Don

> Refuge of the Rashab during World War I and final years of the fifth Rebbe

## Overview

Rostov-on-Don (Ростов-на-Дону) is a city in southern Russia, located on the Don River, approximately 1,000 kilometers south of Moscow. It served as a temporary refuge for Chabad leadership during World War I and became the final resting place of the Rashab.

## Significance in Chabad History

### World War I Refuge (1915-1920)

- **1915:** The Rashab (Rabbi Shalom Dovber Schneersohn) relocated from Lubavitch to Rostov
- **Reason:** German advance during World War I made Lubavitch unsafe
- **Community:** Established a new Chabad center in this southern city
- **Continued Leadership:** The Rashab continued leading Chabad from exile in Rostov

### Death of the Rashab (1920)

- **1920:** The Rashab passed away in Rostov on 2 Nissan 5680
- **Burial:** He was buried in the local Jewish cemetery
- **Historic Note:** Rostov became a holy site due to the Rebbe's resting place

### Under Soviet Rule

- **1920s:** The Rayatz visited Rostov to visit his father's grave
- **Communist Era:** The Jewish cemetery and gravesite faced threats from Soviet authorities
- **Preservation:** Chassidim maintained the gravesite despite persecution

## City Context

- **Location:** Gateway to the Caucasus region
- **Economy:** Major port and commercial center on the Don River
- **Jewish Community:** Established Jewish presence with active religious life
- **Climate:** Milder winters than northern Russia

## Modern Era

- **Post-Soviet:** The gravesite of the Rashab in Rostov has been restored
- **Pilgrimage:** Chabad chassidim visit Rostov to pray at the Rebbe's resting place
- **Ohel:** The gravesite serves as a place of prayer and inspiration
