# Leningrad (St. Petersburg)

> Site of the 1927 arrest and liberation of the Rayatz

## Overview

Leningrad (formerly Saint Petersburg, Petrograd; modern-day Saint Petersburg) was the capital of the Soviet Union during the 1920s and the location of the Rayatz's dramatic 1927 arrest, imprisonment, and ultimate liberation on 12-13 Tammuz.

## Name Changes

- **1703-1914:** St. Petersburg (Санкт-Петербург)
- **1914-1924:** Petrograd (Петроград)
- **1924-1991:** Leningrad (Ленинград)
- **1991-present:** Saint Petersburg

## Significance in Chabad History

### Rayatz's Activities (1924-1927)

- **1924:** The Rayatz moved to Leningrad to intensify underground Jewish work
- **Headquarters:** Center for clandestine Jewish education and religious life
- **Underground Network:** Coordinated resistance against Soviet anti-religious campaigns
- **Danger:** Constant threat of GPU (secret police) surveillance

### The 1927 Arrest

- **Date:** 15 Sivan 5687 (June 14, 1927)
- **Time:** 2:15 AM midnight raid
- **Location:** The Rayatz's home at 22 Mochovaya Street
- **Arresting Officials:** Nachmanson and other GPU agents
- **Prison:** Transported to infamous Spalerno Prison

### Spalerno Prison

- **Location:** Central Leningrad
- **Reputation:** Notorious GPU prison for political prisoners
- **Conditions:** Harsh interrogation, isolation, humiliation
- **Duration:** 18 days, 11 hours, 15 minutes of imprisonment
- **Spiritual Victory:** Rayatz wrote maamar on Rosh Chodesh Tammuz

### Liberation Celebration

- **Date:** 12-13 Tammuz 5687 (July 12-13, 1927)
- **Event:** Complete liberation announced on Rayatz's 47th birthday
- **Celebration:** Massive Jewish celebration across Leningrad
- **Historic Impact:** 12-13 Tammuz became a Chabad holiday

### Jewish Community

- **Size:** Large Jewish population in 1920s
- **Institutions:** Underground synagogues, schools, mikvah
- **Chief Rabbi:** Rabbi Dovid Tevel Katzenelenbogen
- **Persecution:** Constant pressure from Soviet authorities and Yevsektzia

## Post-1927 Period

- **Continued Pressure:** Rayatz remained under surveillance
- **1928:** Forced to leave Soviet Union
- **Underground Work:** Chabad activities continued despite persecution
- **Great Terror:** 1930s purges decimated Jewish leadership
