# Riga, Latvia

> Exile headquarters of the Rayatz and Chabad leadership (1927-1934)

## Summary

Riga, the capital of Latvia, served as the headquarters for Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (Rayatz) and the Chabad movement from 1927 to 1934. Located in the independent Republic of Latvia between the World Wars, Riga provided refuge for the Chabad leadership fleeing Soviet persecution.

## Significance

**Strategic Refuge:**
- Geographically cut off from Russian Chasidim who couldn't cross borders
- Independent country with relative religious freedom compared to Soviet Union
- Safe haven from Bolshevik persecution

**Chabad Community:**
- Modest but growing Chabad community since 1878
- Enhanced by recent emigration from Russia
- Multiple Chabad synagogues throughout the city
- Three adjacent sanctuaries in one structure for different Chabad groups (Lubavitch, Liadi, Kopust)

**Leadership Center:**
- Rayatz established his residence here after exile from Soviet Union
- Directed global Chabad activities from Riga
- Corresponded with Chasidim worldwide
- Maintained connection to underground communities in Soviet Union

**Educational Efforts:**
- No yeshivah initially, unlike Lubavitch's outstanding institutions
- Far cry from Lubavitch's vibrant spiritual community
- Rayatz worked to establish educational framework

## Related Events

- **1927:** Rayatz chooses Riga as exile destination after liberation
- **1927-1934:** Period of residence and leadership from Riga
- **1929:** Efforts to establish yeshivah in Riga
- **1934:** Rayatz moves to Warsaw, Poland
- **1940:** Soviet occupation of Latvia

## Community Description

**Jewish Life:**
- Active Jewish community in independent Latvia
- Multiple synagogues and religious institutions
- Religious freedom compared to Soviet oppression
- Growing refugee population from Soviet Union

**Chabad Presence:**
- Synagogues with three sanctuaries for different Chabad groups
- Community of Chasidim from various backgrounds
- Connection to global Chabad network
- Correspondence hub for Soviet underground
