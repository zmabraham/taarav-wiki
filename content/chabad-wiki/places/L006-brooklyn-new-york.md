# Brooklyn, New York (Crown Heights)

> World headquarters of Chabad-Lubavitch from 1940 to present

## Summary

Brooklyn, specifically the Crown Heights neighborhood, became the permanent world headquarters of the Chabad-Lubavitch movement in 1940 when Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (Rayatz) arrived in America. The movement has remained centered here ever since, with 770 Eastern Parkway serving as the central synagogue and headquarters building.

## Significance

**World Headquarters:**
- Permanent base for Chabad global operations
- Home to **770 Eastern Parkway**, the iconic synagogue and headquarters
- Central administration for worldwide Chabad activities
- Training center for shluchim (emissaries)

**Leadership Residence:**
- **Rayatz** (1940-1950) - Established American headquarters
- **Ramash** (1950-1994) - Built global movement from Brooklyn
- Continues as central hub for Chabad leadership

**Global Center:**
- Administration of thousands of Chabad institutions worldwide
- Publishing house (Kehot Publication Society)
- Yeshivas and educational institutions
- Community services and outreach programs

**Historic Move:**
- March 1940: Rayatz escapes Nazi Europe
- Arrives in America during WWII
- Transplants Eastern European Chabad to Western soil
- Foundation for global expansion

## Related Events

- **1940, March:** Rayatz arrives in America
- **1940s:** Establishment of Chabad institutions in Brooklyn
- **1950:** Passing of Rayatz; Ramash becomes Rebbe
- **1950s-1990s:** Global expansion directed from Brooklyn
- **1994:** Passing of Ramash; headquarters continues in Crown Heights

## 770 Eastern Parkway

**Iconic Building:**
- Central synagogue and study hall
- Rebbe's office and residence
- Architectural replica of 770 built worldwide
- Symbol of Chabad presence

**Community:**
- Crown Heights Chabad community
- Thousands of visitors annually
- Center of Chabad life and learning
- Historic synagogue with spiritual significance
