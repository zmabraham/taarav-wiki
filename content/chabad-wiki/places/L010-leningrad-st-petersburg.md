# Leningrad (St. Petersburg/Petrograd)

> Site of the 1927 arrest and imprisonment of the Rayatz by Soviet authorities

## Summary

Leningrad (formerly St. Petersburg, Petrograd) was the second-largest city in the Soviet Union and the site of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn's dramatic arrest and imprisonment by the G.P.U. secret police in 1927. The city contained several notorious prisons where the Rebbe was held during his 19-day incarceration.

## Significance

**1927 Arrest:**
- Midnight arrest on 14 Sivan 5687 (June 14, 1927)
- Taken from home by armed G.P.U. agents
- Beginning of 19-day imprisonment saga
- Catalyst for international protests and liberation

**Imprisonment Sites:**
- **Spalerno Prison** - Primary incarceration location
- **Shpalernaya Street Prison** - Notorious G.P.U. facility
- **Other detention centers** - Interrogation sites

**Soviet Persecution:**
- Center of anti-religious campaigns
- G.P.U. headquarters and operations
- Targeting of Jewish religious leaders
- Symbol of Soviet oppression of religion

**Historical Name Changes:**
- St. Petersburg (1703-1914) - Imperial capital
- Petrograd (1914-1924) - WWI Russification
- Leningrad (1924-1991) - Soviet period
- St. Petersburg (1991-present) - Post-Soviet

## Related Events

- **1924:** Rayatz moves to Leningrad
- **1924:** Covenant with nine temimim in Leningrad (Adar)
- **1927, June 14 (14 Sivan):** Midnight arrest by G.P.U.
- **1927, June 14-July 3:** Imprisonment in Spalerno
- **1927, July 3:** Exile to Kostroma announced
