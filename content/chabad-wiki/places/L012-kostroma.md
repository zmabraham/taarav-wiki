# Kostroma

> City of exile where liberation was announced on 12 Tammuz 1927

## Overview

Kostroma (Кострома) is a historic city on the Volga River, approximately 300 kilometers northeast of Moscow. It became the place of exile for the Rayatz in 1927 and the site of his miraculous liberation.

## Significance in Chabad History

### Exile Sentence (3 Tammuz 1927)

- **Announcement:** After 18 days in Spalerno Prison, exile to Kostroma announced
- **Sentence:** Three years of internal exile in Kostroma
- **GPU Purpose:** Remove Rayatz from Jewish centers, isolate him from followers
- **Travel:** Train journey from Leningrad to Kostroma

### Arrival in Kostroma

- **First Night:** Stayed in a hotel under GPU guard
- **Conditions:** Ordered to appear before GPU every other day
- **Isolation:** Cut off from chassidim and supporters

### Preparation by Chassidim

- **Reb Michoel Dworkin:** Sent ahead to prepare accommodation
- **Local Community:** Small Jewish community welcomed the Rebbe
- **Living Arrangements:** Found suitable housing for the Rebbe
- **Support:** Despite distance, chassidim maintained contact

### Liberation Announcement (12 Tammuz)

- **Date:** 12 Tammuz 5687 (July 12, 1927)
- **Event:** GPU official summoned the Rebbe and announced complete liberation
- **Birthday:** Also the Rebbe's 47th birthday
- **Divine Timing:** Liberation on his birthday was seen as providential

### Departure Celebration

- **Escort:** Entire Jewish community of Kostroma gave the Rebbe a grand escort
- **Two Days Later:** Returned to Leningrad on 14 Tammuz
- **Celebrations:** News of liberation spread rapidly

## City Context

- **Location:** Historic Golden Ring city northeast of Moscow
- **Volga River:** Major transportation route
- **Jewish Community:** Small but devoted community in 1920s
- **Significance:** Became a symbol of divine providence in Chabad history

## Legacy

The exile to Kostroma and subsequent liberation became one of the most celebrated events in Chabad history. The 12th of Tammuz is marked annually as a day of gratitude and celebration.
