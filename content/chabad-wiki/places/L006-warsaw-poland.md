# Warsaw, Poland

> Pre-WWII refuge and major European Jewish center

## Summary

Warsaw, the capital of Poland, was one of Europe's largest Jewish centers before World War II and served as a refuge for Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (Rayatz) from 1934 to 1940. The city housed a vibrant Jewish community with numerous religious and cultural institutions.

## Significance

**Jewish Center:**
- One of Europe's largest Jewish communities before Holocaust
- Hub of Jewish religious, cultural, and political life
- Multiple synagogues, yeshivos, and Jewish schools
- Center of diverse Jewish movements and organizations

**Chabad Presence:**
- Rayatz moved from Riga to Warsaw in 1934
- Used Warsaw as base for global Chabad leadership
- Corresponded with Chasidim worldwide
- Maintained connections to communities across Europe and America

**Pre-WWII Refuge:**
- Relative safety and stability in interwar Poland
- Center of Jewish publishing and scholarship
- Gateway to Jewish communities worldwide
- Strategic location for international communication

**Historical Context:**
- Independent Poland between World Wars (1918-1939)
- Growing antisemitism in 1930s
- Tensions with Nazi Germany leading to WWII
- Destruction of Jewish community during Holocaust

## Related Events

- **1934:** Rayatz moves from Riga to Warsaw
- **1934-1940:** Period of residence and global leadership
- **1939:** Nazi invasion of Poland
- **1940:** Rayatz escapes to America
- **1940-1945:** Destruction of Warsaw Jewish community
