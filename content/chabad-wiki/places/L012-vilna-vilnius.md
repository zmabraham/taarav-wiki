# Vilna (Vilnius; Vilna; ווילנע)

> "Jerusalem of Lithuania" - preeminent center of Jewish scholarship and culture

## Summary

Vilna (modern Vilnius, Lithuania) was renowned as the "Jerusalem of Lithuania" and was one of the most important centers of Jewish learning, culture, and religious life in Eastern Europe for centuries. The city housed major yeshivos, publishing houses, and Jewish institutions before the Holocaust devastated its community.

## Significance

**Jewish Learning:**
- Home to the **Strashun Library** - largest Jewish library in Europe
- Renowned yeshivos and Torah institutions
- Center of Musar movement
- Publishing capital for Jewish texts

**Cultural Center:**
- Major Jewish writers and thinkers
- Zionist and Bundist activities
- Diverse Jewish religious and secular movements
- Vibrant press and cultural life

**Pre-WWII Community:**
- Large Jewish population (approximately 60,000)
- Multiple synagogues and study halls
- Active religious and communal institutions
- Model of sophisticated Jewish urban life

**Historical Changes:**
- Part of Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth
- Russian Empire period (1795-1918)
- Polish period (1918-1939)
- Soviet annexation (1939-1941)
- Nazi occupation and Holocaust (1941-1944)

## Holocaust Devastation

- **1941-1944:** Nazi occupation
- **Ponary massacres** - Mass murder of Jews
- **Destruction of Jewish community** - 95% perished
- **Loss of institutions** - Libraries, yeshivos, synagogues destroyed
