# Devekut — Cleaving to God

**Hebrew:** דְּבֵקוּת

**Devekut** (from *davak* — to cleave, cling) is the state of intimate union with God — the apex of spiritual achievement in Jewish mysticism. In Chassidus, devekut is both the goal of all service and a constant possibility in daily life.

## Biblical Source
The Torah commands: *"And you shall cleave to Him"* (Devarim 11:22). The Sages ask: how is it possible to cleave to God, who is described as a "consuming fire"? They answer: cleave to His attributes — be compassionate as He is compassionate, kind as He is kind.

Kabbalah and Chassidus deepen this: devekut means genuine consciousness-union, not merely imitation.

## Two Levels of Devekut

### Devekut Through Torah and Mitzvot
The accessible, daily form: when one learns Torah *lishmah* (for its own sake), one's mind cleaves to the Divine mind. When one performs mitzvot with *kavanah* (intention), one's will aligns with the Divine will.

The Alter Rebbe states: the garments of Torah and mitzvot are actually *higher* than the soul itself — they are God's wisdom and will, not merely an expression of it. Therefore, through Torah and mitzvot, a human being can achieve a level of devekut that transcends even the soul's natural capacity.

### Devekut in Prayer
The *Amidah* (standing prayer) is designed as the moment of maximum devekut — standing before the King, absorbed in the Divine presence. At its peak (especially in *Aleinu* and *Mussaf*), the consciousness of the self temporarily dissolves.

## Devekut and the Baal Shem Tov
The Baal Shem Tov made devekut a constant possibility, not an occasional mystical peak:
- **Devekut in everything** — when eating, speak about God; when speaking, remain aware of the Divine presence; when working, serve God through the work
- **"Know Him in all your ways"** — every aspect of life can be made into devekut

## Devekut and Bittul
Devekut requires *bittul* — the self must become transparent for the union to occur. As long as one is preoccupied with oneself (desires, worries, ambitions), one cannot cleave to God.

The path: hitbonenut → bittul → devekut
