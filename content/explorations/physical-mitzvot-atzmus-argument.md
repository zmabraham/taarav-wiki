# The Argument for Physical Mitzvot: Why the Body Matters in the Hemshech

> The Rashab's sustained argument that physical performance of mitzvot draws from a dimension of divinity that spiritual service cannot reach — and why this inverts the usual hierarchy of material vs. spiritual

---

## Introduction

Mysticism typically operates with a hierarchy: spirit over matter, contemplation over action, the inner over the outer. The Hemshech Taarav, at multiple points across its nine years, argues for the **inverse**: the physical performance of mitzvot — leather straps of tefillin, the material sukkah, the specific grain of the etrog — draws from a dimension of divine reality that purely spiritual service cannot access.

This is not a pragmatic concession to the limits of embodied beings. It is a positive metaphysical claim rooted in the hemshech's analysis of *Atzmus* (Essence), *Ta'anug haAtzmi* (essential delight), and the nature of Or Mekif.

---

## The Apparent Problem

The intuitive assumption runs: God is infinite, spiritual, beyond materiality. Spiritual service — prayer, meditation, contemplation — should bring us closer to the divine than physical acts. A person who performs tefillin robotically while thinking about breakfast has done something vastly less valuable than a person who meditates deeply on divine unity without physical props.

The hemshech does not dismiss this intuition. In many registers it is true: the depth of kavvanah (intention) matters, and spiritual elevation is a real category. But the hemshech identifies a specific dimension in which this hierarchy **reverses**.

---

## The Argument from Ta'anug haAtzmi

The hemshech's foundational claim about Ta'anug haAtzmi is that it is the dimension of divinity that does not *face* the world. Ratzon (divine will) faces creation: there are reasons (even if hidden, *ta'am nistar*) for the divine will's direction. Ta'anug faces itself: it is divine delight in the divine's own existence, independent of any world.

This means that if something is to connect to Ta'anug haAtzmi, it cannot do so through the logic of purpose, meaning, or spiritual resonance — those are all within the Ratzon-structured world. It must connect through something else.

The hemshech's answer: **Chukim** (statutes without rational explanation, like the Red Heifer or the prohibition of sha'atnez/mixed fabrics) connect to Ta'anug precisely *because* they bypass rational justification. When a person fulfills a chok, they are not performing a spiritually resonant act — they are doing something whose only grounding is the divine Ratzon, and whose connection to the Ratzon is itself groundless (since the Ratzon is *b'li taam* — without reason). The fulfillment of a chok is therefore the purest Or Chozer available within structured divine service: it ascends not through the ladder of meaning but through sheer compliance with groundless will.

And the further advance: since the Ratzon for Chukim is itself groundless, and since Ta'anug is the dimension *behind* Ratzon (which Ratzon emerges from), the performance of Chukim reaches not just Ratzon but behind it — toward Ta'anug.

---

## The Argument from Or Mekif

Or Mekif (encompassing light) is the dimension of divine light that does not invest itself *within* a vessel but hovers around it. Or Pnimi (inner light) invests within — it becomes the life-force of the vessel, comprehensible to the vessel's inner structure.

The hemshech's analysis of Chanukah lights (*asur lehishtamesh* — forbidden to use for personal illumination, תרע"ג) establishes: Or Mekif *cannot be "used."* You cannot draw utility from encompassing light. This is precisely what distinguishes it from Or Pnimi: Or Pnimi can be integrated, learned from, built upon; Or Mekif can only be *received* as something that surrounds you.

Now: what is the vessel for Or Mekif? The hemshech's answer — developed across multiple years — is that Or Mekif's vessel is not the *soul's* interiority but the *body's exteriority*. Or Pnimi invests in the soul's inner life; Or Mekif encompasses the outer. The body, as the soul's outer garment, is precisely the site where Or Mekif lands.

This creates the hemshech's most striking inversion: **the body's outer performance of mitzvot is the vessel for Or Mekif, which is a *higher* dimension than the Or Pnimi that inner spiritual service accesses.** Not because the body is elevated spiritually, but because Or Mekif's structural nature requires an outer surface — and the body is that surface.

---

## The Argument from Atzmus

The hemshech's climactic claim in Part 2 (העת"ר, 1909-10) is about *Atzmus* — the divine Essence itself, which is "prior to" both Ta'anug and Ratzon:

> "The Torah was given to Israel not only as infinite wisdom but as *Torat Atzmo* — the Torah of His Essence. Therefore physical mitzvot draw from Atzmus, not merely from Chochma or even Ta'anug."

The argument: Chochma (wisdom) is the highest revelatory mode of Or Pnimi. Ta'anug haAtzmi is the deepest mode of Keter, accessible via Teshuva. But Atzmus itself — the divine Essence — transcends even Ta'anug. The only way to access Atzmus is through something that has no mediating structure at all.

Physical mitzvot, performed in the material world with material objects, have this character: they are not *about* anything. A tefillin knot is not a spiritual symbol — it is a physical knot, and its validity is about whether the knot was tied correctly, not whether it symbolizes unity or whether the wearer felt inspired. The total absence of spiritual mediation in purely technical physical performance is precisely what makes it a vessel for Atzmus.

Spiritual service, paradoxically, has too much meaning to reach the Essence: contemplation, kavvanah, and ecstasy are all *about something* — about divine unity, divine love, divine greatness. Atzmus cannot be the object of an about-relation; it can only be the ground of a do-relation. When you do the mitzvah correctly, without understanding why, you are in direct contact with Atzmus — because only Atzmus is the source of an act that doesn't need to mean anything.

---

## The Argument from Naaseh v'Nishma

The hemshech returns repeatedly to the Sinaitic declaration *Naaseh v'Nishma* — "We will do and we will hear." The sequence is significant: action (*naaseh*) before hearing/understanding (*nishma*). The standard explanation (Rashi, etc.) treats this as an expression of Israel's trust. The hemshech treats it as a statement of spiritual structure:

*Naaseh* (doing) comes first because it reaches a higher level than *Nishma* (hearing/understanding). Doing connects to Or Mekif / Atzmus; understanding connects to Or Pnimi / Chochma. The Sinaitic revelation was specifically structured to establish *doing* as the primary act of divine service — because only doing reaches the Essence.

The hemshech reads the angels' surprise at Israel's declaration (*malachim pelilu* — angels wondered: how do they know to say *naaseh* before *nishma*?) as reflecting the angels' own limitation: angels, as purely spiritual beings, only have access to *nishma* — to Or Pnimi levels of divine light. The physical body that enables *naaseh* gives human beings access to a dimension angels cannot reach.

---

## The Counter-Intuitive Hierarchy

Drawing these threads together, the hemshech implies a counter-intuitive hierarchy:

| Mode of service | What it reaches |
|----------------|----------------|
| Intellectual comprehension of Torah | Or Pnimi / Chochma |
| Deep kavvanah in prayer | Or Pnimi at Ratzon level |
| Contemplation reaching *bitul* (self-nullification) | Or Pnimi approaching Or Mekif |
| Teshuva (from Yechida-level) | Ta'anug haAtzmi |
| Physical mitzvot (halachically correct performance) | Or Mekif / Atzmus |
| Collective Israel performing Nitzavim-style | Atzmus itself |

The hierarchy is not that physical performance replaces spiritual depth — both matter in their own registers. But *specifically* for reaching Or Mekif and Atzmus, physical performance has a structural advantage over spiritual contemplation.

---

## Implications for Practice

The hemshech does not draw explicit practical halakhic conclusions, but the conceptual implications are significant:

1. **Halachic correctness is not merely legalistic** — it is the structural requirement for Or Mekif / Atzmus connection. A technically invalid sukkah is not just legally deficient; it has lost its capacity as an Or Mekif vessel.

2. **Children's mitzvot** — children who perform mitzvot without understanding are not doing a lesser thing; they may be doing the more essential thing (in the Atzmus dimension) while adults with understanding add the Or Pnimi dimension of *nishma*.

3. **The value of simple Jews** — simple Jews who perform mitzvot faithfully without scholarship access Or Mekif in a way that the scholar's intellect-based service cannot. Both forms are needed; the hemshech explicitly affirms that the full Or Chozer of the Nitzavim-structure requires both heads (scholars) and wood-choppers.

4. **Rote performance re-evaluated** — the hemshech does not celebrate mindlessness, but it does suggest that even "rote" performance — insofar as it is technically correct and halachically valid — carries an Or Mekif dimension that spiritually charged but technically careless performance lacks.

---

## The Deepest Formulation

The hemshech's most compressed statement of this principle appears in the context of the Red Heifer analysis: the paradox of the Red Heifer is that it purifies the impure while defiling the pure. This is the paradigm of Or Mekif/Atzmus action: it operates by a logic that inverts all rational expectation. The fact that it makes no sense *is* the mechanism by which it reaches Atzmus.

A mitzvah that *makes sense* draws from Chochma — the divine wisdom that the mitzvah embeds. A mitzvah that doesn't make sense draws from beyond Chochma, from the Ta'anug/Atzmus dimension where divine will and divine delight are groundless in any rational framework.

The physical body's non-rational performance of a statute is therefore not a spiritual limitation but a structural opening: it reaches the dimension of divinity that precedes reason, because it bypasses reason entirely.
