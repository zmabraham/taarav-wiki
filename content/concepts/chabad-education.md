# Chabad Education — חינוך חב"ד

**The Yeshiva System, Tomchei Temimim, and Chabad's Educational Philosophy**

---

## Overview

Chabad education is a comprehensive system of Jewish learning that integrates Talmudic scholarship, halachic rigor, and Chassidic philosophy into a unified program of spiritual and intellectual development. Founded with the establishment of Tomchei Temimim in 1897 by the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] (Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn), Chabad's educational system has grown from a single yeshiva in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] to a global network of schools, yeshivas, and educational institutions serving students from early childhood through advanced rabbinic studies.

The defining innovation of Chabad education is its insistence that Torah study and Chassidus are not separate domains but integral parts of a single whole. Where traditional Lithuanian yeshivas focused almost exclusively on Talmud, and Musar yeshivas added ethical instruction, Chabad yeshivas require sustained study of both the "revealed" Torah (Talmud, halacha) and the "inner" Torah (Chassidus, Kabbalah). This dual curriculum, revolutionary at its founding, remains the hallmark of Chabad education.

## Tomchei Temimim

### Founding

Tomchei Temimim ("Supporters of the Sincere") was formally established on Simchat Torah 1898 (5659) by the Rebbe Rashab, with the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (then a young man of 18) serving as its director. The yeshiva was conceived as the Rashab's response to the spiritual challenges of the era — the rise of secularism, socialism, and assimilation that was drawing young Jews away from traditional observance. The Rashab envisioned an institution that would produce not merely scholars but _temimim_ — sincere individuals whose Torah knowledge was internalized and expressed in their character and conduct.

### The Dual Curriculum

The curriculum of Tomchei Temimim was groundbreaking:

- **Nigleh** ("the revealed" Torah): Approximately 8 hours daily of Talmud study, halacha, and Jewish law
- **Chassidus** ("the inner" Torah): Approximately 4 hours daily of Chabad Chassidus, including the study of [[concepts/tanya|Tanya]], discourses of the Rebbes, and related works
- **Mashpia**: Each student was assigned a _mashpia_ (spiritual mentor) — a senior chassid who guided the student's personal spiritual development, a role distinct from the traditional _rosh yeshiva_ (academic head)

This integration of intellectual rigor and spiritual depth was the Rashab's response to what he saw as the limitations of both the Lithuanian model (intellectually rigorous but spiritually dry) and the Musar movement (ethically focused but lacking the philosophical depth of Chassidus).

### Growth and Expansion

From its initial class of 18 students, Tomchei Temimim grew rapidly. By 1903, enrollment exceeded 200, with branches established in multiple cities. The yeshiva survived the upheavals of World War I, the Russian Revolution, and the Soviet period — often operating clandestinely as described in accounts like [[summaries/zichronosai|Zichronosai]] and [[summaries/lemaan-yedau|Lemaan Yede'u]]. After the Rayatz's escape to America in 1940, Tomchei Temimim was reestablished in [[entities/places/crown-heights|Crown Heights]] and continued to expand under the seventh Rebbe.

## The Modern Chabad Educational System

### Yeshiva Track

The contemporary Chabad yeshiva system follows a structured progression:

- **Yeshiva Ketana** (junior yeshiva): Ages 13-16, combining general Jewish studies with introduction to Talmud and Chassidus
- **Zal** (main study hall): Ages 16-20, intensive Talmud and Chassidus study
- **Yeshiva Gedola** (advanced yeshiva): Post-high-school study leading to semicha (rabbinic ordination) for some students
- **Kvutzah** (select group): Advanced students who remain in yeshiva for further study and community leadership preparation

### Day Schools

Beyond the yeshiva system, Chabad operates hundreds of day schools worldwide serving the broader Jewish community. These schools provide Jewish and general education to children from Chabad families and from the wider Jewish community, embodying the [[concepts/kiruv|kiruv]] (outreach) mission in educational form.

### Seminaries for Women

Chabad maintains seminaries for young women, most prominently in [[entities/places/jerusalem|Jerusalem]], [[entities/places/tzfas|Tzfat]], and other cities. These institutions provide advanced Jewish studies for women, combining textual learning with practical skills for Jewish life and community service.

## Educational Philosophy

### The Penimi — Internalization

The ultimate goal of Chabad education is the development of the _penimi_ — a person whose Torah knowledge is not merely intellectual but fully internalized. The Rashab distinguished between the _chitzoniyus_ (external) scholar who accumulates knowledge and the _penimi_ whose learning transforms their character. This emphasis on internalization distinguishes Chabad education from purely academic approaches to Torah study.

### Study as Divine Service

In Chabad philosophy, Torah study is not merely an intellectual exercise but a form of divine service (_avodah_). Through study, the student connects with the mind of God, drawing divine light into the world. The [[concepts/tanya|Tanya]] explains that when a person studies Torah with proper intention, they create a channel through which divine wisdom flows into the physical world.

### The Mashpia System

The role of the _mashpia_ (spiritual mentor) is unique to Chabad education. While traditional yeshivas have a _rosh yeshiva_ (academic head) and _maggid shiur_ (lecturer), Chabad yeshivas add the mashpia — a senior chassid whose role is to guide students in their personal spiritual development, help them navigate challenges, and model the ideal of the penimi. The mashpia-student relationship is considered essential to the educational process.
