# Chabad Holidays and Observances

In addition to the standard Jewish calendar, Chabad-Lubavitch observes a distinctive set of holidays and commemorations rooted in the movement's own history. These dates mark moments of liberation, leadership transitions, and spiritual renewal — and are celebrated with **farbrengens** (Chassidic gatherings), Torah study, and intensified spiritual practice. A striking pattern runs through the calendar: repeated cycles of arrest, persecution, and miraculous liberation, each generating a new holiday.

---

## The Liberation Holidays

### 19 Kislev — Yud-Tes Kislev (1798)

**"The Rosh Hashanah of Chassidus"**

The most important date on the Chabad calendar. On 19 Kislev 5559 (1798), the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] was liberated from imprisonment in the Peter and Paul Fortress in St. Petersburg, where he had been held on charges of treason and sedition brought by opponents of Chassidism.

See: [[events/alter-rebbe-arrest-1798|First Arrest of the Alter Rebbe]]

- **Significance**: The Alter Rebbe's liberation is understood not merely as a personal deliverance but as a heavenly validation of Chassidic teaching itself. The "trial above" mirrored the trial below — the question was whether Chassidus should be disseminated to the broader public.
- **Observance**: Major farbrengens in every Chabad community worldwide; study of Tanya and Chassidus; the custom to complete the annual cycle of Tanya study on this date
- **Date coincidence**: 19 Kislev is also the yahrzeit of the [[entities/people/maggid-of-mezeritch|Maggid of Mezeritch]] (died 1772) — the Alter Rebbe's teacher

### 9–10 Kislev — Yud/Yud Kislev (1826)

On 10 Kislev 5587 (1826), the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (second Rebbe) was liberated from imprisonment on charges of sedition. His birthday falls on 9 Kislev, creating a two-day celebration.

See: [[events/arrest-mitteler-rebbe|Arrest and Liberation of the Mitteler Rebbe]]

- **Significance**: The second instance of the arrest-liberation pattern, reinforcing the reading that opposition to Chabad triggers heavenly judgment followed by vindication
- **Observance**: Farbrengens; study of the Mitteler Rebbe's teachings

### 12–13 Tammuz — Yud-Beis Tammuz (1927)

On 12–13 Tammuz 5687 (1927), the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (sixth Rebbe) was liberated from Soviet GPU imprisonment, after being sentenced first to death, then commuted to exile.

See: [[events/rayatz-liberation-1927|Arrest and Liberation of the Rayatz]]

- **Significance**: The third major arrest-liberation event; interpreted as divine validation of the Rayatz's defiance of Soviet anti-religious policy
- **Observance**: Farbrengens; study of the Rayatz's discourses; the Rayatz's birthday is also 12 Tammuz

## The Pattern: Arrest → Liberation → Holiday

The three liberation holidays share a remarkable structural pattern:

| Element              | Yud-Tes Kislev (1798) | Yud Kislev (1826) | Yud-Beis Tammuz (1927)         |
| -------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Rebbe**            | Alter Rebbe           | Mitteler Rebbe    | Rayatz                         |
| **Oppressor**        | Tsarist Russia        | Tsarist Russia    | Soviet Russia                  |
| **Charge**           | Treason/sedition      | Sedition          | Counter-revolutionary activity |
| **Initial sentence** | Severe                | Imprisonment      | Death                          |
| **Outcome**          | Full liberation       | Full liberation   | Exile, then expulsion          |
| **Holiday created**  | 19 Kislev             | 10 Kislev         | 12–13 Tammuz                   |

This pattern is understood in Chabad thought as reflecting a cosmic dynamic: when the forces of holiness advance (through the dissemination of Chassidus), the forces of concealment resist. The liberation represents the ultimate triumph of revelation over concealment.

## Leadership Transition Dates

### 10 Shevat — Yud Shvat

A date with dual significance:

1. **Yahrzeit of the Rayatz** (1950): The [[entities/people/rayatz|sixth Rebbe]] passed away on 10 Shevat 5710 (January 28, 1950)
2. **Acceptance of leadership by the seventh Rebbe** (1951): Exactly one year later, on 10 Shevat 5711, the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] formally accepted the leadership of Chabad by delivering his first maamar (Chassidic discourse), beginning with the words _"Basi LeGani"_ ("I have come into My garden")

- **Observance**: Intensive study of the _Basi LeGani_ maamar; farbrengens; resolutions for increased Torah study and mitzvah observance. The seventh Rebbe delivered a new chapter of _Basi LeGani_ each year on this date.

### 11 Nissan — Birthday of the Seventh Rebbe

The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] was born on 11 Nissan 5662 (1902). This date is celebrated globally in Chabad communities:

- **Observance**: Grand farbrengens; the Rebbe would deliver major addresses on this date; communities undertake special projects in honor of the birthday
- **The Birthday Campaign**: The Rebbe encouraged every Jew to celebrate their birthday as a personal _Rosh Hashanah_ — a day for introspection, good resolutions, and gratitude

### 3 Tammuz — Yahrzeit of the Seventh Rebbe

The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] passed away on 3 Tammuz 5754 (June 12, 1994). The date is observed as a major day of commemoration:

- **Observance**: Visits to the Ohel (the Rebbe's resting place in Queens, New York); farbrengens; study of the Rebbe's teachings; increased mitzvah observance
- Thousands visit the Ohel on this date annually, including political leaders, celebrities, and Jews from all backgrounds

## Other Significant Dates

### 24 Teves — Yahrzeit of the Alter Rebbe

The [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] passed away on 24 Teves 5573 (January 1813) during the flight from Napoleon's advancing armies. Observed with study of the Alter Rebbe's teachings and farbrengens.

### 2 Nissan — Birthday and Yahrzeit of the Rebbe Rashab

The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] was born on 20 Cheshvan 5621 (1860) and passed away on 2 Nissan 5680 (1920). Both dates are commemorated.

### 15 Elul — Founding of Tomchei Temimim

The anniversary of the founding of [[events/tomchei-temimim-founding-1897|Yeshiva Tomchei Temimim]] (1897). Observed in Chabad yeshivos with special farbrengens and renewed commitment to study.

### 20 Cheshvan — Birthday of the Rebbe Rashab

Marked with farbrengens and study of the Rashab's works, particularly _Kuntres HaAvodah_.

## The Farbrengen

The **farbrengen** (Yiddish: "gathering" or "getting together") is the primary communal observance form for all Chabad holidays. A farbrengen typically includes:

- **L'chaim**: drinks (usually _mashke_ — strong spirits) shared communally
- **Nigunim**: Chassidic melodies sung together, often wordless
- **Divrei Torah**: words of Torah, Chassidic teachings, and personal reflection
- **Personal sharing**: participants share spiritual struggles and resolutions
- **Brotherhood**: the farbrengen creates an atmosphere of deep personal connection and mutual accountability

The farbrengen is not a formal liturgical event but a distinctly Chassidic institution — part celebration, part study session, part spiritual therapy. The Rebbe's farbrengens at 770 could last for hours, sometimes until dawn, and were attended by thousands. They were broadcast and later videotaped, becoming a primary medium for the Rebbe's teachings.

---

## The Chabad Calendar as History

The Chabad holiday calendar is, in effect, a compressed retelling of Chabad history. To observe the yearly cycle is to relive:

- The founding of the movement (19 Kislev)
- The repeated trials and liberations (Kislev, Tammuz)
- The leadership transitions (Shvat, Nissan, Tammuz)
- The institutional milestones (Elul)

Each holiday is simultaneously a historical commemoration and a renewed spiritual mandate — not merely remembering the past but drawing from it the energy for present-day action.
