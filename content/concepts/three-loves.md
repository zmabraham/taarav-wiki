# Three Loves — שלש אהבות

**Ahavas Yisrael, Ahavas HaTorah, Ahavas HaShem**

---

## Overview

The "Three Loves" (Hebrew: שלש אהבות / Shalosh Ahavos) are a foundational triad in the teachings of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]: **Ahavas Yisrael** (love of fellow Jews), **Ahavas HaTorah** (love of Torah), and **Ahavas HaShem** (love of God). The Rebbe consistently emphasized that these three loves are inseparable — that one cannot truly love God without loving His Torah, and one cannot truly love Torah without loving His people. Together, they form the spiritual framework for Chabad's approach to Jewish life and the driving force behind the movement's worldwide [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] and [[concepts/kiruv|outreach]] activities.

The Three Loves represent more than abstract principles. In the Rebbe's teachings, they are practical directives that shape daily conduct, communal priorities, and the overall mission of Chabad. They provide the emotional and spiritual engine for the extraordinary global expansion of Chabad under the seventh Rebbe's leadership.

---

## Ahavas Yisrael — Love of Fellow Jews

Ahavas Yisrael — the commandment to love one's fellow Jew — is perhaps the most widely emphasized of the three in the Rebbe's public teachings. Drawing on the foundational principle articulated by [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi]] that "Ahavas Yisrael is the basis of the entire Torah," the Rebbe made love of every Jew — regardless of background, affiliation, or level of observance — the cornerstone of Chabad's approach.

The Rebbe's emphasis on Ahavas Yisrael was both philosophical and practical. Philosophically, he taught that every Jew possesses a divine soul that is inherently connected to God and to every other Jewish soul. Practically, this meant that Chabad shluchim and community members were directed to reach out to every Jew with warmth, acceptance, and love, without judgment or precondition. This principle underlies the entire [[concepts/kiruv|kiruv]] (outreach) model and the network of Chabad houses worldwide.

---

## Ahavas HaTorah — Love of Torah

The second love, Ahavas HaTorah, encompasses love for Torah study, Torah observance, and the Torah's wisdom as a whole. In Chabad's approach, Torah is not merely a set of laws to be followed but the revealed will of God — a living document whose study brings the student into direct encounter with the divine. The Rebbe's campaign to promote daily Torah study, the establishment of [[concepts/chabad-education|Chabad educational institutions]] worldwide, and the emphasis on publishing and disseminating Torah teachings all flow from this love.

The Rebbe particularly emphasized the study of Chassidus — the inner dimension of Torah revealed by the Baal Shem Tov and systematized by Chabad Rebbes — as essential for every Jew. Through Chassidus, the abstract concepts of Torah become internalized, transforming the person from within. This love of Torah extends beyond personal study to the commitment to share Torah with others.

---

## Ahavas HaShem — Love of God

The third love, Ahavas HaShem, is the ultimate foundation. The [[concepts/tanya|Tanya]] opens with the verse "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your might" as the central imperative of Jewish life. In Chabad philosophy, this love is not merely an emotion to be cultivated but a spiritual state to be achieved through contemplation, study, and divine service.

The Rebbe taught that Ahavas HaShem finds its practical expression through Ahavas HaTorah and Ahavas Yisrael. One demonstrates love of God by studying His Torah and by loving His children. This circular relationship — God, Torah, and Israel bound together in mutual love — is at the heart of Chabad's spiritual vision.

---

## The Three Loves in Practice

The Rebbe frequently cited the Three Loves as the framework for understanding Chabad's mission. The establishment of [[events/founding-merkos|Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch]] and [[events/founding-machne-israel|Machne Israel]] reflected Ahavas Yisrael in institutional form. The vast network of Chabad schools and yeshivas expressed Ahavas HaTorah. And the entire movement's orientation toward divine service — the commitment to bringing Godliness into every aspect of the physical world — expressed Ahavas HaShem.

The Rebbe's public addresses frequently wove the three together, demonstrating that they are not three separate principles but one integrated spiritual reality. A farbrengen that inspired love of fellow Jews, Torah study that ignited love of God, and prayer that deepened love of Torah — all were expressions of the same underlying truth.
