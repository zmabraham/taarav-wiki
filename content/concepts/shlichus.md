# Shlichus — שליחות

**The Emissary Model: Chabad's Engine of Global Expansion**

---

## Definition

_Shlichus_ (from _shliach_ — messenger/emissary) is the Chabad model of trained emissaries sent to establish Jewish life in communities around the world. A _shliach_ (emissary) — typically a young married couple — is dispatched by the Rebbe to a specific location to build a Chabad House, synagogue, educational institution, or outreach center.

The legal principle underlying it: **"A person's agent is like himself"** (_shlucho shel adam kemoso_). The shliach is not acting independently but as an extension of the Rebbe's own presence and mission.

---

## Historical Development

### Origins: The Rashab's Vision

The Rashab saw the graduates of Tomchei Temimim as "soldiers of King David who would stand strong for Jewish principles in the four corners of the world." The yeshiva was designed to produce not just scholars but emissaries (_temimim_) with the character, knowledge, and spiritual fortitude to serve as Chabad representatives.

### The Rayatz's Systematic Deployment

The Rayatz (1920–1950) transformed shlichus from organic community building into a systematic global strategy. His key deployments:

- Underground yeshiva directors in Soviet cities
- Institutional leaders in Riga, Warsaw, and European communities
- The Shanghai students (survival as mission)
- Post-WWII resettlement as deliberate global dispersion: France, Australia, Canada, South Africa, Cuba, Sweden, Israel
- First campus outreach: two yeshiva students to Brandeis University (1949)

His philosophy: every refugee is an emissary. "The reason that divine providence has brought you here is to warm up the tepid America with reverence of Heaven and chassidic passion."

### The Seventh Rebbe's Global Network

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson systematized and massively expanded shlichus after 1951. Beginning with UCLA in 1969, Chabad established Chabad Houses at universities worldwide. By the end of the 20th century, shluchim were present in over 100 countries.

---

## Shlichus as Theology

In Chabad thought, shlichus reflects the deeper purpose of the soul's descent into the physical world: not flight from materiality but transformation of it. The shliach goes _out_ — into remote, irreligious, or hostile territory — to find and elevate the divine sparks hidden there.

---

## The Shlichus Relationship

The shliach maintains:

- Ongoing connection to the Rebbe (via hiskashrus)
- Accountability for the mission's success
- Self-sufficiency (the Rayatz insisted shluchim support themselves rather than depend permanently on central charity)
- Representation of the full range of Chabad life: Shabbat meals, holiday celebrations, lifecycle events, Torah classes, pastoral counseling
