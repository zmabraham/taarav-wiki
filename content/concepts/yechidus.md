# Yechidus — יחידות

**Private Audience with the Rebbe**

---

## Overview

_Yechidus_ (Hebrew: "private meeting" or "alone together") was the practice of private audience with the Rebbe — one of the defining features of Chabad life for over four decades. During yechidus, an individual would meet alone with the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe]] to seek guidance on personal matters, ask questions, receive blessings, or discuss communal concerns. Over the course of his leadership (1951-1994), the seventh Rebbe received tens of thousands of individuals in yechidus, making it one of the most extraordinary pastoral practices in Jewish history.

The yechidus was not merely an audience with a great rabbi. For the chassid, it was a moment of profound spiritual encounter — an opportunity to stand before the Rebbe, who was understood to perceive not only the question being asked but the deeper spiritual dimensions of the questioner's situation. Many accounts describe the Rebbe addressing concerns that had not been voiced, offering guidance that proved remarkably prescient, and responding to each person with total focus and attention.

---

## Structure and Practice

### How Yechidus Worked

During the peak years, yechidus operated with extraordinary efficiency. Visitors would sign up in advance, often waiting weeks or months for an appointment. The meetings typically took place late at night, often continuing until the early morning hours. The Rebbe would receive person after person, each for a few minutes, maintaining complete concentration and presence for each individual regardless of the hour.

The supplicant would enter the Rebbe's study at [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]] alone. The Rebbe would sit behind his desk, often reviewing papers or a sefer between visitors, and then give his full attention to the person before him. Conversations were conducted primarily in Yiddish, though the Rebbe would switch to Hebrew, English, French, or other languages as needed. Visitors came seeking guidance on matters ranging from health and livelihood to marriage, education, spiritual struggles, and communal decisions.

### The Written Petition

Many visitors brought _pidyonos_ — written notes containing their requests and questions. The Rebbe would read the note, sometimes writing a response on it, and then discuss the matter verbally. The practice of written petitions has continued at the Ohel (the Rebbe's resting place at [[entities/places/old-montefiore-cemetery|Old Montefiore Cemetery]]) after 1994.

### Scheduling and Scale

During the busiest periods, yechidus could last for many hours. On occasions such as before Rosh Hashanah, after [[concepts/farbrengen|farbrengens]], or during the Tishrei holiday season, the Rebbe would receive visitors almost continuously. The total number of individuals who met the Rebbe in yechidus over the decades is estimated in the tens of thousands, ranging from ordinary chassidim to heads of state, scholars, and Jewish community leaders from around the world.

## Historical Development

### Earlier Rebbes

The practice of private audience with the Rebbe existed from the earliest days of Chabad. Each of the seven Rebbes received chassidim individually, though the format and frequency varied. The Alter Rebbe, the Mitteler Rebbe, and subsequent Rebbes all maintained the practice of receiving individuals for private discussion and guidance.

### The Seventh Rebbe's Yechidus

The seventh Rebbe's yechidus practice was unprecedented in scale. Beginning in 1951 and continuing for decades, the Rebbe made himself available to an extraordinary range of people. In the early years, yechidus was more frequent and longer. As the demands on the Rebbe's time grew with the expansion of the Chabad movement, the yechidus schedule was adjusted but never abandoned.

After the Rebbetzin's passing in 1988, and as the Rebbe's public schedule became increasingly demanding, the formal yechidus practice was gradually replaced in part by the [[concepts/sunday-dollars|Sunday dollar distributions]], which allowed the Rebbe to see thousands of people each week in a more abbreviated format. Even so, private audiences continued on a more limited basis.

---

## Significance in Chabad Life

Yechidus embodied the principle of [[concepts/hiskashrus|hiskashrus]] — the bond between Rebbe and chassid — at its most personal. The Rebbe was known for his total focus on each individual, often recalling details from previous meetings years earlier. Many accounts describe encounters in which the Rebbe addressed the most pressing concerns of the visitor's life — sometimes concerns the person had not articulated.

For many chassidim, the experience of yechidus was transformative. The Rebbe's guidance was sought on virtually every aspect of life, and his responses were often preserved, reread, and revisited as ongoing sources of direction. Stories of remarkable yechidus encounters — precise advice, unexpected blessings, seemingly supernatural perception — form a substantial body of Chabad oral tradition.
