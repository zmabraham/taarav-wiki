# Sunday Dollars — דאלער חלוקה

**The Rebbe's Practice of Distributing Dollar Bills for Tzedakah**

---

## Overview

The Sunday Dollar distribution was one of the most iconic and widely recognized practices of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe]]'s later years. Beginning in 1986 and continuing until 1992, the Rebbe stood every Sunday outside [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]] and personally handed dollar bills to thousands of people, requesting that each recipient give the dollar to [[concepts/tzedakah|tzedakah]]. What began as a simple gesture grew into one of the most distinctive rituals of the Rebbe's leadership, drawing visitors from around the world and becoming a powerful symbol of the Rebbe's personal connection to every Jew.

The practice combined multiple dimensions of Chabad values: the central mitzvah of [[concepts/tzedakah|tzedakah]], the Rebbe's desire for personal connection with every individual, and the principle that a single act of giving could ignite a chain of goodness. Each dollar, given by the Rebbe and then passed on to charity, represented not just a financial transaction but a spiritual catalyst.

---

## Origins

### The Precipitating Event

The practice began in 1986. The commonly recounted origin involves the Rebbe's response to a particular need: a woman had approached the Rebbe seeking a blessing, and the Rebbe gave her a dollar bill to give to tzedakah. The Rebbe explained that the act of giving charity would serve as a vessel for the blessing. From this initial gesture, a regular practice developed. The Rebbe began distributing dollars on a weekly basis, initially to smaller groups and then to ever-larger crowds.

### Why Dollars?

The choice of a dollar bill was deliberate and symbolic. The American dollar carried universal recognition and value. More importantly, it was a small, accessible denomination — enough to be meaningful as a charitable contribution but modest enough to emphasize the act of giving over the amount given. The Rebbe stressed that the purpose was not the dollar itself but the [[concepts/tzedakah|tzedakah]] it would generate and the blessing it carried.

---

## The Practice

### Weekly Schedule

Every Sunday, the Rebbe would emerge from 770 to stand at a designated spot, often near the entrance to the building. The distribution would typically last for several hours, with the Rebbe maintaining extraordinary stamina and focus throughout. Lines would form early, with people waiting for hours for a few seconds of personal encounter with the Rebbe.

### The Encounter

Each person would approach the Rebbe individually. The Rebbe would hand them a dollar bill, often with a brief word of blessing or encouragement. Many people took the opportunity to hand the Rebbe a _pidyon_ (written petition), ask for a blessing, or simply make eye contact. Despite the enormous volume, the Rebbe was known for giving each person his full attention in the moment — a characteristic that witnesses consistently describe as remarkable.

### Scale

Over the years, the Sunday Dollar distribution grew enormously. At its peak, the Rebbe distributed dollars to thousands of people each Sunday. The annual total reached into the hundreds of thousands of individual encounters. Visitors came from every continent, representing the full spectrum of Jewish life — religious and secular, Chabad and non-Chabad, adults and children.

---

## Significance

### Tzedakah and Blessing

The Rebbe framed the dollar distribution primarily as a [[concepts/tzedakah|tzedakah]] initiative. Each recipient became a vehicle for charity, creating a vast network of giving. But the practice also served as a channel for blessing. Many recipients reported that the Rebbe's dollar, and the brief encounter that accompanied it, became a treasured memory and source of ongoing spiritual strength.

### Connection and Accessibility

The Sunday Dollars made the Rebbe accessible to an unprecedented number of people. While [[concepts/yechidus|yechidus]] (private audience) was limited to those who could obtain an appointment and wait for their turn, the dollar line was open to everyone. This democratic accessibility reflected the Rebbe's insistence that every Jew, regardless of background or station, deserved a personal connection.

### Cultural Impact

The Sunday Dollar distribution became one of the most recognizable features of Chabad in the public eye. Media coverage, personal accounts, and the sheer volume of participants made it a cultural phenomenon. The image of the Rebbe handing out dollars became an iconic representation of his leadership style — personal, generous, and accessible.
