# Kiruv — קירוב

**Jewish Outreach and Return in the Chabad Model**

---

## Overview

_Kiruv_ (Hebrew: "drawing close," from the root ק-ר-ב / karov, meaning "near") refers to the Jewish outreach movement dedicated to bringing Jews closer to their heritage, tradition, and observance. While kiruv as an organized movement gained momentum across the Jewish world in the late 20th century, Chabad's approach to kiruv predates the modern movement by decades and is rooted in the Rebbe's vision of reaching every Jew, wherever they may be.

For Chabad, kiruv is not a separate program or initiative but the very essence of the [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] mission. The Rebbe rejected the term "kiruv" in favor of language that emphasized not "bringing close" those who are far, but rather revealing the inherent closeness that already exists. In the Rebbe's formulation, no Jew is ever truly distant from God or Torah; the task is to help each person recognize and actualize the connection that is always present.

---

## Chabad's Distinctive Approach

### Unconditional Acceptance

Chabad's kiruv model is characterized by unconditional acceptance. The Rebbe instructed his shluchim (emissaries) to welcome every Jew without judgment, regardless of background, level of observance, or personal circumstances. A Chabad House is not a place where one must meet a standard to be accepted; it is a place where every Jew is received with warmth and love. This approach reflects the principle of [[concepts/three-loves|Ahavas Yisrael]] — love of fellow Jews — that the Rebbe identified as the foundation of the entire Torah.

### Meeting People Where They Are

Chabad shluchim are known for going to where Jews are rather than waiting for Jews to come to them. This has produced the extraordinary global network of Chabad centers — on college campuses, in remote cities, in resort towns, in countries with tiny Jewish populations — that has become one of the most recognizable features of contemporary Jewish life. The shluchim model of kiruv is proactive and personal, built on relationships rather than programs.

### No Pressure, No Strings

The Chabad approach to kiruv emphasizes education and experience over coercion. Shluchim invite Jews to experience Shabbos, to put on tefillin, to study Torah — but always as an invitation, never as a demand. The Rebbe's instruction was to share Torah and mitzvos with love, allowing each individual to grow at their own pace.

## Historical Development

### The Rebbe's Vision

From the earliest days of his leadership, the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe]] articulated a vision of reaching every Jew. The founding of [[events/founding-merkos|Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch]] in 1941 (under the Rayatz) and its expansion under the Rebbe, the establishment of the first campus Chabad Houses in the 1950s, and the systematic dispatch of shluchim to Jewish communities worldwide — all were expressions of the kiruv imperative.

### Operation North Africa and Early Campaigns

The [[events/founding-machne-israel|Machne Israel]] organization and early outreach campaigns, including [[summaries/operation-north-africa|Operation North Africa]] and activities in [[entities/places/tel-aviv|Tel Aviv]] and throughout Israel, demonstrated the breadth of the Rebbe's kiruv vision. These efforts reached Jews in diverse cultural contexts, from the traditional communities of North Africa to the secular centers of Israeli and American Jewish life.

### The Global Network

By the late 20th and early 21st centuries, the Chabad kiruv network had grown to encompass over 5,000 centers in more than 100 countries. Each center serves as a beacon of Jewish life, offering religious services, educational programs, community events, and personal support to Jews of all backgrounds.

---

## Kiruv vs. Shlichus

While "kiruv" is the common Hebrew term for Jewish outreach, within Chabad the preferred concept is [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] — mission or emissary work. The distinction reflects a theological difference: kiruv implies bringing someone close who was far, while shlichus implies carrying a mission from the Rebbe to every Jew, recognizing that no Jew is ever truly far from God. The Rebbe's shluchim see themselves not as outreach workers but as emissaries carrying the Rebbe's vision and blessing to every corner of the Jewish world.
