# Tzedakah — צדקה

**Charity and Righteous Giving in Chabad Tradition**

---

## Overview

_Tzedakah_ (Hebrew: "righteousness" or "justice," commonly translated as "charity") is one of the foundational mitzvos in Jewish life and occupies a particularly central place in Chabad thought and practice. The word itself conveys a fundamental principle: giving to those in need is not merely an act of kindness but an obligation of justice. The [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe's]] Shulchan Aruch devotes extensive discussion to the laws of tzedakah, and the Rebbe's [[concepts/sunday-dollars|Sunday dollar distributions]] became one of the most iconic practices of his later years.

In Chabad philosophy, tzedakah is understood not simply as a social good but as a spiritual act that elevates both the giver and the recipient. The Tanya discusses the concept of _pediyus_ (redemption) — that giving tzedakah redeems the soul and draws divine blessing into the world. This theological framework gives tzedakah a significance far beyond its practical social function.

---

## In Chabad Thought

### The Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch

The Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch provides detailed guidance on the laws of tzedakah, including the minimum obligation (a fifth of one's income for the especially generous, a tenth as the standard), the priority of recipients (the poor of one's own family take precedence, then the poor of one's city, then elsewhere), and the proper attitude in giving. The Alter Rebbe emphasizes that tzedakah should be given willingly and joyfully, not grudgingly, and that the act of giving transforms the material currency into spiritual elevation.

### Tanya and Spiritual Dimensions

The [[concepts/tanya|Tanya]] presents tzedakah as one of the primary means through which a person draws divine light into the world. Giving tzedakah is described as an act of _bittul_ (self-nullification) — relinquishing one's material possessions for a higher purpose. This aligns with the broader Chabad emphasis on transforming the material into the spiritual, the physical into the divine.

### Maaser and Chumash

The practice of giving _maaser_ (a tithe, traditionally a tenth of one's income) is widely observed in Chabad communities. Many Chabad families maintain careful accounting of their tzedakah contributions, and the practice is instilled from childhood. The Rebbe strongly encouraged the practice, noting that tzedakah brings blessing to one's livelihood.

## The Rebbe and Tzedakah

### Dollar Distributions

The Rebbe's [[concepts/sunday-dollars|Sunday dollar distributions]], beginning in 1986, became a powerful public expression of the centrality of tzedakah. Each Sunday, the Rebbe would stand for hours distributing dollar bills to thousands of people, asking each recipient to give the dollar to [[concepts/tzedakah|tzedakah]]. The practice combined multiple dimensions: the mitzvah of tzedakah itself, the blessing of the Rebbe conveyed through the dollar, and the opportunity for brief personal encounter.

### Institutional Tzedakah

Under the Rebbe's leadership, Chabad institutionalized tzedakah on a massive scale. [[events/founding-machne-israel|Machne Israel]] and [[events/founding-merkos|Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch]] were established as central organizations to support Jewish communities worldwide. The Chabad network of shluchim operates thousands of institutions — schools, synagogues, community centers, soup kitchens — all funded through tzedakah. The model of the shliach raising funds to support Jewish life in distant locations is itself a form of organized tzedakah.

### Emergency Campaigns

The Rebbe frequently launched emergency tzedakah campaigns, particularly for Jewish communities in distress. Campaigns to support Jews in the Soviet Union, in Israel during wartime, and in other crisis situations mobilized the entire Chabad network.

---

## Tzedakah in Chabad Daily Life

Beyond the formal institutions, tzedakah permeates daily Chabad life. Pushkas (charity boxes) are ubiquitous in Chabad homes, synagogues, and institutions. Children are taught to give tzedakah daily, often before prayer. The practice of giving tzedakah before lighting Shabbos candles, before davening, and at other fixed times creates a rhythm of giving that reinforces its centrality.
