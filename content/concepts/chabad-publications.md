# Chabad Publications

Chabad-Lubavitch has produced one of the most extensive literary corpuses in Jewish history. From the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]'s foundational _Tanya_ (1796) to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]'s 39-volume _Likkutei Sichos_, Chabad's publications span halacha, Chassidic philosophy, mysticism, letters, discourses, and practical guidance. The establishment of **Kehot Publication Society** in 1942 institutionalized this literary tradition, making it one of the most prolific Jewish publishing houses in the world.

---

## Foundational Texts of the Alter Rebbe

### Tanya (Likkutei Amarim) — 1796

The founding document of Chabad Chassidism, written by the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] over twenty years and published in 1796. The _Tanya_ is a systematic guide to the inner spiritual life, structured in five parts:

1. _Likkutei Amarim_ — the path of the _beinoni_ (53 chapters)
2. _Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah_ — divine unity and continuous creation (12 chapters)
3. _Iggeret HaTeshuvah_ — the process of return (12 chapters)
4. _Iggeret HaKodesh_ — collected letters on service and communal responsibility (32 epistles)
5. _Kuntres Acharon_ — supplementary essays

The _Tanya_ has been printed in over 7,000 editions and is studied daily in Chabad communities worldwide. It is sometimes called "the Written Torah of Chassidus."

See: [[events/founding-of-chabad|The Founding of Chabad]]

### Torah Or and Likkutei Torah

These two works contain the Alter Rebbe's Chassidic discourses (_maamarim_) arranged according to the weekly Torah portions:

- **Torah Or** — discourses on Bereishis (Genesis) and Shemos (Exodus); published posthumously in 1837 by the Tzemach Tzedek
- **Likkutei Torah** — discourses on Vayikra (Leviticus), Bamidbar (Numbers), Devarim (Deuteronomy), and the holidays; published in 1848

Together they form the "Oral Torah of Chassidus" — the practical application and elaboration of the principles outlined in the _Tanya_.

### Shulchan Aruch HaRav

The Alter Rebbe's comprehensive code of Jewish law, commissioned by the [[entities/people/maggid-of-mezeritch|Maggid of Mezeritch]]. Unlike the standard _Shulchan Aruch_ by Rabbi Yosef Karo, the Alter Rebbe's version includes:

- The reasoning (_ta'amei hadin_) behind each law, not only the ruling
- Integration of later authorities (Acharonim)
- Mystical dimensions of halachic practice where relevant

Published in multiple volumes over the Alter Rebbe's lifetime and posthumously. Several sections were lost in the fire during the flight from Napoleon (1812). It remains an authoritative halachic reference, particularly in Chabad communities.

## The Mitteler Rebbe's Works

### Derech Chaim (The Way of Life)

The [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]]'s major work on the service of prayer, explaining the process of spiritual contemplation (_hisbonenus_) in systematic detail. Known for its length and depth — the Mitteler Rebbe's discourses were famously expansive, elaborating at great length on each concept.

### Shaar HaEmunah and Shaar HaTeshuvah

Companion works exploring faith and repentance through the lens of Chassidic philosophy.

### Additional Works

The Mitteler Rebbe was extraordinarily prolific, producing major works on Kabbalistic topics including _Imrei Binah_, _Ateret Rosh_, _Torat Chaim_, and many volumes of discourses.

## The Tzemach Tzedek's Works

The [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] (third Rebbe) produced an enormous corpus spanning both halacha and Chassidus:

- **Responsa** (_She'eilos UTeshuvos_) — halachic rulings on a wide range of contemporary questions
- **Or HaTorah** — Chassidic discourses, running to dozens of volumes
- **Derech Mitzvosecha** — mystical explanations of the commandments
- **Yahel Or** — commentary on Psalms

## The Rebbe Rashab's Works

### Hemshech Samech-Vav (1905–1908)

The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]]'s magnum opus — a continuous series (_hemshech_) of Chassidic discourses delivered over three years. _Samech-Vav_ (named for the Hebrew year 5666 = 1905–1906) is considered one of the most profound and systematic works in all of Chassidic literature. It explores the cosmic structure of divine emanation, the nature of the soul, and the purpose of creation.

### Kuntres HaAvodah (Treatise on Divine Service)

A concise, practical guide to the Chassidic approach to prayer and spiritual development. Unlike the expansive _Hemshech Samech-Vav_, _Kuntres HaAvodah_ is designed as an accessible introduction to the method of contemplative worship.

### Kuntres HaTefillah (Treatise on Prayer)

A companion piece to _Kuntres HaAvodah_, focusing specifically on the theory and practice of Chassidic prayer.

## The Rayatz's Works

### Likkutei Dibburim

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]'s collected talks and stories — combining Chassidic teaching with vivid historical narrative. The Rayatz was a masterful storyteller, and his works preserve irreplaceable oral traditions about the earlier Rebbes and Chassidic life in Russia.

### Sefer HaSichos and Sefer HaMaamarim

Collections of the Rayatz's talks (sichos) and formal discourses (maamarim), arranged chronologically.

### Igrot Kodesh

The Rayatz's collected letters — covering personal guidance, communal direction, and institutional management across decades of leadership.

## The Seventh Rebbe's Works

### Likkutei Sichos — 39 Volumes

The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]'s magnum opus: a comprehensive commentary on the Torah and Jewish life delivered over four decades of leadership. The _Likkutei Sichos_ is distinctive for:

- **Analytical method**: each sicha identifies a textual difficulty, surveys multiple interpretative approaches, and resolves the question through a Chassidic insight
- **Integration**: the sichos weave together _pshat_ (plain meaning), _halacha_ (law), _aggadah_ (narrative), and _Chassidus_ (mystical philosophy)
- **Accessibility**: while deeply erudite, the sichos are structured to be followed by a general audience

Published in 39 volumes (primarily in Yiddish with Hebrew), the _Likkutei Sichos_ is studied weekly in Chabad communities worldwide.

### Igrot Kodesh — 32 Volumes

The seventh Rebbe's collected letters, spanning decades of correspondence with individuals and communities worldwide. The letters cover an extraordinary range: personal guidance, halachic questions, philosophical discussions, institutional directives, scientific topics, communal policy, and responses to current events.

### HaYom Yom (1943)

A daily calendar of Chassidic teachings, customs, and aphorisms compiled by the seventh Rebbe (then the Rayatz's son-in-law) at the Rayatz's request. Each day features a brief teaching or practice. _HaYom Yom_ has become one of the most widely studied Chabad texts — its brevity and daily format make it accessible to every level of learner.

### Maamarim and Sichos

In addition to _Likkutei Sichos_, the seventh Rebbe delivered hundreds of formal Chassidic discourses (_maamarim_) and thousands of informal talks (_sichos_), which have been published in numerous volumes arranged chronologically by year.

## Kehot Publication Society

**Kehot** (_Karnei Hod Torah_ — "Rays of the Splendor of Torah") was founded in **1942** by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] as the official publishing house of Chabad-Lubavitch. Under the direction of the seventh Rebbe, Kehot grew to become one of the largest Jewish publishing operations in the world.

### Output

- Thousands of titles in Hebrew, Yiddish, English, Russian, Spanish, French, and dozens of other languages
- Translations of classic Chabad texts into modern languages
- Educational materials for schools and outreach
- Children's books, prayer books, and reference works

### The Tanya Printing Campaign

The seventh Rebbe initiated a campaign to print the _Tanya_ in every city and country with a Jewish community. The goal: to have a local edition of the _Tanya_ printed in every Jewish population center worldwide. By the 21st century, the _Tanya_ had been printed in over 7,000 editions across dozens of countries.

## The Publishing Ethos

Chabad's emphasis on publication reflects a core philosophical principle: unlike other Chassidic movements that emphasized oral transmission through the tzaddik's personal presence, Chabad from its inception valued **written texts** as vehicles for transmitting Chassidic wisdom. The Alter Rebbe's _Tanya_ was explicitly composed as a "written _yechidus_" — a substitute for personal audience with the Rebbe.

This literary orientation distinguishes Chabad from most other Chassidic groups and has been a key factor in the movement's ability to spread globally while maintaining doctrinal coherence.
