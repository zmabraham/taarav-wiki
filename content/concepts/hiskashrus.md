# Hiskashrus — התקשרות

**The Bond Between Chassid and Rebbe**

---

## Definition

_Hiskashrus_ (from the root _kesher_ — connection, knot) is the Chassidic concept of the living bond between a chassid and his Rebbe. More than respect or reverence, hiskashrus is understood as a spiritual connection that transmits divine energy (_chayut_) from the Rebbe to the chassid and upward through prayer and devotion.

In Chabad thought, the Rebbe serves as the _tzaddik_ who mediates between the divine and the world — not by replacing individual spiritual effort, but by channeling and amplifying it.

---

## Hiskashrus in Practice

The bond is maintained through:

- **Learning the Rebbe's Torah**: Studying the Rebbe's discourses (_maamarim_) and letters
- **Following the Rebbe's guidance**: Acting on the Rebbe's directives even without full understanding
- **Yechidus**: Private audience with the Rebbe
- **Farbrengen**: Chassidic gatherings for inspiration and renewal
- **Writing to the Rebbe**: Letters seeking guidance
- **Physical proximity during Tishrei**: Annual pilgrimage to spend the High Holidays near the Rebbe
- **Learning the Rebbe's sichos**: Weekly teachings disseminated globally

---

## Hiskashrus During Persecution

The Soviet era tested hiskashrus in extreme conditions. Chassidim maintained their bond with the Rayatz across thousands of miles and years of separation through:

- Coded letters sent through intermediaries
- The Rebbe's letters preserved and read repeatedly
- The eyeglasses miracle (see [[summaries/zichronosai|Zichronosai]]) — where following specific advice from the Rebbe saved one chassid from Red Army conscription
- The Az Yashir incident during the Great Escape (1946) — where the Rayatz appeared supernaturally connected to the fate of 232 chassidim crossing the Polish border

---

## Hiskashrus After the Rebbe's Passing

The Chabad understanding of hiskashrus extends beyond physical death. After the Rayatz's death (10 Shevat 5710/1950), chassidim continued writing to his gravesite (_ohel_) at Montefiore Cemetery in Queens. After the seventh Rebbe's passing (3 Tammuz 5754/1994), the same practice continues. The ohel receives nearly one million visitors annually.
