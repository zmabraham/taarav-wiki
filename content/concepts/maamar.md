# Maamar — מאמר

**The Formal Vehicle of Chabad Torah Transmission**

---

## Definition

A _maamar_ (Hebrew: saying/discourse) is the formal Chassidic philosophical discourse — the highest register of Chabad Torah teaching. Delivered by the Rebbe in a distinctive chanting mode (_b'neginah_), usually at a [[concepts/farbrengen|farbrengen]], the maamar is distinguished from the informal _sicha_ (talk) by:

- Its formal opening formula (_maamar b'neginah_)
- Its systematic philosophical structure
- Its use of Kabbalistic frameworks (sefirot, worlds, divine names)
- Its basis in earlier Chabad texts (especially the Tanya)

---

## Structure of a Maamar

A typical maamar:

1. Opens with a verse (_pasuk_) or Mishnaic/Talmudic phrase
2. Poses a philosophical question (_kushya_) about the text
3. Develops the answer through layers of Kabbalistic and Chassidic analysis
4. Often addresses the tension between different aspects of divinity (_ratzo v'shov_ — running and returning; _yesh_ and _ayin_ — being and nothingness)
5. Concludes with practical implications for divine service

---

## Historical Maamarim

Each Rebbe's maamarim form the core of his Torah legacy:

**Alter Rebbe**: The maamarim compiled in _Torah Or_ and _Likkutei Torah_ (published posthumously) are the foundational library

**Mitteler Rebbe**: Prolific writer; his maamarim in _Derech Chaim_, _Toras Chaim_, and other works are extraordinarily lengthy and systematic

**Tzemach Tzedek**: His _Derech Mitzvotecha_ and other works apply Chabad philosophy to the 613 commandments

**Rashab**: The _Hemshech Samech-Vav_ (begun 1905-1908) — named for the Hebrew year 5666 — is considered the deepest sustained philosophical discourse in Chabad history, running to hundreds of pages and addressing the nature of divine will (_ratzon_), pleasure (_taanug_), and the structure of creation

**Rayatz**: His maamarim continued the Rashab's line while addressing the spiritual challenges of the Soviet period

**Seventh Rebbe**: Began his leadership with the maamar _Basi L'Gani_ (1951); published maamarim collected in _Sefer HaMaamarim_ series; his first maamar deliberately picked up from the exact word where the Rayatz's last published maamar ended

---

## The "Basi L'Gani" of the Seventh Rebbe

The seventh Rebbe's first maamar — delivered on **10 Shevat 5711 (1951)**, exactly one year after the Rayatz's death, on the occasion of accepting the Chabad leadership — began with "Basi L'Gani" (Song of Songs 5:1: "I have come to my garden"). This was not coincidental: the Rayatz had published a maamar on this verse as his last discourse before his death, and the seventh Rebbe's maamar was a continuation — demonstrating unbroken transmission.

---

## Publication and Transmission

For most of Chabad history, maamarim were transmitted:

- Orally at farbrengens (the Rebbe spoke; students memorized)
- Written by _chozrim_ (those with good memories) shortly after
- Circulated in manuscript (_ktav yad_)
- Eventually printed — but often decades later, as the Rebbes were cautious about premature publication

The seventh Rebbe's maamarim were transcribed with unprecedented speed by [[entities/people/kahn-yoel|Rabbi Yoel Kahn]] and published quickly, giving them wider circulation than any previous Chabad maamarim.
