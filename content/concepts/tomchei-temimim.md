# Tomchei Temimim — תומכי תמימים

**The Flagship Chabad Yeshiva System**

---

## Overview

**Tomchei Temimim** ("Supporters of the Sincere") is the central yeshiva system of Chabad-Lubavitch, founded by the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] (Rabbi Sholom Dovber Schneersohn) on 15 Elul 5657 (September 12, 1897) in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], Russia. It was the first institutionalized yeshiva in Chabad history, created to produce _temimim_ — "wholehearted ones" — trained in both the revealed dimensions of Torah (Nigleh: Talmud, Halacha) and the inner dimensions (Chassidus: mystical philosophy and divine service). The yeshiva system became the backbone of Chabad education, producing the scholars, activists, and [[concepts/shlichus|shluchim]] who sustained the movement through Soviet persecution, the Holocaust, and its postwar global expansion.

The founding was bound to the wedding of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (then 17 years old), who requested a yeshiva as his wedding gift instead of a material present. Two days after the wedding, on September 12, the Rashab gathered approximately 50 remaining wedding guests and announced the establishment of the new yeshiva. It began with 18 students; within five years enrollment reached approximately 200. See [[events/tomchei-temimim-founding-1897|Founding of Tomchei Temimim (1897)]] for a detailed account.

---

## The Revolutionary Dual Curriculum

What distinguished Tomchei Temimim from all previous yeshivos was its formal dual curriculum. In the standard yeshiva model of the time, students studied Talmud and its commentaries exclusively; Chassidic teachings, if studied at all, were pursued privately and informally. The Rashab institutionalized a structured program:

- **8 hours daily**: Talmud, Halacha, and traditional Torah study — under the direction of the _Rosh Yeshiva_
- **4 hours daily**: Chassidus (Chassidic philosophy, [[concepts/tanya|Tanya]], maamarim) — under the direction of the _Mashpia_ (spiritual mentor)

This dual structure introduced a new institutional role: the **mashpia** — a spiritual mentor who guided students in Chassidic study, personal spiritual practice (hisbonenus — contemplation), character development, and farbrengen leadership. The mashpia held parallel authority to the Rosh Yeshiva, governing complementary domains. This model became the template for all subsequent Chabad yeshivos worldwide.

The Rashab personally supervised the curriculum and frequently tested students in both Talmud and Chassidus. He delivered regular [[concepts/maamar|maamarim]] to the student body, some of which became major works, including portions of the monumental _Hemshech Samech-Vav_ (1905–1908).

---

## The Name and Its Meaning

The yeshiva was not initially given a formal name. On **Simchat Torah 5658 (1898)**, the Rashab bestowed the name _Tomchei Temimim_ — "Supporters of the Sincere" (or "Upholders of the Wholehearted"). The name carried a double meaning: the _temimim_ ("sincere/wholehearted ones") refers both to the students themselves, who uphold Torah in its complete form — both revealed and mystical — and to the _temimus_ (wholeness) produced by unifying the intellectual and emotional, the legal and the mystical.

The Rashab later described the temimim as "soldiers of the House of David" — a phrase that took on literal meaning during the Soviet period, when yeshiva graduates risked imprisonment and death to maintain Jewish education underground. In Chabad parlance, a graduate of Tomchei Temimim is called a _tamim_ (singular) and carries that designation for life, reflecting the enduring spiritual formation the yeshiva provides.

## Underground Survival in Soviet Russia

After the Bolshevik Revolution and the Soviet ban on religious education, Tomchei Temimim transformed into a clandestine network. The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]], from his exile in Riga and later in America, directed a vast underground system of disguised yeshivas operating secretly across the USSR. These schools — often disguised as technical schools or artisan workshops — preserved Torah learning and Chassidic training at enormous personal risk.

Underground branches operated in [[entities/places/moscow|Moscow]], [[entities/places/kherson|Kherson]], [[entities/places/mogilev|Mogilev]], [[entities/places/homel|Homel]], [[entities/places/kremenchug|Kremenchug]], and dozens of other cities. Many students and teachers were arrested and sent to labor camps. The network demonstrated the extraordinary [[concepts/mesirus-nefesh|mesirus nefesh]] (self-sacrifice) that characterized Chabad under communist rule and ensured the survival of Chabad's educational tradition through the darkest decades of Soviet repression. See [[events/chabad-underground-schools|Chabad Underground Schools]] for details.

## Reestablishment and Global Expansion

Tomchei Temimim survived the abandonment of Lubavitch (1915), Soviet persecution, the Holocaust, and multiple relocations. It was reestablished successively in:

- **Rostov** (1915–1920s) — after the Rashab evacuated Lubavitch during World War I
- **Warsaw** (1930s) — under the Rayatz's direction
- **Brooklyn, New York** (1940) — by the Rayatz upon arrival in America, centered at [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]]
- **Lod and then Kfar Chabad, Israel** (1940s–1950s) — serving Israeli Chabad community
- **[[entities/places/montreal|Montreal]]** — one of the earliest North American branches, established early in the postwar period

Under the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]], the system expanded dramatically. Today, Tomchei Temimim yeshivos operate on every inhabited continent, with branches in dozens of countries. The yeshiva continues to maintain the Rashab's original dual curriculum of Nigleh and Chassidus, with mashpiim serving alongside Roshei Yeshiva in every branch.

---

## Legacy

Tomchei Temimim proved to be the Rashab's most enduring institutional legacy. Its graduates became the underground teachers who kept Judaism alive under Soviet rule, the activists who rebuilt Chabad after the Holocaust, and the first shluchim dispatched to communities worldwide. The yeshiva's unique combination of rigorous intellectual training and deep spiritual formation produced leaders capable of withstanding extraordinary challenges — from Soviet labor camps to the demands of building Jewish life in remote corners of the globe.

The [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] model that defines modern Chabad rests on the foundation of Tomchei Temimim's educational philosophy: the Rashab saw the graduates as "soldiers of King David who would stand strong for Jewish principles in the four corners of the world." Every Chabad House, every outreach institution, every community built by a tamim traces its spiritual lineage to the yeshiva founded in Lubavitch in 1897.
