# שנת תער"ג (1902–03)

**חלק ראשון (Part 1) — The Year of Malchut and the Hidden Sefirot**

---

## Overview

The year **תער"ג** (1902–03), with its **30 maamarim**, is the hemshech's most expansive single year by count and its most comprehensive early treatment of the inner architecture of divine will. Where תרע"ב established the twin poles — Ta'anug / Ratzon, Or Yashar / Or Chozer — תרע"ג makes the first sustained *application* of that framework to specific Sefirot, specific festivals, and specific moments of divine-human encounter.

Three conceptual advances define this year:
1. **Malchut as Or Mekif perati** — the first identification of a specific Sefirah with a specific mode of Or Mekif, showing how Malchut is not the "lowest" Sefirah but the divine *relational interface* that faces creation without descending into it
2. **The Hidden Sefirot (ע"ס הגנוזות)** — a sustained analysis of the difference between the Sefirot as they exist pre-individuated within the Kav and as they emerge into differentiated vessels in Atzilut
3. **Five levels of Ratzon** — a structural map of will from the absolute (Yechida) through the active (Nefesh), corresponding to the five worlds and the soul-levels

The year begins at Rosh Hashana (the New Year) and traverses a full liturgical cycle — the only year of the hemshech structured around *Tishrei* as the opening pole rather than Shavuot.

---

## Conceptual Arc of This Year

### Opening: Malchut and the Coronation (Maamarim 21–25, Rosh Hashana – Shemini Atzeret)

The year's first maamar (*זה היום תחלת מעשיך*, Rosh Hashana night 2) opens by confronting the Rosh Hashana liturgy's apparent paradox: if God is truly Ein Sof — infinite, beyond all relation — how can there be a meaningful "coronation" (*hakhtatah*) on this day? The resolution is the year's load-bearing first move: **Malchut is identified with Or Mekif ha-perati**, the particular encompassing light that generates each world's existence as a distinct reality. Malchut "encompasses" creation without investing within it; divine kingship and divine transcendence are thus not in tension — Malchut *is* transcendence-facing-toward-creation.

The maamar then constructs **five levels of Ratzon** (divine will), ranging from:
- **Ratzon Pashut** (simple will, Yechida-level — *briש hOrmanuta d'Malka*) — no particulars, not even to itself
- **Glifu Glifu** (the "engraving" of the Ein Sof, Chaya-level) — particulars present but only for itself
- Three post-Tzimtzum levels corresponding to Beriyah/Yetzirah/Asiyah

This structure maps onto the five *Baruch she'amar* phrases in the morning liturgy — one of the hemshech's most elegant applications of liturgy to cosmological architecture.

**Pivotal insight**: The *collective* Or Chozer of *kol Yisrael* on Rosh Hashana uniquely draws down the Malchut-level of Or Mekif. Individual prayer accesses Or Pnimi levels; only the unified national address to God as King touches the Or Mekif dimension. This is the first systematic differentiation between individual and collective Or Chozer in the hemshech.

### Chanukah: The Light That Cannot Be Used (Maamarim 32–33, Kislev–Tevet)

The 20 Kislev and Chanukah maamarim constitute the year's structural center. The Chanukah maamar (*מאי חנוכה*) uses the Talmudic principle that Chanukah lights are **forbidden for personal use** (*asur lehishtamesh*) as the lens for a major advance:

The Or Chanukah = Or Mekif. Because Or Mekif is the level of divine light that does not invest itself within the vessel but hovers as encompassing light, it cannot be "used" — you cannot draw utility from a light that does not enter your interiority. The Greeks' assault on the Temple was not merely physical but aimed at suppressing the Or Mekif dimension of holiness, leaving only the Or Pnimi (inner, instrumental light) which could be co-opted for impure service. The miracle of Chanukah was the *revelation* that Or Mekif had not been extinguished — the one cruse of uncontaminated oil represented the untouchable Or Mekif reservoir.

This maamar permanently establishes in the hemshech the **Chanukah-Or Mekif correspondence** that will recur across subsequent years.

### Shavuot: The Three Crowns and the Hidden Sefirot (Maamarim 35–37)

The Shavuot cluster is the year's most technically elaborate section. The first night maamar (*ועשית חג שבועות — נדבת ידך*) opens three interlocking questions: Why is Shavuot generosity attributed to the *hand* rather than the heart? Why is the festival called "Shavuot" when the seventh week is complete *before* the festival? And why did the angels at Sinai bind only *two* crowns when the Mishnah in Avot counts three?

The resolution articulates the hemshech's most elaborate treatment of **the hidden Sefirot in the Kav**:

- The Kav contains the ten Sefirot in a pre-individuated state (*ע"ס הגנוזות*), analogous to how the 613 root-powers are "contained" in the human soul without existing as distinct powers
- The three crowns in Avot (Torah, Kehunah, Malchut) correspond to the three levels of structured divine service accessible *within* the world-system via Kelim
- The fourth crown (*shem tov*) surpasses all three because it corresponds to the pre-Kelim level — accessed not through structured service but through the overflow of the soul's essential goodness
- At Sinai, Israel received two crowns (not three) because *Naaseh v'Nishma* corresponds precisely to two registers of Or Chozer — the ascent from action (Asiyah) and from comprehension (Beriyah-Yetzirah) — but not yet the full Atzilut-level
- The *נדבת ידך* (generosity of the hand) is decoded: Matan Torah revealed that *practical action* — the lowest external organ, the hand — can carry and embody infinite light. This is Shavuot's unique chiddush: the "outside" becomes "inside"

### Summer–Elul: The Mutual Declaration (Maamarim 38–50)

The summer maamarim work through the Sefirot structure in the post-Tzimtzum worlds, deepening the Or Yashar/Or Chozer analysis through each week's Torah reading. The year's final maamar (*ש"פ תבוא — אתם האמרתם*) closes on the verse from Ki Tavo: *"You have declared God today... and God has declared you today"* — the mutual declaration between Israel and God. This is read as the structural completion of Or Chozer for the year: Israel's declaration of God as king = Or Chozer ascending; God's declaration of Israel as His people = Or Yashar descending in response. The two-directional movement seals the year's central theme of Malchut as the relational interface where Or Mekif and human Or Chozer meet.
