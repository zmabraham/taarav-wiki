# שנת תרד"ע (1903–04)

**חלק ראשון (Part 1) — The Year of Ta'anug and Teshuva**

---

## Overview

The year **תרד"ע** (1903–04), with its **26 maamarim**, is the year the hemshech makes its decisive move *inward* — past Ratzon (will) to **Ta'anug haAtzmi** (essential divine delight). Where תרע"ב established Keter's two faces (Ta'anug and Ratzon) and תרע"ג applied the Or Mekif framework to Malchut and the hidden Sefirot, תרד"ע now shows that Ta'anug is not merely parallel to Ratzon but *deeper*: it is the ground from which Ratzon emerges, and it is the level that Teshuva uniquely reaches.

Three conceptual advances define this year:
1. **Ta'anug haAtzmi as the root of Teshuva** — forgiveness is not divine "change of mind" (Nichum) but a reaching of the Ta'anug level that transcends Ratzon entirely
2. **The Shofar as vehicle of the hidden** (*bakesseh*) — the Rosh Hashana shofar bypasses the Ratzon-structured world and touches the sealed Ta'anug dimension
3. **Tohu vs. Tikkun as states of self-existence** — the root of Tohu's fragmentation was not plurality but *bichinas metzius* (self-perceived existence); Tikkun's gift is *rommemut atzmi* (essential transcendence beyond self-existence)

This year also preserves a notable structural gap: the preserved maamarim jump from Parshat Vayera (autumn) directly to Bamidbar/Shavuot, with no Chanukah or winter maamarim on record.

---

## Conceptual Arc of This Year

### Opening: The Hidden Rosh Hashana (Maamarim 51–56, Rosh Hashana – Shemini Atzeret)

The year's first maamar (*תקעו בחדש שופר בכסה ליום חגינו*) opens with a grammatical puzzle: the verse says "blow the shofar *in the month*" — but which month? Only secondly does it specify "*bakesseh*" (when the moon is hidden). The Rashab uses this as his entry: Rosh Hashana's moon is hidden (*kesseh* = covered). Why does the revelation of divine kingship occur specifically when the natural light-source is at its most concealed?

The answer introduces **Ta'anug haAtzmi** as the dimension of Keter that is concealed not incidentally but essentially:
- Ratzon (Will) = the outward-turning dimension of Keter; it can be "seen" because it faces toward the world
- Ta'anug (Delight) = the inward dimension; essentially concealed because it does not face toward the world at all
- The shofar blast, which bypasses all structured speech (dibur) and even structured thought (machashava), reaches the Ta'anug level directly — it is the one human act that "enters" the sealed zone

The year's opening sequence then extends the five-level Ratzon analysis from תרע"ג but now subordinates all five Ratzon-levels to a sixth, deeper layer: the Atzmus itself, which neither Ratzon nor Ta'anug can fully represent.

**Pivotal passage**: The Rashab distinguishes Ratzon and Ta'anug as both *atzmi* (essential) yet not causally related — "one can have Ta'anug without Ratzon (as in Chukim) and Ratzon without Ta'anug." This freedom from mutual causation is critical: it means Ta'anug is not a *product* of divine will but its own independent mode of the divine essence.

### The Teshuva Analysis (Maamarim 53–54, Shabbat Shuvah – Sukkot)

The Shabbat Shuvah and Yom Tov maamarim build the hemshech's most detailed treatment of **Teshuva** as a Kabbalistic event. The apparent paradox: when God forgives sins through Teshuva, this seems to imply a divine "change of mind" (*Nichum*, consolation/reversal) — but the prophet declares "God is not a man that He should be consoled" (*ki lo adam hu ki lo yinachem*). How can Teshuva work without implying divine change?

The resolution advances the hemshech significantly:
- Nichum (consolation/change) is possible only where intellect and will can be influenced by reasoning — where there is *taam nikhmad* (hidden rationale) behind the will
- *Ratzon atzmi* (essential will, the deepest layer of Ratzon) has no taam at all — "there is no reason for the Ratzon even a hidden reason" — and therefore cannot change
- But *Ta'anug haAtzmi* is even deeper than Ratzon atzmi. Teshuva uniquely reaches the Ta'anug level, not the Ratzon level. At the Ta'anug level, sins "do not take hold" (the vessel for sin is always within the Ratzon-structured world, not within Ta'anug)
- Teshuva does not change God's Ratzon (which would be Nichum) but *bypasses* the Ratzon-level entirely to the Ta'anug dimension where the teshuvah-ba'al is already rooted — hence "only regarding the Avot did God have desire (*chashak*), from them alone apart from performance of Torah and mitzvot"

### Shavuot: Elokim at Sinai and the Rommemut of Tikkun (Maamarim 61–63)

The Shavuot maamar (*וידבר אלקים את כל הדברים האלה*) opens with the question that becomes the year's conceptual anchor: Why does Scripture use *Shem Elokim* (the name associated with Tzimtzum and concealment) to describe the giving of the Torah, when Matan Torah was the greatest divine revelation in history?

The resolution deploys the year's deepest analysis yet of **Tohu vs. Tikkun**:

In Tohu, the Sefirot were in *bichinas metzius atzmi* — each Sefirah perceived its own existence as primary and therefore could not tolerate the existence of other Sefirot (*lo natan makom*). The Rashab makes a careful distinction: the conflict in Tohu was *not* caused by the existence of the other Sefirot (since even in Tikkun, each Sefirah has genuine existence). The cause was that the Tohu-Sefirot's "exaltedness" (*rommemut*) was itself a form of perceived self-existence — a Sefirah that elevated itself over others.

In Tikkun, the Sefirot have *rommemut atzmi* — essential transcendence that is *not* a form of self-perceived existence. Because Tikkun-transcendence is not "self-perceived," the Tikkun-Sefirot can coexist without conflict. Crucially: "when the exalted one is in a state of *beli metzius atzmo* (non-self-existence), then everything before it is truly nothing — like *kela mamiresh kama kela*." The deeper the essential transcendence, the more thoroughly other things are nullified before it without violence.

This analysis then explains Elokim at Sinai: the name Elokim, which represents *tzimtzum* and concealment of the infinite, is precisely the name for the mode of divine self-transcendence (*rommemut atzmi*) that enables revelation *within* the finite world without consuming it. Torah was given through the Elokim-mode because only through essential transcendence (not through direct infinite exposure) can finite minds receive infinite wisdom. The Tzimtzum was not a diminishment but the precondition for genuine *sharing* of Torah.

### Summer: Tohu-Tikkun Applied to the Parshiot (Maamarim 64–76)

The summer maamarim work through the Tohu/Tikkun distinction across the Torah's narratives, deepening the analysis maamar by maamar. The Nitzavim-Vayelech closing maamar returns to the "standing" theme of תרע"ב's ending, but now with the Tohu/Tikkun framework enriched: Israel "stands" before God because they have internalized the Tikkun-mode of *rommemut atzmi* — essential transcendence that is not defensive self-assertion but the very condition for divine habitation in the lower worlds.
