# שנת עתר"ו (1905–06)

**חלק ראשון (Part 1) — The Closing Year of Part One**

---

## Overview

The year **עתר"ו** (1905–06), with only **9 maamarim**, is the most compact year of the hemshech — and the closing year of **Part 1** (*Chelek Rishon*). The brevity is not incidental: the year's nine discourses represent a deliberate consolidation of the accumulated framework of the preceding four years, returning to the hemshech's foundational themes with the full weight of that development now behind them.

> **Note on publication order**: In the printed edition, עתר"ו appears as maamarim 135–143, after Part 2 (העת"ר, maamarim 77–134). This reflects the publication sequence, not the chronological order of delivery. עתר"ו (1905–06) actually precedes העת"ר (1909–10) by four years.

The year begins at Rosh Hashana and ends at Parshat Vayera — only the autumn season is preserved. Whether subsequent maamarim were delivered but not preserved, or whether the Rashab simply paused the hemshech here before resuming in 1909, is not certain from the existing text. What is clear is that these nine discourses function as Part 1's *chazara* (review and consolidation) — covering again the year-opening theme of *Zeh HaYom Techilat Maasecha* and the hidden-disclosure paradox of Avraham and Sodom.

Two conceptual threads mark this year:
1. **Ratzo v'Shov and Yechida** — the deepest integration of the "running and returning" dynamic with the soul's highest level
2. **"המכסה אני" — Divine concealment before the prophet** — what God *hides* from Avraham corresponds structurally to Or Mekif at its most inward

---

## Conceptual Arc of This Year

### Rosh Hashana Revisited: Zeh HaYom (Maamarim 135–138)

The year opens by returning to the same dibur hamaschil as תרע"ג's first maamar: *זה היום תחלת מעשיך זכרון ליום ראשון*. This is not repetition but *elevation*: the question "How can this be both the 'beginning of Your works' and merely a *memory* of the first day?" is asked again after four years of development, and the answer is now architecturally richer.

By עתר"ו, the hemshech has established:
- Keter's dual faces (Ta'anug / Ratzon), with Ta'anug as the ground of Ratzon
- Five levels of Ratzon corresponding to soul-levels and worlds  
- Malchut as Or Mekif perati
- The hidden Sefirot in the Kav
- Ta'anug haAtzmi as the level Teshuva reaches

With all this in place, the Rosh Hashana question is answered in full: *Zeh haYom* = the drawing of **Ratzon haAtzmi** (essential will) that is beyond even the Ratzon of the divine-will structure; and *zikaron leyom haRishon* = the connection this draws to the absolute *Reshit* — the pre-Tzimtzum primordial state of the Or Ein Sof. Rosh Hashana is the annual moment when the Reshimu (the impression of Or Ein Sof remaining after the Tzimtzum) re-contacts its source.

**The Ratzo v'Shov integration**: This year's Rosh Hashana cluster links the annual cosmic cycle of Ratzon-withdrawal (the *shlita* and *dormitta* of divine light at the year's end) and the restoration-through-shofar to the soul's Ratzo v'Shov — the soul's perpetual oscillation between ascending into the Ein Sof (Ratzo = running) and returning to vessel-existence (Shov = returning). Yechida is identified as the soul-level whose Ratzo reaches the Ta'anug dimension rather than only the Ratzon level.

### Sukkot and Shemini Atzeret (Maamarim 138–139)

The Sukkot and Shemini Atzeret maamarim develop the theme of *simchat Torah* as the festival of Or Mekif's most concentrated disclosure. Shemini Atzeret's unusual character — *atzeret* meaning "hold back," the king who asks his guests to tarry one more day — is read as the moment when Or Mekif does not recede but rests temporarily in the Or Pnimi register, producing the unique joy of Simchat Torah where the *encompassing* becomes *intimate*.

### Bereishit and Noach (Maamarim 140–141)

The autumn Parashiot turn to the nature of creation itself. *Bereishit* — "In the beginning" — is analyzed through the lens of Ta'anug haAtzmi: the beginning of the world was not an act of Ratzon (purposive will aiming at an object) but of Ta'anug — divine delight that generated the desire for a world simply by being what it is. The soul analogy: just as a person's deepest desire sometimes arises not from deliberate reasoning but from the overflow of essential pleasure, so too the world arose from Atzmus's Ta'anug rather than from a pre-formed plan.

### The Closing: "המכסה אני" — What God Conceals from Avraham (Maamar 143, Vayera)

The year's final maamar (*וה' אמר המכסה אני מאברהם*) takes as its center the verse from Vayera: "Shall I hide from Avraham what I am about to do?" — God's interior deliberation before informing Avraham of Sodom's impending destruction.

The question: Why is divine deliberation ("shall I hide?") articulated as a question to Himself? What does it mean for the infinite to *withhold* from a prophet?

The answer uses the Or Mekif / Or Pnimi structure: what God considers "hiding" from Avraham is not the *fact* of Sodom's destruction (that will be disclosed) but the level of divine perception from which the decree emerges — the Or Mekif dimension that is by nature beyond prophetic grasp. Avraham, as the embodiment of *Chesed* (Or Pnimi par excellence), cannot directly perceive Or Mekif; the deliberation is about what divine mode can be disclosed to which kind of prophetic receiver.

This maamar caps Part 1's central teaching: **Or Mekif cannot be "received" directly** — it can only be drawn down through the specific channels that Or Chozer opens. The prophet's limit is not a limitation of the prophet but a structural feature of revelation itself.
