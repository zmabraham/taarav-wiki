## Overview

This book chronicles Chabad's presence in Eretz Yisrael (the Land of Israel/Palestine) from the first Chassidic immigration in 1777 through the founding of the State of Israel. It traces the evolution from early Chassidic settlement in Tiberias and Safed through the establishment of Hebron as the Chabad center, the founding of Kfar Chabad, and institutional development through the Rayatz era.

## Chapter Structure

The book is organized into numbered chapters (prakim), covering:

1. **Chapters 1–5**: First Chassidic aliya, settlement in Tiberias (5537/1777 onward) under Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk
2. **Chapter 6**: Settlement in Hebron (5581/1821) — the Mitteler Rebbe orders Chassidim to relocate from Safed/Tiberias to Hebron
3. **Chapters 7–10**: Kolel Chabad — separate fund established 5565 (1805); administration of charitable funds
4. **Chapters 11–18**: Individual chassidim and families in Hebron; relationships with Sephardic community; Moses Montefiore's visits and endowment
5. **Chapters 20–26**: Kolel Chabad administration in the Tzemach Tzedek era; emigration patterns
6. **Chapters 27–31**: Jerusalem settlement; shuls; Tzemach Tzedek synagogue
7. **Chapter 32**: Chabad in Safed and Tiberias (separate treatment for later period)
8. **Chapter 33**: Chabad in Tel Aviv-Jaffa (1924 onward)
9. **Chapters 35–36**: Kollel administration; fundraising envoys (shadranim)
10. **Chapters 38–43**: Kolel under Rayatz; Tochnon (constitution) of 5693 (1933); Va'ad Pekidei Kolel (Committee of Administrators)
11. **Later chapters**: Yeshiva Torat Emet (Jerusalem and Hebron); Tomchei Temimim (Tel Aviv branch); Kfar Chabad

## Key Themes

### First Chabad Aliya (1777)
Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk led a group of ~300 chassidim to the Holy Land in 5537 (1777). They initially settled in Tiberias (Tveria). Key early figures included Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk (who eventually led the community), Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk, and Rabbi Menachem Mendel's successor Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk.

### Controversy with Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk
The book documents the theological dispute between Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk and the Alter Rebbe, which had practical consequences for the distribution of funds from the diaspora. This long-running dispute affected the finances of the Eretz Yisrael Chassidic community for decades.

### Establishment of Separate Kolel Chabad (1805)
In 5565 (1805), a separate Kolel Chabad fund was established, distinct from the general Chassidic kolel. This formalized the Chabad community's financial independence in the Holy Land.

### Hebron as Chabad Center (1821 onward)
The Mitteler Rebbe directed Chassidim to relocate to Hebron (Chevron), which became the primary Chabad settlement for much of the 19th century. Key families settled there, and the Tzemach Tzedek periodically sent family members to reinforce the community.

### Kolel Administration Structure
Throughout the Czarist era, Kolel Chabad had a two-tier structure: (1) a Nasi and Gabai in the diaspora (in Russia) who oversaw fundraising, and (2) Memunei (administrators) in the Holy Land who distributed funds. First Nasi was the Alter Rebbe himself; first Gabai was Rabbi Yaakov of Smilian. Later the Tzemach Tzedek was assisted by his uncle Rabbi Chaim Avraham (son of the Alter Rebbe) and his brother-in-law Rabbi Menachem Nachum (son of the Mitteler Rebbe).

### Yeshiva Torat Emet (Jerusalem, 1921)
Seven select students of Tomchei Temimim were sent from Russia to found Yeshiva Torat Emet in Hebron (later Jerusalem). The Rayatz established a constitution (tochnon) for the Kolel in 5693 (1933), requiring elections and formal governance.

### Tel Aviv Chabad (1924 onward)
Chabad presence in Tel Aviv-Jaffa began with a branch of Yeshiva Torat Emet (Cheider Bnei Temimim, later Yeshiva Achei Temimim) in winter 1924. A second phase 1938–1951 included adult division Tomchei Temimim.

### Earthquake of 1837
The great earthquake of 5597 (1837) destroyed much of Safed (Tzfat), killing multiple Chabad residents including Rabbi Shemiya (who had lived in Safed until then) and R. Lapidot Chalavna and his son Shalom Aharon.
