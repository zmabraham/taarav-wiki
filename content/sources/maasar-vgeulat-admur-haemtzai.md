## Overview

This book presents the archival documents — in Russian translation and Hebrew transcription — from the police and court file concerning the arrest and trial of Rabbi DovBer Schneuri, the Mitteler Rebbe (second Rebbe of Chabad-Lubavitch). The file was discovered in Soviet-era archives and published here for the first time with extensive commentary.

The arrest and trial is one of the most significant events in Chabad's Czarist-era history. The Rebbe's acquittal on 10 Kislev (Yud Kislev) became a Chassidic holiday celebrated annually.

## Historical Background

After the death of the Alter Rebbe (24 Tevet 5573/1812), Rabbi DovBer Schneuri assumed leadership and settled in Lubavitch. Chassidim donated "ma'amad" money — voluntary support payments — to sustain the Rebbe and his extended family. This practice formed the basis of the charges against him.

## Three Stages of the Case

The affair unfolded in three stages across three years:

**Stage 1: Summer 5585 (1825)**
Simcha Kissin of Vitebsk filed three denunciations with Governor Khovansky in Vitebsk. Kissin was a relative of the Rebbe's family who had turned against Chassidus.

**Stage 2: Tishrei-Kislev 5586 (1825)**
Governor Khovansky ordered interrogations. The Mitteler Rebbe was summoned to Vitebsk. Two of his Chassidim — Zalman Roizes of Slonim and Shlomo Freydes of Shklov — were also interrogated (5 October 1825). Special investigator sent to Lubavitch to inspect. Rabbi Aharon of Staroselye was summoned from Staroselye for interrogation (21-28 October 1825). The Rebbe was released to his home on 10 Kislev 5586.

**Stage 3: 5586–5588 (1825–1827)**
The case moved through the Babinowitz district court, then the Mogilev Gubernia Supreme Court. Inquiries were sent to Chassidic communities in Kapust, Orsha, Shklov, and Mogilev to verify the Rebbe's account. The Supreme Court of Mogilev acquitted the Rebbe on 11 Kislev 5587 (29 November 1826). Governor Khovansky appealed to the Senate in St. Petersburg. The Senate upheld the acquittal on 21 Kislev 5588 (28 November 1827) — twelve days after the Mitteler Rebbe had passed away on 9 Kislev 5588.

## The Charges

The main denunciations alleged:

1. **Illegal tax collection**: The "ma'amad" money was characterized as an illegal levy on Jewish communities
2. **Financial fraud**: Simcha Kissin attached a translation of the Rebbe's letter about distribution of 35,000 rubles, alleging (via mistranslation) that the Rebbe had collected 140,000 rubles and distributed only 25% of it
3. **Subversive organization**: The general Chassidic movement was characterized as a sect that undermined authority and corrupted youth

## Legal Framework

The trial took place under the Jewish Statute (Chukkat HaYehudim) of 5565 (1805), which prohibited rabbis from collecting money beyond their fixed salary. The Rebbe's defense was twofold: (1) ma'amad was entirely voluntary, (2) he was not a "rabbi" in the formal/elected sense subject to the statute.

## The Holiday: Yud Kislev

The Mitteler Rebbe was released from Vitebsk to his home on 10 Kislev 5586 (Stage 1 ending). This date became the Chassidic holiday of Yud Kislev, celebrating the Rebbe's liberation — analogous to the Alter Rebbe's liberation on 19 Kislev. The book documents uncertainty about which year (5586 or 5587) the holiday was established.

## Additional Documents

The book also contains Chassidic epistles (letters) found in the court file — including a mystical discourse (ma'amar) on Kabbalah — which were confiscated and translated into Russian. Pages 16 of the source contain extensive footnotes on these Kabbalistic texts.
