# Zichronos (זכרונותי) — My Memoirs

## Book Identification

- **Author**: Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levin (ישראל יהודה לוין)
- **Written**: Begun ~1960 (5720), completed over 35 years; signed 18 Elul 5755 (1995), Kfar Chabad, Eretz Yisrael
- **Published**: Printed in the "Meisaviv LeChassidus" (מסביב לחסידות) series by Kehos/Chabad Library
- **Available at**: chabadlibrary.org/books/5800000000
- **Language**: Hebrew
- **Edited/prepared by**: Son R' Shalom DovBer Levin and grandson R' Yosef Yitzchak Keller
- **Note**: Portions previously published in *Toldos Chabad beRussia haSovietit* (History of Chabad in Soviet Russia), Kehos 5749 (1989)

## Scope and Time Period

The memoir covers the author's life from approximately 5674 (1914) through the early 1960s (5723), with the primary focus on:

- **Early years**: Birth (7 Tammuz 5672 = 1912) in Zhivitiov, Ukraine; family background
- **Pogroms/WWI aftermath**: 5679–5680 (1919–1920) — Haidamak and other gang violence in Zhivitiov
- **Migration to Lipovitz**: Spring 5680 (Passover 1920)
- **Soviet oppression era**: 5680–5692 (1920–1932) — Rise of Yevsektsiya, anti-religious campaigns, underground yeshiva network
- **Underground Yeshiva years**: 5687–5698 (1927–1938) — 10 years of study in clandestine yeshivas across Soviet Russia
- **Marriage and Kharkov**: Late 5698 (1938) — Marriage in Kharkov
- **WWII and Central Asia**: 5701–5706 (1941–1946) — Evacuation to Samarkand; death of son; underground activities
- **Post-war**: 5706–5709 (1946–1949) — Journey from Russia to Eretz Yisrael (arrived early summer 5709)
- **Kfar Chabad**: 5709–5723 (1949–1963) — Settling in Kfar Chabad; first trip to the Rebbe (Tishrei 5723)

The memoir **ends** with the author's first visit to the Rebbe (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson) in New York for Tishrei 5723 (1962).

## Author Background

Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levin was born 7 Tammuz 5672 (1912) in **Zhivitiov** (ז'יוויטוב), Ukraine (near Titov and Dashov, Vinytsia Province). His family:

- **Father**: R' Efraim Shmuel ben HaRav Yosef Levin — melamed (teacher), deeply religious
- **Mother**: Leah Beila (Bilienky) bat R' Yisrael Bilienky — shochet from Dashov
- **Paternal grandfather**: R' Yosef Levin — rabbi in Letichev, Zhivitiov, Dashov, Linitz (died ~5649/1889)
- **Paternal grandmother**: Sara Yehudis (Levin)
- **Father's siblings**: Chaya Bracha (married Malkhman; Dirazshne), Avraham Yaakov (emigrated to USA, died young)
- **Maternal grandfather**: R' Yisrael Bilienky — shochet in Dashov; grandson of R' Avraham Yehuda of Linitz; family from R' Nachman Kassover and R' Moshe his son
- **Siblings**: Brother Yosef (HY"D), sisters Sara, Rivka, Freida
- **Wife**: Ita Devora (Gorkow) — died after 1945; married in Kharkov winter 5698 (1938)
- **Father-in-law**: R' Meir Gorkow — also wrote Chabad memoirs (published in *Sefer haZichronos Divrei haYomim*, NY 5736/1976)
- **Mother-in-law**: Piga Rivka Gorkow
- **Son**: Efraim Shmuel Levin — died 9 Kislev 5702 (age 2¼) in Samarkand
- **Son (Kfar Chabad)**: R' Shalom DovBer Levin — edited/published the memoirs
- **Grandson**: R' Yosef Yitzchak Keller

## Key Themes

1. **Survival under Soviet persecution**: The memoir is a first-hand account of a young man navigating the Soviet anti-religious apparatus, NKVD, and Yevsektsiya
2. **Underground Tomchei Temimim yeshiva network**: The author spent 10 years studying in and helping operate secret Chabad yeshivas across Soviet cities
3. **Pogroms of 1919–1920**: Vivid eye-witness account of Haidamak and other gang atrocities in Zhivitiov and Titov
4. **Soviet documentation/passport system**: Detailed descriptions of the kafkaesque bureaucracy of Soviet identity papers, conscription exemptions, and internal passports
5. **Faith under extreme adversity**: The memoir emphasizes how correspondence with the Rebbe (Rayatz) sustained the author through persecution
6. **The role of the Lubavitcher Rebbe (Rayatz)**: Numerous specific correspondences between the author and the 6th Rebbe are quoted verbatim

## Key Events Mentioned

- Haidamak and gang pogroms in Zhivitiov and Titov (winter 5680/1919–1920)
- Migration of the Levin family from Zhivitiov to Lipovitz (just before Passover 5680)
- The author's first Yud-Tes Kislev farbrengen in Lipovitz (~5688/1927)
- Travel to Vitebsk to join underground Tomchei Temimim (Rosh Chodesh Iyar 5688/1928)
- Multiple arrests by Soviet authorities (NKVD/GPU) in Kiev and other cities
- Soviet conscription miracle (11 Tishrei 5696/1935) — exemption through eyeglasses subterfuge, on advice of the Rebbe
- Death of son Efraim Shmuel in Samarkand (9 Kislev 5702/1941)
- Obtaining forged Polish papers as "Naphtali Bat" to avoid conscription
- Leaving Russia (summer 5706/1946) among first Chabad families to exit
- Settling in Kfar Chabad (early summer 5709/1949)
- First trip to the Rebbe (26 Elul 5722/1962): Paris layover; arriving at 770 for Maariv; Erev Rosh Hashana farbrengen; the Rebbe's blessing in Yiddish
