## Overview

This volume is the fourth in the "Toldos Chabad" series compiled by Rabbi Shalom DovBer Levin, librarian at Lubavitch Library (770 Eastern Parkway). The work covers Chabad history in Czarist Russia across six generations of Rebbes (Alter Rebbe through early Rayatz era), drawing on letters, documents, and memoirs.

The book is organized by Rebbe generation (dor):
1. **First Generation** (Dor Rishon) — Alter Rebbe (Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, ~1770–1812)
2. **Second Generation** (Dor Sheni) — Mitteler Rebbe (Rabbi DovBer Schneuri, 1812–1827)
3. **Third Generation** (Dor Shlishi) — Tzemach Tzedek (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn, 1827–1866)
4. **Fourth Generation** (Dor Revi'i) — Maharash (Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn, 1866–1882)
5. **Fifth Generation** (Dor Chamishi) — Rashab (Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn, 1882–1920)
6. **Sixth Generation** (Dor Shishi) — Rayatz (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, early years)

## Key Themes

### Ancestry of the Alter Rebbe
The book opens with extensive genealogical research on the Alter Rebbe's family line: his grandfather Rabbi Moshe of Pozna (descended from the Maharal of Prague), his father Rabbi Baruch (born Lozna, died ~1792), and his wife Rebbetzin Sterna (daughter of Rabbi Yehuda Leib Segal of Vitebsk). Details family relationships with the Kissin family of Vitebsk — rabbis and community leaders.

### Alter Rebbe at the Magid's Court (Mezeritch)
Documents that the Magid of Mezeritch not only taught Chassidus but also trained disciples in halacha. Records that Rabbi Shmelke of Nikolsburg and his brother Rabbi Pinchas visited the Magid's court and praised the Alter Rebbe's scholarly writings. The Magid specifically chose the Alter Rebbe to compose a systematic halacha compendium — which became the Shulchan Aruch HaRav.

### The Alter Rebbe's Library and Arrests
Details the confiscation of 103 books and 14 manuscript volumes from the Alter Rebbe's home in Lyozna in Kislev 5561 (1800) during his second arrest. Records the Czarist government's interest in whether his writings contained anti-state material.

### MaHaRiL of Yanowitz (Yehuda Leib of Yanowitz)
Extensive treatment of the Alter Rebbe's brother Rabbi Yehuda Leib (MaHaRiL), who served as the primary transcriber (mancheh) of the Alter Rebbe's Torah discourses, compiled the notes that became Torah Or, and after his brother's passing, edited and prepared the Shulchan Aruch HaRav for publication. Served as Moreh Tzedek in Yanowitz. Died between late 5585–early 5586 (1825–1826).

### Mitteler Rebbe's Trial (5586–5588 / 1825–1828)
One of the most detailed sections: the three-stage legal proceedings against Rabbi DovBer Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe). The informant was Simcha Kissin of Vitebsk (a relative of the Rebbe's family who later turned informer). The charges: illegal tax collection from Chassidim ("ma'amad money"). Timeline documented with specific dates from archival sources. Key officials: Governor Khovansky of Vitebsk; Supreme Court of Mogilev Gubernia; the Senate in St. Petersburg. Rabbi Aharon of Starosielye was also summoned for interrogation. Final acquittal: 21 Kislev 5588 (1827) — 12 days after the Mitteler Rebbe's passing on 9 Kislev 5588.

### Tzemach Tzedek's Literary Output
Documents the Tzemach Tzedek's vast responsa literature: at least 14 manuscript volumes of halachic responsa, most of which were destroyed in a fire in Lubavitch. The published Tzemach Tzedek responsa represent only a fraction of his output. He began responding to halachic questions systematically from age 28 (c. 5579/1819). His son Rabbi Yisrael Noach (MaHaRiN) later assisted in answering responsa in his father's final years.

### Maharash Era
The Maharash (Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn) succeeded his father in Lubavitch and inherited the manuscript library. His siblings moved to other towns (Kapust, Lyadi, etc.) to spread Chabad Torah. The manuscripts and printed books were divided among the six sons of the Tzemach Tzedek.
