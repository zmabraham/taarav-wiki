# Simcha Kissin's Denunciations (June–July 1825)

**Also Known As**: The Three Malshinuyot; Kissin's Accusations  
**Dates**: 27 Sivan–26 Menachem Av 5585 (June–July 1825)  
**Location**: Vitebsk, Russian Empire

---

## Overview

Between June and July 1825, [[entities/people/simcha-kissin|Simcha Kissin]], a Jewish resident of Vitebsk, filed three written denunciations with [[entities/people/governor-khavanski|Governor Khavanski]] accusing [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|the Mitteler Rebbe]] of illegal financial practices and abuse of religious authority. These denunciations triggered the imperial investigation that led to the Rebbe's arrest on 1 Tishrei 5586 (October 1825) and nearly resulted in his permanent disqualification from rabbinic leadership.

The three denunciations, submitted in Russian translation with Hebrew original documents attached, became the foundation of the state's case against the Rebbe.

---

## The Three Denunciations

### First Denunciation (27 Sivan–26 Tammuz 5585 / June 1825)

Kissin's initial complaint focused on the expansion of Chassidic influence and alleged oppression of "true Jews who hold to the Old Religion" (Mitnagdim). He accused the Rebbe of:

1. Strengthening the "Karlin sect or Chassidim" (he used both terms interchangeably)
2. Oppressing traditional Jews who did not follow Chassidic customs
3. Using various means of pressure to compel conformity

**Evidence submitted**: None specified in surviving documents.

### Second Denunciation (27 Tammuz–26 Av 5585 / July 1825)

Kissin escalated his accusations with specific charges about the [[entities/concepts/maavad|maavad]] (Chassidic financial support system):

1. The Rebbe collected large sums annually (10,000–14,000 rubles) through special emissaries
2. These collections violated Section 52 of the [[entities/concepts/jewish-statute-1804|Jewish Statute of 1804]], which prohibited rabbis from receiving compensation beyond a fixed salary
3. The practice created financial hardship for Chassidic families

**Evidence submitted**: Documents obtained from the estate of the late [[entities/people/pinchas-shik|Pinchas Shik]].

### Third Denunciation (27 Av–28 Menachem Av 5585 / July 1825)

Kissin's final and most detailed submission presented concrete documentary evidence:

1. A Hebrew document from 1813 (Tevet 5574) detailing a 35,000-ruble distribution supervised by the Rebbe
2. Personal letters from the Rebbe to Kissin
3. The document showed the Rebbe divided funds among family members and relatives
4. Kissin alleged this proved illegal enrichment beyond rabbinic compensation limits

**Key evidence**: The distribution letter, seized from Pinchas Shik's estate after his death, became the centerpiece of Governor Khavanski's investigation.

---

## Kissin's Story of How He Obtained the Documents

Kissin explained to Governor Khavanski that he obtained the incriminating Hebrew document in 1822 under these circumstances:

- He had traveled to Shklov on business
- Tensions existed between Chassidim and Mitnagdim in the community
- The Chassidim had seized a room in the synagogue, leading traditional Jews to consider filing a complaint with the mayor
- [[entities/people/pinchas-shik|Pinchas Shik]], a close associate of the Rebbe, asked Kissin to intervene and prevent the complaint
- As security (ערבון), Shik deposited the Hebrew document in Kissin's hands
- Kissin had the document translated into Russian
- After Shik's death, the Rebbe not only failed to stop the alleged persecution but became more aggressive
- Kissin then decided to use the document as evidence of illegal activity

---

## Legal Framework Exploited

Kissin strategically framed his accusations to invoke specific provisions of the [[entities/concepts/jewish-statute-1804|Jewish Statute of 1804]]:

- **Section 51**: Prohibited rabbis from exercising civil judicial authority or interfering in secular disputes
- **Section 52**: Limited rabbis to a fixed salary approved by the governor, with strict prohibition on collecting additional funds
- **Section 10**: Required rabbis to know Russian, Polish, or German (the Mitteler Rebbe signed documents in Hebrew)

By presenting himself as defending "true Jews" against an abusive sect, Kissin appealed to the state's desire to regulate Jewish communal life and protect subjects from religious exploitation.

---

## Governor Khavanski's Response

Governor Khavanski took Kissin's accusations seriously and immediately ordered an investigation:

1. **Document verification** (September 1825): He ordered Polkovnik [[entities/people/loginov|Loginov]] and Court Counselor [[entities/people/markov-investigator|Markov]] to verify whether the Hebrew documents were authentic and signed by the Rebbe
2. **Financial investigation**: He demanded details about how much money was collected, from whom, by what means, and how it was distributed
3. **Legal analysis**: He asked whether the Rebbe's actions violated Sections 51 and 52 of the 1804 statute

Khavanski's eventual appeal to the Imperial Senate would argue that Kissin's denunciations revealed systemic abuses requiring new legislation to strengthen rabbinic oversight.

---

## The Rebbe's Absence During Denunciations

A tragic dimension: At the very time Kissin was filing his denunciations (summer 1825), the Mitteler Rebbe was at [[entities/places/karlsbad|Karlsbad]] in Bohemia taking a health cure. A letter from the Rebbe to his representatives, written in secrecy and coded language, reveals:

- He knew of the "enemy" working through the "adversary" (Khavanski)
- He feared he might not survive the battle given his health and advanced age
- He was unable to respond immediately while abroad for medical treatment

Only after the Rebbe's return did the full defense begin.

---

## Historical Significance

The Kissin denunciations represent:

1. **Weaponization of inter-Jewish conflict** through state channels (Mitnagdim vs. Chassidim)
2. **The vulnerability of private archives** — estate papers became weapons in hostile hands
3. **The danger of archival evidence** — a single Hebrew document, translated and presented out of context, nearly destroyed Chabad leadership
4. **The state's willingness to investigate** even anonymous or motivated denunciations when they alleged statutory violations

The ultimate exoneration (Imperial Senate decision, 1827) found "no legal basis for the denunciation made by the Jew Kissin," but the episode revealed how easily internal Jewish disputes could escalate to imperial investigations in Czarist Russia.
