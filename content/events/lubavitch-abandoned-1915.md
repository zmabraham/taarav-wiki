# Lubavitch Abandoned — WWI Relocation (1915)

**Date**: 1915 (5675)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] → Rostov-on-Don, Russia

---

## Overview

In 1915, as World War One brought the front lines dangerously close to the town of [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] relocated the Chabad world center to Rostov-on-Don in southern Russia. This ended Lubavitch's 102-year role as Chabad's spiritual capital, a role the town had held since the [[events/chabad-in-lubavitch-1813|Mitteler Rebbe settled there in 1813]].

---

## Background: WWI and Russian Chaos

World War One devastated the Pale of Settlement — the western border region where most Russian Jews lived. German and Austro-Hungarian forces advanced deep into Russian territory; the region from Vilna to Minsk to the western Ukraine became a military theater. Lubavitch, in the Smolensk/Mogilev region, was not immediately on the front but was close enough to be endangered.

The Russian military required the evacuation of Jewish populations from many border regions, often forcibly and with little preparation — creating massive displacement and suffering.

---

## The Decision to Leave

The Rashab chose to relocate rather than risk the community's safety. He moved to **Rostov-on-Don** — a major city in southern Russia (present-day Russia, near the Ukrainian border), which offered more stability than the embattled western regions.

**Tomchei Temimim** relocated with him, attempting to maintain operations in the new location.

---

## The Burning of Lubavitch

The sources document that after the departure:

- **1918**: Following the Russian Revolution, Yeshiva Tomchei Temimim went underground; the physical infrastructure in Lubavitch came under Bolshevik authority
- **1921**: The entire Rebbe's court (the physical building complex in Lubavitch) was burned — likely by Bolshevik or revolutionary forces, ending the visible institutional presence permanently

The town that had been Chabad's world center for 102 years became an ordinary Soviet town, its Jewish religious character erased.

---

## Rostov-on-Don

The Rashab lived in Rostov-on-Don until his death there in 1920. The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] assumed leadership in Rostov and continued there until his arrest and expulsion in 1927.

---

## The Holocaust of Lubavitch Jews

In November 1941 — 26 years after the Rashab left — the Nazis murdered all **483 remaining Jews** of Lubavitch. The town that had been Chabad's world center became yet another site of Holocaust annihilation, leaving virtually no survivors.

See [[events/holocaust-chabad|Chabad and the Holocaust]].

---

## Legacy

Lubavitch's end as Chabad's center marked a before-and-after in the movement's history:

- **Before 1915**: Chabad centered in a specific place — Lubavitch — tied to Russian geography and culture
- **After 1915**: Chabad in perpetual motion — Rostov, Leningrad, Riga, Warsaw, Otwock, and ultimately Brooklyn

The loss of Lubavitch was ultimately transcended by the Chabad principle that the Rebbe's presence — not a physical location — was the center. Yet the town remained embedded in the movement's identity, giving it its permanent name: Chabad-Lubavitch.
