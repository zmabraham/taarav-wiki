# The Alter Rebbe's Family Background and Vitebsk Origins

**The family origins of R' Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe), founder of Chabad, including his Vitebsk family, elder brother, and wife**

## Background

R' Shneur Zalman of Liadi (1745–1812), the founder of Chabad Chassidus, was born in Liozna (also spelled Liadi), White Russia. His family's origins were in the Vitebsk region, and several family members are documented in historical sources.

## Family Members

### Father: R' Baruch of Vitebsk

R' Shneur Zalman's father, R' Baruch, was a respected figure in the Vitebsk Jewish community. He gave his son both the traditional Torah education and an environment of learning and piety.

### Elder Brother: R' Yehuda Leib of Yanowitz (Maharil)

The Alter Rebbe's elder brother, R' Yehuda Leib, served as rabbi of Yanowitz in the Vitebsk region. He was deeply involved in the early Chabad movement and contributed to the editorial work on Torah Or. He lived longer than his younger brother and witnessed the Mitteler Rebbe's early leadership.

### Wife: Rebbetzin Sterna

The Alter Rebbe married Rebbetzin Sterna, daughter of R' Yehuda Leib of Vitebsk. The marriage connected the young Shneur Zalman to an established Vitebsk family. Rebbetzin Sterna managed the household through decades of the movement's growth, two imprisonments, and the final flight from Liadi.

## The Vitebsk Connection

The concentration of the Alter Rebbe's immediate family in the Vitebsk region reflects the geographic heartland of early Chabad. Vitebsk (today the fourth-largest city of Belarus) was one of the most important Jewish cities in White Russia, and the Chabad movement's roots there gave it access to the significant Vitebsk Jewish community from the outset.

## Significance

Understanding the Alter Rebbe's family background illuminates the social fabric from which Chabad emerged. The close relationship between R' Shneur Zalman and his brother, the role of Rebbetzin Sterna in supporting the court, and the Vitebsk origins of the family all contributed to shaping the movement's character and connections.
