# Eliezrov's Appointment as First Chabad Rav of Samarkand (1898)

**Date**: 20 Iyar 5658 (May 1898)  
**Location**: Samarkand, Russian Turkestan (present-day Uzbekistan)  
**Key Figure**: [[entities/people/shlomo-yehuda-eliezrov|Rabbi Shlomo Yehuda Leib Eliezrov]]

---

## Overview

On 20 Iyar 5658 (May 1898), Rabbi Shlomo Yehuda Leib Eliezrov was formally appointed as rabbi of **Samarkand** — a major milestone in the history of Central Asian Jewry and the beginning of systematic Chabad religious leadership in the ancient Bukharan Jewish community.

---

## Background: Why Eliezrov?

Rabbi Eliezrov came to Samarkand as a **shadar** (shaliach d'rabbanan — fundraising emissary) from the Hebron Chabad community. He had been making periodic visits to Bukhara and Georgia since at least 5647 (1887), developing relationships with the local Jewish community and gaining firsthand knowledge of their religious conditions.

The Bukharan Jewish community in Samarkand — one of the oldest Jewish communities in the world — maintained Jewish practice but with significant gaps in standards, particularly regarding ritual slaughter (shechita). They needed a qualified rabbinical authority who could both understand their traditions and strengthen their practice.

---

## Arrangement Through the Rishon L'Tzion

The appointment was arranged through the mediation of the **Rishon L'Tzion** (Sephardic Chief Rabbi of Jerusalem) — **Rabbi Yaakov Shaul Elyashar**, known as "Yisha Brachah" from the title of his responsa collection. The Rishon L'Tzion's involvement was crucial because:

1. The Bukharan Jews were culturally closer to the Sephardic world than the Ashkenazic
2. An appointment endorsed by the Sephardic Chief Rabbi would carry authority with a Sephardic-adjacent community
3. The Rishon L'Tzion could bridge the gap between Eliezrov's Ashkenazic-Chassidic background and the Bukharan community's different traditions

---

## The Contract Terms

The formal appointment contract specified:
- **Term**: Three years
- **Salary**: 2,400 rubles annually
- **Halakhic practice**: Eliezrov was to rule according to **Sephardic custom** (following Maran — Rabbi Yosef Karo's Shulchan Aruch), with permission to be stringent according to Ashkenazic practice where the Shulchan Aruch explicitly permitted greater stringency

The requirement to follow Sephardic custom was essential: demanding Ashkenazic practice would have alienated the Bukharan community rather than strengthening them. Eliezrov accepted this condition, demonstrating both pragmatic wisdom and respect for Bukharan Jewish tradition.

---

## Letters of Support

The appointment was endorsed by two of the greatest halakhic authorities of the era:

1. **The author of the _Shadei Chamad_** (close associate of the Chafetz Chaim) — wrote in support of Eliezrov's appointment (5658)
2. **The _Aruch HaShulchan_** (Rabbi Yechiel Michel Epstein, author of the encyclopedic halakhic work): praised Eliezrov as "a great and distinguished rabbi, sharp and expert, a pious and holy man, a descendant of the Baal HaTanya" — explicitly connecting him to the Chabad Rebbes through his lineage

The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] continued to write Eliezrov letters of support throughout his mission.

---

## Transforming the Samarkand Kehilla

Eliezrov's rabbinate brought dramatic changes to Samarkand's Jewish life:

### The Shechita Reform

His most consequential act was declaring the existing meat supply **treif** (non-kosher). He had investigated the shechita practices and found them halachically deficient. His response was comprehensive:

1. Declared all existing shechita invalid
2. Trained new, properly qualified shochetim (ritual slaughterers)
3. Built a new slaughterhouse with proper gender separation
4. Established a reliable kosher food supply

### Opposition and Miracles

The displaced shochetim mounted fierce opposition:

**Attempt 1 — The Amir**: They appealed to the local Amir (ruler) to have Eliezrov arrested. When the Amir encountered Eliezrov in person, he was so struck by Eliezrov's spiritual bearing that he embraced him instead of arresting him.

**Attempt 2 — Poisoning**: The shochetim sent poisoned food to his home. The household dog ate the food and died — saving Eliezrov's life. These episodes earned him the epithet "**Baal Shem Tov of Bukhara**" among the grateful community.

### Institutional Building

Beyond the shechita reform, Eliezrov founded:
- Yeshivot (advanced Torah study)
- Talmudei Torah (elementary schools)
- Mikvaot (ritual baths)

These institutions created the infrastructure for sustained religious Jewish life in Samarkand — a foundation that would prove critical decades later when thousands of Chabad refugees arrived during World War II.

---

## Legacy

Eliezrov's appointment as Samarkand's first Chabad rabbi established a pattern: the Chabad movement's reach into the most remote corners of the Jewish diaspora, transforming religious practice through personal courage, institutional building, and halachic rigor. His work in 1898–1901 laid the groundwork for Samarkand's role as the major Chabad refuge during World War II.
