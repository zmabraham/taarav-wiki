# Yevsektsiya — Soviet Anti-Religious Campaign Against Jews

**Date**: 1918–1930 (primary period of activity)  
**Location**: Soviet Union

---

## Overview

The **Yevsektsiya** (יעווסעקציע — _Yevreiskaya Sektsiya_, the Jewish Section of the Communist Party) was the Soviet state's specialized instrument for destroying Jewish religious life. Staffed primarily by Jewish Communists who had broken with tradition, the Yevsektsiya targeted the institutions, leadership, and very existence of organized Jewish religious practice — with Chabad as its primary target.

---

## What Was the Yevsektsiya?

The Yevsektsiya was established in 1918 as a section of the Communist Party's agitprop apparatus, specifically designed to:

- Reach and radicalize Jewish workers in their own language (Yiddish)
- Dismantle traditional Jewish communal institutions
- Prosecute religious leaders
- Promote a secular Soviet Jewish identity based on Yiddish culture and class consciousness, not Torah and tradition

It was staffed by Jews who had abandoned religion and often harbored particular hostility toward the traditional world they had left.

---

## Targets of the Yevsektsiya

The Yevsektsiya targeted:

- **Rabbis and religious leaders** — prosecuted, exiled, imprisoned, executed
- **Jewish communal organizations** — shut down
- **Cheder and yeshiva education** — made illegal
- **Synagogues** — closed, converted to warehouses and clubs
- **Shechita (kosher slaughter)** — banned
- **Hebrew language** — suppressed in favor of Yiddish
- **Zionist organizations** — banned as bourgeois nationalism

For Chabad specifically, the Yevsektsiya identified the underground Tomchei Temimim network as a primary enemy — understanding that as long as Chabad's educational network functioned, the movement would survive.

---

## Chabad's Underground Response

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] response to the Yevsektsiya was the organization of a comprehensive underground network:

- **Underground yeshivos**: Small groups studying in private homes, constantly relocating to evade detection. Documented in memoirs like [[summaries/zichronosai|Zichronosai]] and [[summaries/lemaan-yedau|Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu]].
- **Secret maamad funds**: Financial support for underground activities, channeled through coded correspondence
- **Coded communication**: The Rayatz and his network communicated in code, using innocent-seeming business language to transmit information about educational activities
- **Women as activists**: Women operated more freely than men (less likely to be suspected), serving as teachers, messengers, and organizers

---

## The 1927 Climax

The Yevsektsiya's campaign against Chabad climaxed with the **GPU arrest of the Rayatz in June 1927** — an attempt to decapitate the movement by imprisoning or executing its leader. The plan backfired: international pressure secured the Rayatz's release, and his departure from the Soviet Union became a public demonstration that the movement had survived.

See [[events/rayatz-arrest-1927|Rayatz's Arrest and Liberation (1927)]].

---

## End of the Yevsektsiya

The Yevsektsiya was officially disbanded in 1930 — ironically, because Stalin's totalitarianism no longer needed a specialized Jewish anti-religion apparatus. General Soviet repression was sufficient. The Yevsektsiya's Jewish members were themselves subject to later Stalinist purges.

Despite the Yevsektsiya's disbandment, Soviet persecution of Jewish religious life continued for decades — in different institutional forms.
