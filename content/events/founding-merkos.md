# Founding of Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch (1941–1942)

**Date**: 1941–1942 (5702)  
**Location**: Brooklyn, New York  
**Key Figures**: [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]], [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|the Ramash (future seventh Rebbe)]]

---

## Overview

**Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch** (מרכז לענייני חינוך — "Central Organization for Educational Affairs") was founded by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in **1941–1942** as the educational arm of Chabad-Lubavitch. While [[events/founding-kehot|Kehot Publication Society]] focused on publishing books, Merkos focused on **educational programs, schools, and outreach**. Its founding represented the Rayatz's strategic investment in Jewish education as the primary vehicle for Jewish continuity in America and globally.

---

## Background: The Educational Crisis

### American Jewry in the 1940s

When the Rayatz arrived in America in [[events/chabad-america-arrival|1940]], he found:

**Low Jewish Educational Engagement**:

- Most Jewish children received no Jewish education
- Those who did often attended only Sunday school
- After-school Hebrew schools were underfunded and ineffective
- Day schools were rare and experimental

**Assimilation Pressures**:

- American culture strongly encouraged assimilation
- Jewish observance was declining rapidly
- Intermarriage, though not yet at contemporary levels, was beginning

**Holocaust Trauma**:

- News of European destruction was reaching America (1941–1945)
- The Jewish educational infrastructure of Europe was being destroyed
- America would need to become a new center of Jewish learning

### The Rayatz's Vision

The Rayatz understood:

1. **Education is the foundation of Jewish continuity**
2. **Institutional infrastructure** is necessary for large-scale educational work
3. **American Jewish children** needed access to authentic Torah education
4. **Chabad's educational model** — combining deep Torah study with Chassidic spirituality — had something unique to offer

## The Founding: 1941–1942

### Legal and Organizational Structure

The Rayatz established Merkos as:

**Nonprofit Corporation**:

- Formally incorporated under New York law
- Governed by a board of directors
- Able to receive tax-deductible donations
- Separate from but coordinated with other Chabad institutions

**Leadership**:

- The Rayatz served as president and guiding force
- The **Ramash** (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn, the future seventh Rebbe) was deeply involved from the beginning
- Professional directors and educators handled day-to-day operations

**Mission**:

- Establish and support Jewish schools
- Develop educational curricula and materials
- Train teachers and educators
- Promote Jewish education broadly

### Relationship to Agudas Chassidei Chabad

Merkos worked alongside **Agudas Chassidei Chabad** — the umbrella organization of Chabad:

**Agudas Chassidei Chabad**:

- Broader communal organization
- Handled general Chabad affairs
- Legal and property holder for Chabad institutions

**Merkos**:

- Specifically focused on education
- Operated educational programs
- Developed pedagogical materials

The two organizations were distinct but coordinated, with overlapping leadership and shared goals.

## Early Programs and Initiatives

### Torah Umesorah Network

Merkos was a founding participant in **Torah Umesorah** (the National Society for Hebrew Day Schools) — the umbrella organization for Orthodox day schools in America:

**Collaboration**:

- The Rayatz was a founding member
- Merkos worked with other Orthodox groups to establish day schools
- This was unprecedented cooperation among diverse Jewish communities

**Impact**:

- Torah Umesorah helped establish hundreds of day schools across America
- Merkos provided Chabad educational expertise and materials
- The day school movement transformed American Jewish education

### Merkos Schools

Merkos established and supported schools directly:

**Early Schools**:

- **Yeshiva Tomchei Temimim** in Brooklyn — the flagship Chabad yeshiva in America
- **United Lubavitcher Yeshivoth** — network of elementary and high schools
- **Oholei Torah** — founded later (1950s) but built on Merkos foundations

**Curriculum Development**:

- Developed materials integrating Torah study with Chassidus
- Created bilingual (Hebrew-English) educational resources
- Emphasized both intellectual and spiritual development

### Campus Visitation Programs

One of Merkos's most innovative early initiatives was **campus visitation**:

**The Problem**:

- Jewish college students were largely unconnected to Jewish life
- Campuses had few Jewish resources
- Students were vulnerable to assimilation and missionary activity

**The Solution**:

- Merkos sent representatives to college campuses
- They met with Jewish students, organized classes, and created community
- This was a precursor to the modern **Chabad on Campus** movement

**Impact**:

- Thousands of Jewish students were reached
- Many returned to Jewish practice
- The model would be expanded dramatically by the seventh Rebbe

## Summer Programs and Camps

### Gan Yisrael

**Gan Yisrael** — the first Chabad summer camp — was established with Merkos support:

**Founding** (1950s):

- Initially small and experimental
- Grew rapidly under the seventh Rebbe's leadership
- Became a model for Chabad camps worldwide

**Educational Philosophy**:

- Combined recreation with Jewish learning
- Created an immersive Jewish environment
- Reached children who might not otherwise receive Jewish education

**Impact**:

- Thousands of children attended Gan Yisrael camps
- Many returned to Jewish observance because of camp experiences
- The model spread globally

### Other Summer Activities

Merkos organized and supported:

- **Day camps** in cities without overnight camps
- **Traveling educational programs** that reached multiple communities
- **Special programs** for refugees, immigrants, and at-risk youth

---

## The Seventh Rebbe's Role

### The Ramash's Involvement

**Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn** (the Ramash, later the seventh Rebbe) was central to Merkos's founding and early work:

**Hands-On Leadership**:

- Deeply involved in educational programming
- Developed curricula and materials
- Visited schools and evaluated their progress
- Personally guided educators

**Visionary Expansion**:

- Under his guidance (both as Ramash and later as Rebbe), Merkos expanded dramatically
- New programs were constantly developed
- The scope of educational work broadened

**Global Reach**:

- Merkos programs expanded beyond America
- International educational initiatives
- Support for schools in Israel, Europe, and elsewhere

### After 1951: Dramatic Expansion

When the Ramash became the seventh Rebbe in 1951, Merkos entered a new phase:

**Massive Expansion**:

- Hundreds of new schools and programs established
- Global educational initiatives
- Professional staff and infrastructure

**The Shluchim System**:

- Merkos became the institutional home for the shlichus (emissary) system
- Shluchim established schools and educational programs worldwide
- This became the primary vehicle for Chabad's global expansion

## Merkos's Ongoing Work

### Contemporary Programs

Today, Merkos continues its educational mission:

**Schools Worldwide**:

- Supports Chabad schools on every continent
- Provides curricula and materials
- Trains teachers and administrators

**Chabad on Campus**:

- Hundreds of Chabad centers on college campuses
- Educational programs for university students
- Fighting assimilation among young adults

**Online Education**:

- Digital learning platforms
- Remote classes and resources
- Global accessibility

**Teacher Training**:

- Programs to train educators
- Professional development
- Certification programs

---

## Historical Significance

The founding of Merkos in 1941–1942 was transformative:

**Institutional Infrastructure**:

- Gave Chabad a permanent educational institution
- Provided stability and continuity for educational work
- Enabled large-scale, coordinated programming

**Strategic Investment**:

- The Rayatz understood that education is the key to Jewish survival
- Investing in education meant investing in the future
- This investment has paid dividends across generations

**American Jewish Transformation**:

- Merkos contributed to the day school movement
- Helped transform American Jewish education
- Made authentic Torah education widely available

**Global Impact**:

- Merkos programs reach every Jewish community worldwide
- The educational model has been replicated across the Chabad world
- Millions of Jewish children and adults have been touched
