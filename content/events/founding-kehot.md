# Founding of Kehot Publication Society (1942)

**Date**: 1942 (5702)  
**Location**: Brooklyn, New York  
**Key Figures**: [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]], [[entities/people/nissan-mindel|Nissan Mindel]]

---

## Overview

**Kehot Publication Society** (קה"ת — _Kehot_ is an acronym for _Karnos Chabad_ — "Horns of Chabad," from the blessing "May you merit to have the horn of the Messiah be blown in our days") was founded in **1942** by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in Brooklyn, New York. It became the publishing arm of Chabad-Lubavitch, producing thousands of volumes of Chassidic texts in Hebrew, Yiddish, English, and other languages. Kehot's founding represented a strategic investment in the dissemination of Chabad teachings — a recognition that printed books would be the primary vehicle for spreading Chassidus in the modern world.

---

## Background: The Need for a Publishing House

By 1942, the Rayatz had been in America for two years following his [[events/chabad-america-arrival|arrival in 1940]]. He faced several challenges:

**Scarcity of Chabad Texts**:

- Many traditional Chabad books were out of print or unavailable in America
- The Holocaust had destroyed European Jewish publishing infrastructure
- Students and seekers had difficulty accessing foundational texts

**Language Barriers**:

- Most Chabad texts were in Hebrew or Yiddish
- American Jews often spoke only English
- Translation was necessary for outreach

**Institutional Infrastructure**:

- Chabad in America needed institutional foundations
- A publishing house would serve educational and outreach needs

**The Rayatz's Vision**:
The Rayatz understood that the future of Chabad depended on:

1. Making texts accessible
2. Translating into local languages
3. Producing high-quality editions that could reach a global audience

---

## The Founding: 1942

### Organization and Structure

The Rayatz established Kehot as:

**Legal Entity**: A formally incorporated publishing society

- Able to own copyrights
- Could enter contracts and distribute commercially
- Operated as a nonprofit but with business infrastructure

**Editorial Leadership**: Under the direction of **[[entities/people/nissan-mindel|Nissan Mindel]]**

- Mindel served as editor, translator, and writer
- He had a PhD and was fluent in English, Hebrew, and Yiddish
- His role was pivotal in producing English-language materials

**Physical Location**: Initially in Brooklyn, near 770 Eastern Parkway

- Proximity to the Rayatz's headquarters
- Growing as the operation expanded

### Early Publications (1940s)

Kehot's early output focused on foundational texts:

**Tanya**:

- Multiple printings of the foundational Chabad text by the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]
- Hebrew and Yiddish editions
- Eventually English translations

**Maamarim** (Chassidic Discourses):

- Discourses of the Rebbes
- Collected works (Likutei Diburim) of previous generations
- The Rayatz's own discourses

**Classic Texts**:

- Siddurim (prayer books) with Chabad commentary
- Chumashim (Torah editions) with Chassidic insights
- Works of Chabad philosophy

**English Materials**:

- Under Mindel's direction, Kehot began producing English-language books
- This was revolutionary — making Chassidus accessible to English-speaking Jews
- Early English works included introductions to Chabad thought

## Nissan Mindel: The Editor and Translator

**[[entities/people/nissan-mindel|Nissan Mindel]]** (1911–1999) was essential to Kehot's success:

**Background**:

- Born in Riga, Latvia
- Received both traditional Jewish education and secular academic training (PhD)
- Emigrated to America and joined the Rayatz's circle
- Served as the Rayatz's secretary and confidant

**Role at Kehot**:

- Editor-in-chief of most early publications
- Translator of Hebrew and Yiddish texts into English
- Author of original English works on Chabad topics
- Liaison between the Rebbe and the English-speaking world

**Style and Approach**:

- Mindel's translations were accurate but also readable
- He understood how to convey Chassidic concepts in English without losing depth
- His English works introduced Chabad to a generation of American Jews

**Notable Works**:

- _The Philosophy of Chassidus_ — introduction to Chabad thought
- _Letters of the Rayatz_ — translated collections of the Rebbe's correspondence
- _The Early Chabad Movement_ — historical works
- Numerous articles in _Talks and Tales_ (the Chabad magazine)

## Growth and Expansion (1940s–1950s)

### Postwar Expansion

After World War II ended in 1945, Kehot expanded its operations:

**Increased Output**:

- More titles published annually
- Larger print runs
- Expanded distribution networks

**Language Expansion**:

- Hebrew and Yiddish continued as primary languages
- English translations accelerated
- Eventually French, Spanish, Russian, and other languages

**Subject Range**:

- Beyond purely Chassidic texts
- Legal works (Halacha)
- Bible commentaries
- Ethics and character development
- History and biography

### The Seventh Rebbe's Era

When the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] assumed leadership in 1951, he placed enormous emphasis on Kehot's work:

**Vast Expansion of Output**:

- Thousands of volumes published over his tenure
- Systematic publication of all previous Chabad texts
- New editions with extensive commentary and annotation

**Global Distribution**:

- Kehot books distributed worldwide
- Mailed to individuals, libraries, and institutions
- Available at Chabad houses globally

**The Sichos in English Project**:

- Systematic translation of the seventh Rebbe's talks into English
- Making his teachings accessible to a global audience
- Hundreds of volumes published

## Kehot's Impact

### Educational Infrastructure

Kehot became the backbone of Chabad education:

**Yeshiva Curriculum**:

- Tomchei Temimim yeshivos worldwide use Kehot texts
- Standardized curriculum based on Kehot editions
- Continuous supply of new materials

**Outreach Materials**:

- Shluchim (emissaries) use Kehot books for teaching
- Introductory texts for beginners
- Advanced works for serious students

**Reference Library**:

- Kehot has created a comprehensive Chabad library
- Thousands of volumes covering all aspects of Chabad thought
- Ongoing publication of new works

### Cultural Preservation

Kehot played a crucial role in preserving and transmitting Chabad heritage:

**Preservation of Older Texts**:

- Many out-of-print works were republished
- Manuscripts were edited and printed
- Oral traditions were recorded and published

**Standardization of Texts**:

- Kehot editions became the standard reference
- Careful editing ensured accuracy
- Commentaries and aids made texts accessible

**Dissemination**:

- Global distribution of texts
- Libraries worldwide hold Kehot collections
- Digital editions in the modern era

## Kehot Today

**Current Operations**:

- Continues to publish new works annually
- Maintains a vast catalog of titles
- Operates online and physical bookstores
- Has expanded into digital publishing

**Translation Projects**:

- Ongoing translation into multiple languages
- English, Spanish, French, Russian, Persian, Arabic, and more
- Making Chassidus accessible to diverse Jewish communities

**Collaboration with Other Chabad Institutions**:

- Works with [[events/founding-merkos|Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch]] on educational materials
- Collaborates with individual shluchim on specialized publications
- Partners with other Jewish publishers

---

## Historical Significance

The founding of Kehot in 1942 was strategically significant:

**Institutional Foundation**:

- Gave Chabad a permanent institutional presence in America
- Provided infrastructure that supported all other activities
- Established publishing as a core Chabad activity

**Strategic Vision**:

- The Rayatz understood that books would outlive individuals
- Investing in publications meant investing in the future
- The written word became the primary vehicle for spreading Chassidus

**Cultural Bridge**:

- Kehot served as a bridge between Old World Chabad and New World realities
- Translation work made Chassidus accessible to Western Jews
- This enabled Chabad's global expansion
