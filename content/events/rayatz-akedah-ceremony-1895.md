# עקידת יצחק — מינוי היורש

## The Akedah Ceremony: Rashab Designates the Rayatz as Successor (1895)

**Date**: 1895 (approximately 5655)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] — the Rashab's private chambers  
**Participants**: [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] and his son [[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak]] (the Rayatz, age 15)

---

## Overview

In the most formative event of the young [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] early life, the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] called his 15-year-old son into his private chambers and conducted what the Rayatz would later call "the akedah" (the binding of Isaac) — informing him that he would be the next Rebbe and that G-d had demanded the "binding" of the son for the sake of the generation. Both father and son wept during the encounter.

---

## The Biblical Parallel

The term _akedah_ refers to the binding of Isaac by Abraham in Genesis 22 — the supreme test of faith in which a father was asked to sacrifice his son, and then was stopped. The Rashab's choice of this term frames the ceremony as:

- A father called to "offer" his son to a demanding mission
- A son's acceptance of a yoke not entirely of his own choosing
- A divine demand that overrides personal feeling
- A sacrifice that would paradoxically result in blessing

---

## The Content of the Ceremony

According to the _Undaunted_ biography of the Rayatz (the primary English source for this episode), the Rashab:

- Summoned his son to his private chambers
- Told him directly that he would be the next Rebbe
- Explained that the designation was not merely personal succession but a divine demand — G-d had required the "binding" of the son for the generation's sake
- Expressed the emotional weight of this designation through tears

The Rayatz later described this as the moment he understood his life's mission. From this point forward, his role as the Rashab's private secretary and representative took on different meaning — he was not merely assisting his father but being trained for succession.

---

## Aftermath: Immediate Responsibilities

Following this ceremony, the Rayatz's public responsibilities expanded:

- He traveled to **Kovno (1895)** and **Vilna (1896)** to represent the Rashab at conferences
- He managed the Rashab's correspondence with Jewish leaders
- He accompanied the Rashab on missions to St. Petersburg

In **1897**, at his own wedding, the Rayatz made his famous request: "Open a yeshivah that integrates Talmud and Chasidic philosophy" — leading to the founding of [[events/tomchei-temimim-founding|Tomchei Temimim]].

---

## Historical Significance

The Akedah ceremony is one of the few documented instances of a Chabad Rebbe explicitly designating a successor during his own lifetime. The use of the term "akedah" frames Chabad leadership not as privilege but as sacrifice — a dimension of the Rebbe's self-understanding that would shape how the Rayatz approached his later trials (Soviet persecution, exile, wartime leadership).
