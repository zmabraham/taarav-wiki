# The Pleshchenitsy Chabad Community (19th–Early 20th Century)

**The remarkable Chabad community of Pleshchenitsy, a small town in the Minsk gubernia that produced exceptional Chassidim across multiple generations**

## Background

Pleshchenitsy (Pleszczenice; Yiddish: Pleshenits) was a small town in the Minsk gubernia of White Russia (present-day Belarus). Despite its modest size, it developed one of the most distinguished Chabad communities in the region, producing exceptional figures who were recognized by the Rebbes themselves.

## Key Families

### The Kugel Family

The Kugel family was central to Pleshchenitsy's Chabad character:
- **R' Yosef Kugel** — the elder brother, described as even greater in Chassidus than his famous sibling; died c. 1876
- **R' Chanoch (Hendl) Kugel** — the celebrated mashpia who eventually settled in Lubavitch; known personally by the Rayatz
- **R' Gershon Leib Kugel** — son of R' Yosef; died 18 Elul 1877 in Vilna

### The Razenboim Family

**R' Avraham Chaim Razenboim** was the leading Chassid of Pleshchenitsy in the Maharash-Rashab era. Born to a Chabad family in Pleshchenitsy, he was described in glowing terms by all who knew him: "a great scholar in both nigleh [Talmud] and Chassidus, and a great Chassid, punctilious in mitzvos to the utmost, and greatly beloved by all who knew him." He maintained the community through a period of Soviet imprisonment and eventually emigrated.

### R' Reuven Liberman

The shoichet R' Reuven Liberman served as the de facto community leader after R' Avraham Chaim was arrested, maintaining regular Torah study sessions at his home.

## Community Life

The Pleshchenitsy community was characterized by:
- A strong bond of love and brotherhood among its members (described in the sources)
- Regular study of Chassidic texts
- Correspondence with the mashpi'im and Rebbes
- Sending letters and students to Lubavitch

The community gradually declined after the emigration of its key figures in the 1890s. When R' Avraham Chaim left Russia around 1892–93, it was described as "the departure of the glory from our town."

## Significance

The Pleshchenitsy community exemplifies how even tiny Jewish towns in White Russia could develop extraordinary concentrations of Chassidic devotion. The detailed documentation in Toldos Avraham Chaim — including correspondence, descriptions, and reminiscences — provides an unusually rich portrait of daily Chassidic life in a provincial town.
