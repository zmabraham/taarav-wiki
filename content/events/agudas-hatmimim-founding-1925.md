# Founding of Agudas HaTmimim (1925)

**The first organized Chabad institution in America — founded by Tomchei Temimim graduates in New York, October 1925**

## Overview

On Motzaei Shabbat Parshat Shemini 5686 (October 1925), approximately twenty graduates of Tomchei Temimim gathered at the home of R' Eliya Simpson at 116 West Street No. 137 in New York City and formally established Agudas HaTmimim — the Organization of Temimim. This was the first organized Chabad body in America, predating the Rayatz's arrival by fifteen years.

## Background

By the mid-1920s, a small number of Tomchei Temimim graduates had made their way to the United States — some before WWI, others in the aftermath of the Russian Revolution and Civil War. These Temimim found themselves in a country with almost no Chabad infrastructure, no regular farbrengens, no study frameworks aligned with their Lubavitch training, and no institutional connection to the Rebbe.

R' Yisrael Jacobson, who had arrived in America in Tevet 5686 (December 1925), was among those who worked to bring these scattered Temimim together. Upon arriving, he found a small but dedicated nucleus of Lubavitch-trained Jews: the Simpson brothers (Eliya and Gershon) in New York, R' Avraham Eliya Axelrod in Baltimore, and others. Together they organized the founding gathering.

## The Founding Meeting

The founding took place at **R' Eliya Simpson's home, 116 West Street No. 137, New York City**, on **Motzaei Shabbat Parshat Shemini 5686** (October 1925). Approximately twenty Temimim attended.

Officers elected at the founding meeting:
- **President**: R' Eliya (Eliyahu Yeichil) Simpson
- **Treasurer**: R' Gershon Simpson (R' Eliya's brother)
- **Secretary**: R' Yisrael Jacobson

The initial meetings of the organization continued to be held at R' Eliya Simpson's home. R' Yisrael Jacobson described the Simpson brothers as "pillars of our organization" — meaning the founding core upon whom everything else rested.

## Members and Character

Among the founding members documented in R' Yisrael Jacobson's memoir:

- **R' Eliya Simpson** — long-term Lubavitch Temim; rabbi in Harlem; provided his home as the meeting place and served as president
- **R' Gershon Simpson** — R' Eliya's brother; long-term Lubavitch Temim; treasurer
- **R' Yisrael Jacobson** — newly arrived from Russia; secretary; principal chronicler of the organization
- **R' Avraham Eliya Axelrod** — from Kobylnik (Narach, Belarus); had studied at TT for fourteen years; rabbi at Tzemach Tzedek shul in Baltimore; described as among "the first members"
- **R' Gershon Henoch Eichorn** — Temim from Shchedrin; had been working in a laundry for $30/week when Jacobson arrived; one of the first Temimim Jacobson encountered in America

The Temimim who came to America carried with them a complex consciousness about having emigrated — Chabad Rebbes had not generally encouraged travel to America, and some described themselves as having "run away to America" (_ontlofn in Amerika_).

## Goals and Activities

Agudas HaTmimim pursued several organizational aims in its early years:

1. **Fixed times for Torah study and Chassidic learning** (_limud haChassidus_) — maintaining the intellectual and spiritual framework of Tomchei Temimim in the American context
2. **Maamad** — organizing financial support (_maamad_) for Chabad institutions, particularly the central yeshiva network still operating under difficult conditions in the Soviet Union and elsewhere
3. **Cheder** — establishing a Chabad-oriented elementary school for the children of Temimim, to continue the chain of Chabad education in the New World
4. **Discussion of difficulties** — providing a forum for Temimim to address the challenges of adapting Lubavitch religious life to the American environment
5. **Rescue efforts** — coordinating attempts to bring Temimim out of Soviet Russia to America, through visa petitions, financial guarantees, and lobbying

## The Likkutei Torah Printing

One of the earliest significant achievements of Agudas HaTmimim under this leadership was the printing of a new edition of _Likkutei Torah_ (the Alter Rebbe's Torah commentary) in Warsaw. This project was initiated by Jacobson and attributed to the efforts of the organization. Five hundred copies were sent to America and sold for $5 for both volumes — making a foundational Chabad text available to the American Jewish community.

## Connection to the Rayatz

When the Rayatz made his first extended visit to America in 5689–5690 (1929–1930), he encountered the organized community that Agudas HaTmimim had helped to build. Approximately thirty Temimim came to the Rayatz for yechidus (private audience) before Rosh Hashana during this visit — including R' Eliya Simpson, R' Gershon Simpson, R' Avraham Eliya Axelrod, and others. The Rayatz was thus able to see the fruits of the organization's work.

## Significance

The founding of Agudas HaTmimim in 1925 represents the first chapter in the story of organized Chabad in America. It preceded the Rayatz's move to the United States by fifteen years and the Seventh Rebbe's assumption of leadership by twenty-six years. The organization's goals — Torah study, institutional support, rescue of Temimim from the Soviet Union, and education of the next generation — prefigured the full structure that Chabad-Lubavitch would eventually build across America. The three founding officers (Eliya Simpson, Gershon Simpson, and Yisrael Jacobson) represent the bridge generation between the Old World Tomchei Temimim and the New World Chabad movement.
