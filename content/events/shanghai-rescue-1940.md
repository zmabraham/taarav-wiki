# Shanghai Rescue (1940)

During WWII, a group of Lubavitcher chassidim escaped from Poland and Lithuania through the Far East, aided by transit visas issued by Chiune Sugihara (Japanese consul in Kovno) and Jan Zwartendijk (Dutch consul). They spent the war years in Kobe and Shanghai, Japan.

## Key Facts

- **Period**: 1940–1945
- **Route**: [[entities/places/kovno|Kovno]] → Vladivostok → [[entities/places/kobe|Kobe]] → [[entities/places/shanghai|Shanghai]]
- **Key Figures**: [[entities/people/chiune-sugihara|Chiune Sugihara]], [[entities/people/jan-zwartendijk|Jan Zwartendijk]], [[entities/people/meir-ashkenazi|R' Meir Ashkenazi]]

## Significance

The Shanghai group preserved a complete Chabad community during the Holocaust, maintaining a minyan, mikveh, and Torah study. Their survival demonstrated the role of individual moral courage in saving Jewish lives.
