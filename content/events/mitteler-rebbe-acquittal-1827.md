# Acquittal of the Mitteler Rebbe (1827–1828)

**Also Known As**: הזיכוי המלא (Complete Exoneration); Imperial Senate Decision  
**Dates**: 29 November 1826 – 1828 (final closure)  
**Location**: St. Petersburg, Imperial Senate

---

## Overview

Between 1827 and 1828, the Russian Imperial Senate issued a series of decisions that completely exonerated [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|the Mitteler Rebbe]] of all charges brought by [[entities/people/simcha-kissin|Simcha Kissin]] and prosecuted by [[entities/people/governor-khavanski|Governor Khavanski]]. The Senate's rulings:

1. **Rejected Khavanski's appeal** for new restrictive legislation
2. **Upheld the lower courts' acquittal** of all charges
3. **Found "no legal basis"** for Kissin's denunciations
4. **Closed the case permanently** without any punishment or restrictions

This victory at the highest level of the Czarist judicial system affirmed the right of Chassidic communities to support their rebbes voluntarily and maintain independent spiritual leadership outside the state-appointed rabbinate.

---

## The Multi-Level Judicial Process

The Mitteler Rebbe's case passed through four levels of the Russian imperial court system:

### Level 1: Babinovich District Court (June–July 1826)
**Jurisdiction**: Court of Peace (משפט שלום) in Babinovich district, Mogilev gubernia

**Investigation completed**: May 1826

**Decision (20 June 1826)**:
- Reviewed testimony from communities in [[entities/places/kapust|Kapust]], [[entities/places/orsha|Orsha]], and [[entities/places/shklov|Shklov]]
- Found all donations to the Rebbe were voluntary
- No evidence of coercion or oppression
- Dismissed all charges

**Problem**: The court had mishandled the case earlier, causing delays and prompting transfer to the higher court.

### Level 2: Mohilev Gubernia Supreme Court (3 August – 29 November 1826)
**Jurisdiction**: Highest court in the Mogilev province

**Review period**: August–November 1826

**Decision (29 November 1826)**:
- Fully upheld the District Court's acquittal
- Issued detailed legal reasoning:
  - The Rebbe was not a government-appointed rabbi under Section 50
  - He served only those who voluntarily sought his guidance
  - Voluntary charitable giving could not be prohibited
  - Private property rights protected the right to donate
  - Arbitration (borrut) by mutual consent did not violate civil jurisdiction

**Key legal principle**: "A person cannot be prohibited from giving voluntary charity that is not contrary to law, nor can he be prevented from doing as he wishes with his private property."

### Level 3: Governor Khavanski's Appeal to the Senate (15 May 1827)
**Appellant**: General-Governor [[entities/people/governor-khavanski|Prince Dmitri Khavanski]] of Vitebsk

**Appeal filed**: 15 May 1827, document no. 1837

**Khavanski's arguments**:
1. The Rebbe violated Section 52 by collecting 10,000–14,000 rubles annually
2. The 1813 distribution of 35,000 rubles proved illegal enrichment
3. Voluntary donations were a ruse — the real problem was compulsory collections
4. The Rebbe should be disqualified from rabbinic service for not knowing Russian (Section 10)
5. New imperial legislation should explicitly prohibit rabbinic financial collections and civil arbitration

**Khavanski's proposed remedies**:
- Remove the Rebbe from rabbinic leadership until he learned Russian
- Forbid rabbis from conducting any arbitration whatsoever
- Require governors to approve rabbinic salary contracts
- Empower governors to strictly supervise rabbinic activities

### Level 4: Imperial Senate Decision (1827–1828)
**Jurisdiction**: Highest court in the Russian Empire

**Decision issued**: 28 November 1827 (21 Kislev 5588)

**The Senate's response** (communicated through the Minister of Interior):

#### On the allegations:
- **"No legal basis for the denunciation made by the Jew Kissin was found"**
- All charges were dismissed

#### on Khavanski's proposed legislation:
- **Rejected**: No basis for new restrictive laws on rabbis
- The existing [[entities/concepts/jewish-statute-1804|Jewish Statute of 1804]] was sufficient
- Governors should enforce existing laws strictly but not expand them

#### On arbitration (Section 51):
- **Rejected** Khavanski's proposal to prohibit all rabbinic civil arbitration
- Rabbis could serve as voluntary arbitrators when both parties consented
- This did not constitute exercising state judicial authority

#### On financial collections (Section 52):
- **Rejected** Khavanski's claim that voluntary donations violated the law
- Voluntary charitable giving was protected by property rights
- No prohibition on accepting voluntary contributions

#### On rabbinic qualifications (Section 10):
- **No action taken** to disqualify the Rebbe
- The Senate did not address Khavanski's argument about the language requirement

---

## The Minister of Interior's Position

The Senate's decision referenced the Minister of Interior's response to Khavanski's proposals:

- He saw **"no basis for such legislation"** (new rabbinic restrictions)
- He noted that such prohibitions did not appear in the 1835 Jewish Statute reform
- However, he instructed governors to **"supervise this matter"** — ensuring strict enforcement of existing laws

This compromise position allowed the Rebbe's complete acquittal while maintaining state oversight authority.

## Historical Significance

### Legal Victory
The Senate's decision established important precedents:

1. **Voluntary religious support** was protected under Czarist law
2. **Chassidic rebbes** (not government-appointed) had the right to serve their communities
3. **Rabbinic arbitration** by mutual consent was permissible
4. **Internal Jewish disputes** could not automatically trigger state intervention

### Political Victory
The defeat of Khavanski's appeal was significant:

1. **No new restrictions** on rabbinic authority were enacted
2. **Governors were instructed** to enforce existing laws, not expand them
3. **The informant's accusations** were formally rejected as baseless
4. **Chabad's institutional model** survived its first major legal challenge

### Personal Victory
For the Mitteler Rebbe:

1. **Complete exoneration** at the highest imperial level
2. **No restrictions** on his continued leadership
3. **Vindication** of his lifetime of work spreading Chassidus
4. **Freedom to continue** without fear of renewed prosecution

## The Final Documentation

The Senate's decision concluded a paper trail that included:

- **Primary investigation records** (Governor Khavanski's files)
- **Witness testimonies** from Kapust, Orsha, Shklov
- **District Court decision** (July 1826)
- **Supreme Court decision** (November 1826)
- **Khavanski's appeal** (May 1827)
- **Senate ruling** (November 1827)

The Masar book notes that many intermediate documents (the actual trial testimony transcripts, Babinovich court records, Mohilev Supreme Court proceedings) were not found in Khavanski's archive and presumably remain in other repositories.
