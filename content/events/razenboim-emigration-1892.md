# R' Avraham Chaim Razenboim's Emigration from Russia (1892–1893)

**The dramatic escape of the leading Chabad Chassid of Pleshchenitsy from Russia, following his imprisonment and facing further legal proceedings**

## Background

R' Avraham Chaim Razenboim, the leading Chassid of Pleshchenitsy, had been imprisoned 1888–1890 for signing documents that helped young Jews avoid military conscription. After his release, he returned to Pleshchenitsy. However, new legal proceedings were initiated against him.

## What Happened

One Simchat Torah evening (likely 1892 or autumn 5653/1892–93), R' Avraham Chaim received a private warning from a sympathetic local official that he would be arrested again the next day. Despite the occasion of the holiday, he organized a large farbrengen (Chassidic gathering) for all the Chassidim of the town — celebrated "with great joy all day." When night fell, he quietly disappeared.

His enemies — described as mitnagdim (opponents of Chassidism) — heard that night that he had fled and pursued him, but God protected him and they did not catch him.

He first spent Shabbat in Warsaw (where his father's family lived), then traveled to Frankfurt, Germany, where he opened a dairy products shop with one of his sons. After approximately a year, German police ordered him to leave Germany as he lacked residence rights. He then moved to Amsterdam, where he entered a business partnership with R' Abba Persson (a Chassidic merchant from Königsberg).

Eventually, despite his reluctance, he emigrated to America. He had consulted with R' Chaim Yaakov Widrevitz (a Chabad rabbi who had emigrated to New York) about whether to come to America. R' Widrevitz's response was measured, warning that America was good for those willing to work physically but difficult for "soft people." Nonetheless, R' Avraham Chaim eventually emigrated.

## Significance

This episode illustrates several important historical phenomena:

1. **Internal opposition to Chabad** — The "enemies" who pursued R' Avraham Chaim are identified as mitnagdim, showing that the conflict between Chassidim and their opponents persisted into the late 19th century and could have practical dangerous consequences
2. **The Czarist legal system as a weapon** — Even after serving his sentence, R' Avraham Chaim faced continued harassment through the legal system, used by his opponents
3. **The Chabad emigration to the West** — R' Avraham Chaim's path through Western Europe to America mirrors the broader Jewish emigration from Eastern Europe in this period
4. **Holiday as cover** — The use of Simchat Torah celebrations as cover for his flight — organizing a joyful farbrengen while quietly preparing to flee — reflects the characteristic Chabad response of maintaining outward joy amid internal crisis
