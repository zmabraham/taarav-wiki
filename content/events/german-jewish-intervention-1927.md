# German Jewish Intervention to Free the Rayatz (1927)

**Date**: Summer–Autumn 5687 (1927)  
**Location**: Berlin, Germany; Moscow, Soviet Union  
**Key Figures**: [[entities/people/meir-hildesheimer|Rabbi Dr. Meir Hildesheimer]], [[entities/people/leo-baeck|Dr. Leo Baeck]], [[entities/people/oskar-cohn|Dr. Oskar Cohn]], [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]

---

## Overview

When [[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz)]] was arrested by the Soviet GPU in June 1927 and sentenced to death, the international Jewish world mobilized to save him. Among the most effective interventions was that of Berlin's German Jewish community — notable for two reasons: (1) the unprecedented cross-denominational cooperation between Orthodox and Liberal leaders, and (2) the exploitation of Germany's unique diplomatic relationship with the Soviet Union.

---

## The Joint Orthodox-Liberal Petition

In an act of extraordinary Jewish solidarity, two religious leaders from opposite ends of the Berlin Jewish spectrum joined forces:

- **Rabbi Dr. Meir Hildesheimer** — son of Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer; head of the Orthodox Berlin Rabbinical Seminary (Hochschule für die Wissenschaft des Judentums), the leading institution of Neo-Orthodox rabbinical training in Germany
- **Dr. Leo Baeck** — the foremost leader of Liberal (Reform) Judaism in Berlin; professor at the Hochschule für die Wissenschaft des Judentums; later the recognized representative of all German Jewry during the Nazi period

These two men — representatives of religious movements in active doctrinal conflict — jointly petitioned Dr. **Oskar Cohn** to use his political connections on the Rayatz's behalf.

The collaboration was driven by a fundamental principle: however sharp the differences between Orthodox and Liberal Judaism, the life of a Jewish leader demanded a united response.

---

## Dr. Oskar Cohn's Political Network

Dr. Oskar Cohn was a Jewish socialist politician who served in the German Reichstag/Bundestag. His value to the liberation campaign stemmed from two connections:

### The German Government Channel

Cohn met with:
- **Foreign Minister Gustav Stresemann** (the Nobel Peace Prize laureate and architect of German-Soviet diplomatic normalization)
- **Vice-Reichskanzler Weissmann**

Through these meetings, Cohn conveyed the urgency of the Rayatz's situation. Weissmann, in turn, pressed **Soviet Ambassador Krestinsky** in Berlin, who promised to act and relay the request to Moscow.

### The Chicherin Connection

More decisive still: Cohn had a **personal friendship** with **Georgy Chicherin**, the Soviet Commissar for Foreign Affairs. This was not a formal diplomatic channel but a personal relationship — far more effective for a delicate request of this nature. Cohn contacted Chicherin directly, and Chicherin's personal intervention at the Soviet Foreign Ministry level contributed to the commutation of the Rayatz's sentence from death to exile, and ultimately to full release.

---

## The Frankfurt Orthodox Stratagem

In parallel with Cohn's political interventions, the **Frankfurt Orthodox community** (centered around the network of [[entities/people/shlomo-zalman-breuer|Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Breuer]] and the Adath Yeshurun) sent the Rayatz a formal **rabbinical appointment letter** — creating a legal fiction that the Rayatz was wanted as a rabbi in Frankfurt, Germany.

This document gave the Soviet authorities a face-saving rationale: they could release the Rayatz as a "foreign rabbi being recalled to his post" rather than appearing to yield to Jewish pressure or acknowledge the illegitimacy of the original arrest.

---

## The Latvian Channel

Separate from, but parallel to, the German intervention was the Latvian diplomatic campaign led by [[entities/people/mordechai-dubin|Mordechai Dubin]] (a Latvian parliamentary member and devoted Chabad chassid), who leveraged Latvia's trade treaty relationship with the Soviet Union to pressure for the Rayatz's release.

---

## The Rayatz's Liberation and Gratitude

The Rayatz was freed on **12–13 Tammuz 5687 (July 12–13, 1927)** and immediately expelled from the Soviet Union to Riga, Latvia. His first letter from Riga (Tishrei 5688 / autumn 1927) specifically thanked [[entities/people/meir-hildesheimer|Rabbi Dr. Meir Hildesheimer]] for his role.

When the Rayatz came to Berlin in Kislev 5688 (December 1927) for meetings with JDC officials, he paid a personal call on **Vice-Reichskanzler Weissmann** — thanking him in person for the role the German government played in his liberation.

---

## Historical Significance

This episode illustrates:

1. **Cross-denominational Jewish solidarity**: Orthodox and Liberal leaders acting together when a Jewish life was at stake
2. **Germany as intermediary**: The unique German-Soviet diplomatic relationship (the Rapallo Treaty axis) made Germany an effective channel for Jewish intercession with Moscow in the 1920s
3. **Jewish political networks**: The interconnection between Jewish communal needs and German parliamentary politics in the Weimar Republic
4. **The range of the liberation coalition**: Latvian diplomats, German politicians, Frankfurt rabbis, and the Socialist-Chicherin personal friendship all contributed to a single outcome
