## Toras Emes Yeshiva in Jerusalem (1922)

**Date**: 1922  
**Location**: [[entities/places/jerusalem|Jerusalem]]  
**Founder (re-established)**: [[entities/people/zalman-havlin|Rabbi Zalman Havlin]]  
**Background**: Continuation of [[events/toras-emes-hebron-1911|Toras Emes Hebron (1911)]]

---

## Overview

In **1922**, [[entities/people/zalman-havlin|Rabbi Zalman Havlin]] reconstituted the **Yeshiva Toras Emes** in Jerusalem, continuing the institution the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] had established in Hebron in 1911. The Jerusalem yeshiva became the primary Chabad learning institution in Eretz Yisrael for decades.

---

## Background: From Hebron to Jerusalem

In 1911, the Rashab had purchased **Beit Romano** in Hebron and sent Rabbi Zalman Havlin to establish a branch of **Tomchei Temimim** there. Named **Toras Emes**, it was the first Chassidic yeshiva in Eretz Yisrael. WWI forced the Russian students to return to Russia.

After the Rashab's death in Rostov in 1920, Rabbi Havlin faced the task of reconstituting the yeshiva under the new conditions of British Mandatory Palestine. Rather than returning to Hebron — which would suffer the devastating [[events/hebron-riots-1929|1929 riots]] a few years later — Havlin chose **Jerusalem**, now the administrative capital of the Mandate and a more central location for Jewish institutional life.

---

## The Jerusalem Yeshiva

The **1922 establishment** of Toras Emes in Jerusalem marked:

- The first permanent Chabad yeshiva in the capital of Eretz Yisrael
- A transition from the pre-war Hebron model to a more urban institutional presence
- The continuation of the Rashab's vision for Chabad education in the Holy Land under his successor, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]

The yeshiva operated in Jerusalem as a center of Chabad learning and was later joined by the [[events/kfar-chabad-founding|Kfar Chabad settlement (1949)]] as a major institutional pillar of Israeli Chabad.
