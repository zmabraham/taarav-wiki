# 770 Eastern Parkway — Chabad World Headquarters

**Date**: Purchased 12 Av 5700 (August 16, 1940)  
**Location**: 770 Eastern Parkway, Crown Heights, Brooklyn, New York

---

## Overview

The three-story brownstone at **770 Eastern Parkway** in Crown Heights, Brooklyn — purchased in 1940 by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] — became the iconic world headquarters of the Chabad movement and one of the most recognized addresses in the Jewish world. Known simply as "770" (or "7-7-0" in Chabad parlance), it has been replicated in more than 30 locations worldwide and remains the central synagogue, study hall, and spiritual center of Chabad Chassidism.

---

## The Purchase

After [[events/america-arrival-1940|arriving in America in March 1940]], the Rayatz needed a permanent residence and organizational headquarters. His assistants searched for a suitable suburban home — the conventional choice for a distinguished rabbi.

The Rayatz insisted differently: he wanted a visible, prestigious location where Jewish life would be publicly present and the Chabad court could operate with dignity. Crown Heights — then an upscale Brooklyn neighborhood with a growing Jewish population — met this requirement.

The three-story brownstone at **770 Eastern Parkway** was purchased on **12 Av 5700 (August 16, 1940)** for $25,000.

---

## What 770 Became

From the moment of purchase, 770 was more than a residence:

- **Synagogue**: The main floor housed the Rayatz's synagogue, where thousands would pray
- **Study hall**: The building quickly became crowded with students and Chassidim studying
- **Administrative center**: All Chabad institutional correspondence, publications, and organizational work flowed through 770
- **Audience room**: The Rayatz received visitors for private audiences (_yechidus_) — sometimes hundreds per day
- **The symbolic center**: The Chabad world centered on the Rayatz; the Rayatz was at 770; therefore 770 was the center

When the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] assumed leadership in 1951, 770 became even more central — the site of his famous farbrengens (which were broadcast globally in later years), his office, and his ceaseless activity.

---

## The Farbrengen of 10 Shevat 5711

The most famous event in 770's history: on **10 Shevat 5711 (1951)**, [[entities/people/sender-nemtzov|Rabbi Sender Nemtzov]] called out from the floor of 770 for the Ramash to say a maamar — and the seventh Rebbe's leadership of Chabad began. The proclamation "Chassidim, we have a Rebbe!" echoed through the halls of 770 and around the Chabad world.

See [[events/seventh-rebbe-acceptance-1951|Seventh Rebbe's Acceptance (1951)]].

---

## Global Replicas

So central did 770 become to Chabad identity that communities worldwide built replicas:

- Israel (multiple locations)
- Brazil, Australia, France, and others
- More than 30 "770-style" buildings exist globally

The architectural form — a specific style of brownstone with the recognizable facade — became instantly recognizable as "Chabad."

---

## Today

770 Eastern Parkway remains the central Chabad synagogue and a major pilgrimage destination. Tens of thousands visit annually. The adjacent building (purchased later) houses the Chabad-Lubavitch headquarters and Kehot Publication Society.

The Rebbe's office at 770 has been preserved essentially unchanged since his death in 1994.
