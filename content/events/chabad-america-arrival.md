# Chabad Comes to America (1940)

**Date**: March 19, 1940 (9 Adar II 5700)  
**Location**: New York City  
**Key Figures**: [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]

---

## Overview

The arrival of the [[entities/people/rayatz|sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe]] (Rayatz) in New York on March 19, 1940, marked the beginning of a new era in Chabad history — and in American Jewish life. Escaping Nazi-occupied Europe after a harrowing journey, the Rayatz immediately set about transforming the spiritual landscape of American Jewry. Within two years, he had established the institutional infrastructure that would make America the global center of the Chabad movement.

---

## Background: Escape from Europe

The Rayatz had been based in [[events/warsaw-years-1933-1939|Warsaw]] since 1933, directing Chabad operations across Poland and the wider Jewish world. When Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, the Rayatz was trapped in Warsaw as the city was bombed and besieged.

His [[events/escape-from-warsaw-1939|escape from Warsaw]] was engineered through extraordinary diplomatic efforts:

- **Chabad supporters in America** mobilized political contacts
- The **U.S. State Department** intervened through diplomatic channels
- **German military intelligence (Abwehr)** officers were involved in facilitating his departure — motivated variously by humanitarian concern, diplomatic considerations, and the intervention of specific individuals
- The Rayatz traveled from Warsaw → Berlin → Riga → Stockholm → via the Atlantic

---

## Arrival: Pier 97, Manhattan

On **March 19, 1940**, the Rayatz arrived at **Pier 97** on Manhattan's West Side aboard the **SS Drottningholm**, a Swedish passenger liner. He was greeted by a delegation of American Jewish leaders and Chabad chassidim.

The moment was laden with historical weight. For centuries, the centers of Torah and Chassidic life had been in Eastern Europe — in the shtetlach and cities of Poland, Lithuania, Russia, and Ukraine. The Rayatz's arrival symbolized the transfer of that spiritual center to America.

---

## "America Iz Nisht Anderesh"

The Rayatz's most famous declaration upon arriving in America was: **"America iz nisht anderesh"** — "America is no different." This Yiddish statement was a direct challenge to the prevailing assumption in the Orthodox world that America was a spiritual wasteland where Torah observance was impossible.

The conventional wisdom among European rabbis and chassidim was that America's culture of materialism, assimilation, and freedom made it inhospitable to authentic Jewish life. The Rayatz rejected this assessment completely. His message: the same Torah, the same mitzvos, the same standards of observance that applied in Lubavitch, Warsaw, and Riga applied equally in New York.

This declaration was programmatic — it set the agenda for everything that followed.

---

## Immediate Actions

The Rayatz moved with remarkable speed. Despite his frail health (he suffered from partial paralysis and other conditions), he began building institutions within his first week in America.

### Insistence on Opening Tomchei Temimim

Within days of his arrival, the Rayatz insisted on establishing an American branch of **Tomchei Temimim** yeshiva. Despite advisors who counseled patience — arguing that there were not enough students, not enough funding, not enough infrastructure — the Rayatz pressed ahead. The yeshiva was established in the summer of 1940 at 770 Eastern Parkway.

---

## 770 Eastern Parkway

On **August 16, 1940**, the Chabad movement purchased the building at **770 Eastern Parkway** in Crown Heights, Brooklyn. The story of the acquisition:

- Several properties were considered in various neighborhoods
- The Rayatz insisted on **Crown Heights** specifically, rejecting other options
- The three-story brick building at 770 — originally a medical office and residence — was purchased to serve as the Rebbe's residence and Chabad headquarters
- The address **770** became synonymous with Chabad-Lubavitch worldwide

## The Three Organizations (1942)

In 1942, the Rayatz established three central organizations that became the institutional backbone of Chabad worldwide:

### Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch (Central Organization for Jewish Education)

Founded as the educational arm of Chabad, Merkos (literally "Center for Educational Matters") was charged with:

- Establishing and overseeing Chabad schools across America
- Publishing educational materials
- Training teachers
- Running summer camps and youth programs
- Sending "roving rabbis" (_Merkos Shlichus_) to remote Jewish communities

The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|future seventh Rebbe]] was appointed as head of Merkos from its founding.

### Kehot Publication Society

**Kehot** (named after an acronym: _Karnei Hod Torah_ — "Rays of the Splendor of Torah") was established as Chabad's publishing house. Its mission:

- Publish the classic Chabad texts (Tanya, Torah Or, Likkutei Torah) in accessible editions
- Translate Chassidic works into English and other languages
- Produce educational and outreach materials
- Preserve and disseminate the Rebbe's teachings

Kehot became one of the most prolific Jewish publishing houses in history, eventually producing thousands of titles in dozens of languages.

### Machne Israel (Camp of Israel)

**Machne Israel** was established as the social services and outreach arm of Chabad:

- Community services for Jewish families
- Assistance to Jewish soldiers during World War II
- Holiday programs and community events
- Later: the administrative umbrella for the shlichus network

---

## The Challenge to American Jewry

The Rayatz's message to American Jews was direct and uncompromising:

1. **Stop assimilating**: American Jews were hemorrhaging religious identity at an alarming rate. The Rayatz demanded a reversal.
2. **Build institutions**: Day schools, yeshivos, mikvaos, kosher infrastructure — the full apparatus of Jewish communal life needed to be created in America.
3. **Become leaders, not followers**: American Jews should not merely maintain a minimal Jewish identity but should become active forces for Torah and mitzvos.
4. **Don't wait for Europe**: The writing was on the wall regarding European Jewry. America needed to become self-sufficient as a Jewish center immediately.

This was a radical message in 1940. Most American Orthodox Jews were struggling to maintain basic observance in the face of economic pressure, cultural assimilation, and a communal infrastructure that was still rudimentary.

---

## The Rayatz's Final Decade (1940–1950)

The Rayatz spent the last decade of his life in America, directing the movement from 770 Eastern Parkway. Despite declining health:

- He maintained a rigorous schedule of receiving visitors, delivering Chassidic discourses, and directing institutional operations
- He directed the [[events/great-escape|Great Escape]] of chassidim from the Soviet Union (1946–1947)
- He established [[events/kfar-chabad-founding|Kfar Chabad]] in Israel (1949)
- He composed **HaYom Yom** (1943), a daily calendar of Chassidic teachings and customs compiled by his son-in-law (the future seventh Rebbe)
- He published a stream of Chassidic discourses, letters, and directives

He passed away on **10 Shevat 5710 (January 28, 1950)**. His son-in-law, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, assumed leadership as the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] exactly one year later.

---

## Legacy

The Rayatz's decade in America — 1940 to 1950 — transformed Chabad from a persecuted Eastern European movement into an American institution with global reach. The infrastructure he built — 770, Merkos, Kehot, Machne Israel, Tomchei Temimim, the shlichus concept — became the foundation upon which the seventh Rebbe built the global Chabad empire.

His declaration "America iz nisht anderesh" proved prophetic. America did not merely sustain Jewish life; it became the world center of Torah study, Jewish publishing, and religious outreach — largely through the institutions the Rayatz founded and the seventh Rebbe expanded.
