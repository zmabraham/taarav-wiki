# Rayatz's Arrest and Liberation (1927) — Yud-Beis Tammuz

**Date**: Arrested: 15 Sivan 5687 (June 1927); Liberated: 12-13 Tammuz 5687 (July 1927)  
**Location**: Leningrad, Soviet Russia → Kostroma (exile) → Liberation

---

## Overview

The arrest of [[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn]] (the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe) by the Soviet GPU in June 1927 — and his extraordinary liberation following international pressure — is one of the most dramatic events in modern Jewish history. His liberation date, **12-13 Tammuz 5687**, became the Chabad holiday of _Yud-Beis Tammuz_ — "the Festival of Liberation" — celebrated annually alongside 19 Kislev and 9-10 Kislev as the major Chabad liberation holidays.

---

## Background: The Underground vs. the GPU

By 1927, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] seven years of underground resistance to Soviet religious persecution had made him the primary target of the [[events/yevsektsiya-campaign|Yevsektsiya]] and the GPU (State Political Directorate — the Soviet secret police). He had:

- Organized underground yeshivos throughout Soviet territory
- Maintained the secret maamad fund for underground activities
- Communicated in code with underground activists
- Personally traveled to communities despite surveillance
- Refused to close religious institutions on Soviet demand

The GPU had been building a case against him for years.

---

## The Arrest

In **15 Sivan 5687 (June 1927)**, GPU agents entered the Rayatz's residence in **Leningrad** at midnight and arrested him.

The Rayatz was taken for interrogation. During questioning — in the presence of GPU officers including the interrogating officer Lulav (sources preserve this name) — the Rayatz made one of the most famous statements in Chabad history:

_"I recognize only one authority — the Almighty. I am not afraid of your guns."_

The interrogators reportedly showed him a loaded gun on the table, as was customary to intimidate prisoners. The Rayatz's response — drawing on the same tradition of fearlessness his own father had cultivated, and that the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] had modeled in 1798 — was unwavering.

---

## The Sentence

The Rayatz was sentenced:

1. **Initial sentence**: Death
2. **Commuted to**: 10 years in the **Solovki** labor camp (the infamous Arctic island camp, effectively a death sentence for a man of his health)
3. **Commuted again**: Three years of internal exile in **Kostroma** (a city northeast of Moscow)

---

## International Pressure

The commutations reflected intense international pressure:

- **Jewish organizations** worldwide petitioned Soviet authorities
- **Latvian government**: [[entities/people/mordechai-dubin|Rabbi Mordechai Dubin]] (Latvian MP) was a crucial diplomatic channel
- **European governments** raised the case
- **World Jewish Congress and other bodies** mobilized

The Soviets — aware of international opinion despite their domestic authoritarianism — found it easier to allow the Rayatz's departure than to create a diplomatic incident over his martyrdom.

---

## Liberation: 12-13 Tammuz 5687

On **12-13 Tammuz 5687 (July 1927)**, the Rayatz was fully released and permitted to leave Soviet Russia. He traveled to Riga, Latvia.

Thousands of Chassidim gathered at the Leningrad train station to see him off. He described the 18-hour overnight journey as "eighteen hours with anguish in the heart, pain in the soul" — knowing he was leaving everything behind.

---

## Yud-Beis Tammuz

**12 Tammuz** became the Chabad holiday of liberation — celebrated with:

- Farbrengens (Chassidic gatherings)
- Study of the Rayatz's writings
- Reflection on the themes of mesirus nefesh (self-sacrifice) and divine protection
- Joy and thanksgiving

It is the third great liberation holiday in Chabad, alongside 19 Kislev (Alter Rebbe, 1798) and 9-10 Kislev (Mitteler Rebbe, 1826).
