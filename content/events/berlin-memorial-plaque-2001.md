# שלט הזיכרון לרבי בברלין

## Berlin Memorial Plaque for the Seventh Rebbe (2001)

**Date**: 3 Kislev 5762 (November 2001)  
**Location**: 7 Hanse-Ufer Street, Berlin, Germany  
**Key figure**: Mayor [[entities/people/joachim-zeller|Joachim Zeller]] of Berlin  
**Honoree**: [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson]] (the seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe)

---

## Overview

On **3 Kislev 5762 (November 2001)**, the Berlin city government installed a memorial plaque at **7 Hanse-Ufer Street** — the address where [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson]] (the future seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe) had lived while studying in Berlin from **1928 to 1931**. This was the first time Berlin officially honored a Jewish personality with such a commemorative plaque.

---

## Background: The Rebbe in Berlin

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson studied at the **University of Berlin** from 1928 to 1931. Archival research confirmed his address as 7 Hanse-Ufer Street. He subsequently continued his studies in [[entities/places/paris|Paris]] (1931–1941). He assumed the Chabad leadership in 1951.

His Berlin years occurred entirely before the Nazi period — he lived there during the Weimar Republic, leaving years before Hitler came to power. His father-in-law, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]], was headquartered in Berlin during these same years (1928–1933).

---

## The Ceremony

Mayor [[entities/people/joachim-zeller|Joachim Zeller]] presided over the plaque installation ceremony. His remarks articulated the historical significance with striking directness:

_"In those days, antisemitic signs were placed in Berlin. Today we place a memorial sign for the great Jewish leader."_

The contrast between Nazi-era antisemitic signage and this memorial plaque made the symbolic statement unmistakable: Berlin was acknowledging a new era in its relationship with Jews and Jewish heritage.

---

## Significance

Several dimensions make this event historically significant:

1. **First Jewish honoree**: The first time Berlin's city government officially honored a Jewish personality in this way.

2. **Historical reversal**: The same city that administered the Holocaust was now commemorating a leader of the Jewish people — and doing so in a building where that leader had actually lived.

3. **Chabad's response to persecution**: The plaque represents Chabad's ultimate response to centuries of persecution — not merely surviving but being officially honored in the capital of the country that attempted Jewish annihilation.

4. **The Rebbe's connection to Berlin**: The fact that the seventh Rebbe had studied in Berlin — and that this was now publicly commemorated — wove together the personal biography of Chabad's most recent Rebbe with the broader narrative of Jewish-German history.
