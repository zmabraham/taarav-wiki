# Construction of the Lubavitch Courtyard (Maharash and Rashab Eras)

**The construction and expansion of the main Chabad headquarters buildings in Lubavitch during the 4th and 5th Rebbe periods**

## Background

The town of Lubavitch had been the Chabad capital since the Mitteler Rebbe settled there in the early 19th century. Each successive Rebbe maintained and expanded the physical infrastructure of the Lubavitch court. By the time of the Maharash (4th Rebbe, 1866–1882) and especially the Rashab (5th Rebbe, 1882–1920), the Lubavitch courtyard had become a substantial complex.

## Development Under the Maharash

The Maharash's era was characterized by relative prosperity and stability. The Maharash was known for his engagement with the business world and with Russian aristocracy. During his leadership, the Lubavitch facilities were maintained and improved. R' Avraham Zeligson served as the town rabbi during this period, managing the communal infrastructure.

## Major Expansion Under the Rashab

The Rashab's long leadership (38 years) saw major construction and expansion:

- **Tomchei Temimim (1897)** — The founding of the yeshiva required construction of dedicated facilities: classrooms, dormitory space, a dining hall. This was the most significant single expansion of the Lubavitch complex.
- **Guest facilities** — As the yeshiva brought hundreds of permanent students to Lubavitch, alongside the continuing stream of Chassidic pilgrims, guest facilities were expanded.
- **The Rebbe's residence** — The main residential building of the Rebbe was maintained and updated.

R' Dovid Yakovson served as town rabbi throughout the Rashab's era, coordinating the practical community affairs alongside the Rebbe's court.

## Wartime Disruption

World War I disrupted the Lubavitch complex. In 1915, as the front approached, the Rashab evacuated to Rostov, and the buildings were left. War damage, requisitioning by military forces, and neglect caused significant deterioration.

## End of the Era

The Rashab's death in 1920, combined with the 1921 fire, effectively ended the Lubavitch courtyard as a functioning Chabad center.
