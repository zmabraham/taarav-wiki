# Vilna Herem on Chassidim 1797

**The Final Major Excommunication of Chassidim, Declared on Hoshana Rabbah**

**Date**: Hoshana Rabbah, Tishrei 5558 (October 1797)
**Location**: [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]]
**Key figures**: Vilna rabbinical leadership (after death of [[entities/people/vilna-gaon|Vilna Gaon]])
**Impact**: Intensified persecution of Chassidim throughout Lithuania; contributed to 1798 arrests

---

## Overview

On Hoshana Rabbah (the 7th day of Sukkos) 5558 (1797), the rabbinical leadership of [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]] declared a new and intensified herem (excommunication) against the Chassidic movement. This declaration came shortly after the death of the [[entities/people/vilna-gaon|Vilna Gaon]] — indicating that even without their greatest champion, the Vilna Mitnagdim were prepared to continue the war against Chassidism. The herem directly triggered the wave of persecution that led to the arrests of approximately 30 Lithuanian Chassidim in 1798 and, simultaneously, to the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe's]] arrest in Liadi.

---

## Background

### Earlier Herems

The 1797 herem was not the first:

- **1772**: The Vilna Gaon issued the first major herem against Chassidim
- **1781**: A second herem was issued
- **Ongoing tension**: The period from 1772 to 1797 saw repeated attempts to suppress Chassidism
- **Ineffectiveness**: Despite the herems, Chassidism continued to spread

### The Vilna Gaon's Death

The context of the herem:

- **Gaon died**: The Vilna Gaon died on Sukkos 5558 (October 9, 1797)
- **Timing**: The herem was declared on Hoshana Rabbah — just days after the Gaon's passing
- **Statement**: The herem sent a message that the Mitnagdic establishment would continue fighting even without the Gaon

---

## The Declaration

### The Herem Itself

The Vilna herem of 1797:

- **Excommunication**: Formally excluded Chassidim from the Jewish community
- **Prohibition**: Forbade Jews from having any social or commercial contact with Chassidim
- **Religious rejection**: Declared Chassidic practices invalid
- **Geographical scope**: Sent to Jewish communities across Lithuania, calling on them to adopt similar measures

### Content

The herem typically condemned:

- **New prayer rite**: The Chassidic adoption of the Lurianic/Sephardic prayer rite (Nusach Ari/Sefard)
- **New shechita**: Chassidic changes to ritual slaughter practices
- **New customs**: Various Chassidic innovations in religious practice
- **Chassidic leaders**: The Rebbes who were leading what the Mitnagdim saw as a dangerous sect

---

## Consequences

### The 1798 Arrests

The herem directly triggered aggressive action:

- **Government denunciations**: Vilna Mitnagdic leaders, emboldened by the herem, reported Lithuanian Chassidim to Russian government authorities
- **Accusations**: Chassidim accused of being members of a dangerous sect, maintaining secret connections, and undermining established religion
- **1798 arrests**: Approximately 30 Chassidim from Vilna and surroundings (including [[entities/places/vidz|Vidz]]) were arrested
- **Alter Rebbe arrested**: The same denunciations led to the Alter Rebbe's arrest in Liadi in the same year

### Immediate Impact on Communities

For Chassidic communities:

- **Increased pressure**: More intense communal exclusion
- **Fear**: Arrest and government prosecution now real possibilities
- **Underground activity**: Chassidim forced to be more secretive
- **Solidarity**: The persecution also created stronger bonds within the Chassidic community

---

## Resolution

### The Arrests' Outcome

- **Release**: The arrested Chassidim were released
- **Alter Rebbe released**: The Alter Rebbe was released on 19 Kislev 5559 (1798) — celebrated as Chag HaGeulah
- **Government disinterest**: Russian authorities found no criminal basis for charges against Chassidim
- **Mitnagdic defeat**: The attempt to use government power against Chassidism failed

### Long-term Change

After 1798:

- **Declining opposition**: The most intense period of active persecution gradually wound down
- **Exhaustion**: Both sides tired of the conflict
- **Practical accommodation**: Communities began finding ways to coexist
- **Chassidism established**: Chassidism was too widespread to be suppressed

---

## Significance in Chabad History

The 1797 herem:

- **Culmination**: Was the climax of the Vilna anti-Chassidic movement
- **Failed suppression**: Its consequences (1798 arrests) ultimately proved the failure of persecution
- **Martyrology**: The arrested Chassidim became heroes in Chassidic memory
- **Historical pivot**: After 1798, Chabad-Mitnagged relations gradually moved toward accommodation
