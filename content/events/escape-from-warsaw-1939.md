# Escape from Warsaw (1939)

**Date**: September–December 1939  
**Location**: Warsaw, Poland → Riga, Latvia → onward

---

## Overview

The evacuation of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] from Nazi-occupied Warsaw in late 1939 was one of the most dramatic rescue operations of the WWII era — accomplished through a combination of American diplomatic pressure, Chabad organizational effort, and sheer determination. The Rayatz's escape enabled him to reach America in March 1940 and begin the rebuilding of Chabad that would transform Jewish life globally.

---

## Trapped in Warsaw

When Nazi Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939, the Rayatz was in Warsaw. The city fell rapidly. The Rayatz's condition — confined to a wheelchair due to multiple sclerosis — made individual escape impossible without organized assistance.

Warsaw's Jewish population faced immediate ghettoization and mass murder. The Rayatz's presence in the city, as the leader of a major Jewish religious movement, made him both a symbol of resistance and a target.

---

## The Diplomatic Campaign

The rescue operation required:

**American Government Involvement**: Chabad supporters in America mobilized their political connections. **Senator James Byrnes** (later Secretary of State) was among the American officials involved in pressing for the Rayatz's evacuation. The US State Department engaged diplomatically with Nazi German and occupied Polish authorities.

**Document Negotiations**: Exit permits were required from Nazi German authorities — an extraordinary negotiation given the Nazi's systematic murder of Jewish leaders.

**Physical Logistics**: Physically transporting a wheelchair-bound Rebbe through Nazi-occupied territory required careful planning and coordination.

---

## The Route

The Rayatz escaped Warsaw through Nazi-controlled territory, reaching:

- **Riga, Latvia** (then still independent) — a brief stop in his former home city
- Continued westward through neutral countries
- **New York** — arriving March 19, 1940

---

## Significance

The escape from Warsaw was simultaneously:

- A personal miracle — the survival of the movement's leader against enormous odds
- A demonstration of what organized, determined effort could accomplish
- The beginning of the American chapter that would transform Chabad from a devastated refugee movement into a global force

The contrast between the Rayatz's escape and the fate of those left behind — including his daughter Sheina and her husband, and hundreds of Tomchei Temimim students — made survival itself a mandate: to rebuild in honor of those who could not escape.
