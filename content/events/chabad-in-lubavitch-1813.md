# Chabad Establishes Headquarters in Lubavitch (1813)

**Date**: 1813 (5573)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], Mogilev gubernia, Russia (present-day Smolensk region, Russia)

---

## Overview

In 1813, [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Rabbi DovBer Schneersohn]] — the second Lubavitcher Rebbe — settled in the small town of Lubavitch in the Mogilev region of Russia, establishing it as the world center of Chabad Chassidism. This decision, made in the aftermath of his father's (the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe's]]) death during Napoleon's invasion, began the movement's 102-year association with the town that gave it a permanent name: Lubavitch Chassidism, or Chabad-Lubavitch.

---

## Background

After the Alter Rebbe died in December 1812 while fleeing Napoleon's invasion, the Chassidic community needed a new center and new leadership. The Mitteler Rebbe, the Alter Rebbe's son and designated successor, assumed leadership and needed to establish a stable home for the movement.

Several factors may have influenced the choice of Lubavitch:

- Its relative obscurity and distance from major political centers (making it safer from attention)
- Its location in White Russia (Belarus), within the movement's primary geographic stronghold
- The practical availability of a suitable location

---

## What Lubavitch Became

From 1813, Lubavitch housed:

- **The Rebbe's court** — residence, study, and the place where chassidim came for audiences
- **Administrative offices** — correspondence, financial management, coordination of Chabad communities across the empire
- **A printing house** — producing Chassidic texts for distribution
- **A constant flow of pilgrims** — chassidim from across the Russian Empire and beyond came to Lubavitch for Shabbat, holidays, and personal guidance
- **Ultimately, Tomchei Temimim** (1897) — the flagship yeshiva that trained a new generation

The population of Lubavitch — perhaps a few thousand people — lived alongside this extraordinary religious center, creating an unusual social environment in an otherwise ordinary Russian provincial town.

---

## The 102-Year Span

Lubavitch served as Chabad's world center for **102 years** — from the Mitteler Rebbe's arrival in 1813 until the [[events/lubavitch-abandoned-1915|Rashab's departure in 1915]] during WWI, when the front approached and the town became too dangerous.

During this span, five Rebbes led the movement from Lubavitch:

1. [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (1813–1827)
2. [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] (1827–1866)
3. [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Rebbe Maharash]] (1866–1882)
4. [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] (1882–1915)
