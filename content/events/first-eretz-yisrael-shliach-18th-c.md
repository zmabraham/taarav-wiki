# First Chabad Shluchim to Russia from Eretz Yisrael (18th Century)

**The earliest emissaries (shluchim) from the Chassidic community in Eretz Yisrael who traveled to Russia, establishing financial and social connections between the Galilee settlements and the emerging Chabad movement**

## Background

Following the Chassidic aliyah of 1777, when R' Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and R' Avraham of Kalisk led a group of approximately 300 Chassidim to settle in Eretz Yisrael (primarily in Tiberias and Safed), the new communities depended heavily on financial support (chalukah) from Diaspora communities.

To collect this support, the Galilee communities regularly sent emissaries (shluchim or shdarim — plural of shodar) to travel through the Pale of Settlement and other Jewish communities, soliciting donations for the struggling settlers.

## Key Figures

**Yoel Smolianir** — An 18th-century emissary from the Tiberias community who traveled to Russia. His travels placed him in contact with the early Chabad movement. As a shaliach from a community led by R' Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and R' Avraham of Kalisk — who had been prominent Chassidic figures and were in contact with the Alter Rebbe — Yoel Smolianir would have navigated the complex web of relationships between Tiberias Chassidim and the White Russian Chabad community.

## Context

These early emissaries occupied a fascinating intersection:
- They represented the Eretz Yisrael Chassidic settlement to Russian Jews
- They facilitated the financial lifeline that kept the Galilee communities alive
- They were witnesses to both the developing Chabad movement in Russia and the established Chassidic settlement in the Land

Their travels were physically arduous and often dangerous, crossing borders and traversing hundreds of miles on foot or in simple conveyances.

## Significance

The early shluchim from Eretz Yisrael to Russia represent the original prototype of "Chabad shlichus" in a geographic sense — individuals sent far from their community to accomplish a specific mission of outreach and connection. They predate by two centuries the 20th-century shlichus network established by the Seventh Rebbe, but they embody the same fundamental concept of going out to connect distant communities. Their work also maintained the crucial spiritual and financial bond between Eretz Yisrael and the Diaspora.
