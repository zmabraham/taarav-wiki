# Riga Years (1927–1933)

**Date**: 25 Tishrei 5688 (October 21, 1927) — 1933  
**Location**: Riga, Latvia

---

## Overview

The six years the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] spent in Riga (1927–1933) represent one of the most complex and productive periods of his leadership — a time of personal displacement, sustained efforts to maintain Chabad underground in the Soviet Union, and the development of the "principled engagement" philosophy that would guide his American years.

---

## Arrival in Riga

The Rayatz arrived in Riga on **25 Tishrei 5688 (October 21, 1927)** — his first free day outside the Soviet Union. Thousands who had not seen him for 14 years (since the Soviet restrictions began) came to greet him.

Yet from the beginning, he made clear this was not a permanent home. The word "temporary" appeared on his Riga stationery. He wrote: _"I fled the bad, but I have not yet found the good."_

---

## Continuing the Russia Work

Despite being in exile, the Rayatz immediately resumed his work for Soviet Russian Jewry:

- **70 letters** to Russian Chasidim within his first month in Riga
- Established **Vaad Magen UMekhaseh** (Committee for Protection of Russian Jewry)
- Coordinated the underground network through coded correspondence

---

## The 1929 Matzah Campaign

One of the most dramatic achievements of the Riga years was the **1929 Passover Matzah Campaign** — an international effort to ship matzah into starving Soviet Russia despite official Soviet opposition.

[[entities/people/mordechai-dubin|Rabbi Mordechai Dubin]] (Latvian MP) negotiated with the Soviet ambassador in Riga. The Rayatz coordinated with Jewish communities in Berlin, chief rabbis across Europe, and philanthropists. Matzah was shipped into the Soviet Union through diplomatic channels — a remarkable achievement in the face of Soviet hostility.

See [[events/matzah-campaign-1929|Matzah Campaign (1929)]].

---

## The Rayatz's Philosophy: Principled Engagement

In Riga, the Rayatz developed his signature approach to living in the Western world: **"principled engagement"** — neither the insular defensiveness of the shtetl nor the assimilation of Reform Judaism, but active engagement with Western society while maintaining Torah values as the cornerstone.

This philosophy would guide his American period and, through the seventh Rebbe's expansion, become the basis for the global shlichus model.

---

## Key Ally: Rabbi Mordechai Dubin

[[entities/people/mordechai-dubin|Rabbi Mordechai Dubin]] — Latvian MP and devoted Chasid — was the Rayatz's most important political ally in Riga, providing diplomatic connections to Soviet and Latvian authorities.

---

## Travels

From Riga, the Rayatz made extended trips:

- **Berlin and Vienna**: Medical treatment and communal visits
- **Paris and Marienbad**: Health spa stays
- **Eretz Yisrael and America (1929–30)**: A yearlong trip that proved crucial preparation for his eventual American period

---

## Financial Crisis

The Riga years were financially difficult. The Rayatz relied on thin donations from small European Chabad communities and American synagogues. He took loans to keep the Russian underground funded, sometimes taking one loan to repay another.

---

## Medical Condition

During the Riga years, the Rayatz was diagnosed with **multiple sclerosis** — a progressive neurological disease that would confine him to a wheelchair by the time he reached America. Despite worsening health, he maintained an extraordinary pace of activity.

---

## Departure to Poland (1933)

After six years, the Rayatz relocated to Warsaw/Poland in 1933 to be closer to the Tomchei Temimim branches there. Riga had served its purpose as a waystation — he was now moving toward the center of European Jewish life.
