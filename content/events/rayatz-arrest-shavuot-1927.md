# The Rayatz's Arrest on Shavuot Eve (1927)

**Date**: 15 Sivan 5687 (June 15, 1927) — The eve of Shavuot  
**Time**: Approximately 3:00 AM  
**Location**: 22 Machovaya Street, Leningrad, Soviet Russia  
**Key Figures**: [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]], GPU agents, Yevsektsiya operatives

---

## Overview

In the early hours of **15 Sivan 5687 (June 15, 1927)** — just days before the festival of **Shavuot**, which commemorates the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai — GPU (secret police) agents arrived at the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] residence at **22 Machovaya Street in Leningrad** and arrested him. This was the culmination of years of [[events/yevsektsiya-vs-chabad|Yevsektsiya]] surveillance and persecution. The arrest — conducted in the middle of the night, through a door broken down by force — marked the beginning of the most dangerous period in the Rayatz's life and one of the most dramatic chapters in Chabad history.

---

## The Setting: 22 Machovaya Street

The Rayatz had moved to Leningrad in **1924**, establishing a new Chabad headquarters after leaving Rostov-on-Don (where his father, the Rashab, was buried). The apartment at 22 Machovaya Street became:

- **The Rayatz's home**: Where he lived with his family, including his daughter [[entities/people/chaya-mushka|Chaya Mushka]]
- **The underground headquarters**: From where he coordinated the network of secret yeshivos, cheders, mikvaos, and religious courts throughout the Soviet Union
- **A destination for chassidim**: Who traveled to Leningrad to see the Rebbe, receive blessings, and report on underground activities
- **A target under surveillance**: GPU agents monitored who came and went, documenting visitors for use in building a case

The apartment contained:

- The Rayatz's personal quarters
- A study filled with sacred texts
- Spaces for receiving visitors
- Hidden or concealed materials: lists of underground institutions, coded correspondence, maamad (fund) records

---

## The Buildup: Surveillance and Preparation

The Yevsektsiya and GPU had been building a case against the Rayatz for years:

**Documented Activities**:

- Operating illegal religious schools (the Tomchei Temimim underground network)
- Collecting and distributing funds for underground religious activities (the _maamad_)
- Maintaining correspondence with foreign Jewish leaders (considered espionage under Soviet law)
- Refusing to close religious institutions despite Soviet orders

**Surveillance**:

- GPU agents watched the building at 22 Machovaya Street
- Visitors were followed and their names recorded
- The Yevsektsiya recruited informants within the Jewish community
- Mail was intercepted and read

**The Decision to Arrest**:
By spring 1927, Soviet authorities decided to move against the Rayatz. The plan was to arrest him quietly, avoid public disturbance, extract a confession or guilty verdict, and use the trial to intimidate the entire religious Jewish community.

---

## The Arrest: 3:00 AM, 15 Sivan 5687

### The Arrival

Approximately **3:00 AM** on the night between **Wednesday and Thursday**, 14-15 Sivan, GPU agents arrived at 22 Machovaya Street. The timing was deliberate:

- Nighttime arrests minimized witnesses
- The apartment's occupants would be asleep, unprepared to resist or destroy evidence
- The Rayatz could be taken before the community could mobilize

### Breaking Down the Door

When the agents knocked and demanded entry, there was no immediate response — whether due to caution, confusion, or the deep sleep of the household. The agents responded by **breaking down the door**.

The crash of the door being forced open woke the household. The Rayatz, his family, and any guests or assistants present were confronted by armed GPU agents in their home.

### The Arrest Warrant

The agents presented an arrest warrant naming the Rayatz and charging him with **counter-revolutionary activity** — a catchall charge that essentially meant "opposition to the Soviet state." In the context of religious activity, this was a capital offense.

### The Search

The arrest was accompanied by an extensive search of the premises:

**What They Sought**:

- Lists of underground institutions and activists
- Financial records of the maamad fund
- Correspondence with foreign Jewish leaders
- Evidence of illegal religious education

**What They Found**:

- Sacred texts (sifrei kodesh) — Torah scrolls, Talmuds, Chassidic books
- Correspondence, some of which was coded
- Records of underground activities (though some materials had likely been hidden or destroyed in anticipation)
- Personal belongings

The search lasted hours. The GPU agents packed up documents and books as evidence.

---

## The Rayatz's Conduct

### "I Recognize Only One Authority"

When confronted by the GPU agents, the Rayatz made one of the most famous statements in Chabad history:

**"I recognize only one authority — the Almighty."**

This declaration of faith was an act of extraordinary courage. The GPU agents — armed, empowered by the Soviet state, authorized to imprison and execute — were told that their authority meant nothing to a Jew who recognized only God's sovereignty.

### Facing the Gun

One GPU agent, attempting to intimidate the Rayatz, placed a loaded revolver on the table during questioning and said:

**"This little toy has made many a man change his mind."**

The Rayatz's response became legendary:

**"That little toy can intimidate only the kind of man who has many gods and only one world. Because I have only one God and two worlds, I am not impressed by your little toy."**

The "two worlds" refer to **This World** (_Olam HaZeh_) and **the World to Come** (_Olam Haba_). A person who believes only in this world can be intimidated by threats to physical life. A person who believes in both worlds — who sees physical existence as merely the vestibule to eternal spiritual life — cannot be intimidated by death.

### Continued Religious Observance

Even as he was being arrested, the Rayatz continued religious observance:

- He refused to compromise his principles or betray his community
- He maintained his dignity and composure
- He acted throughout with the understanding that a _tzaddik_ (righteous person) does not fear mortal power

This conduct set the tone for everything that followed — the interrogations, the sentencing, the prison time. The Rayatz would not break, would not compromise, would not accept Soviet authority over his religious life.

---

## Family Impact

The arrest was traumatic for the Rayatz's family:

**Rebbetzin Nechama Dina**: The Rayatz's wife (daughter of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]]) watched her husband taken away by armed men.

**Chaya Mushka**: The Rayatz's daughter (who would later marry the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]) was a young woman during this period. The sight of her father arrested by armed agents left an indelible impression.

**Household Disruption**: The apartment was ransacked during the search. Personal belongings were scattered. Family members were questioned.

Despite the trauma, the family maintained faith and composure — reflecting the spiritual training they had received.

---

## The Charges

The Rayatz was charged with three primary offenses:

**1. Operating Illegal Religious Schools**

The Tomchei Temimim underground network — cheders, yeshivos, and study groups operating in private homes across the Soviet Union — was illegal under Soviet law. The Rayatz, as the movement's leader, was held responsible.

**2. Collecting Funds for Religious Institutions**

The _maamad_ — a fund collected from chassidim to support underground teachers, institutions, and families in need — was considered illegal financial activity. The fact that the funds supported religion made it worse in Soviet eyes.

**3. Correspondence with Foreign Jewish Leaders**

Letters to Jewish leaders in Latvia, Poland, Palestine, America, and elsewhere were considered **espionage**. In the paranoid atmosphere of the Soviet state, any contact with foreigners was suspect.

Each charge carried severe penalties, potentially including death.

---

## Immediate Aftermath

### Transport to Prison

After the search was completed, the Rayatz was taken to **Shpalerka prison** in Leningrad — a notorious prison used for political prisoners.

**The Journey**: The Rayatz was transported under guard, likely in a vehicle or on foot through the nighttime streets of Leningrad.

**Arrival at Prison**: He was processed into the prison system — photographed, fingerprinted, assigned a cell.

### First Night in Prison

The Rayatz's first night in Shpalerka prison — alone, in a cold cell, uncertain of his fate — was an act of _mesirus nefesh_ (self-sacrifice) that would be recounted in Chabad tradition for generations.

### Community Shock

News of the arrest spread rapidly through Chabad communities:

**Leningrad**: Local chassidim were devastated. Their leader had been taken.

**Throughout the Soviet Union**: Underground communities learned the news through coded messages and personal messengers.

**Outside the Soviet Union**: Word would soon reach Jewish communities worldwide — triggering the [[events/rescue-campaign-1927|international campaign for his liberation]].

---

## Historical Significance

The arrest on Shavuot eve carries symbolic weight:

**Shavuot**: The holiday that commemorates the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai. The Rayatz — arrested for teaching Torah — was taken into custody precisely when Jews were preparing to celebrate that divine revelation.

**Persecution of Torah**: The timing underscored that the persecution was specifically about Torah — the Soviet state could not tolerate those who kept Torah alive.

**Historical Parallel**: The arrest echoed the persecution of Jews throughout history who were punished for maintaining Torah observance. The Rayatz was understood as continuing this tradition of faithfulness under persecution.
