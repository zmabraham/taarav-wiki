# Rashab's Death and Succession (1920)

**Date**: 2 Nissan 5680 (March 21, 1920)  
**Location**: Rostov-on-Don, Russia

---

## Overview

The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] (Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn) died on **2 Nissan 5680 (March 21, 1920)** in Rostov-on-Don, Russia — surrounded by his son and successor, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]], and key Chassidim. He had been ill for some time, and his passing came during one of the most tumultuous periods in Chabad history — three years after the Bolshevik Revolution, as the Soviet state began systematically dismantling Jewish religious life.

---

## Final Days and Prophecy

Three weeks before his death, the Rashab told the Rayatz:

_"There will be difficult times; you will need to have mesirat nefesh bepoel (actual self-sacrifice). But eventually, the Communist government will disintegrate."_

This prophecy — preserved in the Rayatz's diary — would be cited repeatedly in subsequent decades as the Rayatz faced Soviet persecution. Its fulfillment came in 1990–1991 with the Soviet collapse.

---

## The Succession

The Rayatz assumed leadership of Chabad immediately upon his father's death — though the formal public assumption of the Rebbe's role followed the mourning period and traditional patterns of Chassidic succession. The Rayatz was 39 years old.

He inherited:

- A movement in crisis — the Soviet state actively working to destroy Jewish religious institutions
- An underground educational network that required constant maintenance and support
- The moral and theological authority of the Chabad Rebbe — which he would deploy against Soviet power in one of the most dramatic confrontations in Jewish history

---

## The Rashab's Legacy

At his death, the Rashab left as his legacy:

- **Tomchei Temimim** — the flagship yeshiva, already operating underground in Soviet conditions
- **Samech Vov and other Chassidic discourses** — a corpus of philosophical writing of extraordinary depth
- **The organizational infrastructure** of Chabad communities across Russia
- **His son and successor** — the Rayatz, whose extraordinary qualities the Rashab had recognized and cultivated since the Rayatz's childhood

The Rashab's grave in Rostov-on-Don became a pilgrimage site for Chabad Chassidim.
