# Chabad's Historical Role in German Jewry

**From the Rashab's Medical Visits to Post-Holocaust Renewal**

Full documentation: [[summaries/chabad-germany|אדמו"רי חב"ד ויהדות גרמניה]]

---

## Overview

Chabad's relationship with German Jewry spans more than a century and represents one of the movement's most complex engagements with Western modernity. The Rashab — who despised Kaiser Wilhelm and called Germany "Amalek" — nevertheless engaged deeply with German Orthodox leadership. His son the Rayatz spent years in Berlin. And ultimately, Chabad re-established a major presence in Berlin after the Holocaust.

---

## Key Episodes

### 1885: First Contact

Rashab visits Berlin; meets **Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer** (founder of Adass Jisroel, Berlin Rabbinical Seminary). Hildesheimer receives him with great honor, treating the Rashab as the son of the Maharash whom Hildesheimer had known. This established the working relationship between Chabad and German Neo-Orthodoxy.

### 1889: The Chilazon Search

Rashab visits Berlin Aquarium to observe a creature potentially identified as the _chilazon_ (source of biblical sky-blue _techelet_ dye). He observes a small creature secreting black liquid but does not realize it might be the chilazon. Reports this 20 years later to the Radzyner Rebbe.

### 1906–1907: The Würzburg Year

The Rashab spends an entire year in Würzburg, Bavaria — his most extended stay in Germany:

- Composes **Hemshech Samech-Vav** (one of the deepest Chassidic discourses in the Chabad canon)
- Accesses Kaiser Wilhelm's writing room (through a bribed guard) and writes a maamar on imperial stationery
- Collaborates with Rabbis Shlomo Zalman Breuer (Frankfurt) and Yaakov Rosenheim (Agudas Yisrael) on the **"Histaadus HaYereim"** — a plan to strengthen German Orthodoxy
- Leads a farbrengen on Yud-Tet Kislev attended by German Jews, who are deeply impressed by his two-hour prayer
- The Rayatz joins him in Kislev 1906

### 1928–1933: The Rayatz in Berlin

After escaping Soviet Russia, the Rayatz visits Berlin repeatedly during his Riga exile years. He conducts diplomatic and communal work with German Jewish leadership at the height of the Weimar Republic and into Hitler's rise.

### 1928–1931: The Future Seventh Rebbe's Studies

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (the Ramash, future seventh Rebbe) studies at the **University of Berlin** from 1928 to 1931, living at **7 Hanse-Ufer Street** in Berlin. He later continues studies in Paris. This period precedes his assumption of Chabad leadership in 1951.

**2001:** Berlin city government installs a memorial plaque at 7 Hanse-Ufer Street — the first official Berlin honor for a Jewish personality with such recognition. Mayor Joachim Zeller: "In those days, antisemitic signs were placed in Berlin. Today we place a memorial sign for the great Jewish leader."

### Holocaust

German Jewry is destroyed. The Rashab's 1906 prophecy that Germany would "choke" on what it swallowed proved accurate — though at a cost the Rashab did not live to see.

### 1996–Present: Chabad Berlin

**Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel** arrives in Berlin (1996) to establish Chabad presence. Against all historical expectation, Chabad builds:

- **Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin** — a major center in central Berlin
- A synagogue (capacity 250)
- **"Yudishe Traditsyone Shule Ohr Avner"** — a Jewish school housed in a former Nazi officers' club
- Restaurant, mikveh, library, youth center
- Shabbat minyanim growing from 20 to hundreds

Chancellor Angela Merkel acknowledged the community as evidence of genuine Jewish confidence in Germany's future.

---

## The Ironic Arc

The Rashab, who called Wilhelm II "Amalek" and prayed for Russia's victory over Germany, had a grandson who studied in Berlin — and a great-grandson movement that re-established Jewish life in Germany after the Holocaust. History vindicated his prophecy of Germany's defeat while confounding his apparent assumption that Jewish life in Germany was finished.
