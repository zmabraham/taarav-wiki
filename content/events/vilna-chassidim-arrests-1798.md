# מאסר חסידים בווילנא — הושנא רבה תקנ"ט

## Arrests of Chassidim in Vilna (Hoshana Raba 1798)

**Date**: Hoshana Raba 5559 (autumn 1798)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]] (Vilnius, present-day Lithuania)  
**Perpetrators**: Mitnagdim (opponents of Chassidism) using Czarist legal channels  
**Victims**: [[entities/people/meir-refael-vilna|R' Meir Refael]] and other Chabad chassidim

---

## Overview

On **Hoshana Raba 5559 (1798)**, Mitnagdim in Vilna took the extraordinary step of having Chabad chassidim arrested using Czarist imperial legal mechanisms. This event — documented in the _Toldot Chabad BePolin, Lita veLativya_ (Book 5) — represents the escalation of the Mitnagdim-Chassidim conflict from religious condemnation to legal persecution.

---

## Context: The Vilna Gaon's Campaign

The arrests came in the wake of the [[entities/people/vilna-gaon|Vilna Gaon's]] anti-Chassidic campaign, which had intensified in the 1790s. The Gaon had issued severe pronouncements against Chassidim, and in [[events/the-herem-1796|1796]] had issued his most comprehensive ban against Chassidism. His followers in Vilna were prepared to use any legal means to suppress the Chassidic presence in their city.

---

## The Arrests

On Hoshana Raba (the seventh day of Sukkot) 5559 (1798), Mitnagdim in Vilna had [[entities/people/meir-refael-vilna|R' Meir Refael]] and other Chabad chassidim arrested. The charges are not fully specified in available sources, but the pattern was similar to what the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] had experienced in the same year — accusations of suspicious activity, seditious correspondence, or improper organization.

This date is notable because **1798** was also the year of the [[events/alter-rebbe-arrest-1798|Alter Rebbe's First Arrest]] in St. Petersburg — demonstrating that Chabad leadership and followers faced simultaneous persecution from both state authorities and Jewish opponents in the same year.

---

## Significance

The Vilna arrests represent:

1. **Escalation of internal Jewish conflict**: The willingness to use Czarist legal mechanisms against fellow Jews was a serious step — one that could have endangered not just the Chassidim arrested but potentially the broader Jewish community.

2. **Political dimension of religious dispute**: The Mitnagdim-Chassidim conflict was not merely a theological debate; it had real-world consequences including imprisonment.

3. **Pattern of persecution**: The arrests in Vilna established a pattern that would recur throughout Chabad history — state (or state-aligned) forces used against Chabad figures, requiring courage, legal defense, and diplomatic intervention.
