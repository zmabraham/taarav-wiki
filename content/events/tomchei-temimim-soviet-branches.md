# Tomchei Temimim Soviet Underground Branches (1920s–1930s)

**The network of clandestine Tomchei Temimim yeshiva branches maintained throughout Soviet Russia after the Bolshevik closure of open religious institutions**

## Background

Tomchei Temimim was founded in Lubavitch in 1897 by the Rashab. After the Bolshevik Revolution (1917) and the consolidation of Soviet power, religious education was targeted for elimination. The Yevsektsiya (Jewish Section of the Communist Party) specifically targeted Chabad institutions.

When open operation became impossible, the Rayatz (6th Rebbe) directed that Tomchei Temimim continue operating underground across the Soviet Union.

## The Network

The clandestine Tomchei Temimim operated branches in numerous cities throughout the 1920s and into the 1930s, including:

- **Vitebsk** — branch documented in the Shusterman memoir (1928)
- **Mogilev** — branch documented in the Shusterman memoir (1929)
- **Kherson** — branch documented (winter 1930)
- **Zambin** (Zambin/Zembin) — mentioned in the Lubavitch town history
- **Kovlitz** — mentioned in sources
- **Nevel** — branch in this important Chabad town
- **Harodzishtz** — branch
- **Dakshintz** — branch
- **Babinowitz** — branch
- **Shchedrin** — branch

Additionally, branches operated in the Baltic states (Riga, Vilna) and in Poland (Warsaw, Lodz, Chelm, Otwock) where conditions were less immediately dangerous.

## Method of Operation

The underground branches typically operated in private homes, rotating locations to avoid detection. Students would study in small groups (often 5–10) rather than large classrooms. Mashpi'im and teachers moved between locations. Parents faced severe risks in sending children to these classes, as they could be charged with "teaching religion to minors."

The memoir literature (Lmaan Yadau, Zichronosai) provides firsthand accounts of the experience of studying in these underground conditions.

## Dismantling

The network was progressively disrupted by NKVD operations through the 1930s. By the late 1930s, most branches inside the Soviet Union had been shut down and many of their organizers arrested.

## Significance

The underground Tomchei Temimim branches represent one of the most remarkable examples of religious resistance in Soviet history. Through the efforts of teachers, students, and supporting families, Chabad maintained continuous yeshiva education inside the Soviet Union for approximately two decades after the Bolshevik Revolution. This period forged the character of the Chabad generation that later escaped to the West.
