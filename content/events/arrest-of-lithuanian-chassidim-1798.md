# Arrest of Lithuanian Chassidim 1798

**Approximately 30 Lithuanian Chassidim Arrested; Simultaneous with Alter Rebbe's Arrest in Liadi**

**Date**: 1798 (5558–5559)
**Location**: [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]] and surrounding communities, including [[entities/places/vidz|Vidz]]
**Key figures**: [[entities/people/meir-raphael-yafeh|R' Meir ben Rephael Yafeh]] (Vilna Chabad head); [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]
**Impact**: One of the most severe persecutions of Lithuanian Chabad; ultimately resolved with all released

---

## Overview

In 1798, approximately 30 Chassidim from Vilna and surrounding areas — including the community of [[entities/places/vidz|Vidz]] — were arrested by Russian government authorities on accusations of being dangerous sectarians. This persecution was directly instigated by Mitnagdic leaders in Vilna who, following the [[events/vilna-herem-1797|1797 herem]] and emboldened by the anti-Chassidic climate, reported the Chassidim to the government. Simultaneously, the same wave of denunciations led to the arrest of the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] himself in Liadi — making 1798 the most severe year of persecution for the Chabad movement.

---

## Background

### Post-Herem Environment

Following the [[events/vilna-herem-1797|Vilna Herem of 1797]]:

- **Heightened tensions**: The excommunication declaration intensified anti-Chassidic sentiment
- **Government reports**: Mitnagdic leaders turned to government authorities to accomplish what communal pressure had not
- **Accusations**: Chassidim were accused of being members of a dangerous sect maintaining secret connections with foreign leaders (the Rebbes) and deviating from established Jewish practice

### Denunciation Strategy

The Mitnagdic strategy of government denunciation:

- **Russian Empire context**: In the Russian Empire, religious deviations from recognized communities were potentially criminal
- **Sectarian accusations**: Portraying Chassidim as a dangerous "new sect" that undermined the authority of established Jewish leadership
- **Political framing**: The accusation that Chassidim maintained connections with leaders in foreign territory (Poland) had political dimensions

---

## The Arrests

### Lithuanian Chassidim

- **Number**: Approximately 30 Chassidim were arrested
- **Locations**: From Vilna and surrounding communities, including Vidz
- **Leadership**: [[entities/people/meir-raphael-yafeh|R' Meir ben Rephael Yafeh]], head of the Vilna Chabad community, was among those arrested
- **Process**: Arrested by Russian government authorities and held for interrogation
- **Accusations**: Interrogated about their beliefs, practices, and organizational connections

### The Alter Rebbe's Arrest

Simultaneously with the Lithuanian arrests:

- **Same denunciations**: The reports to the government also named the Alter Rebbe as the leader of the Chassidic "sect"
- **Liadi**: The Alter Rebbe was arrested at his court in Liadi
- **Petersburg imprisonment**: He was taken to Petersburg and held in the Shpalerka prison
- **Capital charge**: Initially faced potentially serious charges
- **Chag HaGeulah**: Released on 19 Kislev 5559 (1798) — this day became the annual "Festival of Redemption"

---

## Interrogation and Release

### The Government's Investigation

Russian authorities investigated the accusations:

- **Interrogation**: Both the Lithuanian Chassidim and the Alter Rebbe were questioned
- **Investigation**: The government examined the accusations and the nature of the Chassidic movement
- **Findings**: The government found no criminal basis for the charges

### Release

- **Lithuanian Chassidim released**: The approximately 30 arrested Chassidim were released
- **Alter Rebbe released**: Released on 19 Kislev 5559 / December 19, 1798
- **No charges**: Russian authorities determined Chassidism was not a criminal or seditious movement
- **Mitnagdic failure**: The attempt to use government power to suppress Chassidism failed

---

## Impact on Chabad History

### Chag HaGeulah

The Alter Rebbe's release:

- **19 Kislev**: Became the most celebrated date in the Chabad calendar
- **"Festival of Redemption"**: Annual celebration of liberation of the "Torah of Chassidus" from imprisonment
- **Spiritual meaning**: The Rebbe's release interpreted as divine confirmation that Chabad's path was legitimate

### Long-term Effects

The persecution and its resolution:

- **Emboldened Chassidim**: The government's finding that Chassidism was legitimate strengthened the movement
- **Weakened opposition**: The Mitnagdic strategy of government persecution had failed
- **Declining hostility**: After 1798, the most violent phase of Mitnagdic opposition gradually faded
- **Precedent**: Established that Chabad could withstand even government persecution

### Memory in Chabad

The 1798 arrests are remembered as:

- **Heroism**: The arrested Chassidim are remembered as heroes who suffered for their beliefs
- **Divine protection**: The release interpreted as divine providence protecting the movement
- **Historical turning point**: The beginning of the end of the most intense Mitnagdic opposition
