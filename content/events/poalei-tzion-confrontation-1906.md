# Poalei Tzion Confrontation at Tomchei Temimim (1906)

**Date**: 1906 (5666)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], Russia

---

## Overview

In 1906, an armed confrontation erupted between Tomchei Temimim yeshiva and the Marxist-Zionist **Poalei Tzion** movement after a yeshiva student defected to join their organization. The confrontation — in which a mob of 200 Poalei Tzion activists shot at the yeshiva — tested the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] courage and leadership under physical threat and demonstrated the violent ideological battles of the revolutionary era.

---

## Background: The Era of Revolution

1906 was in the midst of the tumultuous period following Russia's failed 1905 revolution. Political radicalization swept through Russian Jewish youth — many turning to Zionism, socialism, or various combinations thereof (Poalei Tzion represented the Marxist-Zionist synthesis). The traditional religious world of Lubavitch faced not just intellectual challenge but active recruitment of its students.

---

## The Defection

A Tomchei Temimim student defected to the Poalei Tzion movement. His departure was not merely personal — it was used by Poalei Tzion as a demonstration that even Lubavitcher students were abandoning traditional religious life for progressive revolutionary ideology.

---

## Armed Confrontation

The situation escalated dramatically:

1. **Initial confrontation**: Poalei Tzion activists made armed demands of the yeshiva — apparently seeking some form of recognition or concession related to the defected student.

2. **Rayatz's response**: The Rayatz — then serving as director of Tomchei Temimim — confronted them directly: **"Get out of here! I'm not afraid of your guns."** He refused to submit to armed intimidation.

3. **The mob attack**: Subsequently, a mob of approximately **200 Poalei Tzion members** attacked the yeshiva — shots were fired at the building.

4. **Yeshiva students' defense**: The yeshiva students — apparently organized and prepared — drove off the attackers.

---

## The Rayatz Armed

The biography [[summaries/rayatz-biography|*Undaunted*]] records that during this period, the Rayatz **carried a gun**. He also narrowly escaped an ambush at a train station — suggesting that the threats against him were not isolated incidents but sustained physical danger.

The image of the Chabad Rebbe-director carrying a weapon while running an underground network — later adapted to political, spiritual, and logistical networks under Soviet persecution — reveals the Rayatz as someone who understood force and used it when necessary.

---

## Broader Significance

The Poalei Tzion confrontation illustrates:

- The fierce ideological battles within Russian Jewish society in the revolutionary era
- Chabad's determination not to yield to either external threats or internal defections
- The Rayatz's personal courage — a quality that would later manifest in his confrontation with GPU interrogators in 1927
