# Yud Kislev — Liberation of the Mitteler Rebbe (1826)

**Also Known As**: השתפכות הנשמה (Hishtafkus Haneshamah); Yud/Yud-Tet Kislev  
**Date**: 9–10 Kislev 5587 (late November 1826)  
**Location**: Vitebsk, Russian Empire

---

## Overview

On 9 Kislev 5587 (late November 1826), [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|the Mitteler Rebbe]] was liberated from Czarist custody after more than a year of investigation and interim detention. His liberation on this date — which coincides with his birthday and, one year later, his yahrzeit — made 9–10 Kislev one of the most celebrated holidays in the Chabad calendar, known as _Yud Kislev_ or _Yud-Tet Kislev_.

The liberation came not through imperial pardon but through the Russian judicial system's formal acquittal of all charges.

---

## The Liberation Date

**Significance of the date**:
- **Birth**: The Mitteler Rebbe was born on 9 Kislev 5534 (1773)
- **Liberation**: He was freed on 9 Kislev 5587 (1826)
- **Yahrzeit**: He passed away on 9 Kislev 5588 (1827), exactly one year after liberation

This convergence — birth, liberation, and yahrzeit on the same date — makes 9 Kislev uniquely significant in Chabad consciousness.

---

## The Legal Path to Liberation

The Mitteler Rebbe's liberation was the final stage of a multi-level judicial process:

### 1. Mohilev District Court Decision (July 1826)
The Babinovich District Court (which had mishandled the case earlier) issued a decision acquitting the Rebbe. The court found:
- All donations were voluntary, not coerced
- No fixed salary existed, so Section 52 did not apply
- The Rebbe was not a government-appointed rabbi but a voluntary spiritual leader
- No evidence of oppression or complaints from Chassidic communities

### 2. Mohilev Supreme Court Decision (November 1826)
The Mohilev gubernia Supreme Court reviewed the case and upheld the acquittal. Its reasoning:
- The Rebbe had been invited by Chassidim to serve as their spiritual guide
- His support came from voluntary contributions, not taxation
- He had no coercive power over anyone who did not willingly accept his guidance
- Voluntary private charity could not be prohibited by law

### 3. Imperial Senate Decision (28 November 1827 / 21 Kislev 5588)
The Imperial Senate in St. Petersburg, the highest court of appeal, issued its final ruling:
- Fully upheld the lower courts' acquittal
- Rejected [[entities/people/governor-khavanski|Governor Khavanski's]] request for new legislation
- Found "no legal basis for the denunciation made by the Jew Kissin"
- Ordered governors to enforce existing laws but refused to expand them

The Senate's decision was the ultimate liberation — complete vindication at the imperial level.

---

## Khavanski's Defeat

Governor Khavanski's appeal to the Imperial Senate (15 May 1827) represents the last attempt to punish the Rebbe. He argued:

1. The 1813 distribution of 35,000 rubles proved illegal enrichment
2. Voluntary donations were a ruse to circumvent Section 52
3. The Rebbe should be disqualified from rabbinic service for not knowing Russian
4. New legislation was needed to prohibit all rabbinic arbitration

**The Senate's response** (as communicated by the Minister of Interior):
- No basis for new legislation — the existing 1804 statute was sufficient
- Governors should strictly supervise rabbinic appointments but not overreach
- The Rebbe's voluntary support system did not violate the law
- Khavanski's proposed restrictions were rejected

Khavanski's defeat was total: not only was the Rebbe exonerated, but the Senate refused to use the case as precedent for restricting rabbinic authority.

---

## The Nature of the Celebration

Yud Kislev is celebrated in Chabad communities worldwide with:

- **Farbrengens** (joyous gatherings) where Chassidim recount the liberation story
- **Study of the Mitteler Rebbe's teachings**, particularly his deep philosophical works (Sha'ar HaEmunah, Sha'ar HaYichud, etc.)
- **Expressions of gratitude** (hakkalah hatovah) for divine intervention
- **Reflection on mesirus nefesh** (self-sacrifice) in the face of persecution

The holiday embodies the Chabad principle that spiritual liberation follows physical liberation — the Rebbe's freedom enabled him to continue disseminating Chassidic teachings in his final year.

## Historical Context: Parallel to the Alter Rebbe

The Mitteler Rebbe's arrest and liberation consciously echoed his father's experience:

| Aspect | Alter Rebbe (1798) | Mitteler Rebbe (1825–1826) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Trigger** | Denunciation by Mitnagdim | Denunciation by Simcha Kissin |
| **Charge** | Treason via Palestine funds | Illegal financial collections |
| **Arrest** | 15 Kislev 5559 | 1 Tishrei 5586 |
| **Liberation** | 19 Kislev 5559 | 9–10 Kislev 5587 |
| **Holiday** | Yud-Tet Kislev | Yud/Yud-Tet Kislev |
| **Outcome** | Complete exoneration | Complete exoneration |

This pattern — arrest → liberation → holiday — became foundational to Chabad identity and recurred in [[entities/people/rayatz|the Rayatz's]] 1927 arrest.

---

## The Rebbe's Final Year

Liberation came in late 1826, but the Rebbe's health had been severely weakened by:
- The stress of more than a year under investigation
- His travels to [[entities/places/karlsbad|Karlsbad]] for health treatment during the denunciations
- The physical toll of interrogations and detention

He passed away exactly one year after liberation, on 9 Kislev 5588 (1827), leaving his teachings and the victory over state persecution as his legacy to Chabad.
