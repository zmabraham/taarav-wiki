# Founding of Tomchei Temimim (1897)

**Date**: 15 Elul 5657 (September 12, 1897)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], Russia  
**Key Figures**: [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]], [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]

---

## Overview

The founding of **Yeshiva Tomchei Temimim** ("Supporters of the Sincere") was one of the most consequential decisions in Chabad history. Established by the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] on 15 Elul 5657 (September 12, 1897), the yeshiva created a new model of Jewish education that unified advanced Talmudic study with systematic Chassidic learning. It produced the generation of activists who sustained Chabad through Soviet persecution, the Holocaust, and the movement's postwar global expansion.

---

## Background: The Wedding Gift

The founding is inseparable from the story of the Rayatz's wedding. On September 10, 1897, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (then 17 years old) married Nechama Dina Schneersohn. As a wedding gift, the Rayatz made an unusual request of his father: instead of a material gift, he asked for the establishment of a yeshiva that would teach Chassidus as a formal subject alongside the standard Talmudic curriculum.

Two days later, on September 12, 1897 (15 Elul), the Rebbe Rashab gathered approximately 50 guests who had remained from the wedding celebrations and made his announcement: he would establish a new yeshiva in Lubavitch.

---

## The Revolutionary Curriculum

What made Tomchei Temimim revolutionary was its curriculum. In the standard yeshiva model of the time, students studied Talmud and its commentaries exclusively. Chassidic teachings, if studied at all, were studied informally and privately.

Tomchei Temimim established a formal dual curriculum:

- **8 hours daily**: Talmud, Halacha, and traditional Torah study — under the direction of the _Rosh Yeshiva_
- **4 hours daily**: Chassidus (Chassidic philosophy and mystical texts) — under the direction of the _Mashpia_

### The Mashpia vs. Rosh Yeshiva Distinction

This dual structure introduced a new institutional role: the **mashpia** (spiritual mentor). While the _Rosh Yeshiva_ was responsible for intellectual Talmudic training, the _mashpia_ guided students in:

- Study of Chassidic texts (Tanya, maamarim)
- Personal spiritual practice (hisbonenus — contemplation)
- Character development and ethical refinement
- Farbrengen leadership (Chassidic gatherings)

The mashpia was not subordinate to the Rosh Yeshiva; they held parallel authority over complementary domains. This dual structure became the template for all subsequent Chabad yeshivos.

## Early Growth

The yeshiva began with **18 students** in its first cohort. Growth was rapid:

| Year | Approximate Enrollment |
| ---- | ---------------------- |
| 1897 | 18 students            |
| 1899 | ~60 students           |
| 1902 | ~200 students          |

The Rebbe Rashab personally supervised the curriculum and frequently tested students in both Talmud and Chassidus. He delivered regular maamarim (Chassidic discourses) to the student body — some of which became major works, including parts of the monumental _Hemshech Samech-Vav_ (1905–1908).

---

## The Name "Tomchei Temimim"

The yeshiva was not initially given a formal name. On **Simchat Torah 5658 (1898)**, the Rebbe Rashab bestowed the name _Tomchei Temimim_ — "Supporters of the Sincere" (or "Upholders of the Wholehearted"). The name carried a double meaning:

1. **The students support Torah**: _temimim_ ("sincere/wholehearted ones") refers to the students themselves, who uphold the Torah in its complete form — both its revealed and mystical dimensions
2. **The Torah is wholehearted**: the yeshiva produces a _temimus_ (wholeness) by unifying the intellectual and emotional, the legal and mystical

The Rebbe Rashab later explained that the _temimim_ are "soldiers of the House of David" — a phrase that would take on practical meaning during the Soviet period, when yeshiva graduates literally risked their lives for Jewish continuity.

## Confrontations and Challenges

### The Poalei Tzion Confrontation (March 1906)

In March 1906, members of **Poalei Tzion** (a socialist-Zionist group) confronted yeshiva students in Lubavitch. The clash — which turned physical — reflected the broader ideological battle between traditional Judaism and the revolutionary movements sweeping Russian Jewish youth. The yeshiva students' willingness to physically defend their institution became a point of pride and a demonstration of the Rashab's vision of "soldiers."

See: [[events/poalei-tzion-confrontation-1906|Poalei Tzion Confrontation (1906)]]

### Institutional Sustainability

Funding the yeshiva was a constant challenge. The Rebbe Rashab relied on donations from wealthy supporters and on the communal resources of Lubavitch. He refused to lower educational standards despite financial pressures — a pattern that would repeat throughout Chabad's institutional history.

---

## Legacy

Tomchei Temimim proved to be the Rebbe Rashab's most enduring legacy. Its graduates became:

- The underground teachers who kept Judaism alive under Soviet rule
- The activists who rebuilt Chabad after the Holocaust
- The first shluchim (emissaries) sent to communities worldwide

The yeshiva survived the abandonment of Lubavitch (1915), Soviet persecution, the Holocaust, and multiple relocations. It was reestablished in:

- **Rostov** (1915–1920s)
- **Warsaw** (1930s)
- **Brooklyn, New York** (1940 — by the Rayatz upon arrival in America)
- **Lod and then Kfar Chabad, Israel** (1940s–1950s)
- Dozens of cities worldwide (ongoing)

Today, Tomchei Temimim yeshivos operate on every inhabited continent.
