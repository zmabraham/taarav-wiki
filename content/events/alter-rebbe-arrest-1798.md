# Alter Rebbe's First Arrest (1798) — Yud-Tet Kislev

**Date**: Tishrei 5559 (September/October 1798) — arrested; 19 Kislev 5559 (December 1798) — liberated  
**Location**: St. Petersburg, Russia

---

## Overview

The arrest and liberation of [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi]] (the Alter Rebbe) in 1798 is one of the founding events of Chabad history — establishing the pattern of Chabad Rebbes facing state persecution and emerging vindicated. The liberation date, **19 Kislev 5559**, became the primary Chabad holiday of _Yud-Tet Kislev_ ("the Rosh Hashanah of Chassidism") — celebrated annually with farbrengens, Torah study, and joy.

---

## Background

The Chassidic movement faced fierce opposition from the **Mitnagdim** (opponents), led by the [[entities/people/vilna-gaon|Vilna Gaon]] and his followers. The Mitnagdim viewed Chassidism as dangerous deviation from traditional Judaism — emphasizing emotional experience over scholarship, creating parallel communal structures, and spreading what the Gaon believed were theological errors.

The Gaon issued formal bans (_cherem_) against Chassidism in 1772 and 1781. After his death in 1797, his followers in Vilna continued the campaign. Some Mitnagdim, seeking to weaponize Russian state power, filed denunciations against Chassidic leaders.

The Alter Rebbe, as the leading Chassidic figure in Russia and one of the Maggid of Mezeritch's most distinguished disciples, was a natural target.

---

## The Arrest

In Tishrei 5559 (fall 1798), the Alter Rebbe was summoned to St. Petersburg and arrested by Russian imperial authorities. The charges centered on allegations about the Chassidic movement — particularly accusations that:

- Money collected for the Land of Israel (a standard Chassidic charitable practice) was being sent to Turkey, an enemy of Russia
- The movement had seditious or anti-imperial characteristics

The Alter Rebbe was transported to St. Petersburg and held in the Petropavlovsk Fortress during the investigation.

---

## Liberation: 19 Kislev

After approximately 53 days of imprisonment and investigation, the Alter Rebbe was fully exonerated and released on **19 Kislev 5559** — the date of the Maggid of Mezeritch's yahrzeit (death anniversary). The spiritual significance of the liberation date — coinciding with his teacher's death anniversary — was understood as profoundly meaningful in Chassidic tradition.

The investigation found no substance to the charges. The Alter Rebbe was released and returned to his community.

---

## Theological Significance

The Alter Rebbe interpreted his imprisonment and liberation as having cosmic spiritual significance — connected to the heavenly judgment over whether the revelation of Chassidic teachings would continue. His liberation was thus not merely personal vindication but divine confirmation of Chassidism's legitimacy.

The Alter Rebbe celebrated 19 Kislev as a day of divine revelation and spiritual renewal, and instructed his followers to do the same.

---

## Aftermath and Legacy

**Yud-Tet Kislev as Holiday**: The liberation date became the foremost Chabad holiday — the "Rosh Hashanah of Chassidism" — celebrated with:

- Farbrengens (Chassidic gatherings)
- Study of Chassidic texts
- Joy and thanksgiving
- Resolution to strengthen commitment to Chassidus

**Pattern Established**: The arrest-and-liberation narrative became the template for understanding subsequent Chabad persecution — including the [[events/arrest-mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe's arrest (1826)]] and the [[events/rayatz-arrest-1927|Rayatz's arrest (1927)]]. Each was understood through the lens of this founding event: persecution as heavenly test, liberation as divine vindication.
