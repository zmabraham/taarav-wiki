# Chaya Mushka's Wedding (1928)

**Date**: 14 Kislev 5689 (November 27, 1928)  
**Location**: Warsaw, Poland

---

## Overview

The wedding of [[entities/people/rebbetzin-moussia|Chaya Mushka Schneersohn]] — daughter of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] — to Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (the future [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]) on 14 Kislev 5689 (November 27, 1928) was one of the most celebrated events in Chabad history. It cemented a dynastic succession that would determine the course of the Chabad movement for decades, and it took place in Warsaw — the largest Jewish city in Europe — at a moment of relative stability before the catastrophe to come.

---

## Background: The Match

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (the Ramash — an acronym of his name) was a young man of extraordinary intellectual gifts, already recognized within the Chabad world as a potential leader. The Rayatz — who had no sons — recognized in his son-in-law the qualities that would make him the next Rebbe.

The match was thus not simply a family affair but a dynastic decision — the Rayatz was, in effect, preparing his successor.

---

## The Wedding

The wedding was held in Warsaw on 14 Kislev 5689 — a date noted as significant in Chabad history. Thousands attended from across the Jewish world.

The celebration was extraordinary — befitting both the Rayatz's standing as the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe and the significance of the match. Chabad Chassidim from across Europe gathered in Warsaw to participate.

---

## Immediate Aftermath

After the wedding, Rabbi Menachem Mendel and Chaya Mushka settled in Berlin, where he pursued studies at the University of Berlin. They later moved to Paris, where he studied at the École Spéciale des Travaux Publics.

The couple spent the years 1928–1941 in Europe, living through the Weimar Republic's collapse, the Nazi rise to power, and the fall of France — before escaping to America in June 1941.

---

## 14 Kislev in Chabad History

**14 Kislev** became a date celebrated in Chabad communities — the anniversary of the wedding that led to the seventh Rebbe's position. It falls in close proximity to 19 Kislev (the Alter Rebbe's liberation holiday) and 9-10 Kislev (the Mitteler Rebbe's liberation) — creating a cluster of celebrations in the month of Kislev.
