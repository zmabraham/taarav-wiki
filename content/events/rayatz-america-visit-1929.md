# Rayatz's First Visit to America (1929–1930)

**Date**: 1929–1930 (5690)  
**Location**: United States (and Eretz Yisrael en route)

---

## Overview

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] extended trip to Eretz Yisrael and the United States in 1929–1930 was his first encounter with American Jewry — a community that would become, a decade later, his permanent home and the new center of world Jewry. The visit was ostensibly for fundraising but was also a crucial assessment of American Jewish potential, mediated primarily through [[entities/people/yisrael-jacobson|Rabbi Yisrael Jacobson]], who organized and facilitated the visit.

---

## Purpose

The visit served multiple purposes:

1. **Fundraising**: American Jews were the primary potential donors for the Rayatz's underground activities in the Soviet Union and his rebuilding efforts in Riga
2. **Assessment**: What was the state of American Jewry? Could America become a center of Torah?
3. **Building infrastructure**: Establishing organizational relationships that would support future Chabad activities

---

## American Jewish Reality

What the Rayatz found in America in 1929–1930 was sobering:

- **75% of NY Jews** had not attended synagogue in the previous year
- **Reform and Conservative** movements were dominant
- The **Orthodox community** was small and largely defensive — focused on preservation rather than expansion
- The European immigrant generation was assimilating rapidly; their American-born children showed few signs of maintaining traditional observance

The Rayatz kept detailed notes. This trip prepared him psychologically and strategically for his eventual permanent immigration a decade later.

---

## The Small American Chabad Community

American Chabad was tiny and weak — a handful of communities of Eastern European immigrants. [[entities/people/yisrael-jacobson|Yisrael Jacobson]] was one of the key figures trying to maintain organizational structure through Agudas HaTmimim (graduates of Tomchei Temimim) and Agudas Chassidei Chabad.

---

## Strategic Assessment

Despite the difficulties, the Rayatz identified potential:

- American Jews had resources
- American freedom allowed religious life to be lived without persecution
- The challenge was not survival but transformation

His famous declaration upon permanent arrival in 1940 — "America iz nisht anderesh" (America is no different) — reflected conclusions he had begun forming in 1929–1930.
