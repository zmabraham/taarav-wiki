# Hebron Pogrom (1834)

**Date**: 1834 (5594)  
**Location**: Hebron, Eretz Yisrael (then Egyptian-occupied Palestine)

---

## Overview

In 1834, violent Arab unrest swept through Hebron — part of broader regional upheaval connected to the Egyptian occupation of Palestine under **Ibrahim Pasha** (son of Muhammad Ali). The Jewish community of Hebron, including the [[events/hebron-community-1823|Chabad community]] founded eleven years earlier, suffered devastating attacks.

---

## Historical Context: The Egyptian Occupation

The 1834 violence was not an isolated pogrom but part of a regional uprising against Egyptian rule. Ibrahim Pasha had conquered Palestine from the Ottomans in 1831–1833, and his modernizing, anti-traditionalist policies alienated the local Arab population. In 1834, a major Arab revolt broke out against Egyptian rule.

The revolt created conditions of general lawlessness in which Jewish communities — lacking both political power and physical defenses — were particularly vulnerable. Hebron's Jewish quarter bore the brunt of the local violence.

---

## Impact on the Chabad Community

The attacks on the Jewish community of Hebron were severe. The documentary account in [[summaries/toldot-chabad-eretz-yisrael|*Toldot Chabad b'Eretz HaKodesh*]] records that the community suffered grievously — deaths, injuries, looting, and destruction of property.

The Chabad community, which had been slowly building its presence since 1823, was particularly impacted by the loss of lives and institutional infrastructure.

---

## Recovery: Montefiore's Support

In the aftermath of the 1834 riots, the Jewish community of Hebron received crucial support from **Sir Moses Montefiore** — the prominent British Jewish philanthropist. His charitable intervention helped the community rebuild its physical infrastructure and resume communal life.

The recovery was also spiritual: the community persisted in Hebron despite the violence, reflecting the deep commitment to maintaining a Jewish presence in the city of the Patriarchs.

See also [[entities/people/moses-montefiore|Sir Moses Montefiore]].

---

## Legacy

The 1834 Hebron riots established a painful pattern that would repeat in [[events/hebron-riots-1929|1929]] — when far more devastating attacks would finally end the Chabad community's presence in Hebron. The 1834 experience demonstrated both the vulnerability of the isolated Jewish community and its capacity for recovery.
