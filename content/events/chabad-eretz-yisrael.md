# Chabad in Eretz Yisrael — Early Settlement

**From the First Aliyah of Chassidim (1777) to the Twentieth Century**

---

## Overview

Chabad's presence in the Land of Israel predates the Zionist movement by more than a century. Beginning with the earliest Chassidic immigration to Safed in 1777, Chabad built a continuous community structure — centered primarily in Hebron, with significant presences in Jerusalem, Safed, and later Tel Aviv. This history is documented comprehensively in [[summaries/toldot-chabad-eretz-yisrael|תולדות חב"ד בארץ הקודש]], which covers from 1777 to 1950 in 43 chapters.

---

## First Wave: Safed (1777)

The earliest Chassidic aliyah to the Land of Israel occurred in 1777, when a group of followers of the Maggid of Mezeritch (including Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk) immigrated to Safed (Tzfat). This was not yet a Chabad aliyah per se, as the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] had not yet established Chabad as a distinct movement.

However, this aliyah established the Chassidic presence in the Land and the _ma'amad_ (Palestine support fund) network that the Alter Rebbe later administered from Russia.

**The Alter Rebbe and the Palestine Fund**: The Alter Rebbe was appointed president (_nasi_) of the Kfar Yisrael fund (מעות ארץ ישראל) — responsible for collecting and distributing charitable funds from Russian Jewry to the communities in the Land.

---

## Hebron: The Chabad Settlement (c. 1820s)

The distinctly _Chabad_ settlement in the Land of Israel crystallized in Hebron (חברון), where the Chabad community maintained its central presence from the 1820s through the 20th century. Key milestones:

- **Kol Yisrael fund support**: The [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] provided crucial financial support for the Hebron community
- **Agreement with Sephardi community**: A formal agreement (_haskamah_) between the Chabad (Ashkenazi) community and the local Sephardi community established mutual support arrangements
- **Pogroms of 1834 (5594)**: The Hebron community suffered severely in Arab riots; envoy Rabbi Natan Amram led a fundraising mission to Europe and Russia
- **Tzemach Tzedek's support**: The [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] maintained active financial support for Hebron through the mid-19th century
- **Support of Moses Montefiore**: Sir Moses Montefiore (the British-Jewish philanthropist) visited Hebron and provided support, documented in chapter 17 of Toldot Chabad B'Eretz HaKodesh

---

## Kolel Chabad — The Institutional Structure

**Kolel Chabad** (כולל חב"ד) was the formal organization managing Chabad's communal affairs in the Land of Israel, including:

- Distribution of charitable funds from diaspora to Israel Chabad communities
- Management of community institutions
- Appointment of communal rabbis
- Coordination of fundraising emissaries (_shadarim_) sent to Russia and Europe

The structure of the Kolel — its officers, presidents, and administrators — is documented in chapters 35-43 of Toldot Chabad B'Eretz HaKodesh.

---

## Jerusalem

A Chabad community in Jerusalem grew alongside the Hebron community:

- Chabad synagogues in Jerusalem
- Chabad rabbis serving the Jerusalem community
- _Yeshivat Torat Emet_ (the Chabad yeshiva) established in Jerusalem
- Later, the yeshiva relocated to Hebron before relocating again to Jerusalem during the troubles of 1929

## Shadarim — Fundraising Emissaries

A recurring feature of Israeli Chabad history was the _shadar_ (שד"ר — _shliach derabanon_, rabbinic emissary) — a rabbi sent from the Land of Israel community to raise funds in Russia and Europe. These missions were sometimes dangerous and always demanding, requiring months or years of travel. The book records many individual shadarim with their dates and fundraising territories.

---

## 20th Century and Kfar Chabad

After the Holocaust and the founding of the State of Israel, the Rayatz established **Kfar Chabad** (Village of Chabad) in 1949 as the central Israeli Chabad settlement. Many survivors of the Soviet underground settled there, bringing their spirit of self-sacrifice.
