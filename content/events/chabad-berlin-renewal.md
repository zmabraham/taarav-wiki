# התחדשות חב"ד בברלין

## Chabad Berlin Renewal (1996–)

**Date**: From 1996  
**Location**: [[entities/places/berlin|Berlin]], Germany  
**Leaders**: [[entities/people/yehuda-teichtel|Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel]] and [[entities/people/shmuel-segel|Rabbi Shmuel Segel]]  
**Significance**: Post-Holocaust reestablishment of Jewish life in Germany's capital; fulfillment of the seventh Rebbe's vision

---

## Overview

Beginning in **1996**, Chabad established a major institutional presence in Berlin, Germany — one of the most symbolically charged locations in modern Jewish history. Against all historical expectations, the movement whose leaders had been persecuted by both Czarist and Soviet regimes, and whose followers were murdered en masse in the Holocaust, chose to plant a flag in the capital of the country that had organized that Holocaust.

---

## Background: The Seventh Rebbe's Vision

The _Chabad Germany_ volume documents that the seventh Rebbe ([[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson]]) expressed his vision for Germany's future in a remarkable statement made to a Berlin Jewish activist in **1989**, around the time of the Berlin Wall's fall:

_"Es vet nisht blaybn af lang"_ — "It won't last long."

He also instructed that Jewish activity must continue in **both** East and West Germany — a position that anticipated reunification and the long-term possibility of Jewish renewal in the country.

---

## Institutions Established

[[entities/people/yehuda-teichtel|Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel]] (arriving 1996) and [[entities/people/shmuel-segel|Rabbi Shmuel Segel]] established:

**Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin**: A major Chabad center in central Berlin, funded by **local Jewish donors** (itself remarkable — Jews self-funding their own institutions in post-war Germany).

**Synagogue**: Seating 250 worshippers — a substantial congregation for Berlin's small Jewish community.

**Yudishe Traditsyone Shule Ohr Avner**: A Jewish day school, housed in a building that had previously served as a **Nazi officers' club** — one of the most dramatic physical reclamations in Jewish history.

**Community Services**: A Jewish restaurant, mikveh (ritual bath), library, and youth center.

**Shabbat Services**: Grew from approximately 20 attendees to hundreds over the years.

---

## Angela Merkel's Acknowledgment

German Chancellor **Angela Merkel** acknowledged the significance of the Chabad renewal in Berlin, specifically noting that Jewish donors self-funding their own institutions demonstrated genuine Jewish confidence in Germany's future — something qualitatively different from government-sponsored Jewish restoration projects.

---

## Symbolic Dimensions

The Berlin renewal is the culmination of the deepest ironies in Chabad history:

- The Rashab called Kaiser Wilhelm II "Amalek" from nearby Würzburg
- The Rayatz fled Berlin in 1933 as Hitler rose to power
- The seventh Rebbe studied in Berlin (1928–1931) before the city became the Nazi capital
- Berlin's Jews were almost entirely murdered in the Holocaust
- And yet: Chabad established one of its most visible European centers in this very city

The school built in a former Nazi officers' club encapsulates this reversal — the physical space of Jewish persecution transformed into a place of Jewish education.
