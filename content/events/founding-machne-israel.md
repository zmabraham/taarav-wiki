# Founding of Machne Israel (1941)

**Date**: 1941 (5701)  
**Location**: Brooklyn, New York  
**Key Figures**: [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]

---

## Overview

**Machne Israel** (מחנה ישראל — "Camp of Israel") was founded by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in **1941** as the **social welfare and humanitarian arm** of Chabad-Lubavitch. While [[events/founding-merkos|Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch]] focused on education and [[events/founding-kehot|Kehot]] focused on publishing, Machne Israel focused on **material support, humanitarian aid, and community services**. Its founding reflected the Rayatz's understanding that spiritual work must be accompanied by concern for physical needs — a fundamental Chabad principle.

---

## Background: The Humanitarian Crisis

### Wartime Conditions (1939–1945)

Machne Israel was founded during World War II, a period of unprecedented crisis for world Jewry:

**The Holocaust**:

- By 1941, the Nazi genocide was underway in Europe
- Millions of Jews were displaced, imprisoned, or murdered
- Refugee flows overwhelmed existing support systems

**Refugee Crisis**:

- Jews fleeing Europe reached America in desperate conditions
- They needed housing, food, clothing, and employment
- Existing Jewish social services were overwhelmed

**American Jewish Poverty**:

- Not all suffering was overseas
- Many American Jews lived in poverty, especially older immigrants
- The Great Depression's effects were still felt

### Chabad's Philosophy: Material and Spiritual

Chabad has always taught that **material and spiritual needs are intertwined**:

**The Tanya's Teaching**:

- The [[entities/publications/tanya|Tanya]] (the foundational Chabad text) teaches that loving God includes caring for His children
- Physical support enables spiritual practice
- A hungry person cannot focus on Torah study

**Historical Precedent**:

- Previous Rebbes had always been involved in communal welfare
- The Rashab and Rayatz in Russia had maintained charitable funds
- The Alter Rebbe had taught that "a small hole in the body is a big hole in the soul"

## The Founding: 1941

### Organizational Structure

The Rayatz established Machne Israel as:

**Separate Legal Entity**:

- Distinct from Merkos and Kehot, though coordinated
- Specifically focused on humanitarian work
- Able to receive tax-deductible donations for charitable purposes

**Mission**:

- Provide material support to those in need
- Assist refugees and immigrants
- Support community institutions
- Respond to crises and emergencies

**Leadership**:

- The Rayatz served as president and guiding force
- Professional social workers and administrators handled day-to-day operations
- Volunteers from Chabad communities provided hands-on assistance

---

## Wartime Activities (1941–1945)

### Refugee Assistance

During World War II, Machne Israel focused heavily on **refugee relief**:

**European Refugees**:

- Jews who managed to escape Europe arrived in America with nothing
- Machne Israel provided immediate assistance — food, clothing, shelter
- Helped refugees navigate American systems (immigration, employment, housing)

**Displaced Persons in Europe**:

- Even after the war ended, millions remained in DP camps
- Machne Israel sent aid packages and funds
- Coordinated with relief organizations on the ground

**Family Reunification**:

- Helped locate separated family members
- Assisted with correspondence across war zones
- Sponsored immigration visas when possible

### Support for Chabad Communities

**Existing Communities**:

- Chabad communities in America were struggling financially
- Machne Israel provided funds for synagogues, mikvaos (ritual baths), and community institutions
- Supported families in distress

**New Institutions**:

- As Chabad established new presence in America, institutions needed funding
- Machne Israel helped finance construction and operations
- This included schools, synagogues, and community centers

### Wartime Communications

**Maintaining Contact**:

- During the war, communication with European Jewry was nearly impossible
- Machne Israel attempted whatever channels were available
- Postcards, Red Cross messages, neutral countries' diplomatic channels

**Support from America**:

- American Jews wanted to help their brethren in Europe
- Machne Israel organized fundraising and aid shipments
- Even when delivery was uncertain, the effort mattered symbolically

## Postwar Activities (1945–1950)

### Holocaust Survivors

**She'erit Hapletah** (the surviving remnant) — the Jews who survived the Holocaust — needed enormous support:

**DP Camp Work**:

- Machne Israel worked in displaced persons camps in Germany, Austria, and Italy
- Sent religious materials, kosher food, and financial support
- Helped survivors rebuild religious life

See [[events/chabad-in-displaced-persons-camps|Chabad in Displaced Persons Camps]].

**Immigration Assistance**:

- Helped survivors navigate immigration to Palestine, America, or elsewhere
- Provided travel funds and documentation assistance
- Connected survivors with Jewish communities in destination countries

### Supporting the Great Escape

The **Great Escape** of 1946–1947 — the organized exodus of Chabad chassidim from the Soviet Union — required material support:

**Funding**:

- The escape operation required substantial funds
- Bribes for border guards, travel costs, support en route
- Machne Israel helped raise and transmit these funds

**Reception**:

- Refugees arriving in Western Europe needed immediate assistance
- Machne Israel provided support through representatives in France, Germany, and elsewhere

See [[events/great-escape|The Great Escape (1946–1947)]].

## Expansion Under the Seventh Rebbe

### Post-1950 Growth

When the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] assumed leadership in 1951, Machne Israel's activities expanded dramatically:

**Global Humanitarian Work**:

- Support for Chabad communities worldwide
- Emergency relief for Jewish communities in crisis
- Material support for shluchim (emissaries) establishing new communities

**Social Services**:

- Assistance for the elderly
- Support for families in need
- Hospital and prison chaplaincy services
- Counseling and referral services

**Crisis Response**:

- Responses to wars, natural disasters, and humanitarian crises affecting Jews
- Support for Jews in distress in any location

## Machne Israel's Ongoing Work

### Contemporary Activities

Today, Machne Israel continues its humanitarian mission:

**Community Services**:

- Food distribution programs
- Financial assistance for families in crisis
- Support for the elderly and homebound
- Holiday food and gift programs

**International Aid**:

- Supporting Jewish communities in poverty or crisis
- Emergency response to humanitarian disasters
- Material support for Chabad institutions globally

**Partnerships**:

- Collaboration with other Jewish relief organizations
- Government partnerships for social services
- Interfaith humanitarian cooperation

## Relationship to Other Chabad Organizations

Machne Israel works alongside other Chabad institutions:

**Agudas Chassidei Chabad**:

- The umbrella organization of Chabad
- Coordinates overall Chabad activities
- Machne Israel is its humanitarian arm

**Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch**:

- Educational work
- Machne Israel provides material support that enables Merkos's educational work

**Kehot Publication Society**:

- Publishing
- Machne Israel's humanitarian work complements Kehot's intellectual/spiritual work

## Historical Significance

The founding of Machne Israel in 1941 reflected several important principles:

**Holistic Approach**:

- Chabad addresses both material and spiritual needs
- Physical suffering is addressed alongside spiritual concerns
- The whole person is served

**Institutional Infrastructure**:

- Permanent institutions are necessary for sustained humanitarian work
- Machne Israel provides the infrastructure for ongoing social services
- This enables Chabad to respond effectively to crises

**Global Responsibility**:

- Machne Israel's work is not limited to Chabad communities
- All Jews in need are eligible for assistance
- This reflects a universalist ethic within a particularist framework

**Continuity of Tradition**:

- Machne Israel continues a Chabad tradition of communal welfare
- Previous generations of Rebbes were similarly involved in relief work
- The organization institutionalizes this tradition
