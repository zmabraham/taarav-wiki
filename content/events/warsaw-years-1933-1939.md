# Warsaw and Poland Years (1933–1939)

**Date**: 1933–September 1, 1939  
**Location**: Warsaw → Otwock, Poland; with extensive travel

---

## Overview

After six years in Riga, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] relocated to Poland in 1933 to be closer to the Tomchei Temimim branches in Warsaw and across Poland and Lithuania. The six years in Poland (1933–1939) were a period of intensive institutional rebuilding — creating the largest Chabad yeshiva network in history — before the Nazi invasion of September 1, 1939 catastrophically ended it.

---

## The Move to Poland

The Rayatz relocated to **Warsaw** in 1933. Warsaw was the largest Jewish city in Europe — approximately 350,000 Jews, representing one of the most vibrant Jewish communities in history.

From Warsaw, the Rayatz could:

- Oversee the extensive Tomchei Temimim network across Poland and Lithuania
- Be closer to the major European Jewish population centers
- Access better medical facilities (his multiple sclerosis was advancing)

---

## Residence in Otwock (1936–1939)

After several years in Warsaw, the Rayatz relocated to **Otwock** — a Warsaw suburb with a sanatorium character (its forests and cleaner air were attractive for those with health needs). Otwock became the practical center of Chabad operations in the late 1930s.

---

## The Yeshiva Network

The Polish period saw the Tomchei Temimim network reach its greatest extent:

- Main center at Warsaw/Otwock
- Branches throughout Poland, Lithuania, and Latvia
- Faculty (_mashpiim_, _menahel_/directors) who were senior Chabad figures
- Hundreds of students — the future backbone of postwar Chabad

The [[summaries/toldot-chabad-poland|Chabad history of Poland, Lithuania, Latvia]] — the largest volume in the series — documents this network in extraordinary detail, preserving names, institutions, and stories that would otherwise be lost.

---

## Sheina's Wedding

During the Polish period, the Rayatz's daughter Sheina married Rabbi Menachem Mendel Wolff. The wedding was held in Landwarow, Poland. Sheina and her husband would be killed in the Holocaust — unable to escape the Nazi advance.

---

## The Nazi Invasion (September 1, 1939)

When Nazi Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939, the Rayatz was in Warsaw. The city fell rapidly into Nazi hands. The Jewish community — the largest in Europe — faced immediate existential threat.

---

## Escape from Warsaw

The Rayatz was evacuated through extraordinary diplomatic intervention. The operation involved:

- American government officials (including Senator James Byrnes)
- Chabad supporters with government connections
- Negotiations for exit permits through Nazi-controlled territory

The escape route went through Riga and then onward to the United States.

See [[events/escape-from-warsaw-1939|Escape from Warsaw (1939)]].

---

## The Yeshiva Students Left Behind

The tragedy of the Polish years is that most of the students and faculty of the Tomchei Temimim network could not escape. The [[events/holocaust-chabad|Holocaust]] annihilated these communities — the Warsaw ghetto, the mass shootings in Lithuania, the death camps across Poland. The 15,586 lines of _Toldot Chabad b'Polin, Lita V'Latvia_ preserve what memory could recover.
