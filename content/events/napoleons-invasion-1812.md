# Napoleon's Invasion of Russia and the Alter Rebbe's Death (1812)

**Date**: June 1812 — Napoleon invades; 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812) — Alter Rebbe dies  
**Location**: Liadi → Shneyderova (village near Kursk), Russia

---

## Overview

Napoleon Bonaparte's invasion of Russia in June 1812 forced [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi]] (the Alter Rebbe) to flee his home of Liadi with thousands of his followers. The flight proved fatal — the Alter Rebbe died en route on 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812) at the village of Shneyderova (or Piena, near Haditch/Hadich, Ukraine), while Russian forces pursued the retreating French army through the frozen winter landscape.

---

## The Alter Rebbe's Stance on Napoleon

The Alter Rebbe's position on Napoleon was clear and politically courageous: he supported Russia against Napoleon, despite the fact that Napoleon offered far more civil freedoms to Jews than the Czarist regime.

His reasoning, as preserved in Chabad tradition, was theological and communal:

- Napoleon's liberal ideology threatened to undermine Jewish separateness and Torah observance — assimilation and secularization would follow civil emancipation
- Russian autocracy, despite its hostility to Jews, paradoxically maintained the conditions (poverty, discrimination) that preserved Jewish distinctiveness and religious intensity
- The deeper danger to Judaism was spiritual erosion, not political oppression

The Alter Rebbe is said to have stated: if Napoleon wins, the Jews will grow wealthy but their hearts will grow distant from their Father in Heaven; if Alexander wins, the Jews will be poor but their hearts will be close to their Father in Heaven.

This theological-political stance made the Alter Rebbe a supporter of Russia in the war — leading to his need to flee when Napoleon's forces advanced.

---

## The Flight from Liadi

When Napoleon's Grande Armée invaded Russia in June 1812 and advanced rapidly, the Alter Rebbe organized the evacuation of his court and community from Liadi (his residence since his release from Lubavitch after his second arrest). He set out eastward with thousands of followers.

The journey was grueling — through the chaos of a country at war, in difficult terrain, with the elderly Alter Rebbe in poor health. His disciples, students, and followers formed a massive caravan.

---

## Death

The Alter Rebbe died on **24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812)** at the village of Shneyderova (near Haditch/Hadich), while still in flight. He was 67 years old.

He was buried in Haditch (Hadich), Ukraine, where his grave (_ohel_) remains a pilgrimage site.

---

## Succession

The [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (Rabbi DovBer Schneersohn), the Alter Rebbe's son, assumed leadership of Chabad after his father's death. In 1813, he established [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] as the new world center of the movement — beginning Chabad's 102-year association with that town.

See [[events/chabad-in-lubavitch-1813|Chabad Establishes Headquarters in Lubavitch (1813)]].

---

## Legacy

The Alter Rebbe's death in flight represented both a personal tragedy and a pivotal moment in Chabad history. His 24 Tevet yahrzeit is observed annually in Chabad communities with Torah study and farbrengens.

His major works — including the _Tanya_ (the foundational Chabad philosophical text), the _Shulchan Aruch HaRav_ (his legal code), and his collections of Chassidic discourses — remained the living legacy through which his teachings continue.
