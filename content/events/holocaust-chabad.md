# Chabad and the Holocaust

**שואה — The Destruction of European Chabad (1939–1945)**

---

## Overview

The Holocaust was the most catastrophic single event in Chabad history, destroying the communities that had been the movement's heartland for two centuries. Poland, Lithuania, Ukraine, and Russia — the regions where Chabad had its deepest roots — were systematically annihilated. Chabad's response was simultaneously to mourn, to rescue where possible, and to rebuild in America and Eretz Yisrael.

---

## Scale of Destruction

### Lubavitch Itself

**November 1941:** The Nazis murdered all **483 remaining Jews of Lubavitch** — the town that had been Chabad's world center for 102 years (1813–1915). The entire Jewish population was killed with virtually no survivors. See [[summaries/lubavitch|ליובאוויטש]].

### Poland and Lithuania

The Chabad communities of Warsaw, Vilna, Lublin, and hundreds of smaller towns were annihilated. The Rayatz's daughter **Sheina** and her husband were among the victims.

### Soviet Territory

Jewish communities across Ukraine, Belarus, and Western Russia — including cities where Tomchei Temimim had branches and where underground Chabad networks had operated — were destroyed.

---

## The Rayatz's Response

Operating from Brooklyn, the Rayatz pursued every possible rescue avenue:

### The Shanghai Escape Route (1940–1941)

When Polish-Lithuanian yeshiva students realized Dutch consul **Jan Zwartendyk** would stamp "no visa required to Curacao" and Japanese consul **Chiune Sugihara** would issue transit visas, the Rayatz coordinated:

- US Embassy negotiations for final visas
- Funding ($500 per student for Russian transit fees)
- Logistics for the Vilna → Moscow → Trans-Siberian → Vladivostok → Kobe → Shanghai route
- Approximately 40 Chabad students survived the war in Shanghai under **Rabbi Meir Ashkenazi** (a Chabad chassid, Ashkenazi chief rabbi of Shanghai)

### Diplomatic Pressure

The Rayatz used every contact — including US senators and diplomatic channels — to rescue individuals and communities.

### The Great Escape (1946–47)

After the war, the Rayatz orchestrated the escape of approximately **1,000 Chabad chassidim** from the Soviet Union using forged Polish passports, before the Iron Curtain fully descended. See [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] for details.

---

## Personal Loss

The Rayatz wept uncontrollably after the war when shown photographs of fallen chassidim — including Rabbis Yechezkel Feigin and Shlomo Horowitz, close confidants killed by the Nazis. The personal dimension of loss was inseparable from the communal.

---

## Theological Response

Chabad did not offer simple theodicy in response to the Holocaust. The Rayatz's own position was one of mourning combined with urgent rebuilding: "We must now redouble our efforts." The seventh Rebbe later developed a more systematic theological engagement with the Holocaust's meaning within Jewish history.

---

## Legacy

The Holocaust's destruction of Eastern European Chabad paradoxically enabled its global transformation: the scattered survivors — sent by the Rayatz to France, Australia, Canada, South Africa, Cuba, Sweden, and Israel — became the seed communities of what would become a worldwide movement under the seventh Rebbe.
