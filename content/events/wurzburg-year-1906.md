# Würzburg Year (1906–1907)

**Date**: Cheshvan 5667 (November 1906) — Shavuot 5667 (1907)  
**Location**: Würzburg, Bavaria, Germany

---

## Overview

The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab's]] year-long stay in Würzburg, Bavaria (1906–1907) was one of the most productive periods in Chabad intellectual history. During this year, he composed the famous "Hemshech Samech Vov" — one of the most profound and lengthy Chassidic philosophical discourses in the entire Chabad canon — along with conducting extensive diplomatic work with German Orthodox leaders, and a remarkable act of appropriating imperial space for spiritual purposes.

---

## Background: Why Würzburg?

The Rashab's departure from Lubavitch for Germany was precipitated by:

1. **Medical reasons**: The Rashab suffered from respiratory ailments and sought treatment and healthier air
2. **Safety concerns**: Zionist workers had attacked the Rashab's household in Lubavitch — including the same period as the [[events/poalei-tzion-confrontation-1906|Poalei Tzion confrontation with Tomchei Temimim]] — making Lubavitch temporarily unsafe
3. **Diplomatic opportunities**: Germany offered access to Orthodox Jewish leaders and to Western Jewish political networks

The Rashab arrived in Würzburg in Cheshvan 5667 (November 1906). The Rayatz joined him in Kislev (December 1906).

---

## Hemshech Samech Vov (5666)

The most intellectually significant product of the Würzburg year was the **Hemshech Samech Vov** (המשך תרס"ו) — a connected series of Chassidic discourses begun before the journey and completed (or largely composed) in Würzburg.

The Samech Vov is considered:

- One of the most profound Chassidic philosophical works ever produced
- An exploration of the most fundamental questions of Kabbalistic theology: the nature of the Infinite (Ein Sof), the relationship between divine light and vessels, and the purpose of creation
- A work requiring extraordinary intellectual preparation to study

Its composition in Würzburg — in a Bavarian city, far from the Russian heartland of Chassidism — added to its mystique.

---

## The Kaiser's Stationery

One of the most memorable episodes of the Würzburg year: the Rashab wanted to visit Kaiser Wilhelm's writing room in a nearby palace. The Rayatz bribed a guard to allow entry. The Rashab sat at the Kaiser's desk, used the Kaiser's pen, and on the Kaiser's personal stationery — wrote a **Chassidic discourse** (_maamar_).

This act of appropriating imperial space for spiritual purposes was characteristic of the Rashab's personality: he inhabited the world of secular power without being intimidated by it, and could transform even the German Emperor's writing materials into a vehicle for divine wisdom.

---

## German Orthodox Collaboration

During the Würzburg year, the Rashab engaged intensively with German Orthodox leadership:

**The Histaadus HaYereim Plan**: Together with [[entities/people/shlomo-zalman-breuer|Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Breuer]] (Frankfurt) and [[entities/people/yaakov-rosenheim|Rabbi Yaakov Rosenheim]] (Agudas Yisrael president), the Rashab drafted a detailed organizational plan for strengthening Orthodox Judaism in Germany — called _"HiTaadus HaYereim"_ ("Association of the God-Fearing").

This collaboration bridged Eastern European Chassidism and Western academic Orthodoxy — finding common ground in the need to strengthen Torah observance against Reform Judaism and secularization.

**Yud-Tes Kislev Farbrengen**: On the Chabad holiday of 19 Kislev, the Rashab held a _farbrengen_ in Würzburg attended by German Jews who were deeply impressed by his prayer style — which lasted over two hours. The Rashab's intense, unhurried prayer left a lasting impression on Western observers more accustomed to shorter services.

---

## The Kaiser Prophecy

The Rashab's attitude toward Kaiser Wilhelm II was intensely negative: he called him "**Amalek**" and coined the Yiddish pun "**vill-he-lem**" (meaning "wants to destroy the world" — playing on Wilhelm). He prophesied Germany's ultimate defeat.

Later (during WWI), he would tell his son the Rayatz: "Der Deitsh vet einnemen Rusland, er vet ihr shlingen, un mit ihr vet er zikh dervergn" — "The German will conquer Russia, he will swallow it, and it will choke him." This proved prophetically accurate.

---

## Return to Lubavitch

The Rashab returned to Lubavitch after Shavuot 5667 (1907), leaving Germany with the Hemshech completed, the German Orthodox connections established, and his health somewhat restored.
