# Chabad Riga Yeshiva Period (1927–1933)

**The flourishing of Tomchei Temimim yeshiva and Chabad institutions in Riga during the Rayatz's residence there following his expulsion from the Soviet Union**

## Background

After the Rayatz (6th Rebbe) was arrested in 1927 and eventually exiled from the Soviet Union, he settled in Riga, Latvia (September 1927). Latvia was an independent republic with a significant Jewish community and relatively favorable conditions for Jewish religious life — a stark contrast to the Soviet Union he had left.

## The Riga Period (1927–1933)

During the six years in Riga, the Rayatz rebuilt Chabad's institutional infrastructure outside the Soviet Union:

### Tomchei Temimim Riga

The Tomchei Temimim yeshiva was reestablished as a central institution in Riga. Key educational figures included:

- **R' Eliyahu Chaim Althaus** — mashpia, responsible for the Chassidic spiritual development of students
- **R' Moshe Chefetz** — additional mashpia

Students came from throughout the diaspora — including those who had escaped the Soviet Union — to study at the Riga yeshiva.

### Publishing and Administrative Activity

The Rayatz used his years in Riga to organize Chabad's literary and administrative activities, including correspondence with Chassidim worldwide and the continuation of publishing projects.

### Broader Network

Riga served as the hub for Chabad's network across Eastern Europe. From Riga, the Rayatz directed activities in Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, and maintained connections with underground Chabad in the Soviet Union.

## Transition to Warsaw (1933)

In 1933, the Rayatz moved to Warsaw, Poland, becoming the center of Chabad's European presence there through 1939.

## Significance

The Riga period was a critical rebuilding phase for Chabad after the traumatic years of Soviet persecution. The yeshiva established there, with its dedicated mashpi'im, produced a generation of Chabad leaders who later spread throughout the world. Riga became a way-station for Chabad's transition from Eastern Europe to the West.
