# Founding of Agudas Chassidei Chabad in Eretz Yisrael (1941)

**Establishment of the official organizational body representing the Chabad community in British Mandatory Palestine; key step in institutionalizing Chabad presence in the Land of Israel**

## Background

By 1941, the Chabad community in Eretz Yisrael had grown substantially through immigration from Eastern Europe, particularly from the former Russian Empire. Chabad communities existed in Jerusalem, Hebron, Tzfat (Safed), Tel Aviv, and other cities. However, the community lacked a single unified organizational structure.

The Rayatz (6th Rebbe) had moved to New York in 1940 after escaping Nazi-occupied Poland. From Brooklyn, he directed Chabad activities worldwide, including in Eretz Yisrael. The establishment of a formal organization in Eretz Yisrael would create a clearer channel for coordinating with the Rayatz's central leadership.

The year 1941 was particularly fraught — World War II was devastating European Jewry, and the British Mandate was facing threats on multiple fronts. The immigration of refugees who needed absorption, along with the desire to maintain institutional Chabad identity, motivated the organizational step.

## What Happened

In 1941, leading figures of the Chabad community in Eretz Yisrael came together and formally established Agudas Chassidei Chabad (Association of Chabad Chassidim) in the Land of Israel. The founding committee included:

- **R' Shaul Dov Zislin**
- **R' Moshe Gourarie**
- **R' Chaim Yosef Rozenblum**

These three figures, along with other community members, created the organizational charter, elected leadership, and established the formal structure for representing Chabad interests in the Yishuv (the pre-state Jewish community).

## Significance

The founding of Agudas Chassidei Chabad in Eretz Yisrael in 1941 represents an important step in the institutionalization of Chabad outside Eastern Europe. Unlike the older Chabad communities in Hebron and Jerusalem that had existed since the 18th century, this organization was a modern institutional framework designed to coordinate activities across the entire Yishuv. It provided infrastructure for:

- Receiving and absorbing refugees
- Maintaining kashrut and religious services
- Coordinating with the Rayatz in Brooklyn
- Representing Chabad's interests in Yishuv politics

The organization became the predecessor to the modern Chabad institutions in Israel.
