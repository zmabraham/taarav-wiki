# Passover Matzah Campaign (1929)

**Date**: Pre-Passover 5689 (spring 1929)  
**Location**: Riga (coordination) → Soviet Union (delivery)

---

## Overview

The 1929 Passover Matzah Campaign was an extraordinary international diplomatic and humanitarian operation organized by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] from Riga — shipping matzah into the Soviet Union for Jews who could not obtain it domestically, during the height of Stalinist collectivization and famine. The operation required diplomatic negotiations at multiple levels and demonstrated Chabad's capacity for sophisticated organizational response to the crisis of Soviet Jewry.

---

## Background: Soviet Jews Without Matzah

By 1929, the Soviet campaign against Jewish religious practice had made it essentially impossible for most Soviet Jews to obtain matzah for Passover:

- Jewish communal baking of matzah had been outlawed or severely restricted
- The _Yevsektsiya_ (_Chag Ha-Pesach_ — the Passover holiday campaign) actively targeted Passover observance
- The broader context of Stalinist collectivization and famine meant that food of any kind was scarce
- Soviet Jews were isolated from world Jewry with no obvious channel for receiving religious goods

For Jews committed to Passover observance — including the many who maintained at least minimal Jewish practice even under Soviet pressure — the lack of matzah was a spiritual crisis.

---

## The Operation

From Riga, the Rayatz organized a multi-pronged effort:

**Diplomatic Negotiations**: [[entities/people/mordechai-dubin|Rabbi Mordechai Dubin]] — Latvia's Parliamentarian and a devoted Chasid — negotiated directly with the Soviet ambassador in Riga. Using his standing as a member of the Latvian parliament, Dubin pressed for permission to ship matzah as a humanitarian measure.

**European Jewish Community Coordination**: The Rayatz coordinated with:

- Germany's Jewish community in Berlin
- Chief rabbis across Europe
- The Vaad Magen UMekhaseh (Committee for Protection of Russian Jewry) that the Rayatz had established from Riga

**Fundraising**: Significant funds were required to purchase, bake, pack, and ship matzah — and to cover the administrative and diplomatic costs of the operation.

**Logistics**: The actual shipment of matzah into Soviet territory required careful coordination, given that the Soviet border was not normally permeable for religious goods.

---

## Significance

The matzah campaign represents a remarkable intersection of:

- **Religious necessity** — Passover observance as an existential Jewish priority
- **Cold War-era diplomacy** — engaging Soviet authorities through legitimate diplomatic channels
- **Chabad organizational creativity** — mobilizing resources across multiple countries for a specific, time-sensitive mission

The campaign succeeded. Matzah was shipped into the Soviet Union — reaching Jews who would otherwise have gone without for Passover.

---

## Broader Pattern

The 1929 matzah campaign was one of many operations the Rayatz organized from Riga to support Soviet Jewry. It demonstrated that even from exile, with limited resources, a determined leadership could create meaningful lifelines for trapped communities.
