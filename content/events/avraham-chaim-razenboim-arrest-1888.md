# Arrest of R' Avraham Chaim Razenboim (1888–1890)

**Imprisonment and subsequent exile of the leading Chabad Chassid of Pleshchenitsy on charges related to forging exemption documents for Jewish military draftees**

## Background

R' Avraham Chaim Razenboim was the leading figure in the Chabad community of Pleshchenitsy. He served as the de facto head of the local Chassidic circle after R' Hendl Kugel moved to Lubavitch. He was a man of considerable learning, multilingual ability, and public standing.

In the 1880s, his uncle served in a local administrative capacity (uprava — local government office). The uncle's Jewish assistant, R' Chaik Rappaport, used the official seal to issue false exemption documents for young Jewish men who faced conscription into the Russian Imperial Army. Military service was dreaded by observant Jews — soldiers were often forced to violate Shabbat and kashrut, and service lasted 25 years under Nicholas I (reduced later, but still onerous). The false documents stated that these young men were "single sons" with no brothers, which entitled them to exemption from service.

## What Happened

The fraudulent documents were eventually discovered by authorities. Razenboim's uncle, as the official in whose name the documents were signed, was arrested. R' Avraham Chaim himself was also arrested, as he had apparently signed some of these documents as well — writing in his own arrest letter: "I was sentenced only for my signature on what someone else wrote."

He was held in Borisov for approximately a year awaiting trial, then transferred to Smolensk (1888 to summer 1890) where he served his sentence in a labor brigade (Rostansky rota). He maintained correspondence with R' Hendl Kugel and other Chassidim during his imprisonment, and some of his remarkable letters on Chassidic philosophy were written from prison.

After release, he faced additional legal proceedings and fled Russia (c. 1892–93), going to Frankfurt, then Amsterdam (business partnership with Chabad Chassid R' Abba Persson), and finally to America.

## Significance

This case illuminates multiple dimensions of Jewish life in the late Czarist period:

1. **Military exemption as a moral issue** — The network of forged documents was motivated by the desire to save Jewish young men from the spiritually destructive experience of military service; many in the community viewed this as a pious act
2. **Cost of leadership** — Razenboim paid a severe personal price for his community involvement
3. **Resilience of Chassidic community** — Despite the absence of their leader, the Pleshchenitsy community maintained its Chassidic character under R' Reuven Liberman
4. **Connection to diaspora** — Razenboim's subsequent life in Western Europe and America illustrates the broader pattern of Russian Jewish emigration in the late 19th century
