# Alter Rebbe's Death in Pyena

## The Final Flight and Passing (1812)

**Date:** 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812)
**Location:** Pyena (also spelled Piena), a village in present-day Ukraine
**Key figure:** [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|R' Schneur Zalman of Liadi]] (Alter Rebbe, aged 67)
**Context:** Fleeing Napoleon's invading army
**Significance:** The passing of Chabad's founder; the beginning of the succession to the Mitteler Rebbe

---

## Overview

On **24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812)**, the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] (R' Schneur Zalman of Liadi) died in the village of **Pyena** (Piena) while fleeing eastward to escape **Napoleon Bonaparte's** invading army. He was **67 years old** and had been leading Chabad for approximately 37 years. His death during this desperate flight marked the end of an era and the beginning of a new chapter in Chabad history under the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]].

---

## Historical Context

### Napoleon's Invasion of Russia (1812)

In **June 1812**, Napoleon Bonaparte invaded Russia with over **600,000 soldiers**:

- The **largest invading force** Europe had yet seen
- Aimed at **punishing Russia** for defying the Continental System
- Expected a **quick victory** over the seemingly backward Russian army

### The Alter Rebbe's Stance

The Alter Rebbe had taken the controversial position of **supporting Russia against Napoleon**:

- Believing that French victory would bring **spiritual harm** to Jews
- Arguing that **material freedom** would lead to assimilation
- Supporting the **Russian war effort** actively
- **Refusing to welcome** Napoleon as a liberator

This stance placed him and his followers in danger as French forces advanced.

---

## The Flight from Liadi

### Decision to Flee

As French forces approached **Liadi** (the Alter Rebbe's court), he:

- **Organized a mass evacuation** of Chabad chassidim
- **Refused to remain** under French occupation
- **Chose to flee eastward** away from the front lines
- **Led the community personally** despite his age and frail health

### The Journey

The evacuation was grueling:

- **Winter conditions** — snow, ice, extreme cold
- **Constant danger** from advancing armies and roaming bands
- **Limited food and shelter** for refugees
- The Alter Rebbe's **failing health** adding urgency

### The Community

The flight included:

- **Hundreds of chassidim** following their Rebbe
- **Families** with children and elderly
- **Communal property** and sacred objects
- **Few provisions** for the difficult journey

---

## Final Days

### Arrival in Pyena

The fleeing community reached the village of **Pyena** (Piena), where:

- The Alter Rebbe's **health deteriorated rapidly**
- The strain of the journey proved too much
- **Death approached** despite the community's prayers

### Final Teachings

According to tradition, in his final days the Alter Rebbe:

- Continued to **teach and guide** his followers
- Spoke about the **future of Chabad**
- Encouraged his followers to **remain strong**
- Did not explicitly designate a successor but implied who should follow

### Last Words

The Alter Rebbe's final words are reported to have been:

> "I am going to heaven, but my feet remain with you."

This statement conveyed:

- His **continuing connection** to the community
- His **spiritual presence** despite physical absence
- The **enduring bond** between Rebbe and chassidim

---

## The Death

### Date and Time

The Alter Rebbe died on:

- **24 Tevet 5573** (December 27, 1812)
- In the **village of Pyena**
- While **still fleeing** the retreating French army
- At approximately **67 years of age**

### Circumstances

His death occurred in:

- **Primitive conditions** — a refugee in a strange village
- **Separation** from his established court and community structures
- **Uncertainty** about the future of the community
- **Physical exhaustion** from the difficult journey

### Immediate Aftermath

The community faced:

- **Grief** at their Rebbe's passing
- **Practical challenges** of burial in strange territory
- **Uncertainty** about leadership succession
- **Continuing danger** from the war

---

## Burial

### Transfer to Haditch

According to tradition, the Alter Rebbe's body was:

- **Initially buried** in Pyena where he died
- Later **transferred to Haditch** (present-day Ukraine)
- Reinterred in a **permanent burial site**

### The Ohel in Haditch

The burial site in Haditch became:

- A **pilgrimage destination** for chassidim
- An **ohel** (memorial structure) marking the grave
- A place of **prayer and petition**
- Connection to the **founding Rebbe** of Chabad

---

## Succession

### The Leadership Question

The Alter Rebbe's death created immediate questions:

- Who would **succeed him** as Rebbe?
- How would the **scattered community** reorganize?
- What would happen to **Chabad leadership**?

### Chaim Avraham and DovBer

The Alter Rebbe had two sons:

- **R' Chaim Avraham** — the eldest son
- **R' DovBer** — the younger son (future Mitteler Rebbe)

Despite Chaim Avraham being the eldest, the community turned to DovBer as successor. Chaim Avraham appears to have **deferred** to his brother, possibly recognizing DovBer's greater spiritual capacity or the community's clear preference.

### Smooth Transition

Despite the difficult circumstances:

- The transition was **peaceful and unified**
- The community **coalesced around DovBer**
- No **conflict or dispute** emerged
- The **dynastic succession** was established

---

## Historical Significance

### End of an Era

The Alter Rebbe's death marked:

- The end of the **founding generation**
- The transition from **charismatic founder** to **dynastic leadership**
- The beginning of **Chabad's institutional development**

### Foundation for Succession

The peaceful succession established:

- The principle of **family dynastic leadership**
- The importance of **spiritual capacity** over mere birth order
- The possibility of **graceful transition**
- The pattern that would continue through **seven generations**

### Legacy of Leadership

The Alter Rebbe's passing demonstrated:

- His **enduring influence** beyond his lifetime
- The **strength of the community** he built
- The **resilience** of Chabad in crisis
- The **power of his teachings** to sustain followers

---

## Napoleonic Context

### The Broader War

The Alter Rebbe's death occurred during:

- **Napoleon's disastrous Russian campaign**
- The **destruction** of the Grand Armée
- The **turning point** in the Napoleonic Wars
- A period of **enormous suffering** across Russia

### Vindication of His Stance

The Russian victory ultimately:

- **Vindicated** the Alter Rebbe's support for Russia
- Demonstrated the **wisdom of his position**
- Showed that **material freedom** under Napoleon would have brought spiritual harm
- Strengthened Chabad's **tradition of accommodation** with state authority
