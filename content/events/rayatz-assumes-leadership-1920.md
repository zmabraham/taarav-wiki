# קבלת הנשיאות — הרייצ"א מקבל את ההנהגה

## Rayatz Assumes Chabad Leadership (1920)

**Date**: Spring 1920 (after 2 Nissan 5680)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/rostov|Rostov-on-Don]], Russia  
**Significance**: Succession from the Rashab to the Rayatz; beginning of Chabad's Soviet-era leadership

---

## Overview

After the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] died in [[entities/places/rostov|Rostov-on-Don]] on **2 Nissan 5680 (1920)**, his son [[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn]] assumed leadership of the Chabad movement — inheriting the most hostile environment any Chabad Rebbe had faced. The Soviet state, through the _Yevsektsiya_ (Jewish Section of the Communist Party), was systematically dismantling Jewish religious life.

---

## The Rashab's Final Instructions

Three weeks before his death, the Rashab told the Rayatz: _"There will be difficult times; you will need to have mesirat nefesh bepoel (actual self-sacrifice). But eventually, the Communist government will disintegrate."_

This prophecy — fulfilled in 1991 — frames the entire Soviet-era Chabad story.

---

## The New Rebbe's Situation

The Rayatz assumed leadership in Rostov — a city far from the traditional Chabad heartland of [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] (already abandoned in 1915) and [[entities/places/vitebsk|Vitebsk]]. The Bolshevik government had just consolidated power. The [[events/yevsektsiya-campaign|Yevsektsiya]] was actively working to close Jewish religious institutions.

---

## The Underground Network

The Rayatz's response was immediate and determined: he organized a clandestine educational network, including:

- Underground yeshivos in multiple cities
- Secret _maamad_ funds to support activities
- Coded correspondence with underground activists
- Personal visits and public appearances under surveillance

The seven years from 1920 to 1927 — until his arrest — represent the Rayatz's most intense underground activity inside the Soviet Union.
