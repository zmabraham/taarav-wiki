# Starodub Arrests (1881)

**Mass arrest of approximately 80 Chabad Chassidim in Starodub; one of the largest coordinated anti-Chabad persecutions in the late Czarist period**

## Background

The assassination of Tsar Alexander II in March 1881 triggered a wave of anti-Jewish pogroms and repressive measures throughout the Russian Empire. The new government under Alexander III pursued harsh policies against Jews, including restrictions on residence, economic activity, and religious practice. In this climate, Chassidic communities — already viewed with suspicion as a potentially subversive movement — became targets of local authorities.

Starodub, a town in the Chernigov gubernia, had a well-established Chabad community with a yeshiva led by R' Yehoshua Natan Gnesin (Hershel Neta) and a rabbinic leader in R' Meshulam Zalman Neymark. The community's vitality apparently attracted the attention of local officials.

## What Happened

In 1881, Czarist authorities conducted a sweep and arrested approximately 80 Chassidim in Starodub. The charges typically invoked in such cases included unlicensed religious assembly, "fanaticism," or teaching religion to minors in violation of state regulations. R' Meshulam Zalman Neymark, the community's rabbi, was among those arrested.

The mass nature of the arrests — nearly 80 people at once — suggests a coordinated operation by local authorities rather than individual targeting. This was likely connected to the broader government crackdown on Jewish religious life following Alexander II's assassination.

## Significance

The 1881 Starodub arrests represent one of the largest documented single-incident persecutions of a Chabad community in the late Czarist period. They illustrate the vulnerability of even established Chabad communities to sudden government action. The incident accelerated emigration from affected communities, as families sought greater security elsewhere. R' Neymark himself subsequently moved to Vitebsk and Nevel, demonstrating how the arrests disrupted rabbinic tenure.
