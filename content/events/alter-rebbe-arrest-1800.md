# Alter Rebbe's Second Arrest (1800)

**Date**: 1800 (5560–5561)  
**Location**: St. Petersburg, Russia

---

## Overview

The [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] was arrested for a second time by Russian imperial authorities in 1800 — just two years after his [[events/alter-rebbe-arrest-1798|first arrest and liberation]] in 1798. The second arrest was similarly resolved through legal exoneration and his release, but the repeated persecution underscored the precarious position of Chassidic leadership in imperial Russia.

---

## Background

Despite his exoneration in 1798, the Alter Rebbe remained a target of both the Mitnagdim's hostility and Russian imperial suspicion. The charges in 1798 had been dismissed but not entirely laid to rest; anti-Chassidic agitators continued to file denunciations.

The broader political context was significant: the reign of Tsar Paul I (1796–1801) was a period of Russian political instability, with shifting policies toward minorities including Jews.

---

## The Second Arrest

The specific charges in the second arrest are less fully documented in available sources than the 1798 arrest. The general pattern was similar — accusations filed with imperial authorities, transport to Petersburg for investigation, detention, and ultimately release.

---

## Liberation

The Alter Rebbe was again exonerated and released. The second liberation reinforced the theological framework already established by the first arrest: divine protection of the Chassidic mission against the forces opposing it.

---

## Context in Chabad Memory

The second arrest is less commemorated than the first (which gave rise to the _Yud-Tet Kislev_ holiday) but is part of the accumulated narrative of the Alter Rebbe's life as one of sustained sacrifice and divine protection.

Together, the two arrests established the Alter Rebbe — and by extension Chabad — as a movement that had faced and survived state persecution, creating the psychological and theological framework that would sustain the movement through far worse persecution in the Soviet era.
