# קבלת הנשיאות — הרבי מקבל את ההנהגה

## Seventh Rebbe Assumes Leadership (10 Shevat 5711 / 1951)

**Date**: 10 Shevat 5711 (January 17, 1951)  
**Location**: [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]], Brooklyn, New York  
**Significance**: Beginning of the seventh Rebbe's leadership; transformation of Chabad into a global movement

---

## Overview

On **10 Shevat 5711 (January 17, 1951)** — exactly one year after the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] passing — a farbrengen was held at [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]]. After the Rayatz's son-in-law, [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson]], had repeatedly declined calls to become Rebbe, elder chassid [[entities/people/sender-nemtzov|Rabbi Sender Nemtzov]] rose and called out: _"We want the Rebbe to say Chasidut!"_

The Ramash (the Rebbe's pre-leadership title) — after a moment of silence — began his first maamar (Chassidic discourse), building on the last maamar the Rayatz had published before his passing. The maamar began with the word _"Basi leGani"_ (I have come to my garden).

When Rabbi Sender Nemtzov saw that the Ramash had begun to speak, he leaped onto a table and cried: **"Chasidim, we have a Rebbe!"**

---

## Background: The Year of Refusal

From the Rayatz's passing on 10 Shevat 5710 (January 28, 1950) through the first anniversary, the Ramash had repeatedly refused all calls to become Rebbe. He was deeply reluctant — aware of the immense responsibility, the living legacy of his father-in-law, and perhaps also the enormity of what was being asked.

The _Undaunted_ biography describes the succession as:

- The Rayatz weeping with the pictures of fallen chassidim (Rabbis Feigin, Horowitz, and others killed by the Nazis)
- The Ramash's reluctance as genuine and profound
- Sender Nemtzov's famous cry as the moment the community's will became irresistible

---

## The First Maamar

The first maamar — _Basi leGani_ — was built on the Rayatz's final published discourse (also titled _Basi leGani_), creating a deliberate chain of continuity. The choice signaled that the new Rebbe understood himself as continuing, not replacing, his father-in-law's work.

---

## Significance

The assumption of leadership by the seventh Rebbe began what would become the most expansive period in Chabad history:

- The global shlichus (emissary) network expanded to every continent
- The Tefillin Campaign (1967), Mitzvah Tank campaigns, and other outreach programs began
- The movement grew from a post-Holocaust survivor community to a worldwide presence
