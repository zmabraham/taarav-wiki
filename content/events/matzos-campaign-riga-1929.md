# Matzos Campaign for Soviet Jews 1929

**The Rayatz Organizes Matzos Provision for Jewish Communities in the Soviet Union**

**Date**: 1929 (leading up to Pesach 5689)
**Base**: [[entities/places/riga|Riga]]
**Context**: Soviet Union had suppressed Jewish religious life including matzo baking
**Significance**: A major humanitarian effort combining religious provision with covert Chabad network support

---

## Overview

In 1929, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]], operating from his headquarters in [[entities/places/riga|Riga]], organized a campaign to provide matzos (unleavened bread for Passover) to Jewish communities suffering under Soviet oppression. This humanitarian campaign addressed one of the most pressing religious needs of Soviet Jews — the suppression of matzo baking by Soviet authorities — while also serving as a vehicle for maintaining connections with and providing support to the underground Chabad network inside the Soviet Union.

---

## Background

### Soviet Suppression of Jewish Religious Life

By 1929, the Soviet Union had severely restricted Jewish religious life:

- **Matzo baking banned or restricted**: The Soviet regime attacked all visible religious practice, including the communal baking of matzos before Passover
- **Religious persecution**: Synagogues closed, rabbis arrested, religious schools shut down
- **Passover difficulty**: Without matzos, Jews could not properly observe Passover
- **Desperate need**: Jewish communities inside the USSR desperately needed both matzos and moral support

### Rayatz's Riga Base

From Riga (1927–1933), the Rayatz directed multiple efforts to support Soviet Jews:

- **Underground network**: Maintained a network of Chabad emissaries and communities inside the USSR
- **Financial support**: Sent funds to underground communities
- **Material support**: The matzos campaign was part of this broader support effort

---

## The Campaign

### Organization

The Rayatz organized from Riga:

- **Production**: Arranged for matzos to be produced in Riga or other accessible locations
- **Funding**: Raised funds from Jewish communities in the free world to pay for the matzos and their transport
- **International outreach**: Appealed to Jewish organizations and wealthy donors worldwide
- **Logistics**: Organized the complex logistics of getting matzos across the Soviet border

### Delivery

Getting matzos into the Soviet Union:

- **Diplomatic channels**: Where possible, used diplomatic connections to bring in matzos
- **Covert routes**: Relied on the underground network for covert delivery in many cases
- **Border crossings**: Trusted emissaries who could cross the border
- **Distribution**: Once inside, the Chabad underground distributed matzos to communities in need

---

## Significance

### Religious Relief

For Soviet Jews, receiving matzos:

- **Passover observance**: Enabled even minimally observant Jews to perform the central Passover ritual
- **Spiritual sustenance**: A tangible sign that the Jewish world outside had not forgotten them
- **Morale boost**: Knowing that someone was actively working to support them was enormously encouraging

### Covert Chabad Support

The campaign also served broader purposes:

- **Network maintenance**: The distribution network used for matzos was the same network used for other Chabad support
- **Information flow**: Couriers bringing matzos could also carry information and communications
- **Relationship building**: Local Jews who received matzos became more connected to the Chabad network

### Diplomatic and Public Relations

The campaign:

- **International attention**: Drew attention to the plight of Soviet Jews
- **Jewish solidarity**: Demonstrated that diaspora Jews were actively working to help Soviet brethren
- **Rayatz's leadership**: Established the Rayatz as a leading voice on behalf of Soviet Jews

---

## The Campaign in Context

### Part of Larger Efforts

The 1929 matzos campaign was part of a continuing pattern:

- **Annual Pesach efforts**: The campaign became part of the annual effort to provide matzos
- **Broader support**: Beyond matzos, the Rayatz organized support for Soviet Jewish religious needs year-round
- **Until 1933**: These efforts continued from Riga; after moving to Warsaw, they continued from there
