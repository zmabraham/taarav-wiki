# מנוסת חסידי חב"ד מברית המועצות

## The Great Escape from the USSR (1946–1947)

**Dates**: 1946–1947  
**Scale**: Approximately 1,000 Chabad chassidim escaped  
**Method**: Forged Polish passports; "repatriation" of Polish citizens  
**Coordination**: [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] from Brooklyn; [[entities/people/binyamin-gorodetsky|Binyamin Gorodetsky]] in Europe

---

## Overview

Following World War II, approximately **1,000 Chabad chassidim** successfully escaped the Soviet Union in an organized operation using forged Polish identity documents. The operation — coordinated by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] from Brooklyn and executed by trusted operatives in the USSR — represents one of the most remarkable logistical achievements in twentieth-century Jewish history.

The _Undaunted_ biography of the Rayatz documents this as a multi-year operation carefully orchestrated at the highest Chabad leadership level.

---

## Background

During WWII, hundreds of Chabad chassidim had fled the Nazi advance eastward into Soviet Central Asia — primarily to [[entities/places/samarkand|Samarkand]] and other Uzbek cities. After the war ended in 1945, they found themselves trapped in the Soviet Union with no legal means of emigration. The Soviet regime had no interest in allowing religious Jews to leave.

---

## The Method: Polish Repatriation

The key to escape was the post-war arrangement by which **Polish citizens** could be "repatriated" from Soviet territory to Poland — a legitimate diplomatic process. Chassidim in Uzbekistan:

1. **Acquired Polish papers**: Obtained Polish identity documents on the black market
2. **Counterfeiting**: Skilled counterfeiters modified names and details
3. **Women as couriers**: Women (less conspicuous) served as messengers and transporters of documents
4. **Cattle cars**: Groups were transported from **Lvov** across the Polish border in cattle cars

---

## The Rabbinical Court in Lvov

A secret **rabbinical court of 23 judges** was established in Lvov (now Lviv, Ukraine) to:

- Set halachic guidelines for the operation (including the use of forged documents)
- Pool resources
- Coordinate timing
- Make decisions about who could and should attempt escape

This ad hoc rabbinical court — convened under conditions of extreme secrecy — represents an example of halachic authority operating in crisis conditions.

---

## The Rayatz's Role from Brooklyn

The Rayatz coordinated the operation from his residence at [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]] in Brooklyn:

- Communicating in **code** through intermediaries
- Providing financial support
- Making strategic decisions

One account in _Undaunted_ describes the Rayatz reciting _Az Yashir_ (the Song of the Sea) and swaying as if walking — then declaring "they got through." Shortly after, a telegram arrived confirming that **232 persons** had safely crossed the border. This was interpreted as a moment of prophetic connection to his chassidim.

---

## The Destinations

After crossing into Poland, the escapees dispersed westward:

- **France** — became the nucleus of the post-war European Chabad community
- **Australia** — 6 families sent to Melbourne and surroundings (coordinated by [[entities/people/chaim-gutnick|Rabbi Chaim Gutnick]])
- **Canada, US, UK, South Africa, Cuba, Sweden, South America**
- **Israel** — culminating in the founding of [[entities/places/kfar-chabad|Kfar Chabad]] (1949)

The Rayatz deliberately dispersed the refugees globally rather than concentrating them — seeing each refugee as a potential emissary in a new community.

---

## Significance

The Great Escape is one of the most dramatic instances of Chabad collective action. It required:

- Halachic flexibility (use of forged documents)
- Organizational sophistication (coordination across thousands of miles)
- Individual courage (risk of arrest and imprisonment for each participant)
- Spiritual motivation (the conviction that rebuilding Jewish life was worth any risk)

It also set the stage for the global dispersion of Chabad that characterized the movement's post-war growth.
