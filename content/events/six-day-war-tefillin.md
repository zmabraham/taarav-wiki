# The Tefillin Campaign (1967)

**A Mitzvah as Shield and Bridge**

---

## Overview

In June 1967, as Israel faced existential threat in the Six Day War, the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] launched one of Chabad's most impactful and enduring outreach initiatives: the **Tefillin Campaign**. The campaign transformed the ancient practice of donning leather boxes containing Torah verses into a global Jewish encounter that continues to this day.

---

## Background: The Six Day War

In May-June 1967, Egyptian President Nasser blockaded the Straits of Tiran, massed troops on Israel's borders, and called for Israel's destruction. Jordan and Syria joined the threatened attack. The Jewish world held its breath.

On June 5, Israel launched a pre-emptive air strike; within six days (hence "Six Day War"), Israel had captured the Sinai Peninsula, the West Bank including Jerusalem's Old City, the Golan Heights, and Gaza.

---

## The Rebbe's Response

As the crisis built, the seventh Rebbe told his students to go out and ask Jewish men — particularly Israeli soldiers and Jews in the street — to put on tefillin. His reasoning:

- Tefillin is described in the Torah as a sign between G-d and Israel
- The verse says: "All the nations of the earth will see that the name of G-d is called upon you, and they will fear you" — which the Talmud understands as referring to tefillin
- Physical protection comes through the fulfillment of mitzvos
- Jewish soldiers going to war needed this connection

Students from 770 went to New York street corners with tefillin. Israeli soldiers put on tefillin at the front. The Rebbe himself stood for hours helping people put on tefillin.

---

## The Mitzvah Tank

From the Tefillin Campaign grew the **Mitzvah Tank** — a mobile outreach vehicle (often a converted RV or truck) stocked with tefillin, mezuzahs, Shabbat candles, and other Jewish items. Chabad students drive these through Jewish neighborhoods, stop on street corners, and offer passersby the chance to put on tefillin or light Shabbat candles.

The Mitzvah Tank program expanded globally and became one of Chabad's most visible outreach methods.

---

## Impact

The Tefillin Campaign became permanent. By the 1970s:

- Chabad outposts worldwide maintained tefillin stations
- Millions of Jewish men who had never worn tefillin did so through Chabad
- The practice of "Chabad guys approaching you with tefillin" became a Jewish cultural experience worldwide

The seventh Rebbe estimated that the mitzvah of tefillin had been fulfilled by more Jews in the years since the campaign than in any comparable period in history.

---

## Theological Dimension

The Tefillin Campaign expressed a core Chabad principle: **every mitzvah has infinite value regardless of the person's level of observance**. A completely secular Jew who puts on tefillin once has fulfilled a Torah commandment with full spiritual effect. This non-judgmental approach — meeting people where they are, offering mitzvos rather than demanding transformation — defines Chabad's outreach philosophy.
