# Founding of Toras Emes Yeshiva in Hebron (1911)

**Date**: 1911 (5671)  
**Location**: Beit Romano, Hebron, Eretz Yisrael

---

## Overview

In 1911, the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] purchased **Beit Romano** — a large complex in Hebron — and sent [[entities/people/zalman-havlin|Rabbi Zalman Havlin]] to establish a formal Chabad yeshiva there, naming it **Toras Emes** ("Truth of Torah"). This became the **first Chassidic yeshiva in the Jewish homeland** — a historic milestone for both Chabad and Eretz Yisrael.

---

## Background

By 1911, the [[events/hebron-community-1823|Chabad community in Hebron]] had existed for nearly 90 years. It had survived the [[events/hebron-pogrom-1834|1834 riots]] and been spiritually anchored by [[entities/people/menucha-rochel|Rebbetzin Menucha Rochel Slonim]] from 1845 to 1888. The community maintained a learning center (known as Magen Avos), but it was not a fully organized yeshiva in the Tomchei Temimim model.

The Rashab's decision to formalize the Hebron institution as a Tomchei Temimim branch represented the extension of his flagship educational project to the Land of Israel.

---

## Beit Romano

The Rashab purchased **Beit Romano** — a large, impressive complex built in the nineteenth century by Chaim Romano, a wealthy Sephardic Jewish merchant. The complex was suitable for housing a yeshiva with dormitory facilities, study halls, and communal spaces.

The purchase itself was significant — a major financial investment by Chabad's central leadership in physical infrastructure in Eretz Yisrael.

---

## Toras Emes

The new yeshiva was named **Toras Emes** — "Truth of Torah." As a Tomchei Temimim branch, it followed the flagship yeshiva's model:

- Combination of Talmudic and Chassidic study
- Mashpia-centered spiritual guidance
- Full board and room for students
- Goal of producing _pnimiyim_ — internalized Chassidic personalities

Russian students came to study in Hebron alongside local students, creating an unusual cosmopolitan community.

---

## WWI Disruption

World War One (which began in 1914) disrupted Toras Emes severely. The Ottoman Empire (which controlled Palestine) was at war with Russia. Russian nationals — including most of the Tomchei Temimim students — were in a hostile position in Ottoman territory. They were forced to return to Russia, effectively closing the yeshiva during the war years.

---

## Reconstitution in Jerusalem (1922)

After the Rashab's death in 1920 and the British conquest of Palestine, conditions changed. Rabbi Zalman Havlin reconstituted Toras Emes in **Jerusalem** in 1922 — moving it from Hebron to the capital, where it could operate more sustainably.

The Jerusalem incarnation became the direct ancestor of the Chabad educational institutions in Israel today.

---

## Historical Significance

Toras Emes Hebron (1911) was:

- The **first Chassidic yeshiva in Eretz Yisrael** — a historic first
- A formal extension of Chabad's institutional network into the Land of Israel
- The fulfillment of Chabad's dual commitment to Jewish education and Eretz Yisrael
