# Rebbetzin Nechama Dina Schneersohn

**Wife of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz); accompanied him through decades of Soviet persecution, exile, and eventual rescue to America; mother of three daughters.**

---

## Overview

Rebbetzin Nechama Dina Schneersohn (nee Horenstein) was born in 1880 and became the wife of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe. As Rebbetzin during one of the most turbulent periods in Jewish history, she endured extraordinary hardships — the Soviet persecution of her husband's movement, his arrest and near-martyrdom in 1927, the years of exile in Riga and Warsaw, and ultimately the harrowing escape from Nazi-occupied Europe in 1940. Through all of this, she was her husband's steadfast companion.

She was the mother of the Rayatz's three daughters: Chana (b. 1899), Chaya Mushka (b. 1901), and Sheina (b. 1903). The tragedy of Sheina's failure to escape — she perished in the Holocaust along with her husband Rabbi Menachem Mendel Horenstein in 1942 — was one of the deepest griefs of the Rebbetzin's life. Nechama Dina survived the Holocaust herself, having escaped with the Rayatz to America in 1940. She passed away in 1971.

## Life and Role

Nechama Dina's life as the Rayatz's wife meant sharing in all of his extraordinary experiences — the Chassidic court in Lubavitch, the disruptions of World War I, the Soviet era of underground leadership, her husband's arrest by the GPU in 1927, the exile years in Riga and Warsaw, and the terrifying weeks of the Nazi invasion of Poland in 1939. Through each of these upheavals, she was present and steadfast.

The escape from Warsaw in early 1940 was orchestrated with great difficulty through American diplomatic channels. The Rebbetzin accompanied her husband and daughters Chana and Chaya Mushka (with their husbands, the Rashag and the Ramash) on the journey through Nazi-controlled Europe. The knowledge that their youngest daughter Sheina and her husband remained behind — unable to secure exit documents — haunted the family's arrival in America. Sheina and Menachem Mendel Horenstein were murdered in the Holocaust in 1942.

In Brooklyn, Rebbetzin Nechama Dina was a figure of dignity and grace in the Chabad community at 770 Eastern Parkway. After the Rayatz's passing in 1950, she lived on for more than two decades, witnessing the extraordinary growth of Chabad under her son-in-law the seventh Rebbe. She was deeply respected by the community as the last living link to the Rayatz's household. She passed away in 1971.
