# Tomchei Temimim Underground (Soviet Era)

The **underground Tomchei Temimim** was the clandestine continuation of the Chabad yeshiva network in Soviet Russia after the official yeshiva was closed by Soviet authorities. Operating from the late 1920s through the 1930s, it maintained Jewish education at enormous personal risk, constantly moving to evade Soviet police.

## Background

**Tomchei Temimim** (תומכי תמימים — "Supporters of the Wholesome") was founded by the [[rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] (R. Shalom DovBer Schneersohn) in 5657 (1897) in Lubavitch. It was the flagship Chabad yeshiva, training students in both Talmud study and Chabad Chassidic philosophy.

After the Bolshevik Revolution (1917) and especially after 1921, Soviet pressure on religious institutions intensified. By the late 1920s, all official religious educational institutions were illegal.

## Underground Operations

Rather than ceasing operations, Tomchei Temimim went underground:

### Cities of Operation (as described in *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu*)

| City | Period | Status |
|------|--------|--------|
| **[[vitebsk|Vitebsk]]** | Winter 5689 (1929) | Operating secretly |
| **Mogilev** | 1929–1930 | Yeshiva in hiding |
| **[[kremenchug|Kremenchug]]** | 1929–1930 | Active branch |
| **Odessa** | 1929–1930 | Active branch |
| **[[kherson|Kherson]]** | Winter 5690 (1930) | Yeshiva, then raided |

### Constant Relocation

The yeshiva constantly moved when a location was compromised. Students would receive word to relocate — sometimes with only hours' notice. When the Kherson branch was raided (c. 1930), its teachers and students were arrested; other students (like the memoir narrator) arrived to find it gone and had to retreat to Kremenchug.

### Covert Practices

- Students used cover occupations (bookbinders, workers) to avoid suspicion
- Learning took place in private homes, not institutions
- Teachers traveled under assumed purposes
- Communication was in person or by trusted messenger (phones were monitored)

## Personnel

**Teachers/Maggidei Shiur** documented in the memoir:
- [[yechezkel-himelshtein]] — Maggid Shiur, Kherson branch; arrested in Kherson raid
- [[mendel-neveler-gribov]] — Maggid Shiur, Kremenchug branch
- [[avraham-sverdlov]] — Teacher, Odessa branch; arrested 1938 in Leningrad

**Students** included the anonymous narrator (born Zlobin 1914) and dozens of others.

## Suppression

The [[yevsektsia|Yevsektsia]] (Soviet Jewish Communist Section) was particularly aggressive in suppressing the underground yeshiva. Many students "turned" (became irreligious) under the combined pressure of poverty, fear, and ideological bombardment — something the memoir narrator describes as more painful than physical persecution.

## Legacy

Despite constant suppression, the underground Tomchei Temimim:
- Trained hundreds of students who survived and became rabbis in Israel and America
- Preserved Chabad education through the darkest period of Soviet anti-religious campaigns
- Served as a model for later Chabad underground networks in Communist countries
