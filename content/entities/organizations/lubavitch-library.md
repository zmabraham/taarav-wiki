# Lubavitch Library (Bibliothek Lyubawitch)

The personal library of [[entities/people/rayatz-yosef-yitzchak-schneersohn]] (Rayatz), 6th Lubavitcher Rebbe. One of the most important collections of Hebrew books and manuscripts of its time.

## Founding

In **5685 (1924)**, Rayatz purchased the collection of bibliographer R' [[entities/people/shmuel-weiner]] in Leningrad for **$5,000**. The contract was signed **8 Marcheshvan 5685**. Permission to export the collection from Soviet Russia was obtained through R' [[entities/people/mordechai-dubin]] (Riga) and Dr. A. Cohen (Berlin).

The collection included a "censorship collection" (books that had been subjected to tsarist censorship).

## Growth

The library grew substantially over the years. By 5698 (1937–38), it contained approximately **13,000–23,000 volumes**, including:
- Rare early printed books (incunabula)
- Hundreds of editions of the Passover Haggadah in translations to all world languages
- 900 varieties of Selichot from different communities
- Manuscripts of the Baal Shem Tov, the Maggid of Mezeritch, and others
- A manuscript by R' [[entities/people/avraham-saba]] (author of Tzror HaMor), written ~440 years earlier
- A Venice siddur from 1475 (among the world's oldest)
- A 1600 manuscript dictionary in Hebrew, Aramaic, and Syriac
- A Ralbag (Gersonides) Torah commentary, printed 1476
- A Ramban, printed in Lisbon 1489
- Many "Tsenerene" (Yiddish Torah for women), responsa, etc.

R' [[entities/people/chaim-lieberman]] served as librarian and compiled a working typed catalog.

| 5701– (1941–) | New York (770 Eastern Parkway) |

Books were largely stored in crates in Rayatz's residence due to lack of proper shelving space, as Rayatz lamented in a 5698 letter.

## Rescue

See [[entities/events/lubavitch-library-rescue]] for full details.

In brief: Attorney [[entities/people/aharon-blum]] arranged export of 100 boxes via Stockholm (arriving New York Sivan 5701). 17 boxes were lost in Warsaw; 3 boxes of manuscripts deposited at the US Embassy were also ultimately lost.

Post-war, R' [[entities/people/shlomo-zilberberg]] and R' [[entities/people/pinchas-klein]] found some surviving books in Warsaw.

## Researchers

Even before the library was fully cataloged, scholars came to use it:
- R' [[entities/people/david-frankel]] (Husyatin) — a visitor during the Riga period (reported in HaOlam, London, 22 Elul 5688)
- Various unnamed researchers
