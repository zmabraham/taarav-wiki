# Yevsektsia

The **Yevsektsia** (Еврейская секция — "Jewish Section") was the **Jewish Section of the Soviet Communist Party**, active from 1918 to 1930. It was staffed primarily by Jewish communists and tasked with spreading the communist revolution among the Jewish population — specifically by **eliminating Jewish religious and traditional cultural life**.

## Purpose and Methods

The Yevsektsia pursued a program of:
- **Closing synagogues** — converting them to workers' clubs, storage, or demolishing them
- **Shutting down yeshivos** — including Chabad's Tomchei Temimim
- **Arresting rabbis and religious leaders** — including many Chabad figures
- **Suppressing Hebrew** — promoting Yiddish instead as a "secular" Jewish language
- **Anti-religious propaganda** — "show trials" of religious observance
- Infiltrating and informing on religious communities

## Composition

Ironically, the Yevsektsia was composed largely of **Jewish communists** — often young men and women who had rejected traditional Judaism and embraced Marxism. They were sometimes more zealous in suppressing Jewish religion than their non-Jewish comrades, perhaps to prove their commitment to the revolution.

## Impact on Chabad

The memoir *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* describes the Yevsektsia's systematic destruction of Chabad institutions:
- Underground yeshiva operations had to relocate constantly to avoid Yevsektsia agents
- Many fellow yeshiva students "turned" (*azoy geshtorbn* — became irreligious) under pressure, which the narrator calls the most painful aspect of Soviet persecution
- The Yevsektsia's informers penetrated Chabad cells repeatedly

The Rayatz (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn) was arrested in 1927 partly due to Yevsektsia-driven pressure.

## Abolition

The Yevsektsia was abolished by Stalin in **1930** — not because of any change in anti-religious policy, but because of bureaucratic reorganization. Its functions were absorbed by other Soviet agencies (primarily the GPU/NKVD). Many Yevsektsia leaders were themselves arrested and killed in the 1930s purges.

## Historical Judgment

The Yevsektsia is remembered in Jewish history as one of the most painful examples of Jews persecuting Jews. Its members understood Jewish life intimately — and used that knowledge to destroy it effectively.
