# American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee (JDC)

The **American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee** (JDC), known as "the Joint" or simply "the JDC," was the principal American Jewish overseas relief organization. Founded in 1914, it provided food, clothing, medical care, and emigration assistance to Jews in need worldwide — particularly during and after World War II.

## Role in Post-War Refugee Relief

After WWII, the JDC was the largest single non-governmental relief organization serving Jewish displaced persons and refugees in Europe. It operated in:
- Germany and Austria (DP camps)
- France and other Western European countries (transit refugees)
- Eastern Europe (when permitted)

## Connection to Chabad Refugees

The memoir *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* describes the JDC's support for post-war Jewish refugees in [[paris|Paris]]:
- **Housing**: The Joint rented a mansion in Sèvres (a Paris suburb) to house refugees, including the memoir narrator and his family
- **Initial accommodation**: The "La Chapelle" hotel in Paris
- **Coordination**: The Joint coordinated with the [[lishka-le-refugees|Lishka leRefugees]] and other organizations

## Funding of the Bricha

The JDC was one of the primary funders of the [[bricha|Bricha]] — the clandestine network that helped Jews escape from Eastern Europe. Without this American funding, the massive organized escape of hundreds of thousands of Jews would have been impossible.

## Relationship with Jewish Religious Community

The JDC maintained a generally neutral stance toward religious observance, focusing on material relief regardless of affiliation. However, in practice it worked closely with organizations like the Va'ad HaHatzala and Chabad's Lishka, as these organizations identified and helped serve the most vulnerable populations.
