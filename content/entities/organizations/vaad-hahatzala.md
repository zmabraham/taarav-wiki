# Va'ad HaHatzala

The **Va'ad HaHatzala** (ועד ההצלה — "Rescue Committee") was an American Orthodox Jewish rescue organization established in 1939, primarily by Agudath Israel and Orthodox rabbinic organizations, to rescue European rabbis and yeshiva students during and after the Holocaust.

## Mission

The Va'ad HaHatzala focused specifically on **rescuing Torah scholars** — arguing that these individuals were irreplaceable for the future of Jewish life and should be prioritized for rescue even when general rescue was impossible. This created controversy with other Jewish organizations that preferred universal criteria.

## Post-War Activities

After WWII, the Va'ad HaHatzala continued its work:
- Providing funds and documentation to yeshiva students and rabbis in DP camps
- Coordinating with the [[bricha|Bricha]] underground escape network
- Working with immigration authorities to secure visas for religious scholars
- Supporting the reestablishment of yeshivos in America

## Connection to Chabad

The Va'ad HaHatzala's work intersected with Chabad's own rescue efforts:
- The memoir *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* mentions the Va'ad HaHatzala as one of the organizations providing **coordination and funding** for the Bricha escape network
- In [[paris|Paris]], the Va'ad coordinated with the [[lishka-le-refugees|Lishka leRefugees]] (Chabad's refugee office) to assist refugees

## Relationship with JDC

The Va'ad HaHatzala sometimes had a complicated relationship with the [[joint-distribution-committee|JDC]], which had a more secular orientation and different priorities. However, in the post-war chaos, both organizations frequently worked in the same spaces and with the same populations.
