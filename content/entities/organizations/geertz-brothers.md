# Geertz Brothers Printing House

The **Geertz Brothers** (דפוס האחים גערץ) was a Jewish printing house located near **770 Eastern Parkway** in Crown Heights, Brooklyn — the headquarters of Chabad-Lubavitch. In the late 1940s, it was the primary printer of Chabad publications in America.

## Location and Period

- Located near 770 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn
- Active in the late 1940s (at least 5708 / 1948 onward)
- Named for the Geertz brothers who owned and operated it

## Significance to Chabad

The Geertz Brothers press was the **first to print** several significant Chabad texts in America:

| Publication | Year |
|-------------|------|
| **Kitzurim veHaaros al HaTanya** by the Tzemach Tzedek | 5708 (1948) |
| **Or HaTorah al Shemos** (Tzemach Tzedek) | 5708+ |
| **Mafteach Maamarei Admur Maharshab** (index to Rashab's discourses) | 5708+ |
| Kuntresim (pamphlets) for each Yom Tov | ongoing |

## The Narrator's Work There

The narrator of *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* worked as a **linotype typesetter** at the Geertz Brothers press after settling in Brooklyn in 5708 (1948). He set type directly from manuscripts handed to him by the [[menachem-mendel-schneerson|Rebbe]] (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn), who personally supervised the typesetting of Chabad texts.

The memoir describes the Rebbe handing manuscripts to the narrator, the narrator setting type, and receiving smiles (*panim shokhakot* — "shining face") from the Rebbe upon completing significant projects. This created a personal relationship between the worker and the Rebbe centered on the preservation of Chabad Torah literature.

## Later Career

After working at Geertz Brothers, the narrator also worked at **Modern Linotype** — the shop of [[yechiel-shapira|R. Yechiel Shapira]].

## Historical Significance

The Geertz Brothers press played a crucial role in the **revival and preservation of Chabad literature** in America after the Holocaust. Much Chabad literature had been scattered or destroyed during the Soviet era and WWII. Brooklyn printing houses like Geertz Brothers produced the first American editions of foundational Chabad texts.
