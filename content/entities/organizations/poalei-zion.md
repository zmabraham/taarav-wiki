# Poalei Zion

Poalei Zion (פועלי ציון, "Workers of Zion") was a socialist Zionist political movement active in the early twentieth century, particularly among Jewish communities in Eastern Europe and the Russian Empire. During the early Soviet period, it operated briefly as a legal party before being suppressed.

## Description
Poalei Zion combined Zionist aspirations for a Jewish homeland with socialist ideology. In the early Soviet period, factions of Poalei Zion attempted to operate within the Soviet system, arguing that Zionism could be compatible with Marxism. However, the party was ultimately suppressed along with all other non-Communist political movements.

In Lipovetsh, Poalei Zion was among the political groupings active in the early 1920s alongside the Yevsektsia. The Zichronos memoir mentions it as one of the competing ideological forces that sought to draw young Jews away from traditional religious life during this turbulent period.

The movement stood in contrast to both the anti-religious Yevsektsia (which sought to destroy religious life while remaining in the USSR) and the Chabad community (which sought to maintain Torah observance). Poalei Zion's Zionist aspirations also put it at odds with Soviet policy once Stalin consolidated power.
