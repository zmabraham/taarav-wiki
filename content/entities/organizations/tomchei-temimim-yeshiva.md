# Tomchei Temimim (תומכי תמימים)

**The flagship yeshiva of Chabad-Lubavitch; founded 5657 (1897) by the Rebbe Rashab; produced generations of Chabad leaders and emissaries.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Founded** | 5657 (1897) in Lubavitch |
| **Founder** | Rebbe Rashab (R' Shalom Dovber Schneerson, 5th Rebbe) |
| **Original director** | Rayatz (R' Yosef Yitzchak Schneerson) from 5657 |
| **Name meaning** | "Supporters of the Wholesome/Innocent" |
| **Students called** | "Tmimim" (תמימים) |

## History

Tomchei Temimim was founded in Lubavitch in 5657 (1897) by the Rebbe Rashab. Its founding philosophy emphasized combining deep Torah scholarship with the study of Chassidic philosophy (Chassidus), practice of mikveh immersion, and intense spiritual growth. The Rayatz managed it from the outset.

The yeshiva grew in stages:
- Initially a gathering of older students studying independently
- Then structured learning groups for various levels
- By ~5666 (1906): the first children's division opened, with R' Menachem Mendel Lyader as first teacher
- First graduates sent as shluchim in 5666 (1906)
- By Jacobson's arrival (5668/1908): multiple levels, large hall for advanced students, classroom sections for younger ones

**Student body:** Drew students from all over the Russian Empire — White Russia, Ukraine, and beyond. Multiple Chabad branches in cities served as feeder schools.

## Physical Setup (Lubavitch)

- **Kleiner Zal** (small hall): Where younger classes studied and the Rashab gave his Chassidic discourses in the early years
- **Ulam Gadol** (large hall): Where advanced (Ulam) students studied
- **Shilem's rooms** (at R' Shilem Reich's): Student housing
- **Kutan's home**: Where shechita apprenticeship took place

## Branches

Tomchei Temimim operated branches in multiple cities simultaneously:
- Lubavitch (main campus)
- Babinovich (באבינאוויטש)
- Schedrін (שצעדרין)
- Kremenchug (קרעמענטשוג)
- Kherson (חרסון)
- Later: Poltava and others

**After WWI:** When Lubavitch was abandoned (5675/1915), the yeshiva moved to Rostov. Under Soviet pressure, it was driven underground, operating in secret.

**In Europe:** After the Rayatz was expelled from the USSR (1927), branches were reestablished in Poland and elsewhere. Otwock, near Warsaw, became the main location.

**In America:** After the Rayatz's arrival in New York (1940), Tomchei Temimim was reestablished in Brooklyn.

## Curriculum and Culture

- Primary study: Talmud with Tosafos (Bava Kamma, Gittin, Ketubot, etc.)
- Secondary: Chassidic philosophy (Chassidus / Dach)
- Shechita apprenticeship: Standard part of training
- Farbrengens: Every Friday night until dawn, and on Jewish holidays
- Immersion in mikveh twice weekly (Monday and Thursday)
- No secular studies (contrast with Yeshivas Reines in Lida)

## Student Welfare System

Students who could not afford meals received:
- "Rebbetzin's krupnik" — daily soup/stew from Rebbetzin Sterna Sarah
- Eating days (essen teg / ימים) at local families' homes
- Small stipends from the yeshiva office (e.g., 1 ruble/month for evening meals; 0.5 rubles for morning food)
- Shabbat meals arranged at local homes
