# Lishka leRefugees

The **Lishka leRefugees** (לשכה לפליטים — "Office for Refugees"), also called simply "the Lishka," was a Chabad rescue and relief organization established by the **Previous Rebbe** ([[rayatz|Rayatz]], R. Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn) and active primarily in **[[paris|Paris]]** in the post-WWII period (1945–1950s).

## Purpose

The Lishka served as the **central coordination office** for Chabad refugees and Holocaust survivors seeking:
- Documentation for emigration (to America, Israel, or elsewhere)
- Housing and immediate support upon arrival in Paris
- Connection to immigration authorities and relief agencies
- Assistance navigating the post-war legal bureaucracy

## Location

The Lishka operated in **Paris**, which served as a major transit point for Jewish refugees from Eastern Europe heading to America or Israel. The memoir narrator arrived in Paris before Shavuot 5707 (1947) and interacted with the Lishka.

## Context: Paris Refugee Community

Paris in 1946–1948 had a large Jewish refugee community, including:
- Holocaust survivors from Poland, Hungary, and Romania
- Soviet-era escapees (via the Bricha underground network)
- Former residents of the DP (displaced persons) camps in Germany and Austria

The [[joint-distribution-committee|JDC (Joint Distribution Committee)]] and [[vaad-hahatzala|Va'ad HaHatzala]] also operated in Paris, with some coordination with the Lishka.

## Housing for Refugees

The Lishka, working with the Joint, arranged housing for arriving refugees. The memoir narrator and his family were initially housed at the "La Chapelle" hotel, then moved to a mansion in "Sevre" (Sèvres, a Paris suburb) rented by the Joint for the refugee community.

## Significance

The Lishka leRefugees was part of the Rayatz's broader post-war effort to rebuild the Jewish Chassidic world, centered on Brooklyn but with outposts in Europe. It connected individual Chassidim with the resources needed to rebuild their lives.
