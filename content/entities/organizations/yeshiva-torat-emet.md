# Yeshiva Torat Emet

**Yeshiva Torat Emet** (ישיבת תורת אמת — "Academy of True Torah") was the principal Chabad yeshiva in the Holy Land (Eretz Yisrael), operating from 5681 (1921) through the 20th century. It served as the flagship institution of Chabad scholarship in the Land of Israel.

## Founding (1921)

In 5681 (1921), the [[rayatz|Rayatz]] (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn) sent **seven select students** from Tomchei Temimim (the main Chabad yeshiva in Russia) to found the yeshiva in the Holy Land. They established it initially in **[[hebron|Hebron]]**, where the historic Chabad community was centered.

### First Faculty

The founding faculty included:
- **[[alter-shimchovitch|R. Alter Shimchovitch]]** — Mashpia (spiritual guide) at Torat Emet for **12 years** (until his death in 5696/1936); one of the most influential figures in the yeshiva's early period

## Relocation to Jerusalem

After the **1929 Hebron Massacre** (when Arab rioters killed 67 Hebron Jews and the Jewish community was evacuated), Yeshiva Torat Emet relocated to **[[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]**, where it continued to operate. The yeshiva maintained a presence at the historic Tzemach Tzedek synagogue.

## Branch in Tel Aviv-Jaffa

*Toldos Chabad beEretz HaKodesh* documents a **branch operation in Tel Aviv-Jaffa** beginning in winter 5684 (1924):

**First period (1924–1929)**: Called "Cheider Bnei Temimim" and "Yeshiva Achei Temimim" — a children's division under the general umbrella of Torat Emet
- General director: [[shlomo-zalman-havlin|R. Shlomo Zalman Havlin]] (managing director of Yeshiva Torat Emet in Jerusalem)
- Local supporter: R. David Patash (prominent Tel Aviv communal figure)

**Second period (1938–1951)**: Included an adult division as "Yeshiva Tomchei Temimim"

## Connection to the Memoir

The memoir *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* mentions Yeshiva Torat Emet in the context of R. [[alter-shimchovitch|Alter Shimchovitch]], who is named as a mashpia there.

## Significance

Yeshiva Torat Emet served as:
1. **Educational institution** — training Chabad scholars in the Holy Land
2. **Community anchor** — providing a center for Chabad life in Israel
3. **Cultural bridge** — connecting the Russian Chabad tradition with the emerging Israeli Chabad community
