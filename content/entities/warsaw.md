# Warsaw (Polish Period 1933–1939)

**Capital of Poland; home of the Rayatz from 1933 to 1939; site of the celebrated Chaya Mushka–Ramash wedding in 1928; starting point of the harrowing escape from Nazi occupation in 1940.**

---

## Overview

Warsaw, the capital of Poland, was the Rayatz's base of operations for six years, from his move from Riga in 1933 until the German invasion of Poland on September 1, 1939. Warsaw was home to one of the largest Jewish communities in the world — an estimated 350,000 Jews lived there by the 1930s — making it an ideal center from which to lead a movement with significant European Jewry connections.

Warsaw also holds an earlier significance in Chabad history: the celebrated wedding of Chaya Mushka Schneersohn and Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn on 14 Kislev 5689 (November 27, 1928) took place in Warsaw when the Rayatz's court was temporarily there before the formal move from Riga. This wedding — the "royal wedding" of Chabad — drew thousands of Chassidim and was one of the great celebrations of interwar Chabad life.

The Warsaw chapter ended catastrophically on September 1, 1939, when Germany invaded Poland. Within days, Warsaw was under bombardment and Chabad leadership was in danger. The Rayatz was at his summer residence in Otwock near Warsaw when the invasion began, and the subsequent weeks — trapped in Nazi-occupied territory, with international efforts underway to secure his escape — were among the most harrowing of his life. The successful rescue, orchestrated through American diplomatic channels, brought the Rayatz and his family out of Nazi-occupied Poland in early 1940.

## Significance in Chabad History

Warsaw represents the last major European home of the Rayatz before the Holocaust consumed European Jewry. The community that gathered around him there, the yeshivos, the publications, the organizational work — all of it was destroyed or displaced by the German invasion. Many of the Chassidim who could not escape shared the fate of Warsaw's Jews: ghetto, deportation, murder.

The specific memories associated with Warsaw include the Rayatz's court at Otwock (the suburban estate), the bustling Jewish quarter, the network of Chassidic families who had settled near the Rebbe, and the final terrible weeks of the German occupation during which the rescue mission was underway. The contrast between the wedding of 1928 and the escape of 1940 — twelve years in which the world of Eastern European Jewry moved from relative stability to destruction — is one of the starkest expressions of what "Undaunted" attempts to capture.
