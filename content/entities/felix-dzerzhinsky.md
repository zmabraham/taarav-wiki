# Felix Dzerzhinsky (Founder of the Cheka)

**Polish-born Bolshevik revolutionary; founder of the Cheka (first Soviet secret police); his organizational legacy was the instrument of the Rayatz's persecution.**

---

## Overview

Felix Edmundovich Dzerzhinsky (1877–1926) was a Polish-born Bolshevik revolutionary who founded the Cheka (Vserossiyskaya Chrezvychaynaya Komissiya — All-Russian Extraordinary Commission), the first Soviet secret police organization, in December 1917 — just weeks after the Bolshevik Revolution. Known as "Iron Felix" for his relentless and ruthless approach, Dzerzhinsky built an apparatus of political repression that would, under successive names (Cheka, GPU, OGPU, NKVD, KGB), pursue, arrest, and murder political and religious opponents of the Soviet regime for seven decades.

Dzerzhinsky himself died of a heart attack on July 20, 1926 — less than a year before the Rayatz's arrest in June 1927. He did not personally direct the persecution of the Rayatz. However, the institutional apparatus he created — which became the GPU and then OGPU — was the organizational framework within which agents Nachmanson and Lulav operated. In this sense, Dzerzhinsky is the architect of the machinery that arrested, imprisoned, and nearly executed the Rayatz.

## Life and Role

Dzerzhinsky was born to a Polish gentry family in what is now Belarus. He converted from Catholicism to revolutionary politics as a young man and spent years in Czarist prisons for political agitation. After the Bolshevik Revolution, Lenin appointed him to head the new political police — an organization designed explicitly to defend the revolution through terror against class enemies, political opponents, and religious figures.

Under Dzerzhinsky's leadership, the Cheka carried out the Red Terror — a systematic campaign of mass arrests and executions targeting the revolution's perceived enemies. Religious leaders of all faiths were prime targets, as religion was viewed by the Bolsheviks as incompatible with Communist ideology and as a source of alternative authority that competed with the state. Jewish communal leaders — rabbis, communal workers, Zionists, and Chassidic leaders — were specifically targeted through the Yevsektsiya (the Jewish section of the Communist Party) and through the Cheka/GPU.

By the time the GPU arrested the Rayatz in 1927, Dzerzhinsky had been dead for nearly a year — but the institution he created and its methods were functioning precisely as he had designed them. The "Iron Felix" statue stood in Lubyanka Square in Moscow until 1991, symbolizing the enduring legacy of his creation.
