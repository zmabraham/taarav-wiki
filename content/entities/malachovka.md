# Malachovka (Summer Retreat Near Moscow)

**Town near Moscow; summer retreat for the Rayatz and family during his time in Soviet Russia; site of some Chabad activity during warmer months.**

---

## Overview

Malachovka (also transliterated Malakhovka) is a town southeast of Moscow that served as a summer retreat for the Rayatz (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn) and his family during his years of leadership within the Soviet Union. During the Soviet period, when the Rayatz was based primarily in Leningrad and conducting underground Chabad activities across Russia, summers in Malachovka provided a slightly different environment — closer to Moscow, and with somewhat more freedom of movement than the city during the year.

Soviet-era Russian summer culture involved significant movement from cities to country dachas (summer homes), and this applied to Jewish communities as well. For the Rayatz and the Chabad community around him, summer retreats like Malachovka were used for study, meetings, and organizational work conducted in a somewhat less surveilled environment than the urban centers.

## Significance in Chabad History

Malachovka appears in the Rayatz's biography as one of the secondary locations of his Soviet-era activity — a place of relative respite within the broader context of underground leadership. The summer months in Malachovka allowed the Rebbe and his circle to gather, learn, and organize with slightly less intensity of surveillance than in Leningrad.

The town had a Jewish community during this period, and the Rayatz's presence there contributed to the religious life of that community. Like many similar summer settlements near major Russian cities, Malachovka's Jewish character reflected the patterns of Jewish migration within the Pale of Settlement and the nearby urban centers.
