# Spalerno Prison (Soviet Prison, Leningrad)

**Soviet prison in Leningrad where the Rayatz was held following his arrest in June 1927; site of the famous interrogation sessions with GPU Agent Lulav.**

---

## Overview

Spalerno Prison (also known as Shpalernaya or Spalernaya, from the street on which it was located) was a Soviet political prison in Leningrad. After his midnight arrest on June 15, 1927, Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz) was taken to Spalerno Prison, where he was held and interrogated by GPU agents, including the interrogator known as Agent Lulav.

The prison was one of the primary facilities used by the GPU/OGPU for holding political prisoners in Leningrad. Its location in the city made it convenient for the GPU's operational purposes, and its reputation for harsh conditions and effective interrogations was well established. Political prisoners — including religious leaders, academics, and opponents of the regime — passed through Spalerno's cells.

The Rayatz's time in Spalerno Prison — a period of solitary confinement, harsh conditions, and interrogation — became the setting for some of the most significant episodes in the "Undaunted" biography. His composure under these conditions, including the famous exchange with Agent Lulav about the pistol and the "two worlds and one G-d" response, became defining examples of Chabad's approach to mesirat nefesh (self-sacrifice) and the capacity of genuine faith to transcend physical threat.

## Significance in Chabad History

The imprisonment in Spalerno is one of the central episodes in the Rayatz's biography. The conditions were designed to break prisoners — isolation, fear, uncertainty about one's fate (the initial sentence was death), and interrogation sessions that mixed threats with offers of leniency. The Rayatz, according to his own accounts and the accounts of those who later recorded the events, maintained a composure and clarity throughout that astonished even his jailers.

The exchange with Agent Lulav — in which the GPU interrogator used a pistol as a threat and the Rayatz responded with the theological statement about having "two worlds and one G-d" — encapsulates the entire encounter between Soviet materialism and Chassidic spirituality. From the Chassidic perspective, the encounter was not primarily about physical survival but about the demonstration that there are forms of inner freedom that no prison can reach.

After the death sentence was commuted (following international pressure) to three years' exile in Kostroma, and then the exile was converted to expulsion from the USSR, the Rayatz left Soviet territory with his composure intact. The prison had tested him and, from the Chabad perspective, confirmed what his entire life had prepared him for.
