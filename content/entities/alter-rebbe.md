# Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (Alter Rebbe)

**Founder of Chabad-Lubavitch; author of the Tanya and the Shulchan Aruch HaRav; the first Lubavitcher Rebbe.**

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (1745–1812), known as the Alter Rebbe (the "Old Rebbe" in Yiddish), was the founder of the Chabad branch of Chassidism and the first Lubavitcher Rebbe. Born in Liozna (modern Belarus) in 1745, he was a disciple of Rabbi DovBer of Mezeritch (the Maggid), the primary successor of the Baal Shem Tov. From his teacher he received the full transmission of the nascent Chassidic movement, and he channeled it into what became Chabad — a system of Chassidism with a uniquely intellectual orientation.

The Alter Rebbe's foundational literary achievement is the Tanya (published 1796), also known as the Likkutei Amarim — a systematic work of Chassidic psychology, theology, and practical guidance that became the canonical text of Chabad. Alongside the Tanya, he composed the monumental Shulchan Aruch HaRav, a comprehensive halachic code. He was twice imprisoned by the Czarist government (1798 and 1800), accused by opponents (mitnagdim) of crimes against the state, and both times released — the first liberation (on 19 Kislev 1798) becoming the greatest annual celebration in Chabad. He established his court in Liadi, Belarus. When Napoleon invaded Russia in 1812, the Alter Rebbe — who opposed Napoleon despite the French offer of religious freedoms — fled eastward with his followers. He passed away during this flight on 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812) in Pyena.

## Life and Role

The Alter Rebbe's system of Chassidism — known by the acronym Chabad (Chochma, Bina, Da'at: Wisdom, Understanding, Knowledge) — distinguished itself from other forms of Chassidism by its emphasis on intellectual comprehension as the gateway to emotional transformation in divine service. Where other Chassidic masters emphasized simple faith and fervent prayer, the Alter Rebbe insisted that a person must understand what they believe before the belief can transform their heart. This approach, laid out systematically in the Tanya, became the defining characteristic of Chabad thought across all subsequent generations.

His twice-imprisonment became a defining narrative in Chabad history. The charges, brought by opponents of Chassidism and by rival communal leaders, reflected the deep tensions between the new Chassidic movement and traditional Ashkenazic Jewish leadership. Both times the government investigations found no criminal substance to the allegations and the Rebbe was released. The first release, on Yud-Tet Kislev (19 Kislev), is commemorated as the "New Year of Chassidism" — the day the Alter Rebbe was freed and the movement given, in effect, divine vindication.

His opposition to Napoleon is a frequently cited example of his broader worldview: the material freedoms Napoleon offered Jews came with spiritual dangers (assimilation, secularism), and the Alter Rebbe calculated that a Russian victory, despite its short-term harshness toward Jews, was spiritually preferable. He died before seeing the outcome of his choice, passing away in flight from Napoleon's armies.
