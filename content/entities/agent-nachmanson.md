# Agent Nachmanson (Soviet GPU)

**Soviet GPU secret police agent who led the midnight arrest of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn on June 15, 1927, in Leningrad — one of the defining moments in Chabad history.**

---

## Overview

Agent Nachmanson was the Soviet GPU (secret police) officer who led the operation to arrest Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn at midnight on June 15, 1927 (14-15 Sivan 5687) in Leningrad. The midnight arrest became one of the most famous moments in Chabad history — a scene that crystallized the confrontation between Soviet totalitarianism and Jewish religious commitment.

The GPU (Gosudarstvennoye Politicheskoye Upravleniye — State Political Directorate) was the Soviet secret police agency that succeeded the Cheka and preceded the OGPU/NKVD. Its operations against religious leaders — Jewish, Christian, and Muslim alike — were systematic and often brutal. Nachmanson's arrest of the Rayatz was part of a broader campaign to eliminate what the Soviets regarded as the organizational backbone of illegal religious activity.

## The Midnight Arrest

The arrest of the Rayatz at midnight on June 15, 1927 was not accidental in its timing — midnight arrests were a deliberate Soviet tactic designed to maximize disorientation and fear. Nachmanson and his agents entered the Rayatz's residence and took him into custody. The Rebbe, according to Chabad accounts, remained composed and dignified throughout the arrest and subsequent imprisonment — a composure that itself became legendary.

The charges against the Rayatz centered on his organization of illegal religious activity — specifically, the underground network of yeshivos, religious education, and Chassidic life that he had built to circumvent Soviet anti-religious laws. The GPU regarded these as political crimes. The initial sentence was death — reflecting how seriously the Soviets viewed the Rebbe's network as a threat to the regime's ideological goals.

Nachmanson's role was that of a Soviet functionary carrying out state policy, not a personal vendetta. But the historical weight of the arrest placed him, in Chabad memory, as the human face of the Soviet assault on Jewish life. International pressure, orchestrated by Jewish organizations and the American diplomatic corps, eventually secured the commutation of the death sentence and the Rayatz's eventual expulsion from the Soviet Union.
