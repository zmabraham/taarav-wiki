# Shimon Dimanstein (Yevsektsiya)

**Head of the Yevsektsiya (Jewish Section of the Soviet Communist Party); former yeshiva student turned Bolshevik; overseer of campaigns to destroy Jewish religious life.**

---

## Overview

Shimon Dimanstein (1886–1938) headed the Yevsektsiya — the Evreiskaya Sektsiya, the Jewish Section of the Soviet Communist Party — during its early years. The Yevsektsiya was a particularly vicious instrument of Soviet anti-religious policy because it was operated by Jewish Communists who used their knowledge of Jewish community life, language, and institutions to maximize the effectiveness of the assault on Jewish religious practice.

Dimanstein's personal background is striking: he was a product of the yeshiva world — trained in traditional Jewish learning — before his conversion to Bolshevism. This trajectory made him simultaneously knowledgeable about his targets and ideologically committed to destroying them. The Yevsektsiya under his oversight conducted raids on synagogues, organized show trials of religious leaders, forced the closure of chederim (Jewish schools) and yeshivos, and systematically dismantled the infrastructure of Jewish communal life in the Soviet Union.

Chabad was a primary target of the Yevsektsiya's activities, as the movement maintained the most organized network of underground religious education and the most determined resistance to Sovietization. The Yevsektsiya's campaign against Chabad intersected directly with the Rayatz's own underground operations and contributed to the environment that culminated in his 1927 arrest.

## Life and Role

The Yevsektsiya's operations were conducted in Yiddish — the language most Soviet Jews spoke — and used cultural and ideological tools specifically designed to appeal to and manipulate the Jewish population. Dimanstein oversaw propaganda campaigns framing Zionism, religious Orthodoxy, and the traditional kehilla (community) as class enemies. Show trials of rabbis and communal leaders were organized as public spectacles designed to discredit traditional Jewish authority.

The targeting of Chabad was particularly intense because the Rayatz's network was exactly what the Yevsektsiya most feared: an organized, covert, religiously-motivated resistance to Soviet cultural dominance. The Yevsektsiya's informer networks within Jewish communities were the primary mechanism through which Chabad underground activities were discovered and their participants arrested.

Dimanstein himself suffered the fate of many Soviet functionaries who knew too much and were associated with the wrong factions: he was arrested during Stalin's Great Purge and executed in 1938. This ironic fate — the persecutor becoming a victim of the same system he served — reflects the broader tragedy of those who built the Soviet repression apparatus.
