# Rebbetzin Chana Gurary

**Eldest daughter of the Rayatz; wife of Rabbi Shmaryahu Gurary (Rashag); survived Nazi Europe by escaping with her family in 1940.**

---

## Overview

Rebbetzin Chana Gurary was born in 1899 as the eldest daughter of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz) and Rebbetzin Nechama Dina. Her marriage to Rabbi Shmaryahu Gurary (the Rashag) in 1926 was a celebrated event in the Chabad world. As the eldest daughter, she held a position of seniority among the Rayatz's daughters, though the more prominent role in Chabad's ongoing leadership fell to her younger sister Chaya Mushka through her marriage to Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn.

Chana lived through the full trajectory of her father's leadership: the Soviet era, the exile in Riga and Warsaw, and the escape from Nazi Europe in 1940. She and the Rashag were among the family members who successfully escaped with the Rayatz to America. In contrast to the fate of her youngest sister Sheina — who perished in the Holocaust — Chana survived and built her life in Brooklyn. She passed away in 1974.

## Life and Role

Growing up as the eldest daughter of the sixth Rebbe, Chana experienced the dramatic upheavals of her father's leadership from an intimate vantage point. She was present in Riga (1927–1933) when the Rayatz rebuilt Chabad after his exile from the Soviet Union, and in Warsaw (1933–1939) during the Polish period. Her marriage to the Rashag in 1926 — before her younger sister Chaya Mushka's celebrated marriage to the Ramash in 1928 — was the first of the two great family unions that would define Chabad's leadership into the next generation.

The invasion of Poland in September 1939 and the subsequent occupation of Warsaw by Nazi Germany placed the entire Rayatz household in mortal danger. The rescue mission — orchestrated through American diplomatic channels, particularly through the efforts of Chabad representatives in Washington and American sympathizers — successfully extracted the Rayatz, his wife, both daughters in Poland, and their husbands. The youngest daughter Sheina was unable to escape and perished in the Holocaust. This tragedy marked the family's arrival in America with profound grief alongside relief.

In Brooklyn, Chana and the Rashag became part of the Chabad community centered at 770 Eastern Parkway. She outlived her father (d. 1950). Chana passed away in 1974, fourteen years before her sister Chaya Mushka.
