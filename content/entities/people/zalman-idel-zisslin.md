# Rabbi Zalman Idel Zisslin

Rabbi Shneiur Zalman Idel Zisslin (c. 5635/1875 – 11 Adar 5696/1936) was a Chassid of the Rebbe Rashab who served in close proximity to the Lubavitch court, as a copyist of Chassidic manuscripts and a printer of books.

## Biography

Born in Mglin (Chernigov region) to his mother Henya and father Rabbi Baruch Yehuda Leib, who served there as a government-appointed rabbi.

In 5660 (1900) he worked on proofreading and preparing the Tanya for publication. He resided in Lubavitch during certain periods, including 5673–5675 (1913–1915), and supervised young students at Tomchei Tmimim yeshiva.

Over the years he copied many Chassidic manuscripts (ma'amarim), including the long discourses "Yom Tov shel Rosh Hashana 5666" and "BeShaa sheHikdimu 5672." The Rebbe Rashab also engaged his services to copy the work "Yeraach Yakar." He was known for his meticulous and punctual nature — he asked about every detail, large or small, from the Rebbe Rashab.

After the Rebbe Rashab moved to Rostov, Zalman Idel followed and lived in a room in the Rebbe's house, becoming very close to the household. He helped save the Rebbe's property during difficult times.

In 5677 (1917) he printed the Rebbe Rashab's letter "Chasidei Hashem Azakir" as a single printed page, with textual corrections before printing. In 5678 (1918) he printed the Rostov Siddur.

In the summer of 5680 (1920), the Frierdiker Rebbe ordered him to burn manuscripts in the basement. When Zalman Idel told students about this, they discovered notes and writings of the Frierdiker Rebbe. He did not burn all the manuscripts; the Frierdiker Rebbe was displeased, saying: "This touches my very life — how could he not have mercy on his own life?" He also printed copies of the Rebbe Rashab's tzava'ah (ethical will) and distributed it among Chassidim, adding a clause for the Frierdiker Rebbe, which the latter objected to.

In 5681 (1921) he was one of the directors of Tomchei Tmimim yeshiva in Rostov. When the yeshiva directors were arrested, he was also detained for five days. He worked to maintain the yeshiva as an "academic institution" eligible for government funding.

In 5696 (1935–36) he wrote to the Frierdiker Rebbe proposing to reprint the Siddur Tehillat Hashem and raised questions about its text. He also suggested the notation of Chabad melodies in musical notation — a project that later came to fruition with the publication of the Sefer HaNigunim.

He passed away as a bachelor on 11 Adar 5696 and was buried near Rabbi Chaim Ben-Zion Raskin.
