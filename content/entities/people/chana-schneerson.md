# Rebbetzin Chana Schneerson

**Born**: 1880, Ukraine
**Died**: 6 Tishrei 5725 (September 23, 1964), New York
**Maiden name**: Yanovsky
**Husband**: [[entities/people/levi-yitzchak-schneerson|Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson]]
**Son**: [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (seventh Rebbe)]]
**Other children**: Yisrael Aryeh Leib, and a daughter

---

## Overview

Rebbetzin Chana Schneerson (née Yanovsky) was the wife of [[entities/people/levi-yitzchak-schneerson|Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson]], Chief Rabbi of Dnepropetrovsk, and the mother of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe]]. She is remembered for her extraordinary devotion — following her husband into Soviet exile in Kazakhstan, sustaining him through deprivation, and preserving his scholarly legacy at great personal cost.

Her life story exemplifies the heroism of Jewish women under Soviet persecution. Without her courage and dedication, the works of her husband — Rabbi Levi Yitzchak — would have been lost, and the seventh Rebbe's early education might have been very different.

---

## Early Life and Family Background

**Birth and Family**: Chana Yanovsky was born in 1880 into a distinguished rabbinical family in Ukraine. The Yanovsky family was known for Torah scholarship and devotion to Chabad Chassidus. From an early age, she exhibited remarkable intelligence and spiritual sensitivity.

**Education**: Unlike many girls of her generation, Rebbetzin Chana received a solid Jewish education. She learned Torah, Chassidic teachings, and practical halakha (Jewish law). This education would later serve her well when she helped preserve her husband's scholarly works.

**Character**: Those who knew her described Rebbetzin Chana as possessing exceptional memory, keen intelligence, and deep devotion to Torah and mitzvot. Her home was always open to guests, and she was known for her hospitality and generosity.

---

## Marriage to Rabbi Levi Yitzchak

**The Match**: Chana Yanovsky married Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneerson, a brilliant young scholar who would become the Chief Rabbi of Dnepropetrovsk. The union brought together two distinguished Chabad families.

**Life in Dnepropetrovsk**: The couple settled in Dnepropetrovsk (Ekaterinoslav), where Rabbi Levi Yitzchak served as Chief Rabbi. Rebbetzin Chana supported her husband's work, managing their home and raising their children while he attended to the needs of the community.

**Raising the Future Rebbe**: Their son Menachem Mendel was born in 1902. Rebbetzin Chana played a central role in his early education, instilling in him the values that would later define his leadership. The seventh Rebbe's later emphasis on education and the role of women reflected his mother's influence.

---

## The Soviet Period and Arrest

**Maintaining Jewish Life**: As the Soviets tightened their grip on religious life, Rebbetzin Chana worked alongside her husband to maintain Jewish observance. She prepared kosher food, organized secret religious education for children, and supported her husband's clandestine religious activities.

**The NKVD and Arrest**: In 1939, the NKVD (Soviet secret police) arrested Rabbi Levi Yitzchak as part of a broader campaign against religious leaders. Rebbetzin Chana was left alone with her children, facing uncertainty and fear.

---

## The Extraordinary Decision: Voluntary Exile

When Rabbi Levi Yitzchak was sentenced to exile in Chi'ili, a remote settlement in Kazakhstan, Rebbetzin Chana made the extraordinary decision to follow him voluntarily into exile.

**Why She Went**: Most people in her position would have stayed behind to care for their children and maintain some semblance of normal life. The exile settlement was desperately poor, with inadequate shelter and little food. Following her husband meant sharing his fate under conditions that might well prove fatal. Yet she chose to go.

**The Journey**: The journey to Kazakhstan was arduous. Rebbetzin Chana traveled by train and then by primitive transportation across the vast steppe. She left behind her home, her community, and any comfort to share her husband's suffering.

**What She Left Behind**: She left behind her son Menachem Mendel (the future Rebbe), who had already escaped the Soviet Union. She left behind her daughter and other family members. She chose exile over separation from her husband.

---

## Life in Exile: Kazakhstan (1939-1944)

**Conditions in Chi'ili**: The settlement of Chi'ili was desperately poor. There was little food, inadequate shelter, no proper medical care, and no religious infrastructure. The steppe climate was harsh — freezing winters and scorching summers. Rebbetzin Chana endured all of this at her husband's side.

**The Homemade Ink**: The most famous story from their exile concerns the making of ink. Rabbi Levi Yitzchak was determined to continue his scholarly work despite his circumstances, but he lacked writing materials. Rebbetzin Chana gathered herbs and berries from the surrounding steppe and prepared homemade ink so that he could write his Torah insights.

**Preserving the Manuscripts**: The manuscripts produced with this homemade ink — later published as _Likkutei Levi Yitzchak_ — represent one of the great treasures of Kabbalistic literature. Without Rebbetzin Chana's devotion, these works would have been lost forever. She carefully preserved the manuscripts through the remaining exile years and beyond.

**Sustaining Her Husband**: Rebbetzin Chana cared for Rabbi Levi Yitzchak as his health declined from the harsh conditions. She cooked what little food was available, kept him warm, and provided emotional support. Her presence gave him the strength to continue his scholarly work despite the deprivation.

**Her Role in His Scholarship**: Remarkably, Rebbetzin Chana was not merely preserving her husband's work — she was participating in it. Her prodigious memory allowed her to recall and write down teachings that he composed but could not physically record. She became his scribe and editor.

---

## Rabbi Levi Yitzchak's Death and Its Aftermath

**The Final Days**: Rabbi Levi Yitzchak's health continued to decline through the exile years. Rebbetzin Chana cared for him until his final moments. He passed away on 20 Av 5704 (1944) in Alma-Ata, Kazakhstan, where they had been transferred for medical reasons.

**Arranging the Burial**: Under Soviet conditions, arranging a proper Jewish funeral was extremely difficult. Rebbetzin Chana somehow managed to secure a Jewish burial for her husband in Alma-Ata. The gravesite would later become a place of pilgrimage.

**Preserving the Manuscripts**: After her husband's death, Rebbetzin Chana's primary mission became preserving his manuscripts. These writings — produced with homemade ink during exile — represented her husband's life's work. She guarded them carefully, knowing they would one day be published.

---

## Escape from the Soviet Union

After World War II, Rebbetzin Chana faced the daunting task of escaping the Soviet Union with her husband's manuscripts. The journey was perilous and required enormous courage and resourcefulness.

**The Escape Route**: Rebbetzin Chana eventually made her way out of the Soviet Union through connections and arrangements that remain partly shrouded in mystery. The escape involved bribes, dangerous travel, and the constant risk of arrest.

**Preserving the Manuscripts**: Throughout her escape, she guarded the precious manuscripts. These handwritten pages, produced with homemade ink during the exile years, represented her husband's scholarly legacy. She refused to leave them behind despite the risk.

**Reaching the West**: After a harrowing journey, Rebbetzin Chana reached the West. She had survived exile, her husband's death, and escape from the Soviet Union — all while preserving his writings.

---

## Life in America: Reunion with Her Son

**Settling in New York**: Rebbetzin Chana eventually settled in New York, near her son Rabbi Menachem Mendel — the seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe. After years of separation and suffering, mother and son were reunited.

**The Rebbe's Reverence**: The seventh Rebbe expressed deep love and reverence for his mother throughout her years in America. He visited her regularly, was attentive to her needs, and honored her in numerous ways. Their relationship was one of mutual respect and affection.

**Sharing Her Memories**: In her later years, Rebbetzin Chana recorded her memories of the Soviet period and exile. These memoirs provide a rare firsthand account of Jewish life under Soviet persecution and the extraordinary mesiras nefesh (self-sacrifice) of religious Jews under communism.

**Publishing the Manuscripts**: Rebbetzin Chana oversaw the beginning of the publication process for her husband's works. The first volumes of _Likkutei Levi Yitzchak_ were published during her lifetime, allowing the world to access the teachings she had preserved at such great cost.

---

## Character and Qualities

**Prodigious Memory**: Rebbetzin Chana was known for her extraordinary memory. She could recall verbatim Torah teachings, conversations, and events from decades earlier. This memory allowed her to preserve her husband's teachings and later record her memoirs in vivid detail.

**Spiritual Sensitivity**: Despite not having the formal education of a scholar, Rebbetzin Chana possessed profound spiritual understanding. Her insights into Torah and Chassidus were respected by scholars, including her son the Rebbe.

**Courage and Devotion**: Her courage in following her husband into exile, preserving his works under dangerous conditions, and escaping the Soviet Union demonstrated extraordinary devotion. She literally risked her life for Torah and for her husband's legacy.

**Humility**: Despite her remarkable achievements and her status as the Rebbe's mother, Rebbetzin Chana remained humble. She saw herself simply as doing what was necessary — no more, no less.

---

## Passing and Legacy

**Death**: Rebbetzin Chana passed away on 6 Tishrei 5725 (September 23, 1964) in New York. The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] mourned her deeply.

**The Yahrzeit**: The date of her passing, 6 Tishrei, is observed in Chabad as a significant yahrzeit. The Kinus HaShluchos (annual convention of Chabad women emissaries) is often held in proximity to her yahrzeit, reflecting her status as a model of Jewish womanhood and devotion.

**Her Memoirs**: Rebbetzin Chana's memoirs, recording her experiences during the Soviet period and exile, were published and provide a rare firsthand account of Jewish life under Soviet persecution. These memoirs are studied for their historical value and spiritual inspiration.

**The Manuscripts**: The manuscripts she preserved — _Likkutei Levi Yitzchak_ — continue to be published and studied. New volumes appear regularly, containing teachings that Rebbetzin Chana saved at enormous personal cost.

**Role Model**: Rebbetzin Chana has become a role model for Jewish women, particularly in Chabad. Her example of devotion to Torah, support for her husband's scholarship, and courage under persecution inspires new generations.

---

## Stories and Anecdotes

**The Homemade Ink**: The most famous story concerns Rebbetzin Chana making ink from herbs and berries in the Kazakhstan steppe so that her husband could write Torah. This act symbolizes her entire approach — doing whatever was necessary to preserve Torah under any conditions.

**The Rebbe's Visits**: The seventh Rebbe visited his mother regularly in her later years. Those who witnessed these visits noted his profound respect and attentiveness to her needs. He would often consult her on matters relating to the history of Soviet Jewry.

**The Hidden Scholar**: Despite not having a formal scholarly education, Rebbetzin Chana had deep Torah knowledge. She once corrected a scholar who was misquoting a passage from her husband's works — demonstrating that she had internalized the teachings she had preserved.

**The Blessing**: According to a story, Rebbetzin Chana once blessed a woman who was struggling with fertility. The woman later had a child, and attributed the blessing to Rebbetzin Chana's prayers.

---

## The Annual Kinus HaShluchos

The Kinus HaShluchos (International Conference of Women Emissaries), held annually in New York, often takes place near Rebbetzin Chana's yahrzeit. This timing is deliberate, honoring her as the paradigm of a Chabad woman — devoted to Torah, supportive of her husband's work, and courageous in the face of adversity.

Thousands of Shluchos from around the world gather each year to draw inspiration from her example. Her life story demonstrates the vital role that women play in preserving Jewish life and scholarship, often without recognition or public honor.
