# Rabbi Dr. Leo Baeck — ד"ר ליאו בק

**Born**: 1873, Lissa (Poznań), German Empire  
**Died**: 1956, London  
**Role**: Chief rabbi of the Liberal Jewish community of Berlin; leader of German Jewry during the Nazi period; theology professor  
**Major Work**: _The Essence of Judaism_ (Das Wesen des Judentums)

---

## Overview

Rabbi Dr. Leo Baeck was the foremost representative of Liberal (Reform) Judaism in Germany and — particularly during the Nazi years — became the recognized leader of German Jewry as a whole, serving as chairman of the _Reichsvertretung der Deutschen Juden_ (Representative Council of German Jews). He was a theologian, rabbi, and communal leader of extraordinary personal courage.

From a Chabad perspective, the most significant documented contact involves Baeck's unlikely collaboration with [[entities/people/meir-hildesheimer|Rabbi Dr. Meir Hildesheimer]] (an Orthodox leader) to help free the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] from Soviet imprisonment in 1927.

---

## Background

Baeck studied at the Breslau Jewish Theological Seminary and the Hochschule für die Wissenschaft des Judentums in Berlin. He received a doctorate in philosophy from the University of Berlin. His intellectual work — particularly _The Essence of Judaism_ (1905) — defended Judaism against the dismissive characterizations of liberal Protestant theologians (particularly Adolf von Harnack's _The Essence of Christianity_), arguing for Judaism's spiritual depth and continuing relevance.

He served as rabbi in various communities before becoming the leading figure of Berlin Liberal Jewry. By the 1920s he was widely acknowledged as one of the most important Jewish religious thinkers in Germany.

---

## The 1927 Intervention for the Rayatz

In 1927, when the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] was arrested by the Soviet GPU and sentenced to death (later commuted), German Jewish leaders mobilized to help. In an extraordinary example of cross-denominational cooperation:

**Rabbi Dr. Meir Hildesheimer** (head of the Orthodox Hildesheimer Rabbinical Seminary) and **Dr. Leo Baeck** (leader of Berlin Liberal Jewry) jointly petitioned Dr. **Oskar Cohn** — a socialist Reichstag member with personal connections to Soviet Foreign Commissar Chicherin — to intervene.

That these two figures — representing opposite ends of the German Jewish religious spectrum — acted together in this cause speaks to the profound sense of Jewish solidarity that transcended religious divisions when a Jewish leader's life was threatened.

---

## Leadership During the Nazi Period

After Hitler's rise to power (1933), Baeck became the most prominent figure of German Jewish communal leadership. He chaired the _Reichsvertretung der Deutschen Juden_ (1933–1943), representing all German Jewish denominations to the Nazi government and to international Jewish organizations. He consistently refused offers to emigrate, choosing to remain with his community.

He was ultimately deported to **Theresienstadt** concentration camp in 1943. He survived the war. After liberation, he immigrated to Britain, where he taught at the Hebrew Union College in Cincinnati and at other institutions.

---

## Theological Legacy

Baeck's theology — centered on the ethical demands of Judaism and the prophetic tradition — influenced Liberal Judaism throughout the 20th century. Leo Baeck College in London (founded 1956) was named in his honor and continues to train Liberal rabbis.

---

## Relationship to Chabad

The 1927 intervention represents the only documented direct intersection between Leo Baeck and the Chabad world. Its significance is that it demonstrates:
1. The willingness of non-Orthodox leaders to intercede for an Orthodox leader in danger
2. The breadth of the coalition assembled to free the Rayatz
3. The common Jewish interests that could unite Chabad's Rayatz and Germany's most prominent liberal rabbi
