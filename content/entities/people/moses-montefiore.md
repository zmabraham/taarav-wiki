# Sir Moses Montefiore

**Born**: October 24, 1784, Livorno, Italy  
**Died**: July 28, 1885, Ramsgate, England  
**Role**: British Jewish philanthropist and statesman; provided crucial support to the Chabad community in Hebron after the 1834 riots

---

## Overview

Sir Moses Montefiore was the most prominent Jewish figure in Victorian Britain — a financier, politician, and philanthropist who dedicated much of his long life (he lived to 101) to the welfare of Jewish communities worldwide. He was knighted by Queen Victoria and served as Sheriff of London. His seven visits to Eretz Yisrael made him one of the most significant figures in the history of Jewish settlement there.

---

## Connection to Chabad Hebron

Following the devastating [[events/hebron-pogrom-1834|1834 Hebron Riots]] — when Arab unrest connected to the Egyptian occupation under Ibrahim Pasha resulted in violent attacks on the Jewish community — the Chabad community in Hebron suffered grievous losses and faced an uncertain future.

Sir Moses Montefiore's subsequent philanthropic support helped the community rebuild. His intervention was part of his broader effort to support Jewish communities throughout Eretz Yisrael that were vulnerable to violence and poverty.

While Montefiore himself was not a Chabad chassid (he was a Sephardic Jew from a distinguished Italian family), his support for the Hebron community regardless of its particular religious affiliation demonstrated the pan-Jewish solidarity that characterized his philanthropic work.

---

## Significance

Montefiore's role in the rebuilding of the Hebron Chabad community after 1834 illustrates a recurring pattern in Chabad history: outside support — whether from wealthy philanthropists, sympathetic governments, or international Jewish organizations — playing a critical role at moments of crisis. The Chabad community's ability to leverage such support without compromising its identity was a key survival skill.
