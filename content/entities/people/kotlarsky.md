# Rabbi Moshe Kotlarsky

**Born**: c. 1950, Brooklyn, New York  
**Role**: Vice chairman of Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch; director of Chabad's global shlichus infrastructure

---

## Overview

Rabbi Moshe Kotlarsky is one of the key administrators of the global Chabad movement, serving as vice chairman of Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch (the educational arm of Chabad) and as the primary coordinator of the worldwide network of Chabad shluchim. He is the principal organizer of the annual Kinus HaShluchim (International Conference of Chabad Emissaries) in Brooklyn, the largest annual gathering of Chabad leadership.

---

## Role Under the Seventh Rebbe

Born in Brooklyn, Kotlarsky became a trusted administrator under the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]], working within the central Chabad organizational structure. While most prominent Chabad figures of his generation were sent out as shluchim to distant cities and countries, Kotlarsky's role was equally critical: managing the global infrastructure that supported and coordinated the thousands of shluchim in the field.

---

## Coordinating the Global Network

Kotlarsky's responsibilities include:

- **Supporting new shluchim** — helping young couples establish themselves in new locations, providing guidance, resources, and institutional backing
- **Coordinating between shluchim** — managing the complex relationships and jurisdictions of thousands of Chabad centers worldwide
- **Fundraising and development** — securing resources for Chabad operations globally
- **Representing Chabad** at communal and governmental events

---

## The Kinus HaShluchim

Perhaps Kotlarsky's most visible role is organizing the annual **Kinus HaShluchim** (International Conference of Chabad Emissaries), held each year in Brooklyn around the anniversary of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]]'s passing. The Kinus brings together thousands of shluchim from around the world for workshops, networking, inspiration, and the famous group photograph. Kotlarsky serves as the master of ceremonies for the grand banquet, which has become one of the signature events of the global Chabad movement.

---

## Significance

Kotlarsky represents the essential but often less visible administrative backbone of the [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] enterprise. The global Chabad network — with thousands of centers in over 100 countries — requires sophisticated coordination, and Kotlarsky has been at the center of that effort for decades.
