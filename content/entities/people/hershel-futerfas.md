# Rabbi Hershel Futerfas

**Born**: Early 20th century, Ukraine
**Role**: Soviet-era Chabad chassid; brother of Reb Mendel Futerfas

---

## Overview

Rabbi Hershel Futerfas was a Soviet-era Chabad chassid and the brother of the legendary Reb [[entities/people/mendel-futerfas|Mendel Futerfas]]. While his brother's story is more widely documented, Reb Hershel also participated in the underground network that maintained Jewish life under Soviet persecution, demonstrating the same commitment to Torah and mesirus nefesh that characterized his generation.

The Futerfas brothers — like many sibling pairs in Soviet-era Chabad — represent how entire families were committed to maintaining Jewish life under impossible conditions. Their individual stories, intertwined yet distinct, paint a picture of communal resistance and spiritual heroism.

---

## Family Background

Reb Hershel was born in Ukraine in the early 20th century into the Futerfas family, which was devoted to Chabad Chassidus.

**The Futerfas Family**: The Futerfas family was part of the network of Chabad families that kept the tradition alive through the Soviet period. Like other such families, they transmitted Torah values to their children despite the official suppression of religion.

**Brother to Reb Mendel**: Hershel was the brother of Reb [[entities/people/mendel-futerfas|Mendel Futerfas]], who would become one of the most famous Soviet-era chassidim. The brothers shared a commitment to Torah and Jewish life, though their paths would diverge.

---

## Underground Activities

Reb Hershel, like his brother, was active in the Chabad underground network that maintained Jewish religious life throughout the Soviet Union.

**Teaching and Religious Activities**: Reb Hershel participated in clandestine religious activities — teaching Torah, organizing secret minyanim, facilitating circumcisions, and maintaining Jewish observances under conditions of constant danger.

**Network Operations**: The Soviet underground was a sophisticated operation requiring coordination between individuals and communities. Reb Hershel played a role in maintaining these connections, helping to keep scattered Jewish communities linked together.

---

## The Soviet Experience

Life as a religious Jew in the Soviet Union required extraordinary commitment, ingenuity, and courage.

**Constant Danger**: Participation in underground religious activities carried the constant risk of arrest, interrogation, and imprisonment. The NKVD (later KGB) maintained extensive surveillance of religious activists.

**Resourcefulness**: Maintaining Jewish life under Soviet conditions required extraordinary resourcefulness — obtaining religious books that were officially banned, finding ways to perform circumcisions in secret, arranging for kosher food, and coordinating communications between scattered communities.

---

## Relationship with Reb Mendel

While Hershel and Mendel were both committed to maintaining Jewish life under Soviet conditions, their stories took different paths.

**Complementary Roles**: The brothers likely played complementary roles in the underground network. While Reb Mendel's story is more widely documented and includes time in the Vorkuta labor camp, Reb Hershel's contributions, though less documented, were no less valuable.

**Family Bonds**: The Futerfas brothers, like many families in the Soviet underground, drew strength from their family bonds. These connections provided emotional support and shared purpose in the face of persecution.

---

## Legacy

Reb Hershel Futerfas is remembered as one of the many Soviet-era chassidim who kept Judaism alive through decades of persecution. Though his story is less documented than his brother's, his contribution was significant.

**The Unsung Heroes**: Many Soviet-era chassidim never had their full stories recorded. Their heroism lives on through oral tradition and the testimony of those who knew them. They represent the foundation upon which contemporary Jewish life was rebuilt.

**Family Legacy**: The Futerfas family — including both Hershel and Mendel — represents how entire families were committed to maintaining Torah and Chassidus under impossible conditions. Their legacy continues to inspire contemporary Jews.
