# Rabbi Shlomo of Karlin (ר' שלמה מקארלין)

**Chassidic master and disciple of Aaron the Great of Karlin; expelled from Karlin by Mitnagdim; founded independent chassidic dynasty**

## Biography

Rabbi Shlomo of Karlin was among the most important early chassidic leaders in the Karlin-Pinsk region of Lithuania-Belarus. He was the primary disciple of Rabbi Aaron the Great of Karlin (Reb Ahrele), who was himself a celebrated disciple of the Maggid of Mezeritch. When Reb Ahrele died young in 1772, Rabbi Shlomo became the de facto leader of the Karlin chassidic following.

The Karlin chassidic tradition was known for its passionate, fiery style of prayer — loud, physically expressive davening that drew particular hostility from the Mitnagdic establishment, which saw it as undignified and potentially dangerous. The Vilna Gaon's 1772 herem (ban of excommunication) against Chassidim in Vilna was followed by waves of persecution against Chassidic communities throughout Lithuania, and the Karlin community was among those targeted.

Around תקל"ד–תקל"ה (1774–1775), Rabbi Shlomo was forcibly expelled from Karlin by Mitnagdic opponents. The expulsion was not merely a social sanction but a physical removal — the Mitnagdim used communal pressure, in some cases backed by local authorities, to drive Chassidic leaders out of their towns. Rabbi Shlomo relocated to Ludmir (Vladimir-Volynsky), where he continued his leadership.

Rabbi Shlomo of Karlin was martyred in 1792, reportedly killed during the Kosciuszko Uprising when Cossack forces attacked the town of Ludmir. He became a celebrated figure in chassidic tradition, honored both for his spiritual depth and for dying al kiddush Hashem (in sanctification of God's name).

His connection to early Chabad history lies in the broader context of Mitnagdic persecution: the same wave of opposition that expelled him from Karlin was the environment in which the Alter Rebbe operated. The Toldos Chabad BeRussia source records the expulsions of 1774–75 as a coordinated period of anti-Chassidic action that affected multiple communities simultaneously.

## Significance

Rabbi Shlomo of Karlin's expulsion is a case study in the methods and scope of Mitnagdic persecution during the 1770s. The Karlin school's distinctive prayer style made it an especially visible target, but its fate was symptomatic of the broader campaign to uproot Chassidism from traditional Jewish communities. His martyrdom in 1792 transformed him into a symbol of Chassidic suffering at the hands of both Jewish opponents and external violence, and the Karlin-Stolin dynasty he founded continues to this day.
