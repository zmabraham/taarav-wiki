# Rabbi Chaim Schneur Zalman Schneersohn of Liadi

**Born**: 1814  
**Died**: 1880  
**Father**: [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]]  
**Court**: Liadi, Belarus

---

## Overview

Rabbi Chaim Schneur Zalman Schneersohn was a son of the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] who established the Liadi branch of Chabad after his father's passing in 1866. He was named after the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] (Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi, the founder of Chabad), and in a poetically resonant move, he returned to settle in Liadi — the town from which the Alter Rebbe had led the movement.

---

## The Liadi Branch

After the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek's]] passing, when each of his sons established independent courts, Rabbi Chaim Schneur Zalman chose Liadi as his seat. This was symbolically significant: Liadi was the Alter Rebbe's town, the original center of the Chabad movement before the upheavals of the Napoleonic Wars forced the Alter Rebbe's flight eastward.

The Liadi branch maintained Chabad practice and study but operated as a distinct court from [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], where the [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Maharash]] served as primary Rebbe. Like the other non-Lubavitch branches, the Liadi line gradually diminished over the following generations as the Lubavitch branch consolidated its position as the central Chabad movement.

---

## Significance

Rabbi Chaim Schneur Zalman's return to Liadi represents an attempt to reclaim the original geographic and spiritual center of Chabad. His very name — identical to the founder's — and his choice of location suggest a conscious identification with the movement's origins.
