# Sheina Horowitz

**Born**: c. 1906 (some sources indicate c. 1907 or later)
**Died**: 2nd day of Rosh Hashanah 5703 (September 1942), Treblinka extermination camp
**Role**: Third daughter of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]; killed in the Holocaust
**Also known as**: Sheina Horenstein (née Schneersohn)

---

## Overview

Sheina Horowitz (Horenstein) was the daughter of [[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn]] (the sixth Rebbe) and [[entities/people/nechama-dina|Rebbetzin Nechama Dina]]. She was murdered in the Holocaust along with her husband, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Horenstein — a tragedy that left her father in inconsolable grief. Her death at Treblinka stands as one of the most personal devastations in the Rayatz's life.

---

## Early Life and Family

Sheina grew up in the Rayatz's household during the tumultuous years of the Russian Revolution and the Rebbe's underground activities on behalf of Soviet Jewry. In a letter the Rayatz wrote from Riga after his expulsion from the Soviet Union, he described renting two apartments for his family: "The upper floor is my dwelling with my daughters Chaya Mushka and Sheina, may they live." Sheina was thus one of the two unmarried daughters who accompanied the Rayatz into exile.

Her older sister [[entities/people/chana-gurary|Chana]] had married Rabbi Shmaryahu Gurary, and her other sister [[entities/people/rebbetzin-moussia|Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka]] would later marry the [[entities/people/rebbe|Lubavitcher Rebbe]] (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson). Sheina was the youngest of the three daughters.

---

## Marriage

Four years after Chaya Mushka's wedding, Sheina married **Rabbi Menachem Mendel Horenstein** (also spelled Horowitz/Hornstein) on 10 Sivan 5692 (1932). The wedding was held in **Landwarow** (Landwarowa), Poland — a town situated between Warsaw and Vilna, approximately twenty minutes from the Vilna train station. The ceremony took place during the Rayatz's Polish period (1933–1939), when he had relocated Chabad's center to Warsaw and Otwock following his expulsion from Riga.

The choice of Landwarow for the wedding reflected the Chabad community's presence in the region and the network of Polish Jewish communities that supported the Rayatz's court.

---

## Life in Poland

Following the wedding, Rabbi Mendel and Sheina Horenstein remained in Poland. The Rayatz's court was based in Otwock, a resort town near Warsaw where the Rebbe maintained his residence and conducted the affairs of the Chabad movement throughout the 1930s. During this period, the Horensteins were part of the intimate circle of the Rebbe's family.

---

## The Holocaust

When Nazi Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, the Rayatz was evacuated through extraordinary diplomatic effort (see [[events/escape-from-warsaw-1939|Escape from Warsaw (1939)]]). But Sheina and her husband were unable to escape. They were caught in the Nazi net that swept through Polish Jewry with devastating completeness.

According to the Chabad historical record in _Toldot Chabad bePolin, Lita, veLatvia_, efforts were made to rescue the Horensteins, but this rescue operation failed. The fact of their deaths was concealed from the Rayatz for a time to spare him additional anguish.

Sheina was murdered on the **second day of Rosh Hashanah 5703 (1942)** at the **Treblinka** extermination camp. Approximately one month later, her husband Rabbi Menachem Mendel Horenstein was also killed at the same camp. The precision of this date — rare in Holocaust records — suggests that testimony from survivors reached the Chabad community.

---

## Her Father's Grief

The Rayatz never recovered from Sheina's loss. According to [[summaries/rayatz-biography|the biography *Undaunted*]], after the war, the Rayatz sat with photographs of his fallen Chasidim — including Sheina and Rabbis [[entities/people/yechezkel-feigin|Feigin]] and others killed by the Nazis — weeping uncontrollably. Only visits from relatives brought him temporary relief from grief.

He carried her memory throughout the final years of his life in Brooklyn. Her sister Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka Schneerson wore Sheina's ring for the rest of her life — a small, private memorial to the sister lost in the flames of Treblinka. According to one account, the Rebbe gave the ring to Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka with instructions to bury it with her, and it was placed in her coffin at her passing.
