# Czar Nicholas I of Russia

**Born**: July 6, 1796, Gatchina, Russia  
**Died**: March 2, 1855, Saint Petersburg  
**Role**: Russian Emperor during the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] era; implemented the infamous Cantonist decrees and government-sponsored Haskalah policies

---

## Overview

Czar Nicholas I ruled the Russian Empire from 1825 to 1855 — a period that largely coincided with the leadership of the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] (1827-1866). His reign was the most oppressive era for Russian Jewry in the 19th century, marked by systematic efforts to undermine Jewish communal life, coerce religious conversion, and forcibly integrate Jews into Russian society.

---

## The Cantonist System

Nicholas I's most notorious anti-Jewish policy was the Cantonist system (introduced 1827). Under this decree:

- Jewish communities were required to supply boys — officially aged 12 and above, though in practice often younger — for **25 years** of military service
- The explicit goal was to separate Jewish children from their families, communities, and religious life
- Boys were sent to remote military preparatory schools (_cantonist battalions_) where they were subjected to intense pressure to convert to Christianity
- Communities faced impossible quotas; the burden fell disproportionately on the poor, as wealthier families could often buy exemptions

The Cantonist system was one of the great traumas of Russian Jewish history. The [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] waged a sustained campaign against it, using both behind-the-scenes advocacy and public moral leadership.

---

## The 1843 Rabbinical Assembly

In 1843, Nicholas I convened a government Rabbinical Assembly (_Rabbinic Commission_) in Saint Petersburg, ostensibly to consult Jewish leaders on communal reform. In reality, the government sought to impose its own modernization agenda — including government-sponsored Jewish schools designed to spread the Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment) and weaken traditional religious life.

The [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] attended this assembly and, at great personal risk, resisted the government's proposals. His principled stand earned him imprisonment and threats, but he refused to compromise on matters he saw as threatening Jewish religious life. This episode became a defining moment of [[concepts/mesirus-nefesh|mesirus nefesh]] in the Tzemach Tzedek's legacy.

---

## Government-Sponsored Haskalah

Nicholas I also established a network of government Jewish schools (_Kazionye uchilishcha_), designed and staffed by maskilim (Enlightenment-oriented Jews), with the explicit aim of drawing Jewish youth away from traditional Torah education. The Tzemach Tzedek and other traditional leaders viewed these schools as existential threats to Jewish religious continuity.
