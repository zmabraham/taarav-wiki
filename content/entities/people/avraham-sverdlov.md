# R. Avraham Sverdlov

A Chabad Chassid and educator who was active in the underground **Tomchei Temimim** yeshiva in **Odessa** during the Soviet era. He is remembered in the memoir *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* as a man of remarkable all-around scholarship and abilities, and as a victim of Soviet terror.

## Description

The memoir describes R. Avraham Sverdlov as **"a man of broad knowledge"** (*ish ashlot*):
- Expert in **Nigleh** (revealed Torah — Talmud and halacha)
- Expert in **Chassidus** (Chabad mystical teachings)
- **Outstanding preacher** (*peh mefi marganita bedrusha* — "a mouth that brings forth pearls in homiletical discourse")
- **First-class Torah reader** (baal kore of the highest caliber)

He was one of the organizers of a specially organized learning group (*machloket*) in Odessa for more advanced students like the memoir's narrator, who had outgrown the standard curriculum.

## Arrest and Martyrdom

In 5698 (1938) — at the height of Stalin's Great Terror — R. Avraham Sverdlov was **arrested in Leningrad** where he had been located. **His fate is unknown** (*v'akivotav lo nod'u* — "his footsteps were not known"), the memoir's way of recording that he disappeared into the Soviet prison system and was never heard from again. The memoir notes his death with the martyrological abbreviation **ה"יד** (*HaYD* — "may God avenge his blood").

## Significance

R. Avraham Sverdlov represents the cadre of Chabad educators who maintained the underground yeshiva at great personal risk. His disappearance in the 1938 purges was not unique — many Chabad rabbis and educators were arrested in that wave of terror.
