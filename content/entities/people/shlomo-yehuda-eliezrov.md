# Rabbi Shlomo Yehuda Leib Eliezrov — ר' שלמה יהודה ליב אליעזרוב

**Born**: 5623 / 1863, Jakobstadt (Jekabpils), Latvia  
**Role**: Chabad emissary (_shadar_) to Bukhara and Central Asia; first Chabad rabbi of Samarkand  
**Epithet**: "Baal Shem Tov of Bukhara"  
**Original surname**: Kazarnovsky (later changed to Ashkenazi, then Eliezrov — Bukharan-style patronymic)

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shlomo Yehuda Leib Eliezrov was the Chabad movement's primary emissary to the ancient Jewish communities of Central Asia — particularly the **Bukharan Jewish community** of present-day Uzbekistan and Tajikistan. Born in Latvia to a distinguished Chabad family, raised in Hebron, married into another Chabad family, and ultimately appointed as the first Chabad rabbi of **Samarkand**, he built institutions and transformed religious life in one of the most remote corners of the Jewish world.

---

## Family Background

**Ancestry**: His great-grandmother was **Rebbetzin Menucah Rachel Slonim** — a daughter of the **Mitteler Rebbe** (Rabbi DovBer Schneersohn, second Chabad Rebbe). This placed Eliezrov within the direct lineage of the Chabad dynasty, giving him intrinsic status in the Chassidic world.

**Father**: Rabbi Eliezer Shimon Kazarnovsky of Jakobstadt (Jekabpils), Latvia.

**The Name Changes**:
- Born: Kazarnovsky
- Changed to: **Ashkenazi** (wife's family name, taken upon marriage — unusual but documented)
- Final name: **Eliezrov** — a Bukharan-style patronymic (from "Eliezer," his father's name), adopted when he settled permanently in Central Asia

---

## Childhood in Hebron

When Eliezrov was approximately 10 years old, his family moved to **Hebron** in Ottoman Palestine — then home to an important Chabad community centered on the "Beis Menachem" shul and the grave of the patriarchs. Growing up in Hebron immersed him in both the Chabad community of the Holy Land and the broader Sephardic/Middle Eastern Jewish world — cultural preparation for his later Central Asian mission.

---

## Marriage

He married **Chana Bracha Ashkenazi** (on **11 Sivan 5640 / 1880**), daughter of Rabbi Dovber "Ber'l Kalisk" Ashkenazi. His father-in-law had learned both with the **Tzemach Tzedek** (third Chabad Rebbe) and with the **Maharash** (fourth Chabad Rebbe) — giving the family deep roots in Chabad's fourth and fifth generations.

---

## Co-founding Magen Avot Yeshiva in Hebron

Before his Central Asian mission, Eliezrov co-founded the **Magen Avot yeshiva** in Hebron together with:
- Rabbi Menachem Mendel Na'ah (his brother-in-law)
- Rabbi Shneur Zalman Slonim (his cousin — who later served 37 years as rabbi of the Tel Aviv-Jaffa Chabad community)

This yeshiva served the Chabad community of Hebron and was one of several institutions that made Hebron a center of Chabad activity in 19th-century Palestine.

---

## The Bukharan Mission: Shadar Years

Beginning approximately 5647 (1887) or earlier, Eliezrov undertook **shadar** (shaliach d'rabbanan — fundraising emissary) trips to Bukhara and Georgia on behalf of Hebron's Chabad institutions.

**Stopover at Lubavitch**: During one of these early journeys (documented in Chanukah 5653 / December 1892), he stopped at Lubavitch and stayed as a guest in the **Rashab's** courtyard.

**First arrival in Bukhara**: Documented in a letter from the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] dated **24 Menachem Av 5653 (1893)**, granting him support and authorization for his mission.

The Rashab wrote him multiple letters of support throughout his shlichus, recognizing the critical importance of his work among the isolated Bukharan community.

---

## Permanent Settlement in Central Asia (5657–5658 / 1897–1898)

Around 5657 (1897), Eliezrov transitioned from periodic shadar visits to permanent residence in the Bukhara region. He was first posted in **Shakhrisabz** (a city in present-day Uzbekistan, south of Samarkand).

---

## Appointment as Rav of Samarkand (5658 / 1898)

On **20 Iyar 5658 (May 1898)**, Eliezrov was formally appointed as **Rav of Samarkand** — the largest and most important Jewish community center in Russian Central Asia. The appointment was arranged through the intervention of the **Rishon L'Tzion** (Sephardic Chief Rabbi of Jerusalem), **Rabbi Yaakov Shaul Elyashar** ("Yisha Brachah" — the author known by his responsa collection's name).

**The Contract Terms**:
- Three-year term
- Annual salary of **2,400 rubles**
- Agreement to rule according to **Sephardic custom** (Shulchan Aruch / Maran) — with permission to be stringent according to Ashkenazic practice where the Shulchan Aruch explicitly permits more stringency

The agreement to follow Sephardic custom was essential: the Bukharan Jews had a distinct Sephardic-adjacent tradition, and a rabbi insisting on Ashkenazic practice would have been unable to serve effectively.

**Letter of Support**: The appointment received enthusiastic endorsement from:
- The **Chafetz Chaim's** close associate whose **Shadei Chamad** (an encyclopedic halakhic work) praised Eliezrov (5658)
- The **Aruch HaShulchan** (Rabbi Yechiel Michel Epstein) who called him: "a great and distinguished rabbi, sharp and expert, a pious and holy man, a descendant of the Baal HaTanya" — connecting him explicitly to the Chabad Rebbes

---

## Reforming the Samarkand Kehilla

Eliezrov found the ritual practices of Samarkand's Jewish community significantly compromised. His most dramatic intervention was in **shechita** (ritual slaughter):

**The Shechita Crisis**: Discovering that the existing shochetim (ritual slaughterers) were not following proper halachic standards, Eliezrov declared all the meat in Samarkand **treif** (non-kosher). He then:
1. Trained new, properly qualified shochetim
2. Built a new slaughterhouse with proper gender separation
3. Established a proper kosher food supply for the community

**The Shochtim's Counterattack**: The displaced shochetim fought back fiercely:
- They attempted to have him arrested by the **Amir** (the local ruler)
- When the Amir encountered Eliezrov, he was so struck by his spiritual bearing and countenance that he embraced him instead of arresting him
- The shochetim then attempted **poisoning** — sending poisoned food to his home. The family's **dog** ate the food and died, saving Eliezrov's life

These episodes contributed to his reputation as a miracle worker — the "**Baal Shem Tov of Bukhara**."

---

## Halakhic Work

**Responsum on the Zerafshan River** (5660 / 1900): Eliezrov composed a detailed halakhic responsum about the rivers of Samarkand for the purposes of **gittin** (Jewish divorce documents), which must specify the city's water sources with precision. He analyzed:
- The **Zerafshan River** and its tributaries
- Local geographical details relevant to the halakhic formula

This responsum demonstrates both his serious halakhic scholarship and his deep familiarity with Central Asian geography.

---

## Institutional Building

During his tenure in Samarkand, Eliezrov founded:
- **Yeshivot** (advanced Torah study institutions)
- **Talmudei Torah** (elementary Torah schools)
- **Mikvaot** (ritual baths) — essential for Jewish family life

These institutions transformed the religious infrastructure of Samarkand's Jewish community.

---

## Return to Hebron and Appointment as Rav

After his three-year Samarkand contract ended (approximately 5661–5662 / 1901–1902), Eliezrov returned to Hebron. The **Shadei Chamad** played a role in facilitating his appointment as **Rav of Hebron** (approximately 5663 / 1903). The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] gave his enthusiastic blessing for this appointment — recognizing Eliezrov's exceptional qualities.

---

## Legacy

Rabbi Shlomo Yehuda Leib Eliezrov represents an unusual figure in Chabad history: an emissary who bridged the Chabad world of Eastern Europe and Palestine with the utterly different Jewish civilization of Central Asia. His work established a Chabad presence and transformed religious standards in Samarkand decades before the massive Chabad refugee influx of World War II.
