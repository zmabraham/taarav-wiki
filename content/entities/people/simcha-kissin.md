# Simcha Kissin

**Lived**: early 19th century, Vitebsk, Russia  
**Role**: Informant who filed denunciations against the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (1825), triggering his arrest

---

## Overview

Simcha Kissin of Vitebsk was the informant who filed the legal denunciations against Rabbi DovBer Schneersohn — the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] — with Russian imperial authorities in 1825, triggering one of the most significant crises in early Chabad history.

---

## The Denunciations

According to Russian imperial court documents reproduced in [[summaries/masar-vegeulat-hamtza|*Maasar V'Geulat Admur HaEmtza'i*]] (the primary-source documentary collection about the Mitteler Rebbe's arrest), Simcha Kissin filed charges accusing the Rebbe of:

1. **Collecting money for the Land of Israel** — which under Russian imperial law could be construed as seditious activity (directing funds outside the empire to a foreign territory)
2. Various other offenses related to his leadership of the Chabad community

The documentary collection reveals the bureaucratic machinery through which a private denunciation became an imperial investigation, involving multiple levels of Russian administration from local Vitebsk authorities up to the gubernia (provincial) court and ultimately the Imperial Senate in Petersburg.

---

## Motivation

The sources do not fully explain Kissin's motivation. In the context of early nineteenth-century Russian Jewish life, informants (known by the Hebrew term _malshin_ or _moser_) were a tragic feature of the community — individuals who for personal grievances, financial gain, or ideological opposition used the Russian imperial system against fellow Jews.

The Mitnagdim-Chassidim conflict that had riven Jewish communities for decades may have played a role — anti-Chassidic sentiment sometimes expressed itself through legal denunciation. Personal rivalry or resentment within the Jewish community may also have been factors.

---

## Historical Significance

Kissin's denunciation set in motion the chain of events that became one of the foundational trauma-and-triumph narratives of early Chabad history — the Mitteler Rebbe's arrest, detention, and three-stage legal exoneration (though the final exoneration came posthumously). The 10th of Kislev became a Chabad holiday of liberation, celebrating the Mitteler Rebbe's first release from custody.

See [[events/arrest-mitteler-rebbe|Arrest and Liberation of the Mitteler Rebbe (1826)]].
