# Yehuda Yafeh of Kapust

**Active**: early 19th century, Kapust (Kopys), Russia  
**Role**: Chabad chassid; printer in Kapust; official Russian-language translator for the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] during the 1825 investigation

---

## Overview

R' Yehuda Yafeh (son of R' Feivish Yafeh) was a Chabad chassid and printer residing in [[entities/places/kapust|Kapust]] (Kopys). During the 1825 investigation of the Mitteler Rebbe, he served as the Rebbe's official Russian-language translator and representative — signing official Russian documents on the Rebbe's behalf, since the Rebbe himself could not read or write Russian (he signed documents in Hebrew).

The source footnotes identify him as: "the chassid R' Yehuda son of R' Feivish Yafeh, the printer of Kapust." He is also noted as having served as translator for the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] during the Alter Rebbe's second arrest (see Kerem Chabad, p. 80).

---

## Role in the Investigation

When the investigators presented formal questions to the Mitteler Rebbe in October 1825, the Rebbe responded in Hebrew and signed in Hebrew. The investigators noted this as unusual under Russian law (Section 10 of the Jewish Statute of 1804 required rabbis to know Russian, Polish, or German). R' Yehuda Yafeh appeared on behalf of the Rebbe as the Russian signatory.

In a key document (13 October 1825), [[entities/people/markov-investigator|Court Counselor Markov]] required that the disputed Hebrew text of the distribution document be re-presented with a fresh Russian translation, signed by both the Rebbe and R' Yehuda Yafeh — with both accepting legal responsibility for translation accuracy. R' Yehuda Yafeh complied and his signed translation confirmed the correct reading of the disputed passage.

Later court documents reference that the Rebbe "signed in Hebrew, and instead of him, the resident of Kapust, Yehuda Yafeh, signed" — and this was used by Governor Khavanski as one of several arguments that the Rebbe should be disqualified from his rabbinical position.

---

## The Printer of Kapust

The identification of R' Yehuda Yafeh as the Kapust printer is significant. The printing house of Kapust was one of the important early Chabad printing operations, producing works of Chabad chassidus. His dual role as printer and community representative reflects the multi-faceted nature of leadership in small Chabad communities.
