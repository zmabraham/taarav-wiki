# Rabbi Menachem Mendel Futerfas

**Born**: 1906, Ukraine
**Died**: 1995, Israel
**Role**: Legendary Soviet Chabad chassid; mashpia (spiritual mentor); storyteller
**Imprisoned**: 1951–1956 (Soviet labor camps)

---

## Overview

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Futerfas was one of the most legendary Soviet-era Chabad chassidim. Known to all simply as "Reb Mendel," he spent decades maintaining underground Jewish life under Soviet persecution, survived five years in Siberian labor camps, and eventually escaped to freedom. His farbrengens (gatherings) were famous for his fiery stories of _mesirus nefesh_ (self-sacrifice) that inspired a new generation of Chassidim.

Reb Mendel's life reads like an adventure novel of spiritual heroism — from secret chadarim (schools) in Ukraine, to the Soviet gulag, to the refugee community in Shanghai, to building Chabad in England and finally Israel. His stories, told in his inimitable style, became the oral tradition through which later generations learned about Soviet-era mesirus nefesh.

---

## Early Life in Ukraine

Reb Mendel was born in Ukraine in 1906 into a Chabad family. From his earliest years, he was exposed to the world of underground Jewish life that had developed under Czarist and early Soviet rule.

**Childhood**: Even as a child, Reb Mendel demonstrated unusual spiritual commitment. Stories from his youth describe him walking great distances to hear a Torah class or obtain a religious book — both dangerous activities under Soviet rule.

**Education**: He studied in underground _cheder_ (Jewish school) as a child and later in the clandestine Tomchei Temimim network that operated throughout the Soviet Union. His teachers were the heroic _mashpiim_ who risked their lives daily to transmit Torah to the next generation.

---

## Underground Activities in the Soviet Union

As a young man, Reb Mendel became active in the Chabad underground network that maintained Jewish life under Soviet persecution. This was dangerous work that required constant vigilance and ingenuity.

**Teaching Torah**: Reb Mendel taught Torah in secret locations throughout Ukraine. This included organizing underground _chedarim_ for children, teaching groups of adults, and preparing boys for _bar mitzvah_ — all activities that could lead to arrest.

**Circumcision**: Performing _brit milah_ (circumcision) was particularly dangerous under the Soviets, who viewed it as mutilation. Reb Mendel participated in the clandestine network of _mohalim_ who performed circumcisions in secret, often at night, moving constantly to avoid detection.

**Holiday Observances**: Reb Mendel helped organize secret observances of Jewish holidays. This included arranging _matzah_ distribution for Passover, organizing secret _minyanim_ (prayer quorums) for the High Holidays, and facilitating other religious observances that could not be done publicly.

**Network Communications**: The Chabad underground maintained contact between communities through a network of messengers. Reb Mendel was part of this network, traveling between cities to deliver messages, funds, and instructions — all at great personal risk.

---

## Arrest and Imprisonment (1951–1956)

In 1951, the Soviet authorities finally caught up with Reb Mendel. He was arrested by the MGB (predecessor to the KGB) for "anti-Soviet activities" — essentially, for teaching Torah and maintaining Jewish religious life.

**Interrogation**: Like many Soviet-era prisoners, Reb Mendel underwent harsh interrogation. The authorities sought to extract information about the Chabad network and to pressure him to renounce religious activity. Despite the pressure, Reb Mendel refused to betray his fellow Chassidim or compromise his principles.

**The Sentence**: Reb Mendel was sentenced to 8 years in Siberian labor camps. He was sent to Vorkuta — one of the most notorious camp complexes in the Soviet gulag, located above the Arctic Circle.

---

## The Vorkuta Labor Camp Experience

Reb Mendel's five years in Vorkuta (he was released early after Stalin's death in 1953) became legendary among Chassidim. His stories from the camps — told with humor, wisdom, and profound spiritual insight — offer a window into both the horrors of the gulag and the resilience of the Jewish spirit.

**Conditions**: Vorkuta was a coal mining camp where prisoners worked in brutal conditions — freezing winters, inadequate food, and constant danger. Prisoners died from exposure, exhaustion, and accidents.

**Spiritual Resistance**: Despite conditions that would have broken most people, Reb Mendel maintained Jewish observance in the camps. He found creative ways to keep _kosher_, managed to pray when possible, and even taught Torah to other Jewish prisoners.

**Stories from the Camps**: Reb Mendel's camp stories became legendary:

- _The "Kosher" Problem_: How he managed to avoid eating non-kosher food by trading his rations for vegetables and explaining that he was "allergic" to meat.
- _The Prayer Minyan_: How Jewish prisoners somehow gathered a minyan for Yom Kippur despite restrictions and danger.
- _The Torah Class_: How he taught a fellow prisoner basic Hebrew and Torah concepts without written materials.
- _The NKVD Officer_: A story about a camp guard who, despite his role, showed unexpected respect for Reb Mendel's principles.

**Relationships with Other Prisoners**: Reb Mendel's warmth and humor earned him respect even among non-Jewish prisoners. He became known as someone who could be trusted, who would share what he had, and who maintained human dignity despite the camp's efforts to degrade everyone.

**Release**: After Stalin's death in 1953, there was a gradual release of political prisoners. Reb Mendel was among those freed in 1956, having served approximately five years of his eight-year sentence.

---

## The Shanghai Years

After his release from the gulag, Reb Mendel eventually made his way to Shanghai, China — which had become a refuge for European Jews during World War II and continued to host a Jewish community in the postwar years.

**The Shanghai Jewish Community**: Shanghai was home to a diverse Jewish community including refugees from Nazi Europe, Iraqi Jews who had been there for generations, and some Soviet Jews like Reb Mendel. Rabbi [[entities/people/meir-ashkenazi|Meir Ashkenazi]], a Chabad chassid, served as chief rabbi.

**Teaching in Shanghai**: In Shanghai, Reb Mendel could finally teach Torah openly. He became a teacher and mashpia, transmitting Torah to Jews who had survived the Holocaust and Soviet persecution.

**Building Community**: The Shanghai community was temporary but vibrant. Reb Mendel helped prepare Jews for their eventual dispersal — to Israel, America, and other destinations. His teaching gave them spiritual resources for their next journeys.

---

## Escape to the West

From Shanghai, Reb Mendel eventually made his way to the West. The exact route reflects the complex refugee movements of the postwar period.

**Arrival in England**: Reb Mendel settled in England, where there was a small but growing Chabad community. He became a mashpia, teaching and inspiring a new generation of Chassidim who had never known Soviet persecution.

**The London Community**: In London, Reb Mendel played a central role in building the Chabad community. His farbrengens drew people who wanted to hear his stories of mesirus nefesh and learn from his spiritual wisdom.

---

## The Legendary Farbrengens

Reb Mendel's farbrengens became legendary. These gatherings — typically held on Shabbat, Jewish holidays, or special occasions — were characterized by his storytelling, spiritual insights, and personal warmth.

**The Stories**: Reb Mendel told stories of Soviet-era Chassidim with remarkable detail, humor, and spiritual depth. His stories weren't merely entertainment — they were vehicles for transmitting values, teaching lessons in divine service, and inspiring mesirus nefesh.

**The Style**: Reb Mendel's storytelling style was distinctive. He had a dry wit, perfect timing, and the ability to find the spiritual dimension in seemingly mundane events. Listeners would laugh one moment and be moved to tears the next.

**The Impact**: Those who attended Reb Mendel's farbrengens described them as transformative experiences. Young Chassidim who had never known persecution came away with a deeper understanding of what mesirus nefesh really meant — and a commitment to live up to that legacy.

**Notable Stories**: Some of Reb Mendel's most famous stories include:

- The "Potato Story" — about ingenuity and faith in a Soviet prison
- The "Passover Matzah" story — about an underground matzah baking operation
- The "Bar Mitzvah" story — about preparing a boy for bar mitzvah under Soviet surveillance
- The "Farbrengen in the Camp" — about celebrating a Jewish holiday in Vorkuta

---

## Final Years in Israel

In his later years, Reb Mendel moved to Israel, where he continued teaching and inspiring until his passing in 1995.

**Kfar Chabad**: Reb Mendel spent time in Kfar Chabad, the Chabad village in central Israel established for Soviet-era refugees. Here he found a community of people who understood his background and valued his wisdom.

**Teaching the Next Generation**: In Israel, Reb Mendel continued teaching — both formally and informally. His apartment was always open to those seeking wisdom, stories, or just a warm word.

**Relationship with the Rebbe**: Reb Mendel maintained a connection with the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]], who valued him as a link to the Soviet-era mesirus nefesh tradition. The Rebbe once referred to Reb Mendel as "one of the giants of mesirus nefesh."

**Passing**: Reb Mendel passed away in 1995 at the age of 89. His funeral drew thousands from throughout Israel who came to pay respects to this last link to the heroic Soviet underground.

---

## Legacy and Influence

Reb Mendel's legacy lives on through his stories, his students, and the example of his life.

**The Oral Tradition**: Reb Mendel was a living repository of the Soviet Chabad oral tradition. His stories preserved the memory of heroic Chassidim who might otherwise have been forgotten. Many of these stories have been published and continue to circulate.

**Inspiration for Shluchim**: The Rebbe's global emissary movement (shlichus) draws inspiration from figures like Reb Mendel. His stories of mesirus nefesh under Soviet conditions give contemporary shluchim perspective on their own challenges.

**The Concept of Mesirus Nefesh**: Through his stories and example, Reb Mendel taught that mesirus nefesh isn't only about dying for Torah — it's about living for Torah under any conditions, with joy and devotion.

**Published Works**: Several collections of Reb Mendel's stories and teachings have been published posthumously, ensuring that his wisdom continues to inspire new generations.
