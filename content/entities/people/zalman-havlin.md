# Rabbi Zalman Havlin

**Born**: 1872  
**Died**: 1933, Jerusalem  
**Role**: Sent by [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] to establish Toras Emes yeshiva in Hebron (1911); later reconstituted it in Jerusalem

---

## Overview

Rabbi Zalman Havlin was a Chabad chassid and scholar whom the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] selected and sent to Eretz Yisrael in 1911 to establish a Chabad yeshiva branch in Hebron. The yeshiva — named _Toras Emes_ — became the first Chassidic yeshiva in the Jewish homeland, representing a historic milestone for both Chabad and the Jewish community in Eretz Yisrael.

---

## Mission to Hebron (1911)

The Rashab had purchased **Beit Romano** in Hebron — a large complex capable of housing a yeshiva. He dispatched Rabbi Zalman Havlin to Hebron to establish a branch of Tomchei Temimim there, naming it _Toras Emes_ ("Truth of Torah").

The choice of Hebron was significant: it is the city of the Patriarchs, one of the four holy cities of Judaism (along with Jerusalem, Tzfat, and Tiberias), and home to an existing [[entities/people/menucha-rochel|Chabad community]] dating back to 1845.

_Toras Emes_ was not merely a branch but a genuine yeshiva — the first formal Chassidic yeshiva established in Eretz Yisrael. It represented the Rashab's conviction that Chabad's educational mission had to extend to the Land of Israel itself.

See [[events/toras-emes-hebron-1911|Founding of Toras Emes in Hebron (1911)]].

---

## WWI Disruption and Jerusalem Reconstitution

World War One forced the Russian students studying at Toras Emes to return to Russia — Ottoman-controlled Palestine was not safe territory for Russian nationals. The yeshiva effectively ceased operations during the war.

After the Rashab's death in 1920, Rabbi Havlin reconstituted Toras Emes in **Jerusalem** (1922) — moving it from Hebron to the capital and reestablishing it as a functioning Chabad institution in the new British Mandate Palestine. This Jerusalem incarnation became the direct ancestor of the Chabad educational institutions in Israel today.

---

## Significance

Rabbi Zalman Havlin's mission represents an early and significant expression of [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] — the concept of being sent as an emissary to accomplish a specific mission on behalf of the Rebbe. His willingness to leave Russia and establish an institution in the challenging environment of early twentieth-century Palestine required considerable personal sacrifice and faith.
