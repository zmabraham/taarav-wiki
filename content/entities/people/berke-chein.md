# Rabbi Dov Ber (Berke) Chein

**Born**: Late 19th century, Russia
**Died**: Mid-20th century
**Role**: Soviet-era Chabad chassid known for extraordinary spiritual fire

---

## Overview

Rabbi Dov Ber Chein, known to all as "Reb Berke," was one of the fiery Soviet-era Chabad chassidim who maintained Jewish religious life underground under Communist persecution. His stories of encounters with the NKVD and his unwavering commitment to Torah observance under impossible conditions became legendary in Chabad oral tradition.

Reb Berke exemplified the concept of _hislahavus_ — spiritual fire and enthusiasm — that characterized the best of the Soviet chassidim. Despite facing constant danger, arrest, and persecution, he maintained joy in divine service and inspired those around him.

---

## Early Life and Background

Little detailed information survives about Reb Berke's early life, but he was born in the late 19th century into a Chabad family in Russia. He received his education in the Chabad yeshiva system and became a devoted follower of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] and later the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]].

**Connection to the Rebbes**: Like many chassidim of his generation, Reb Berke had personal connections to the Chabad Rebbes. He traveled to Lubavitch and later to Rostov to see the Rebbe, absorbing the teachings that would sustain him through the Soviet years.

---

## Underground Activities in the Soviet Union

After the Bolshevik Revolution, Reb Berke became active in the Chabad underground network that maintained Jewish life throughout the Soviet Union.

**Teaching Torah**: Reb Berke taught Torah in secret locations, continuing the tradition of underground education that kept Judaism alive despite Soviet suppression. He was known for his fiery teaching style that inspired students despite the constant danger.

**Circumcision and Religious Services**: Like other Soviet-era chassidim, Reb Berke participated in clandestine religious services — performing circumcisions, organizing secret minyanim, and facilitating holiday observances. Each of these activities carried the risk of arrest.

**The NKVD Encounters**: Reb Berke's encounters with the NKVD (Soviet secret police) became legendary. Stories describe how he used his quick wit, spiritual presence, and sometimes sheer chutzpah to navigate dangerous situations.

---

## Stories and Anecdotes

While many details of Reb Berke's life have been lost to time, several stories about him survive in Chabad oral tradition:

**The NKVD Interrogation**: In one famous story, Reb Berke was arrested and brought in for interrogation. The NKVD officer tried to intimidate him, but Reb Berke's calm demeanor and spiritual presence were so disarming that the officer eventually became unnerved. According to the story, Reb Berke's unwavering commitment to Torah — even under threat — made a deep impression.

**The Secret Minyan**: Reb Berke was known for organizing secret prayer services under impossible conditions. In one story, he somehow arranged a minyan for Yom Kippur in a location where being caught would have meant arrest or worse. Despite the danger, the participants reported feeling Reb Berke's joy and enthusiasm.

**The Farbrengen**: Reb Berke was known for his farbrengens — gatherings of chassidim for Torah teachings, singing, and spiritual inspiration. Even under Soviet conditions, he found ways to hold these gatherings, always emphasizing joy in divine service despite the surrounding darkness.

---

## Character and Qualities

**Spiritual Fire (Hislahavus)**: Reb Berke was characterized by _hislhavus_ — spiritual fire and enthusiasm. This quality was particularly valued in Chabad thought as essential for overcoming obstacles and serving G-d with joy.

**Quick Wit**: Stories about Reb Berke highlight his quick wit and ability to think on his feet. This quality served him well in his encounters with Soviet authorities.

**Spiritual Presence**: Those who knew Reb Berke described his imposing spiritual presence — something that emanated from his deep connection to Torah and Chassidus. This presence inspired those around him and sometimes even affected his oppressors.

---

## Legacy

Reb Berke Chein is remembered as one of the fiery Soviet-era chassidim who kept Judaism alive through the darkest years of Communist persecution. His stories continue to be told in Chabad farbrengens as examples of _mesirus nefesh_ (self-sacrifice) and spiritual heroism.

**Oral Tradition**: Like many Soviet-era chassidim, much of what we know about Reb Berke comes through oral tradition — stories passed down from generation to generation. These stories preserve the memory of his heroism and continue to inspire.

**Inspiration for Later Generations**: The example of Reb Berke and others like him inspired later generations of Chabad shluchim (emissaries) who spread Judaism throughout the world. Their understanding of mesirus nefesh was shaped by these Soviet-era heroes.
