# R' M. Shusterman of Zlobin

**Chabad Chassid from Zlobin; memoirist of Soviet-era Chabad life; studied at Tomchei Temimim branches in Vitebsk, Mogilev, and Kherson**

## Biography

R' M. Shusterman was born on Erev Rosh Chodesh Elul 5674 (1914) in Zlobin, a town in White Russia situated between Homel (to the east), Bobruisk (to the west), Rogachev (to the north), and Volhynia-area towns (to the south). He grew up in a Chabad household — his father served as a shamash in the local synagogue — in a town with a strong Chassidic atmosphere including multiple synagogues, all of which used the Nusach Ari.

He studied at Tomchei Temimim branches in Vitebsk, Mogilev, and Kherson during the Soviet period (1928–1930), documenting the yeshiva's clandestine operations under Soviet pressure. In 1938 he moved to Moscow to work as a photo retoucher.

In 1940 he married in Moscow, and in 1941, with the German invasion, he fled to Samarkand in Central Asia along with many other Chabad families. After the war he eventually made his way via Paris to America (arriving 18 Adar Sheni 1948), settling in Brooklyn near 770.

Later in life he wrote his memoirs (Lmaan Yadau Banim Yivaledu — "So That Children May Know"), a valuable first-person account of Jewish life in Soviet Russia, Tomchei Temimim branches under Soviet oppression, and the Chabad refugee experience.

## Significance

Shusterman's memoir provides rare ground-level documentation of Chabad life in Soviet Russia: the specific locations of underground yeshiva branches, the social world of Chassidim under communism, and the refugee experience. The "great wedding" of Chabad-Berditchev alliance mentioned in his memoir (referring to a celebrated Schneersohn-family wedding held in Zlobin) places his hometown on the Chabad map in a distinctive way. His account also documents the convergence of Chassidic and Mitnagdic Jews under Soviet pressure, which broke down traditional sectarian barriers.
