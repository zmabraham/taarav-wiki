# Rabbi Pinchas Horowitz (Baal HaHaflaah)

## Overview
Rabbi Pinchas Elijah Horowitz, known as the Baal HaHaflaah (author of Haflaah), was a distinguished disciple of the Maggid of Mezeritch. He served as Chief Rabbi of Frankfurt am Main. He and his brother Rabbi Shmelke of Nikolsburg were among the Maggid's most prominent students.

## Details
He and his brother Rabbi Shmelke (Rabbi Shmuel Shmelke Horowitz of Nikolsburg) traveled together to visit the Maggid of Mezeritch before taking up their rabbinical appointments. They visited Rovneh (Ravenye) where the Maggid was then residing.

When they arrived in Rovneh, they viewed the Alter Rebbe's manuscript of Shulchan Aruch (specifically the laws of Tzitzit and Pesach) which he had just completed, and praised it highly, encouraging him to continue: "Be strong and courageous, to complete the work — it is fitting and appropriate for you."

He was selected for the rabbinate of Frankfurt am Main in spring 1771, arrived there in Tevet 5532 (January 1772), and served there until his passing on 24 Tammuz 5565 (1805). His son Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Horowitz delivered the eulogy, which stated he served in Frankfurt "more than 33 years."

He is mentioned in the context of the Alter Rebbe's first imprisonment (1799), where the Alter Rebbe sent letters to prominent rabbis.
