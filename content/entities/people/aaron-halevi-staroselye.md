# Rabbi Aaron HaLevi of Staroselye

**Born**: c. 1766, Staroselye, Belarus  
**Died**: 1828, Staroselye  
**Role**: The [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]'s most gifted disciple; founder of the Staroselye branch of Chabad

---

## Overview

Rabbi Aaron HaLevi of Staroselye was among the most distinguished students of the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]. Born in Staroselye (near Shklov), he became the Alter Rebbe's closest student and was widely regarded as his most brilliant disciple. After the Alter Rebbe's death during the flight from Napoleon in 1812, Rabbi Aaron became a central figure in the succession crisis that shaped Chabad's future.

---

## The Succession Dispute

After the Alter Rebbe's passing on 24 Tevet 5573 (1812), Rabbi Aaron refused to accept the leadership of the Alter Rebbe's son, the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (Rabbi DovBer Schneuri). The disagreement was not personal but deeply philosophical:

- **Rabbi Aaron** emphasized _bitul_ (self-nullification) and ecstatic experience in prayer. He held that the primary goal of Chassidic service was to lose oneself entirely in divine contemplation, reaching states of spiritual rapture.
- **The Mitteler Rebbe** favored a more intellectual approach — detailed, sustained meditation on Chassidic concepts (_haskalah_), where understanding deepens rather than dissolves the self.

This philosophical divergence reflected a genuine tension within the Alter Rebbe's own teachings, which contained both elements.

---

## The Staroselye Dynasty

Rabbi Aaron founded a separate Chabad court in Staroselye, drawing a significant number of the Alter Rebbe's former chassidim. The Staroselye branch continued for several generations but eventually declined, while the main Chabad line through the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] and [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] became the dominant Chabad movement.

---

## Literary Works

Rabbi Aaron authored two major Chabad philosophical works:

- **Avodas HaLevi** (_Service of the Levite_) — also known as **Shaarei HaAvodah** (_Gates of Service_), a systematic treatment of divine service and prayer
- **Shaarei HaYichud VeHaEmunah** (_Gates of Unity and Faith_) — an exploration of divine unity, paralleling concepts found in the Alter Rebbe's Tanya

These works remain studied in Chabad circles and represent an important variant within early Chabad philosophy.
