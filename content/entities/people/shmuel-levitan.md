# R' Shmuel Levitan — ר' שמואל לעוויטאן

**Chabad Rabbi of Rakishok, Lithuania; Survived the Holocaust; Continued in America**

**Full Name**: Rabbi Shmuel Levitan
**Dates**: (early 20th century – mid/late 20th century)
**Position**: Chabad rabbi of [[entities/places/rakishok|Rakishok]] (Rokiškis), Lithuania
**Affiliation**: Chabad-Lubavitch
**Post-war**: Continued rabbinical work in the United States

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shmuel Levitan served as the Chabad rabbi of [[entities/places/rakishok|Rakishok]] (Rokiškis) in northeastern Lithuania during the interwar period. His community was honored by a visit from the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in 1931. Unlike most Lithuanian Chabad rabbis, R' Levitan survived the Holocaust and continued his rabbinical work in the United States — making him one of the precious living links to the pre-Holocaust Lithuanian Chabad world.

---

## Career in Rakishok

### Service as Chabad Rabbi

In Rakishok:

- **Community leadership**: Provided rabbinical guidance to the Chabad community
- **Spiritual direction**: Guided community members in Chassidic practice
- **Part of the network**: Connected to the Lithuanian Chabad network coordinated through Kovno and beyond
- **Interwar period**: Served during Lithuania's years of independence (1918–1940)

### The Rayatz's Visit (1931)

A highlight of R' Levitan's tenure:

- **Date**: Adar 5691 (February/March 1931)
- **First Lithuanian tour**: This was part of the Rayatz's first significant journey to Lithuania
- **Reception**: R' Levitan, as the local Chabad rabbi, played a central role in receiving the Rayatz
- **Impact**: The visit strengthened the community and R' Levitan's own connection to Chabad leadership
- **Personal encounter**: Gave him a personal meeting with the Rayatz

---

## Survival

### Escape from Lithuania

When Nazi Germany occupied Lithuania in 1941:

- **R' Levitan's escape**: Unlike the vast majority of Lithuanian Chabad leaders, he managed to escape
- **Circumstances**: The specific circumstances of his escape are not fully detailed in available sources
- **Survival**: He was among the very few Lithuanian Chabad rabbis to survive the Holocaust

---

## Post-War Activity in America

### United States

After the war, R' Levitan continued his work in America:

- **Rabbinical service**: Continued serving as a rabbi in the United States
- **Living testimony**: As a survivor from Lithuanian Chabad, he was a living link to that world
- **Transmission**: Could transmit the traditions, stories, and character of pre-war Lithuanian Chabad
- **Chabad in America**: Part of the growing Chabad presence in America under the Rayatz (who had arrived in 1940) and later the Rebbe

---

## Significance

### A Rare Survivor

R' Shmuel Levitan's significance lies partly in his survival:

- **Living memory**: He could personally testify to what Lithuanian Chabad was like
- **Lithuanian Chabad voice**: One of the few who could speak from firsthand experience about this world
- **American Chabad contribution**: His experience enriched the American Chabad community

### The Rakishok Connection

- **Rayatz visit preserved**: His survival meant someone could personally recall the Rayatz's 1931 visit to Rakishok
- **Community memory**: The memory of the Rakishok community was partially preserved through him
