# R' Mordechai Menachem Lokshin

**Director of Agudas Chassidei Chabad; Rabbi of Tzemach Tzedek of Williamsburg; the "spirit of life" of the American Chabad community**

## Overview

R' Mordechai Menachem Lokshin was the administrative director (_menahel_) of [[entities/places/agudas-chassidei-chabad|Agudas Chassidei Chabad]] in America during the 1920s and early 1930s, and served as the rav of the Tzemach Tzedek congregation at 125 Marcy Street in Williamsburg, Brooklyn. He was one of the central figures of the small but formative early American Chabad community, and his unexpected death in 1933 was described as a severe blow.

## Role in the Community

R' Lokshin served as the practical organizational backbone of Agudas Chassidei Chabad. When the Rayatz sent a package of letters to American Jewish leaders in 5694 (1933–1934) — concerning lobbying efforts around the Soviet delegation in Washington — the letters were addressed to R' Lokshin for distribution. In his private note to the secretary R' Yechezkel Feigin, the Rayatz explained why: "You are right [that the letters don't concern him], but in truth he is the _ruach hachaim_ there" — the animating spirit of the community.

Jacobson's memoir describes R' Lokshin warmly as someone who had been trained in Lubavitch and carried that spirit into the American context, working to hold together the scattered Temimim and Chabad-associated baalei batim in New York.

## Tzemach Tzedek Congregation

R' Lokshin presided over the Tzemach Tzedek shul at **125 Marcy Street in Williamsburg**, Brooklyn — one of the main Chabad nusach Ari congregations in New York. This was the same shul where R' Tovya Belkin was a regular attendee. On one occasion the Rayatz himself came to the shul and delivered a maamar, drawing a crowd estimated at approximately 5,000 people; the police had difficulty clearing a path to the bimah, and the Rayatz's voice filled the packed hall without amplification.

## Reception Delegation (1929)

R' Lokshin was part of the official delegation that traveled by government cutter to meet the Rayatz's ship before it docked on **13 Elul 5689 (September 18, 1929)**. The delegation included R' Lokshin, attorney Yekutiel Kremer, R' Chaim Zalman Kremer (chairman of Agudas Chassidei Chabad), Judge Philip Kleinfeld, and R' Shmuel Elyezer Nelson.

## Sudden Death (Cheshvan 5694 / 1933)

R' Lokshin died suddenly and unexpectedly in **Cheshvan 5694 (November 1933)**. On the day of his death, he had been preparing to attend an assembly of rabbinical organizations that had just unified — including Agudas HaRabbanim and other New York rabbinical bodies — to address matters of kashrut supervision in slaughterhouses. He was still at home getting ready when he was struck suddenly (likely by a stroke from high blood pressure) and died before noon that day.

The Agudas Chassidei Chabad leadership convened immediately to arrange a proper funeral according to Chabad custom — the decision was made that no hespedim (eulogies) would be delivered. The large crowd at the funeral, combined with the rabbinical leaders' surprise that the Chabad community maintained its custom of no eulogies, highlighted the standing R' Lokshin had in the broader Orthodox community.

A remarkable episode surrounded his death: the Rayatz's packet of letters, which had R' Lokshin as the address for distribution, arrived precisely at the time of his passing — as if the Rayatz had known, though the letter itself was dated two weeks earlier. Jacobson interpreted this timing as a providential sign.

## Significance

R' Mordechai Menachem Lokshin represents the generation of Tomchei Temimim alumni who brought Chabad structure and spirit to America before the Rayatz's permanent immigration. His role as organizational director of Agudas Chassidei Chabad during the critical decade of 1924–1933 — when the community was small, poorly resourced, and geographically scattered — required both communal dedication and personal sacrifice. The Rayatz's characterization of him as "the spirit of life" captures how essential he was to maintaining an organized Chabad presence in America.
