# Eliyakim (Philip) Wolf — אליקים פיליפ וואלף

**Location**: Würzburg (later Frankfurt), Germany  
**Period**: Late 19th – early 20th century  
**Role**: Host of the Rashab in Würzburg; one of two German directors of the Rashab's "Philanthropy" company

---

## Overview

Eliyakim (Philip) Wolf was a German Jewish businessman who served as the host of [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] during the celebrated [[events/wurzburg-year-1906|Würzburg year (1906–1907)]]. His home at **Sandre Glassis Str. 41, Würzburg** was the Rashab's residence for a substantial portion of the year-long stay. Wolf also played a practical role in the Rashab's efforts to support Chabad's educational network by serving as one of the two German directors of the legally constituted "Philanthropy" company.

---

## The Rashab's Würzburg Residence

When the Rashab arrived in Würzburg in Cheshvan 5667 (November 1906), he initially stayed at Allee Str. 20 before moving to the home of Eliyakim Wolf at Sandre Glassis Str. 41. Wolf's home served as the base for the Rashab's extraordinary year of Chassidic composition (the [[concepts/hemshech-samech-vov|Hemshech Samech Vov]]) and communal work.

---

## The "Philanthropy" Company (Aktiengesellschaft)

One of the Rashab's practical challenges during his years in Germany was finding a legal mechanism to support [[entities/places/lubavitch|Tomchei Temimim]] yeshiva — since direct Russian-German institutional connections would have been viewed with suspicion by the Czarist authorities. The Rashab organized a formally constituted German company called "Philanthropy" (an _Aktiengesellschaft_ — joint-stock company) with two German directors.

Eliyakim Wolf was one of these two German directors, providing the legal German corporate structure that allowed funds to flow to support Chabad's educational work in Russia. This was a remarkable example of creative institutional thinking — using German business law to enable Torah education in Russia.

---

## The Rashab's Blessing and Its Fulfillment

Perhaps the most remarkable story connected to Eliyakim Wolf involves a personal miracle:

Wolf and his wife had been childless for many years. The Rashab, knowing of their situation, blessed them. Sometime after the Würzburg year, the Wolfs moved to Frankfurt. In fulfillment of the blessing, they eventually had **four sons and two daughters** — a complete family transformation that the Chabad-Germany sources present as a direct fulfillment of the Rashab's blessing.

This story follows the classic pattern of Chassidic _yechiduṣ_ stories in which a rebbe's blessing produces concrete results, but it is documented in the specific context of the Würzburg year.
