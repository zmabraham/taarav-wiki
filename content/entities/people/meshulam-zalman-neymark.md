# R' Meshulam Zalman Neymark

**Chabad rabbi who served in Harki, Starodub, Vitebsk, and Nevel; arrested during the 1881 Starodub Chassidim affair**

## Biography

R' Meshulam Zalman Neymark was a prominent Chabad rabbi who served multiple communities in the Russian Empire during the latter half of the 19th century. He began his rabbinic career in Harki (a small town in White Russia), later moved to Starodub (in the Chernigov gubernia), and subsequently served in Vitebsk and Nevel.

His most dramatic chapter came in 1881, when Czarist authorities conducted a mass arrest of Chassidim in Starodub. Approximately 80 Chassidim were arrested in what became one of the larger anti-Chabad persecutions of the post-emancipation era. R' Neymark was among those arrested. The charges typically invoked in such cases related to unlicensed religious practice, sedition, or "fanaticism." Following the arrests, the community faced disruption of its religious and communal life.

R' Neymark's subsequent career in Vitebsk and Nevel indicates that he eventually resumed rabbinic leadership after the Starodub affair. He represents the class of Chabad rabbis who maintained communal life under constant government pressure during the late Czarist period.

## Significance

The 1881 Starodub arrests illustrate how Chabad communities were targeted by Czarist authorities alongside the broader wave of anti-Jewish policies following the assassination of Tsar Alexander II. R' Neymark's multi-city career — spanning four different communities — reflects the mobile nature of Chabad rabbinical leadership in Russia, where rabbis often moved between posts as conditions changed.
