# Rabbi Yehuda Leib Schneersohn of Kopust

**Born**: 1811  
**Died**: 1866  
**Father**: [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]]  
**Court**: Kopust (Kapust), Belarus

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yehuda Leib Schneersohn was a son of the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] and the founder of the Kopust (Kapust) branch of Chabad Chassidism. When the Tzemach Tzedek passed away in 1866 and [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Rabbi Shmuel (Maharash)]] was recognized as the primary successor in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], Rabbi Yehuda Leib established his own Chassidic court in the town of Kopust (also rendered as Kapust), creating one of the most significant offshoots of the Chabad movement.

---

## The Kopust Branch

The Kopust branch became the most prominent of the non-Lubavitch Chabad offshoots. Rabbi Yehuda Leib was known for his deep scholarship in Chassidic philosophy and attracted a significant following. The branch continued through his descendants and eventually produced several sub-branches:

- **Zlobin** branch
- **Bobroisk** (Babruysk) branch
- **Niezhin** offshoots

The Kopust tradition maintained the core Chabad approach to Chassidic study and practice but operated independently of the Lubavitch center led by the [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Maharash]] and later the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]].

---

## Historical Context

The fragmentation of Chabad leadership after the Tzemach Tzedek's passing was not unusual in Chassidic dynasties. Many Chassidic movements experienced similar splits when a Rebbe left multiple qualified sons. What was distinctive about the Chabad case was the intellectual nature of the tradition — each son had mastered the Tzemach Tzedek's vast body of teachings and could legitimately claim to continue that tradition.

Over time, the Lubavitch branch (through the [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Maharash]], [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]], and [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]) became the dominant and eventually sole surviving branch of Chabad, while the Kopust and other branches gradually declined.
