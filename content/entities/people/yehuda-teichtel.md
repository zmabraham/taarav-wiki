# Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel

**Born**: c. 1972  
**Role**: Founder and director of Chabad of Berlin; arrived 1996; built the major postwar Chabad institution in Germany

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel is the Chabad emissary who arrived in Berlin in 1996 and built a thriving Jewish community center against all historical expectations — establishing Chabad's major presence in Germany's capital 50 years after the Holocaust.

---

## Arrival and Foundation (1996)

Rabbi Teichtel arrived in Berlin in 1996, sent by the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe's]] worldwide shlichus network (the Rebbe had passed in 1994, but the institutional structure of sending continued). Together with his colleague Rabbi Shmuel Segel, he began building what would become the **Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin**.

The choice to establish a major Chabad center in Berlin was historically resonant and controversial — Germany's capital, site of the Nazi regime that had murdered six million Jews, was now to host a vibrant Jewish center.

---

## Building the Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin

From modest beginnings, Rabbi Teichtel built an extraordinary complex:

- A major community center in central Berlin
- A synagogue seating 250 people
- A Jewish school ("Yudishe Traditsyone Shule Ohr Avner") — housed in a building that had served as a Nazi officers' club, a profound historical irony
- A Jewish restaurant, mikveh, library, and youth center
- Regular Shabbat minyanim that grew from 20 people to hundreds

The institution attracted Berlin's young Jewish population — many of them immigrants from the former Soviet Union — and became a center of Jewish life in the new, reunified Berlin.

---

## Recognition

Chancellor Angela Merkel acknowledged the significance of the Berlin Chabad center — noting that a Jewish community self-funding its own institutions represented evidence of genuine Jewish confidence in Germany's future. This recognition from the German head of government carried deep symbolic weight.

---

## Connection to the Rebbe's Prophecy

The Berlin Wall fell in November 1989. Weeks before, the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] had told a Berlin Jewish activist: _"Es vet nisht blaybn af lang"_ — "It won't last long" — and instructed that Jewish activity must continue in both East and West Germany.

Rabbi Teichtel's work fulfilled this directive, transforming the Rebbe's prophetic instruction into institutional reality.
