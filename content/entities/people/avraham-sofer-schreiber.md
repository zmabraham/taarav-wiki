# Rabbi Avraham Sofer Schreiber

Rabbi Avraham Sofer Schreiber (13 Tishrei 5658 – 17 Nisan 5742 / 1898–1982) was a rabbi and editor-publisher of Rishonim literature, known especially for his critical edition of the Meiri's Beit HaBechirah. He maintained close contact with the Rebbe regarding his publications and served as a living link in the chain from the Chasam Sofer. In 5741 he received the Israel Prize for Torah literature.

## Biography

Born in Erlau (Eger), Hungary to Rabbi Shimon Sofer (a descendant of the Chasam Sofer). He studied under his father, then at the Pressburg yeshiva where he received rabbinic ordination.

In 5681 (1921) he married and moved to Vienna. He subsequently served as rabbi in Corfu (5685–5688), Gorizia, Italy (5688–5697), and Fiume (5697–5700). In 5700 (1940) he emigrated to Eretz Yisrael and settled in Jerusalem.

During his Italian rabbinate he began working on ancient Rishonic manuscripts. He devoted many years to new critical editions of the Meiri's Beit HaBechirah and to republishing his grandfather's works. He also taught periodically at the Jewish Theological Seminary in New York.

## Connection to the Rebbe

His connection with the Rebbe began in Iyar 5718 when he came to yechidus and presented the Rebbe with his Meiri editions. The Rebbe subsequently sent him a long letter with comments on the Meiri and guidelines for publishing Rishonic works. Rabbi Sofer was so impressed that he published these remarks in the "HaOhel" journal of Erlau Yeshiva in Jerusalem.

Their relationship continued for decades. When Rabbi Sofer cited a passage from the Chasam Sofer in discussion, the Rebbe gently corrected his wording — and then produced the actual book from his shelf to show him he was right. Rabbi Sofer later exclaimed to yeshiva students: "How does the Rebbe have time to know all the Chasam Sofer's formulations by heart?!"

On another occasion, Rabbi Sofer entered the Rebbe's room directly after Menachem Begin had been there for hours. He was astounded: "The Rebbe instantly switched topic, dove into Talmudic analysis and Chasam Sofer texts. He appeared as if I were the first person he spoke to all evening. No tension or fatigue was noticeable."

The Rebbe also transmitted to him that the deepest secrets of Torah are hidden in the legal concept of "miggo" — and acknowledged that this tradition, passed down from the Chasam Sofer, was also known to the Rebbe from his own tradition.
