# Rabbi Elimelech of Lizhensk

## Overview
Rabbi Elimelech of Lizhensk (1717–1787) was one of the most prominent disciples of the Maggid of Mezeritch and a central figure in spreading Chassidism to Poland and Galicia. He was the brother of Rabbi Meshullam Zusya of Anipoli.

## Details
Rabbi Elimelech and his brother Rabbi Zusya were famous for their wandering travels through Jewish communities, spreading the teachings and spirit of Chassidism. Together they studied under the Maggid of Mezeritch.

While Rabbi Elimelech led the Chassidic community in Galicia and Poland (based in Lizhensk/Lezhajsk), his brother Rabbi Zusya remained closer to the Russian Chassidic world and wrote the introduction to the first printing of the Tanya.

Rabbi Elimelech's primary work is the Noam Elimelech. His approach to Chassidism emphasized the central role of the Tzaddik as an intermediary between the people and God — a point on which the Alter Rebbe differed somewhat, emphasizing instead that every individual must work in their own divine service (as famously expressed in the Tanya).
