# Maharil of Yanovitch (Rabbi Yehuda Leib)

## Overview
Rabbi Yehuda Leib of Yanovitch (known as Maharil — Morenu HaRav Yehuda Leib) was the brother of Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (the Alter Rebbe) and one of the most important figures in the transmission and editing of Chabad teachings.

## Details
He was born around 1769 and served as the Moreh Tzedek (religious law decisor) and Mashpiah (spiritual guide) of the town of Yanovitch.

Beit Rebbi describes him as "the great Rabbi in Israel, God-fearing and trembling at the word of God, Author of the book Sheerit Yehuda on the laws of salting meat, with many responsa at the end... He was the mainstay of the Rebbe's household, and most of the Rebbe's affairs were decided through him."

His most important contribution was as the primary transcriber (meiniach) of the Alter Rebbe's discourses. He recorded the majority of the discourses in Torah Or, which the Tzemach Tzedek later edited for publication. The Alter Rebbe himself proofread and corrected these transcriptions.

After the Alter Rebbe's passing, the Maharil proofread and prepared the Shulchan Aruch for publication, as noted in the introduction by the Mitteler Rebbe.

He also signed legal decisions together with the Alter Rebbe (e.g., a compromise for the community of Horodishtz in 1796) and visited Lubavitch regularly during its market fairs (yarmarkas), where he adjudicated legal cases.

He passed away sometime between late 5585 (1825) and the end of Adar I 5586 (1826).

His son was Rabbi Yeshaya, and his grandson Rabbi Zeev Wolf who published Sheerit Yehuda in 1841 and later died in Eretz Yisrael in 1882, buried on the Mount of Olives.
