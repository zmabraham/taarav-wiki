# Napoleon Bonaparte

**Born**: August 15, 1769, Ajaccio, Corsica  
**Died**: May 5, 1821, Saint Helena  
**Role**: French Emperor; his 1812 invasion of Russia directly shaped Chabad history and precipitated the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]'s death

---

## Overview

Napoleon Bonaparte's relevance to Chabad history centers on two episodes: his 1807 Grand Sanhedrin and his 1812 invasion of Russia. Both events forced the Jewish world — and the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] in particular — to reckon with the meaning of political emancipation for Jewish spiritual life.

---

## The 1807 Grand Sanhedrin

In 1806-1807, Napoleon convened an Assembly of Jewish Notables and then a "Grand Sanhedrin" in Paris, compelling French Jewish leaders to affirm Jewish compatibility with French citizenship and civil law. Traditional Jewish leaders across Europe viewed this with deep suspicion, seeing it as a political maneuver designed to accelerate Jewish assimilation and erode religious autonomy.

---

## The 1812 Invasion and the Alter Rebbe's Response

When Napoleon invaded Russia in June 1812, the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] publicly and actively supported the Russian Empire — a position that was not universal among Jewish leaders. His reasoning was theological:

- **Napoleon's victory** would improve Jewish material conditions (civil rights, economic freedom) but would destroy Jewish spiritual life. Emancipation would lead to assimilation, weakening of religious observance, and the dissolution of Jewish communal bonds.
- **Russia's victory** would perpetuate Jewish poverty and persecution, but this suffering would keep Jews close to God, to Torah, and to their identity.

The Alter Rebbe chose spiritual survival over material comfort — a decision that exemplified [[concepts/mesirus-nefesh|mesirus nefesh]] in the realm of communal leadership.

---

## The Alter Rebbe's Flight and Death

As French forces advanced, the Alter Rebbe and his family were forced to flee eastward. [[entities/people/moshe-meisels|Moshe Meisels]], a chassid serving as interpreter for the French army, reportedly provided intelligence that helped coordinate the escape.

The arduous flight through the Russian winter broke the Alter Rebbe's health. He died on 24 Tevet 5573 (December 1812) in the village of Piena. Napoleon's invasion thus directly caused the death of the founder of Chabad.

---

## Legacy in Chabad Thought

Napoleon functions in Chabad historiography as a symbol of the seductive danger of secular modernity — the promise of material freedom that conceals spiritual destruction. The Alter Rebbe's opposition to Napoleon is understood as a paradigmatic act of spiritual discernment, choosing the harder but truer path.
