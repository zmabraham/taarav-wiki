# Yeshaya Berlin

**Born**: c. 1840  
**Died**: 1908, Lubavitch  
**Role**: Primary early funder and philanthropist of [[events/tomchei-temimim-founding-1897|Tomchei Temimim]] yeshiva

---

## Overview

Yeshaya Berlin was a wealthy Lubavitch-based philanthropist who served as the primary early financial benefactor of Yeshiva Tomchei Temimim — the flagship Chabad yeshiva founded by the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] in 1897. His generous support during the critical founding years made the institution viable at a time when skeptics doubted it could attract students or sustain itself.

---

## Support for Tomchei Temimim

When the Rashab founded Tomchei Temimim in 1897, he faced the challenge of creating a yeshiva unlike any that existed — one that combined Talmud and Chassidic philosophy, provided students with room and board (rather than the _essen teg_ system of eating at different homes), and paid faculty. This required substantial ongoing funding.

Yeshaya Berlin stepped forward as the primary financial patron, funding the construction of the main study hall (the _zal_) and providing operational support that allowed the yeshiva to grow from 18 students in its first year to hundreds by Year 5.

The budget grew from 3,120 rubles in 1899 to over 20,000 rubles by 1903 — growth that required sustained philanthropic commitment. Berlin's support was essential to this expansion.

---

## Death and Impact

Yeshaya Berlin died in 1908, before Tomchei Temimim reached its full maturity but after he had secured its institutional foundations. His death represented a significant financial loss for the yeshiva, which then needed to find new donors and funding sources.

The fact that Tomchei Temimim survived and thrived after his death — and went on to produce the generation of Chabad activists who sustained underground yeshivos in the Soviet Union, escaped the Holocaust via Shanghai, and rebuilt Chabad globally — is the ultimate testament to the institution Berlin helped launch.
