# Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Breuer

**Born**: March 10, 1850, Offenbach, Germany  
**Died**: June 22, 1926, Frankfurt, Germany  
**Role**: Orthodox rabbi of Frankfurt; son-in-law of Rabbi Samson Raphael Hirsch; collaborated with the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] on German Orthodox organization

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Breuer was the leading Orthodox rabbi of Frankfurt am Main in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and one of the key figures in German Orthodox institutional life. As the son-in-law and successor of Rabbi Samson Raphael Hirsch (the founder of the Torah Im Derech Eretz school of thought), he led the Israelitische Religionsgesellschaft (IRG) — the separatist Orthodox congregation in Frankfurt.

---

## Connection to the Rashab

During the [[events/wurzburg-year-1906|Rashab's year in Würzburg (1906–1907)]], Rabbi Breuer collaborated with the Rashab and [[entities/people/yaakov-rosenheim|Rabbi Yaakov Rosenheim]] to draft a detailed organizational plan — called _"HiTaadus HaYereim"_ ("Association of the God-Fearing") — for strengthening Orthodox Judaism in Germany.

The collaboration was significant because it brought together:

- The Chabad tradition (mystical, Eastern European)
- The Hirschian tradition (rationalist, Western, Torah Im Derech Eretz)
- The Agudas Yisrael approach (Rosenheim's organization)

Despite their different philosophical orientations, all three saw the urgent need to strengthen Torah observance against the powerful forces of Reform Judaism and secularization.

---

## The Frankfurt Orthodox Community

Rabbi Breuer led the IRG (Israelitische Religionsgesellschaft) — the separatist Orthodox congregation founded by Rabbi Hirsch, which maintained a strict policy of _Austritt_ (separation from the general Jewish community). This approach, which rejected any communal framework that mixed Orthodox and Reform Jews, was controversial but created one of the most vibrant Orthodox communities in Germany.

His son, Rabbi Joseph Breuer, emigrated to New York in 1939 and established Khal Adath Jeshurun in Washington Heights — transplanting the Frankfurt model to America.
