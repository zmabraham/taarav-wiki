# Rabbi Michael HaKohen of Fulda — ר' מיכאל הכהן מפולדה

**Role**: Orthodox rabbi of Fulda, Germany; correspondent of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]]  
**Location**: Fulda, Germany  
**Period**: Late 19th – early 20th century

---

## Overview

Rabbi Michael HaKohen was the rabbi of Fulda, a historic German city, and one of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab's]] most warm personal correspondents among German Orthodox rabbis. Their relationship is documented in the Chabad-Germany volume (_Toldot Chabad Beyahadut Germania_).

---

## Correspondence with the Rashab

The letters between the Rashab and Rabbi Michael HaKohen were notably personal in tone — warm and affectionate beyond typical rabbinic correspondence. The Rashab sent him birthday blessings, including a letter rich with Torah content; he also prayed at the ohalim (memorial graves) of the Chabad Rebbes in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] on behalf of Rabbi Michael's health during periods of illness.

This correspondence illustrates the Rashab's ability to forge genuine human connections across the cultural divide between Eastern European Chassidic and Western Orthodox German Jewry.

---

## Historical Context

Fulda had a historic Jewish community; the city was home to one of Germany's earliest documented Jewish communities in the medieval period. Rabbi Michael HaKohen served this community in an era when German Orthodoxy was navigating the tensions between Neo-Orthodox Hirschian tradition, modern Wissenschaft des Judentums, and the demands of civic emancipation.
