# Rabbi Chaim Halberstam of Sanz (Divrei Chaim)

Rabbi Chaim Halberstam of Sanz (5557–25 Nisan 5636 / 1793–1876), known as the "Divrei Chaim" after his work, was the rabbi of Sanz (Nowy Sącz) in Galicia and the founder of Sanz Chassidus. He was one of the greatest Torah leaders and Chassidic masters of Eastern European Jewry in his generation.

## Biography

Born in Tarnogrod to Rabbi Aryeh Leibush. A child prodigy, he was betrothed at age 13 to the daughter of Rabbi Baruch Frankel-Teomim (author of "Baruch Taam"). He married four times and had eight sons and seven daughters.

His teachers in Chassidus were Rabbi Naftali Tzvi of Ropshitz and Rabbi Tzvi Hirsh of Zidichov. He received rabbinic ordination from Rabbi Ephraim Zalman Margaliot of Brody. Rabbi Chaim was famed in his generation as one of its greatest figures — a gaon in both revealed and hidden Torah, righteous and holy. His prayer was conducted with burning intensity and self-transcendence. He gave away virtually all his money to the poor, embodying all three pillars upon which the world stands: Torah, divine service, and acts of kindness.

During his lifetime Sanz Chassidus had thousands of followers. He passed away on 25 Nisan 5636.

## Connection to Chabad

Rabbi Chaim wrote of the Alter Rebbe with admiration: "Who among the later generations is greater than him?" He referred to the Tzemach Tzedek as "my mechutan [in-law], the Gaon-Chassid, Reb Mendel of Lubavitch." A celebrated story: when a complex halachic question arrived from the Tzemach Tzedek, Rabbi Chaim spent significant time in deep contemplation before responding, ensuring his answer incorporated the mystical dimension as well.

## Descendants and Successor Dynasties
- **Rabbi Yechezkel Shraga Halberstam** of Shinova (most famous son), ancestor of Stropkov Chassidus
- **Rabbi Baruch Halberstam** of Rudnik, ancestor of Gorlitz, Zanzmigrod, and Klausenberg-Sanz dynasties
- **Rabbi Shlomo Halberstam** (grandson) — founder of Bobov Chassidus
- Other sons founded Kshanov, Ratsfurt (killed in Holocaust), and Tchekhiv dynasties
