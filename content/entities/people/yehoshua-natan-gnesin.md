# R' Yehoshua Natan Gnesin (Hershel Neta)

**Head of the Chabad yeshiva in Starodub; scholar and Chassidic leader in the Chernigov region**

## Biography

R' Yehoshua Natan Gnesin, known by his nickname Hershel Neta, served as head of the Chabad yeshiva in Starodub, a town in the Chernigov gubernia of the Russian Empire (present-day Russia, near the Ukrainian border). He was a significant figure in the Chabad community of the region during the era of the Maharash and Rashab.

Starodub was home to an important Chabad community. The town's yeshiva, which R' Gnesin led, trained a generation of Chabad scholars. The community's vibrancy is evidenced by the fact that it was targeted in the 1881 mass arrests, when approximately 80 Chassidim were taken by Czarist authorities.

R' Gnesin's family name became notable in Hebrew literature — the writer Uri Nissan Gnessin (1879–1913), a pioneering figure of Hebrew modernist fiction, may be related to this family, illustrating the intersection of Chabad religious culture with the emerging Hebrew intelligentsia in the Pale of Settlement.

## Significance

R' Gnesin's leadership of the Starodub yeshiva demonstrates that Chabad communities outside the major cities maintained significant educational institutions. His position as rosh yeshiva in a provincial town reflects the decentralized educational network that Chabad built throughout the Pale of Settlement before the founding of Tomchei Temimim in 1897.
