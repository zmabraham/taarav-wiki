# Radzyner Rebbe — רבי גרשון הנוך ליינר מראדזין

**Born**: 1839  
**Died**: 1891  
**Full name**: Rabbi Gershon Henoch Leiner  
**Title**: Third Radzyner Rebbe  
**Known for**: Reviving the use of techelet (sky-blue ritual dye); correspondence with the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]]

---

## Overview

Rabbi Gershon Henoch Leiner, the third Radzyner Rebbe, was one of the most original and controversial halachic thinkers of nineteenth-century Poland. He is best known for his campaign to revive the use of **techelet** — the sky-blue dye used in tzitzit (ritual fringes) and Temple vestments, which had been lost for over a millennium. His research brought him into contact with the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]], who had independently observed what might have been the techelet creature.

---

## The Techelet Research

Rabbi Leiner conducted extensive research, including a visit to the Naples aquarium, to identify the sea creature (chilazon) from which the biblical sky-blue dye was extracted. He concluded that the cuttlefish (_Sepia officinalis_) was the chilazon and introduced techelet made from its ink. His conclusions, published in _Sefunei Tema Tzefunah_ and subsequent works, were controversial and rejected by most poskim (halachic decisors).

---

## The Rashab Connection

The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]], during his 1889 visit to Berlin, visited the Berlin Aquarium specifically to observe the possible chilazon. He saw a small creature that secreted black liquid; not immediately recognizing it as potentially significant, he did not study it closely at the time. Twenty years later, when the Radzyner Rebbe was actively researching the chilazon, the Rashab reported this observation in correspondence — providing an independent data point for the ongoing halachic discussion.

This exchange is documented in the Chabad-Germany volume, illustrating how the Rashab's travels, even for medical or personal reasons, intersected with broader halachic questions of the era.

---

## Legacy

The Radzyner dynasty continued the techelet practice, and in the 20th century a new identification of the chilazon as the murex snail gained broader acceptance. The Radzyner Rebbe's pioneering research, though rejected by most authorities, helped revive serious halachic inquiry into the lost techelet.
