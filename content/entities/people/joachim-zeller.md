# Joachim Zeller — יואכים צלר

**Role**: Mayor/official of Berlin; presided over memorial plaque ceremony for the seventh Rebbe  
**Location**: Berlin, Germany  
**Period**: Early 21st century

---

## Overview

Joachim Zeller was a Berlin municipal official (described in sources as "mayor") who presided over the ceremony installing a memorial plaque at the site of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe's]] former residence at **7 Hanse-Ufer Street** in Berlin, on **3 Kislev 5762 (2001)**. This was the first time the Berlin city government officially honored a Jewish personality with such a plaque.

---

## The Memorial Plaque Ceremony

At the ceremony, Zeller articulated the symbolic significance of the plaque in stark historical terms:

_"In those days, antisemitic signs were placed in Berlin. Today we place a memorial sign for the great Jewish leader."_

This acknowledgment — from a German municipal official in the city that had been the administrative capital of the Nazi regime — represented a remarkable statement of historical reckoning and Jewish-German reconciliation.

---

## Historical Context

The Rebbe (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson) had studied in Berlin from **1928 to 1931**, living at 7 Hanse-Ufer Street while studying at the University of Berlin. His years in Berlin predated his assumption of Chabad leadership in 1951. The archival research confirming this address allowed for the placement of the plaque decades after the fact.
