# Rabbi Yisrael Meir Lau

Rabbi Yisrael Meir Lau (born 22 Sivan 5697 / 1937) served as Ashkenazic Chief Rabbi of Israel (1 Nissan 5753 – 29 Adar 5763 / 1993–2003) and as Chief Rabbi of Tel Aviv-Yafo. He is a Holocaust survivor, having been liberated from Buchenwald at age 8 as one of the youngest survivors. He currently serves as president of Yad Vashem.

## Biography

Born as the youngest child of Rabbi Moshe Chaim Lau of Piotrkow. During the early war years he survived in the Piotrkow ghetto (the first ghetto in occupied Poland). In Tishrei 5703 (1942) his father and older brother were deported to Treblinka extermination camp (where they perished 11 Marcheshvan 5703). He survived a selection with his mother's help, then was saved by his older brother Naftali, who kept him alive. They were sent to the Buchenwald concentration camp, where a Russian soldier aided and protected the young Lau.

When American forces liberated Buchenwald, young Israel ("Lulek") was known as the youngest prisoner freed, at age 8. The brothers soon emigrated to Israel and were detained at the Atlit detention camp.

After the war he studied at the Kol Torah, Knesset Chizkiyahu, and Ponevezh yeshivot. He married Chaya Yota, daughter of Rabbi Yitzchak Yadidya Frankel (later Chief Rabbi of Tel Aviv). He taught in Petach Tikvah and Tel Aviv.

He served as rabbi in various capacities and in 5739 (1979) became Chief Rabbi of Netanya. In 5753 (1993) he was elected Ashkenazic Chief Rabbi of Israel alongside Sephardic Chief Rabbi Yitzchak Yosef.

## Relationship with the Rebbe

Rabbi Lau maintained continuous contact with the Rebbe, meeting him face-to-face in yechidus and at dollars distribution. The relationship began in Chol HaMoed Pesach 5730 (1970) when Rabbi Hadkov invited him to speak on "Who is a Jew" matters.

In his first yechidus (23 Adar 5734/1974), the Rebbe stood when Lau entered and thanked him for his work on Jewish identity. During the lengthy late-night meeting, the Rebbe blessed him to become "master of his city and then of all Eretz Yisrael" — a prophecy that was fulfilled when he became Chief Rabbi of Israel.

In his autobiography ("Al Tishallach Yadecha El HaNa'ar"), Rabbi Lau devoted fourteen pages to his connection with the Rebbe and Chabad. He describes a moving night yechidus in which the Rebbe discussed Jewish history and wept during a story Lau told. He also recounts a Pesach Sheni farbrengen with the Rebbe at which his father-in-law Rabbi Frankel was present.

In 11 Nissan 5753, ten days after being elected Chief Rabbi, while the Rebbe stood on his balcony, Rabbi Lau blessed the Rebbe in a live broadcast from Israel with warm words, ending: "Together with you, Lubavitcher Rebbe, we shall together receive the face of our righteous Mashiach, speedily in our days, amen."

At a Chabad farbrengen in Kfar Chabad shortly after his second yechidus, Rabbi Lau declared: "Since the days of Abraham our Father, no leader like the present Rebbe has arisen, whose sun shines to tremendous distances and publicizes the Name of God in every remote corner, and whose holiness and righteousness illuminates greatly the darkness of exile."
