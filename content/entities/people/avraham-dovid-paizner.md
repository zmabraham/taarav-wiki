# R' Avraham Dovid Paizner

**Mashpia at Tomchei Temimim, Lubavitch; one of the most distinguished Temimim of his generation**

## Overview

R' Avraham Dovid Paizner (Poizner) was from Klimovitchi (Klymavitsy), Belarus, and was among the earliest and most distinguished students of Tomchei Temimim yeshiva in Lubavitch. He was known for his extraordinary yiras shamayim, refined character, and his singular closeness to the Rebbe Rashab. He was appointed as mashpia (spiritual mentor) at Tomchei Temimim, a position that reflected the Rebbe's deep confidence in him.

## Character and Piety

R' Yisrael Jacobson, who knew him personally at the yeshiva, described him in unusually superlative terms:

**Closeness to the Rebbe Rashab:** He would enter the Rebbe Rashab's private chamber (the "hekhal kodesh") directly, without needing a prior appointment — a privilege almost unheard of among even distinguished students. He would come to clarify points in a maamar or discuss matters of avodah, and the Rebbe received him warmly. According to reports, the Rebbe Rashab would draw him very close.

**Yiras Shamayim in daily practice:** His fear of heaven expressed itself in meticulous practice at every level. When washing his hands before eating (netilat yadayim), he would first wash with a large vessel (a quart bottle), then again with a cup (a glass), and then again with another vessel — one inside another — all out of concern for various possible halachic issues. Similarly, when he would see someone with a new, beautiful pair of tefillin, he would quietly approach and ask to borrow them for Kriat Shema — and in this way would put on multiple pairs of tefillin (of the Rashi type) each day.

**Saintly simplicity:** Despite being a man of great spiritual depth, he was not one to raise his voice or draw attention to himself. He would walk along the sides of the study hall (beis midrash) rather than prominently through the center. Yet in farbrengens (Chassidic gatherings), when he would speak, he would do so with great taste and insight, addressing the specific condition and needs of each individual student — showing that he truly knew each person "inside and out."

**Support:** His material situation was never prosperous. When asked once how he sustained himself, he answered with a Yiddish word: _Povestkes_ — meaning he received postal notifications (money orders sent by supporters). He lived off the support of those who knew and valued him.

## At the Fire in Lubavitch

In the summer of 5677 (1917), a large fire broke out in Lubavitch. Jacobson and R' Avraham Dovid happened to be at the mikveh together when the fire alarm bells rang. R' Avraham Dovid's reaction revealed his priorities: he immediately and urgently begged Jacobson with the full force of his character — in a way Jacobson had never seen from this typically composed man — "Yisrael, come help me save my Shas!" He had a complete Vilna Shas in his quarters, and this was what he rushed to rescue. They managed to save the books to a clearing before the fire spread.

## Teaching Style

His approach to Chassidic discourse (divrei Torah) was characterized by: careful, refined language (_eidelike verter_); the ability to convey great depth in simple words; and attention to the spiritual level and inner condition of the listener. He had internalized the Chassidic ideal that a mashpia must know each student — not just their learning but their inner character.

## Later Period

R' Avraham Dovid was appointed as mashpia at Tomchei Temimim around 5673 (1913). He had been coming to Lubavitch periodically as a guest for some years before settling there. His appointment as mashpia represented a formal recognition of what the students already knew: that he was one of the few people capable of guiding others in the deepest matters of avodah.

After the yeshiva dispersed due to World War I and the subsequent revolution, his fate is not fully known from the Zachor memoirs. A R' Shalom Paizner (Poizner) appears later in the memoirs as a Temim in Eretz Yisrael and eventually America, likely from the same family.

## Significance

R' Avraham Dovid Paizner exemplifies the Chabad ideal of the mashpia — a figure who combines profound scholarly knowledge with intimate personal attention to each student's soul. The portrait of him in Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael is among the most vivid firsthand accounts of what a Chabad mashpia actually looked and felt like in the golden era of Tomchei Temimim.
