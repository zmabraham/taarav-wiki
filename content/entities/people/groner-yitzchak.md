# Rabbi Yitzchak Dovid Groner

**Born**: 1931, New York, USA  
**Died**: 2008, Melbourne, Australia  
**Role**: Chabad shliach to Australia; founder and director of Chabad of Victoria

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yitzchak Dovid Groner was one of the longest-serving and most successful shluchim of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]]. Sent to Melbourne in the 1950s, he built Chabad of Victoria from a modest outpost into one of the largest Chabad communities outside of Brooklyn and Israel, transforming the landscape of Australian Jewish life over more than five decades of leadership.

---

## Early Life and Appointment

Born and raised in New York, Groner studied in Chabad yeshivos and became a close disciple of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]]. In the mid-1950s, the Rebbe sent him to Melbourne, Australia, to expand and consolidate the Chabad presence that had been initially established by [[entities/people/chaim-gutnick|Rabbi Chaim Gutnick]] (sent by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in 1941).

Where Gutnick had laid the foundations, Groner was tasked with building the superstructure — schools, synagogues, outreach programs, and communal institutions on a scale that would make Melbourne a world center of Chabad life.

---

## Building Chabad of Victoria

Over more than fifty years of tireless work, Groner established:

- **Yeshivah College** — a comprehensive day school system from preschool through high school
- **Beth Rivkah Ladies College** — girls' education
- **Multiple synagogues** across Melbourne's Jewish neighborhoods
- **Outreach centers** reaching unaffiliated Jews across Victoria
- **Community services** — mikvaos, kashrus supervision, youth programs, and holiday programming

Melbourne's Chabad community grew to become one of the most vibrant in the world, rivaling major Chabad centers in New York and Israel.

---

## Legacy

Groner's achievement in Melbourne demonstrated the full potential of the [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] model: a single emissary, armed with the Rebbe's vision and blessing, could build an entire Jewish infrastructure in a distant land. His decades of service made him one of the iconic figures of the global shlichus enterprise.

He passed away in 2008 after more than fifty years of service in Melbourne.
