# Rabbi Yisrael Jacobson

**Born**: c. 1896, Russia  
**Died**: 17 Sivan 1975, USA  
**Role**: Organizer of Chabad activities in America; author of _Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael_

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yisrael Jacobson was one of the founding figures of American Chabad, serving as a key institutional organizer and preserving vital historical memory through his memoirs [[summaries/zikaron-lebnei-yisrael|*Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael* (Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael)]]. He organized the _maamad_ (fund) in America that sustained Chabad operations and led a wartime mission to Europe on behalf of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]].

---

## Yeshiva Years

Jacobson studied at Tomchei Temimim in its original Lubavitch location and at branch yeshivas including Shchedrin, Kremenchug, and Kherson. His memoirs provide rare ground-level documentation of life in the flagship Chabad yeshiva in its final Russian years, capturing the atmosphere of intense study, proximity to the Rebbe Rashab, and the world about to be destroyed by revolution.

---

## Soviet Period (Homel)

After the Revolution, Jacobson was based in Homel (Gomel, Belarus) until 1926, experiencing the early Soviet period and witnessing the beginning of the campaign against Jewish religious life.

---

## American Chabad Builder (from 1926)

Jacobson emigrated to America in 1926 — arriving before the main wave of Soviet-era Chabad immigrants. In New York he became an essential institutional figure:

**Agudas HaTmimim:** He helped found this organization of Tomchei Temimim graduates, creating the organizational infrastructure for broader Chabad activities.

**Agudas Chassidei Chabad:** He played a central role in running the umbrella Chabad organization in America during its formative years.

**Organizing the Maamad:** Jacobson organized the American _maamad_ — the fund that supported the Rayatz's operations in Russia and later in Riga and Warsaw. This financial network was critical to sustaining underground Chabad activities.

---

## The Rayatz's American Visit (1929–1930)

When the Rayatz made his first trip to America in 1929–1930, Jacobson was at the center of organizing and facilitating the visit. He coordinated meetings with Jewish community leaders, synagogues, and potential donors. His account in _Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael_ provides inside documentation of how this historic visit unfolded and how American Chabad's foundations were laid.

---

## Wartime Europe Mission

During World War Two, the Rayatz sent Jacobson on a wartime mission to Europe — an extraordinary and dangerous undertaking given the Nazi occupation of most of the continent. The mission aimed to assess conditions, coordinate rescue, and maintain connections with surviving Chabad communities.

---

## The Memoirs

In 1958, during a private audience (_yechidus_) with the seventh Rebbe, Jacobson received a direct instruction: write down your memories for posterity. The Rebbe understood that Jacobson's memories were irreplaceable documentation of a world that no longer existed.

Jacobson wrote his memoirs over the following years, serialized in _Di Yiddishe Heim_ (the Chabad women's periodical) until his death in 1975. Published as [[summaries/zikaron-lebnei-yisrael|*Zikaron LeBnei Yisrael*]], they became one of the most important first-person accounts of Chabad life from Czarist Russia to American rebuilding.
