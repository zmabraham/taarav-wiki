# Rabbi Yechezkel (Chatche) Feigin

**Born**: Early 20th century, Russia
**Role**: Soviet-era Chabad chassid; brother of Yechezkel Feigin

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yechezkel Feigin, known as "Chatche," was a Soviet-era Chabad chassid who maintained Jewish religious life under Communist persecution. While less widely known than his brother Yechezkel Feigin (the more famous figure in Chabad history), Reb Chatche was also active in the underground network that kept Torah alive through the Soviet period.

The Feigin brothers represent a pattern in Soviet-era Chabad — entire families committed to maintaining Jewish life under impossible conditions. Their individual stories, intertwined yet distinct, illustrate how the underground network was built on family bonds and communal solidarity.

---

## Family Background

Reb Chatche was born in early 20th century Russia into the Feigin family, which was devoted to Chabad Chassidus.

**The Feigin Family**: The Feigin family was part of the network of Chabad families that kept the tradition alive through the Soviet period. Like other such families, they transmitted Torah values to their children despite the official suppression of religion.

**Brother to Yechezkel**: Chatche was the brother of Yechezkel Feigin, who would become a more widely documented figure in Chabad history. The brothers shared a commitment to Torah and Jewish life, though their paths and stories may have diverged.

---

## Underground Activities

Reb Chatche participated in the Chabad underground network that maintained Jewish religious life throughout the Soviet Union.

**Secret Religious Services**: Reb Chatche helped organize clandestine prayer services, Torah study sessions, and holiday observances. These activities required constant vigilance and the willingness to risk arrest.

**Educational Work**: The Soviet authorities made Jewish education virtually impossible. Reb Chatche participated in efforts to transmit Torah knowledge to the next generation, teaching children and adults despite the danger.

**Network Coordination**: The Soviet underground was a sophisticated operation requiring coordination between individuals and communities. Reb Chatche likely played a role in maintaining these connections.

---

## The Feigin Brothers

While both brothers were committed to maintaining Jewish life under Soviet conditions, their stories took different paths.

**Complementary Roles**: The Feigin brothers likely played complementary roles in the underground network. While Yechezkel's story is more widely documented, Chatche's contributions, though less known, were no less valuable.

**Family Bonds**: The Feigin brothers, like many families in the Soviet underground, drew strength from their family bonds. These connections provided emotional support and shared purpose in the face of persecution.

---

## Challenges of Soviet Life

Life as a religious Jew in the Soviet Union involved constant challenges and dangers.

**Risk of Arrest**: Participation in underground religious activities carried the constant risk of arrest. Many chassidim spent years in Soviet prisons and labor camps for the "crime" of maintaining Jewish observance.

**Resource Scarcity**: Religious necessities were scarce under Soviet rule. Religious books were confiscated, kosher food was unavailable, and religious items like tefillin and mezuzot had to be hidden or smuggled.

**Pressure to Assimilate**: Beyond official persecution, Soviet Jews faced cultural pressure to abandon religious observance. Maintaining Jewish commitment required inner strength and community support.

---

## Legacy

Reb Chatche Feigin is remembered as one of the many Soviet-era chassidim who kept Judaism alive through decades of persecution. Though his story is less documented than his brother's, his contribution was significant.

**The Unsung Heroes**: Many Soviet-era chassidim never had their full stories recorded. Their heroism lives on through oral tradition and the testimony of those who knew them.

**Family Legacy**: The Feigin family — including both Chatche and Yechezkel — represents how entire families were committed to maintaining Torah and Chassidus under impossible conditions.
