# Rabbi Leibel Groner

**Born**: 1931, Brooklyn, New York  
**Died**: April 2, 2020, Brooklyn, New York  
**Role**: Personal secretary and gatekeeper to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] for decades

---

## Overview

Rabbi Leibel (Aryeh Leib) Groner served as the primary personal secretary to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] for over four decades, functioning as the essential gatekeeper between the Rebbe and the thousands of people who sought his guidance, blessing, and attention. If [[entities/people/hodakov|Rabbi Hodakov]] ran the institutional machinery and [[entities/people/krinsky|Rabbi Krinsky]] handled the public-facing role, Groner managed the Rebbe's personal schedule, correspondence, and the flow of people into and out of the Rebbe's presence.

---

## Early Life

Groner was born and raised in Brooklyn, in the orbit of 770 Eastern Parkway and the Chabad community that coalesced around the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] after his arrival in America in 1940. As a young man, Groner became deeply devoted to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] and entered his service as a personal secretary.

---

## The Rebbe's Gatekeeper

Groner's central role was managing access to the Rebbe — a task of extraordinary sensitivity and complexity. The Rebbe received thousands of letters daily, met with individuals for private audiences (yechidus), led farbrengens attended by thousands, and maintained correspondence with world leaders, scholars, and ordinary Jews seeking guidance.

Groner managed this flow with discipline and devotion:

**Yechidus Appointments**: Private audiences with the Rebbe were the most intimate form of interaction in Chabad life. People waited months for a yechidus. Groner scheduled these meetings, prepared the Rebbe's files, and ensured the smooth flow of visitors — often late into the night, as the Rebbe's yechidus sessions could run until the early morning hours.

**Correspondence**: The Rebbe received and responded to an extraordinary volume of mail. Groner processed incoming letters, organized them for the Rebbe's review, and dispatched the Rebbe's responses. Many of these letters — collected in the multi-volume _Igros Kodesh_ — contain the Rebbe's guidance on every conceivable life question.

**Daily Schedule**: The Rebbe maintained a grueling schedule — davening, study, audiences, public addresses, correspondence, and visits to the Ohel. Groner coordinated the logistics of this daily routine for decades.

---

## The Rebbe's Later Years

After the Rebbe suffered a stroke in 1992 that left him unable to speak, Groner's role became even more critical. He helped interpret the Rebbe's communications — nods, gestures, and facial expressions — for visitors and the broader community. He was one of the few people who could read the Rebbe's non-verbal cues with reliability.

During the Rebbe's final years (1992–1994), Groner was a constant presence at his side.

---

## Working Style

Groner was known for several distinctive qualities:

- **Absolute discretion**: The Rebbe's private audiences covered the most intimate details of people's lives — marriages, health crises, business decisions, spiritual struggles. Groner maintained total confidentiality.
- **Fierce protectiveness**: He guarded the Rebbe's time and energy with intensity, sometimes earning a reputation for brusqueness — but always in service of ensuring the Rebbe could function at maximum capacity.
- **Institutional memory**: Having served for decades, Groner possessed an unparalleled knowledge of the Rebbe's preferences, past decisions, and relationships.

---

## Passing and Legacy

Rabbi Groner passed away on April 2, 2020, at the age of 88. His death was mourned throughout the Chabad world as the loss of one of the last living links to the Rebbe's daily life and personal presence.

He served continuously from the early years of the Rebbe's leadership through the Rebbe's passing and beyond — a lifetime of service that few could match in its intimacy and devotion.
