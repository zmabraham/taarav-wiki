# Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk

**Born**: c. 1741, Kalisk (Kolyshki), Belarus  
**Died**: 1810, Tiberias, Eretz Yisrael  
**Role**: Chassidic leader; co-leader of the 1777 aliyah to Eretz Yisrael; settled in Tiberias

---

## Overview

Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk was a major Chassidic leader and disciple of the [[entities/people/maggid-of-mezeritch|Maggid of Mezeritch]] who co-led the 1777 Chassidic aliyah to Eretz Yisrael alongside [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]]. He settled in Tiberias and led the community there after Menachem Mendel's death in 1788.

---

## Character and Controversy

Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk was known for his intense, exuberant style of Chassidic practice — including demonstrations of joyful ecstasy during prayer that some found excessive. His emotional, unconventional approach drew criticism from the more restrained Lithuanian Torah world, and even some tension with the intellectually-focused [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]].

Despite these tensions, Rabbi Avraham maintained his Chassidic commitments and his relationship with the broader movement.

---

## The 1777 Aliyah

Rabbi Avraham was among the leaders of the historic 1777 Chassidic immigration to Eretz Yisrael — one of the largest organized pre-modern aliyot. The group of approximately 300 Chassidim faced enormous hardships during their journey and in settlement.

They settled in Tiberias, on the Sea of Galilee — one of the four holy cities and a city with existing Sephardic and Ashkenazic communities.

---

## Leadership in Tiberias

After [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk's]] death in 1788, Rabbi Avraham took on the leading role in the Tiberias Chassidic community. He served for over two decades, corresponding with diaspora Chassidic leaders and maintaining the community — always in precarious financial circumstances dependent on diaspora _chalukah_ (charitable distribution).

He died in Tiberias in 1810.

---

## Correspondence and the Alter Rebbe

Rabbi Avraham's correspondence with the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] constitutes some of the earliest documentation of the Chabad community's connection to Eretz Yisrael. Despite moments of friction, the Alter Rebbe supported the Tiberias community financially and spiritually.
