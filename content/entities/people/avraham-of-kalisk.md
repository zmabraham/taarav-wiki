# Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk

**Born**: c. 1741, Kalisk (Kolyshki), Belarus  
**Died**: 1810, Tiberias, Eretz Yisrael  
**Role**: Chassidic leader; co-leader of the 1777 aliyah to Eretz Yisrael; settled in Tiberias

---

## Overview

Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk was a major Chassidic leader and disciple of the [[entities/people/maggid-of-mezeritch|Maggid of Mezeritch]] who co-led the 1777 Chassidic aliyah to Eretz Yisrael alongside [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]]. He settled in Tiberias and led the community there after Menachem Mendel's death in 1788.

---

## Character and Controversy

Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk was known for his intense, exuberant style of Chassidic practice — including demonstrations of joyful ecstasy during prayer that some found excessive. His emotional, unconventional approach drew criticism from the more restrained Lithuanian Torah world, and even some tension with the intellectually-focused [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]].

Despite these tensions, Rabbi Avraham maintained his Chassidic commitments and his relationship with the broader movement. His path emphasized the primacy of _emunah_ (faith) and _bittul_ (self-nullification) as the central pillars of divine service, a perspective rooted in the Talmudic dictum "the righteous shall live by his faith" (Habakkuk 2:4). He devoted considerable effort in his correspondence to articulating this approach, positioning it as an alternative to the intellectualist Chabad methodology.

---

## The 1777 Aliyah

Rabbi Avraham was among the leaders of the historic 1777 Chassidic immigration to Eretz Yisrael — one of the largest organized pre-modern aliyot. The caravan originally set out from Russia in the month of Adar 5537 (1777) with a select group of the Maggid's disciples, including [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]] and Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk. Along the way, more than four hundred people joined the procession, swelling it far beyond its original size.

The journey, which lasted approximately six months, was marked by extraordinary hardship. One of the ships carrying immigrants sank in a storm at sea, and survivors arrived destitute in the Crimean city of Gozlov, where local Chassidim took them in. The survivors pressed on to Eretz Yisrael. The group arrived on 5 Elul 5537, settling primarily in **Tiberias** on the Sea of Galilee. Some Chassidim, including followers of [[entities/people/levi-yitzchak-of-berditchev|Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev]], settled separately in **Tzfat**.

Rabbi Avraham wrote a detailed account of the journey and its travails in a letter to the community of Smilian, dated winter 5538, published in _Likkutei Amarim_ (Epistle 29). This letter remains one of the primary sources for the history of the 1777 aliyah.

## Leadership in Tiberias

After [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk's]] death on 1 Iyar 5548 (1788), Rabbi Avraham assumed the leading role in the Tiberias Chassidic community. The Alter Rebbe referred to him in letters as "the Kohen Gadol [High Priest] who fills the place of our holy master in holiness and purity" — a title reflecting both his priestly lineage and his succession to Menachem Mendel's position.

Rabbi Avraham served for over two decades, corresponding with diaspora Chassidic leaders and maintaining the community. He wrote to the Alter Rebbe's chassidim in Russia nearly every year, encouraging them to follow the Alter Rebbe's teachings: "Your eyes behold your teacher, the great one, beloved of God, Mount Sinai — the famous and holy rabbi, Rabbi Schneur Zalman — warm yourselves in his light, you shall see the light of the world and the treasure of life and peace" (letter, 5550/1790). This endorsement was significant in legitimizing the Alter Rebbe's leadership among chassidim.

The community remained in precarious financial circumstances, dependent on diaspora _chalukah_ (charitable distribution). Rabbi Avraham appointed emissaries (_shadarim_) to collect funds in Russia and Poland, though conflicts over the distribution of these funds would later strain his relationship with the Alter Rebbe.

---

## Correspondence and the Alter Rebbe

Rabbi Avraham's correspondence with the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] constitutes some of the earliest documentation of the Chabad community's connection to Eretz Yisrael. Together with Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk, Rabbi Avraham had urged the Alter Rebbe to accept leadership of the chassidim in White Russia and Lithuania — even writing to chassidim in Russia to accept him as their leader.

Despite this early cooperation, significant tensions emerged between 5563 and 5565 (1803–1805). Malicious emissaries from Eretz Yisrael spread false rumors between Rabbi Avraham and the Alter Rebbe regarding both the leadership of the chassidim and the management of the Eretz Yisrael fund. The conflict escalated until Rabbi Avraham separated from the Alter Rebbe and appointed Rabbi Mordechai of Lechovitch to manage the Eretz Yisrael funds in Russia instead. The Alter Rebbe wrote a lengthy response defending himself (Igrot Kodesh, Epistle 51, summer 5565), and ultimately the dispute was resolved. Rabbi Avraham acknowledged the Alter Rebbe's response and wrote: "The earlier matters shall not be remembered" — signaling reconciliation.

He died in Tiberias in 1810.
