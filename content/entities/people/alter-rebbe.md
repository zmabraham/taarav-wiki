# Alter Rebbe

## Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi (c. 1745–1812)

**Title:** כ"ק אדמו"ר הזקן (His Holiness Our Master and Teacher, the Elder)
**Also known as:** The Alter Rebbe (Yiddish for "Old Rebbe"), the Baal HaTanya, the Rav, R' Schneur Zalman
**Born:** 18 Elul 5505 (September 4, 1745), Liozna, Belarus
**Died:** 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812), Pyena (Piena), Russia
**Buried:** Haditch, Ukraine
**Father:** Baruch Schneerson
**Role:** Founder of Chabad Hasidism

---

## Genealogy and Early Life

Research documented in the Czarist Russia history volume traces the Alter Rebbe's ancestry through a distinguished rabbinic line:

**R' Moshe of Pozna → R' Baruch → R' Schneur Zalman of Liadi**

This lineage connects through several generations to the **Maharal of Prague** (R' Judah Loew ben Bezalel, c. 1520–1609) — placing Chabad's founder within the network of the most distinguished Ashkenazic rabbinic families.

### Birth and Childhood

Schneur Zalman was born in **Liozna** (near Vitebsk, in present-day Belarus) on **18 Elul 5505** — exactly one day before the Baal Shem Tov's passing (the Baal Shem Tov died on 19 Elul 5520, but Schneur Zalman was born 15 years earlier). This timing would later be noted as significant: the soul of the founder of Chabad entered the world as the soul of the Baal Shem Tov was preparing to depart.

From childhood, he displayed extraordinary intellectual gifts. By age five he was already studying Torah independently. His father, R' Baruch, recognized his son's unusual capacities and arranged for him to study with the finest teachers in the region.

### Early Education

The young Schneur Zalman studied in Liozna and nearby Vitebsk. His reputation as a **ilui** (child prodigy) spread quickly. By his early teens, he had mastered the entire Talmud and its commentaries, as well as Kabbalistic texts that were rarely studied by anyone under thirty.

At approximately age 16, he married **Sterna Segal**, the daughter of a wealthy merchant from Vitebsk. This marriage provided him with financial independence to pursue his studies without the burden of earning a livelihood.

---

## The Journey to Mezeritch (1764)

The pivotal moment in the Alter Rebbe's life came in **1764 (5524)**, when at age 19 he decided to travel to **Mezeritch** to study under the **Maggid of Mezeritch**, the Baal Shem Tov's principal successor.

This decision was not made lightly. The Alter Rebbe had heard about Chassidism and was deeply skeptical. He gathered arguments against the Chassidim and traveled to Mezeritch with the intention of refuting what he considered to be their heretical deviations from traditional Judaism.

### The Meeting with the Maggid

When the Alter Rebbe arrived in Mezeritch, the Maggid received him warmly but did not engage him in theological debate. Instead, he said: "A soul such as yours does not need arguments. Go to the study hall and review the tractate the students are studying."

The Alter Rebbe went to the study hall and reviewed the material. When he returned, the Maggid asked him what he had learned. The Alter Rebbe explained that he had understood the material intellectually, but now he perceived new depths — spiritual dimensions that transformed the entire meaning of the text.

The Maggid then engaged him in profound discussion about the nature of divine service, the transformation of character, and the pathway to closeness to God. The Alter Rebbe was overwhelmed. His skepticism evaporated, replaced by recognition that the Baal Shem Tov's teachings represented not a departure from Torah but the revelation of its deepest soul.

### Years in Mezeritch

The Alter Rebbe remained in Mezeritch for approximately **eight years** (c. 1764–1772), studying under the Maggid. The Maggid gave him special attention, recognizing both his extraordinary intellectual capacity and his spiritual purity.

During this period, the Alter Rebbe:

- Mastered the Chassidic teachings of the Baal Shem Tov as transmitted by the Maggid
- Began developing his own distinctive approach, which would later become Chabad
- Formed close relationships with other students of the Maggid, including R' Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and R' Avraham of Kalisk
- Learned the art of mystical contemplation and the transformation of consciousness

The Maggid once said of the Alter Rebbe: " Schneur Zalman, you are called by that name for a reason — 'Schneur' means 'two lights,' and you possess both the light of revealed Torah (niglah) and the light of hidden Torah (nistar). You will illuminate both worlds."

---

## After the Maggid's Passing (1772)

When the Maggid died on **19 Kislev 5533 (1772)**, the Chassidic movement faced its first succession crisis. The Maggid had not designated a single successor. Various students emerged as leaders in different regions.

The Alter Rebbe, then only 27 years old, returned to his hometown of **Liozna** and began attracting his own followers. However, for approximately **16 years (1772–1788)**, he did **not** publicly assume the title of "Rebbe." Instead, he taught and guided individuals without establishing a formal court.

During this period, he:

- Established his family in Liozna, where several children were born
- Began writing what would become the foundational texts of Chabad
- Corresponded extensively with other Chassidic leaders, particularly R' Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk
- Developed the distinctive Chabad approach to intellectual mystical contemplation

---

## Founding of Chabad

Around **1788 (5548)**, at approximately age 43, the Alter Rebbe was recognized as an independent Rebbe and formally established his court in **Liadi** (a different town from his birthplace of Liozna). This marked the official beginning of Chabad as a distinct Chassidic movement.

### What is Chabad?

The name "Chabad" (חב"ד) is an acronym for three intellectual sefirot (divine attributes) in Kabbalistic thought:

- **Chochmah** (Wisdom) — the flash of intuitive insight
- **Binah** (Understanding) — the development and elaboration of insight
- **Da'at** (Knowledge) — the internalization and integration of wisdom

Unlike other Chassidic approaches that emphasized emotional ecstasy and simple faith, Chabad emphasized **intellectual contemplation** as the primary pathway to divine connection. The Alter Rebbe taught that by deeply understanding the nature of divinity and the cosmic order, a person could transform consciousness from ego-centered to God-centered — and that this transformation would then manifest in emotional and behavioral change.

This intellectualist approach made Chabad particularly attractive to educated Jews, scholars, and those who found purely emotional religiosity insufficient.

## The Tanya and Other Writings

The Alter Rebbe was a prolific author whose works form the foundation of Chabad thought.

### Likkutei Amarim (The Tanya)

Around **1796 (5556)**, the Alter Rebbe began composing his magnum opus, **Likkutei Amarim** ("Collection of Discourses"), universally known as the **Tanya**. This work:

- Presents a systematic guide to spiritual life based on Chassidic thought
- Explains the psychological makeup of the human personality
- Provides practical guidance for overcoming negative traits and cultivating positive ones
- Addresses the existential problem of the "beinoni" — the person who is neither perfectly righteous nor wicked, but struggles with imperfect impulses
- Integrates Kabbalistic concepts into an accessible philosophical system

The Tanya was revolutionary in several ways:

1. **Accessibility**: It made mystical concepts comprehensible to ordinary Jews
2. **Systematic**: It presented Chassidic thought as a coherent philosophical system
3. **Practical**: It provided specific techniques for spiritual growth
4. **Inclusive**: It validated the spiritual struggles of ordinary people

The Tanya has been reprinted thousands of times in hundreds of locations worldwide. During Soviet persecution, Chabad chassidim risked imprisonment to print and distribute it. Today, it remains the foundational text of Chabad spirituality.

### Other Major Works

- **Sha'ar HaYichud V'HaEmunah** ("Gate of Unity and Faith") — a philosophical exposition of divine unity
- **Shulchan Aruch HaRav** — a comprehensive code of Jewish law, noted for its clear explanations of the reasoning behind halachic decisions
- **Likkutei Torah** — Chassidic discourses on Torah portions, festivals, and other themes
- **Torah Or** — Chassidic discourses with a different organizational structure
- **Igrot Kodesh** — collection of his extensive correspondence
- **Biurim L'Tanya** — explanatory notes on the Tanya
- **Siddur Im Dach** — a prayer book with extensive Chassidic commentary

## The Two Arrests (1798, 1800)

### The First Arrest (1798)

In **1798 (5558)**, the Alter Rebbe was arrested by Czarist authorities and taken to **St. Petersburg**. He was accused of anti-government activity, largely based on denunciations by opponents of Chassidism (the Mitnagdim, led by the Vilna Gaon).

**Specific charges:**

1. Sending funds to Eretz Yisrael (Palestine), which could aid the Ottoman Empire — then at war with Russia
2. Operating an unauthorized religious organization
3. Teaching doctrines that allegedly undermined state authority

The Alter Rebbe was imprisoned for approximately **seven weeks** and subjected to interrogation. The government minister in charge of his case, a certain Prince Kurakin, initially believed the denunciations but became convinced of the Alter Rebbe's innocence after meeting him.

The Alter Rebbe was released on **19 Kislev 5559 (November 30, 1798)** — a date that became the primary Chabad holiday, celebrated as **Yud-Tes Kislev** ("the Rosh Hashanah of Chassidism").

### The Second Arrest (1800)

In **1800 (5560)**, the Alter Rebbe was arrested again on similar charges. This second imprisonment lasted approximately **eight weeks** before ending in another complete acquittal.

These arrests established a pattern of **mesirus nefesh** (self-sacrifice) in the face of state persecution that would characterize Chabad history for generations. The Alter Rebbe's composed dignity during imprisonment, his refusal to compromise his principles, and his complete exoneration became legendary in the Chabad world.

## The Relationship with Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk

The Alter Rebbe maintained a close but complex relationship with **Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk**, another leading disciple of the Maggid who settled in Eretz Yisrael.

### The Aliyah of 1777

In **1777 (5537)**, R' Menachem Mendel led approximately **300 Chassidim** in aliyah (immigration) to Eretz Yisrael. The Alter Rebbe considered joining them but ultimately remained in Russia, having received guidance from the Maggid (after the Maggid's passing through mystical communication) that his mission was to develop and disseminate Chabad teaching in the diaspora.

### Correspondence and Influence

Despite the geographical separation, the two maintained extensive correspondence. R' Menachem Mendel's letters — published as **Pri HaAretz** — contain profound spiritual guidance and reflect his respect for the Alter Rebbe's leadership. The Alter Rebbe, in turn, supported the Eretz Yisrael community both financially and spiritually.

This relationship established Chabad's enduring connection to the Land of Israel, which would blossom in subsequent generations with the establishment of Chabad communities in Hebron, Tzfat, and eventually Jerusalem.

---

## The Vilna Gaon and the Cherem (Ban)

The most significant opposition to Chassidism — including Chabad — came from the **Vilna Gaon** (R' Elijah of Vilna, 1720–1797) and his followers, known as **Mitnagdim** (opponents).

### Attempts at Reconciliation

The Alter Rebbe made two journeys to Vilna (in **1783** and **1784**) seeking to meet with the Vilna Gaon and reconcile the growing divide between Chassidim and Mitnagdim. Tragically, the Gaon refused to receive him on both occasions.

The reasons for the Gaon's refusal remain debated:

- Some suggest he genuinely believed Chassidism to be heretical
- Others suggest he was misinformed about Chassidic teachings
- Still others suggest that political and communal factors influenced his stance
- Many Chassidic sources suggest the Gaon had no personal animus toward the Alter Rebbe but was pressured by his followers to take a public stand

### The Cherem of 1796

On **Hoshana Rabbah 5559 (1796)**, the Vilna Gaon issued his most severe **cherem** (ban) against Chassidism. This ban declared:

- Chassidic books to be heretical and forbidden
- Chassidim to be excluded from communal religious positions
- Marriage with Chassidim to be discouraged

The ban was particularly severe in Lithuania, which explains why Chabad's strongholds were primarily in Belarus and Russia rather than Lithuania. Despite this persecution, the Alter Rebbe refused to respond in kind. He instructed his followers to respect the Vilna Gaon's greatness and to avoid counter-bans or polemics.

## Family and Personal Life

The Alter Rebbe and his wife **Sterna** had a large family. Their children included:

1. **R' Chaim Avraham** (c. 1770–1815) — eldest son, briefly succeeded his father but deferred to his younger brother
2. **R' Dov Ber** (1773–1827) — the Mitteler Rebbe, second Chabad Rebbe
3. **R' Moshe** — died young
4. **R' Moshe** (another son with the same name) — survived but was not a Rebbe
5. **Daughter** — married R' Moshe of Vitebsk
6. **Daughter** — married R' Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev (according to some sources)

The Alter Rebbe was known for his extraordinary humility despite his greatness. He would often say: "I am nothing but a simple Jew who tries to serve God." When praised for his piety, he would respond: "If you only knew my true self, you would not praise me."

## Napoleon's Invasion and the Flight from Liadi (1812)

When **Napoleon Bonaparte invaded Russia** in 1812, the Alter Rebbe faced a momentous choice that would define Chabad's political orientation for generations.

### The Dilemma

Many Russian Jews hoped that French liberation would improve their conditions. Napoleon had emancipated the Jews in territories he conquered, dismantling the Pale of Settlement and granting civil rights. The prospect of French victory seemed to promise freedom from Czarist restrictions.

The Alter Rebbe took the opposite view: **he supported Russia against Napoleon**. His reasons were both spiritual and practical:

**Spiritual concerns:**

- Material freedom without spiritual discipline would lead to assimilation
- French liberalism would erode Jewish distinctiveness and observance
- Jewish emancipation on French terms was not true freedom but spiritual bondage

**Practical concerns:**

- Napoleon was ultimately hostile to tradition religion of all kinds
- The Russian government, despite its restrictions, permitted Jewish religious life
- A French victory might bring short-term gain but long-term spiritual loss

### The Flight

As French forces advanced, the Alter Rebbe organized a massive evacuation of Chabad chassidim eastward, away from the front lines. At age 67, despite his frail health, he personally led this exodus.

The journey was grueling:

- Travel in winter conditions
- Constant danger from advancing armies
- Limited food and shelter
- The Alter Rebbe's failing health

### Death in Pyena

The Alter Rebbe died on **24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812)** in the village of **Pyena** (also spelled Piena), while still fleeing the retreating French army. His final words were reported to be: "I am going to heaven, but my feet remain with you."

His body was later transferred to **Haditch, Ukraine**, where his ohel (burial site) became a major pilgrimage destination.

## Key Disciples of the Alter Rebbe

The Alter Rebbe trained a generation of Chassidic leaders who spread Chabad throughout Russia:

1. **R' Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk** (1730–1788) — led the first major Chassidic aliyah to Eretz Yisrael
2. **R' Avraham of Kalisk** (1741–1810) — co-leader of the Eretz Yisrael community
3. **R' Aharon HaLevi of Staroselye** (1766–1828) — founded a branch of Chabad in Staroselye
4. **R' Hillel of Paritch** (1795–1864) — major mashpia (spiritual mentor) of the Tzemach Tzedek era
5. **R' Pinchas Reizes of Shklov** — compiler of early Chabad texts
6. **R' Moshe Vilenker** — fiery early chassid known for unusual devotions

These disciples established Chabad communities throughout White Russia, Ukraine, and Lithuania, creating the network that would become the Chabad movement.

## Legacy and Impact

The Alter Rebbe's legacy is impossible to overstate:

### 1. Chabad Philosophy

He created a systematic philosophical approach that integrated:

- Kabbalistic mysticism
- Rational philosophy
- Halachic (Jewish legal) rigor
- Psychological insight
- Practical guidance for daily life

This synthesis made Chassidic thought accessible to intellectual Jews while maintaining the depth of mystical experience.

### 2. Legal Scholarship

His **Shulchan Aruch HaRav** remains one of the most important codes of Jewish law, studied by yeshiva students across the Orthodox world — not only within Chabad.

### 3. The Tanya

The Tanya has been reprinted thousands of times. During Soviet persecution, chassidim risked imprisonment to print it. Today, it is studied globally in dozens of languages.

### 4. Leadership Model

The Alter Rebbe established the model of Rebbe-chassid relationship that would characterize Chabad:

- The Rebbe as spiritual guide and mentor
- Hiskashrus (spiritual bonding) between Rebbe and chassid
- The Rebbe as intercessor who elevates the prayers of his followers
- The principle of **bittul** (self-nullification) before the Rebbe's wisdom

### 5. Political Stance

His choice to support Russia against Napoleon established Chabad's pattern of:

- Accommodation with secular authority when it permits religious life
- Resistance when secular authority demands religious compromise
- Prioritizing spiritual survival over material benefit

### 6. Dynastic Leadership

He founded both a religious movement and a dynasty. The leadership passed from him to his son (Mitteler Rebbe), grandson (Tzemach Tzedek), and continued through seven generations of Rebbes — culminating in the seventh Rebbe, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (1902–1994), who transformed Chabad into a global movement.
