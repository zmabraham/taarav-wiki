# Israel Baal Shem Tov (Besht)

Rabbi Israel ben Eliezer, known as the Baal Shem Tov (abbreviated Besht), was the founder of the Chassidic movement. Born 18 Elul 1697 in Tlust (Podolia) and passed away 6 Sivan 1760 (first day of Shavuot) in Medzhibozh, Ukraine.

## Biography

Orphaned at age five, the young Israel was taken under the community's care. He spent years studying in private, frequenting forests for solitary contemplation. At age 14 he joined the society of hidden tzaddikim (anshei nistarin), and at 18 was formally appointed their leader. He received teachings from his heavenly master Achiya HaShiloni for ten years, and at age 36 (17 Elul 5494 / 1734) was commanded to reveal himself publicly.

The Besht traveled widely across Podolia, Volhynia, and Galicia, healing the sick through remedies and amulets, uplifting ordinary Jews, and teaching a revolutionary approach to divine service centered on joy, love of God, and the equal spiritual worth of every Jew. He eventually settled in Medzhibozh around 1740, where thousands of disciples gathered around him.

On Rosh Hashana 5507 (1746) he ascended spiritually to the chamber of Mashiach and asked when the redemption would come; Mashiach answered: "When your wellsprings spread outward" — linking the spread of Chassidic teachings to the redemption. He recorded this in a letter to his brother-in-law Rabbi Gershon of Kitov (the famous "Igeret HaGeulah").

In 26 Tammuz 5519 he defeated the Frankist sect in Lvov. He passed away on the first day of Shavuot 5520, having predicted his own death.

## Core Teachings
- Divine Providence (hashgacha pratit) extends to every detail of creation
- The power of thought: "Where a person's thought is, there is the person himself"
- Uplifting the common Jew through simple faith and joy
- The hidden spark of holiness even within evil

## Students
Key disciples include: the Maggid of Mezeritch (successor), Rabbi Yaakov Yosef of Polonne, Rabbi Pinchas of Koritz, Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk, Rabbi Nachman of Horodenko, and others of the "Chevra Kadisha."

## Family
- Son: Rabbi Tzvi
- Daughter: Adel (whose sons include Rabbi Moshe Chaim Ephraim of Sudilkov and Rabbi Baruch of Medzhibozh)
- Great-great-grandchild: Rabbi Nachman of Breslov
