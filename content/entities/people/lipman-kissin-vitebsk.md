# Lipman (Hananya) Kissin of Vitebsk

**Parnas (lay leader) of the Vitebsk Jewish community; brother of Rabbi Yisrael Kissin; father of the informer Simcha Kissin**

## Biography

Lipman Kissin — also known by the Hebrew name Hananya — served as a Parnas, or lay communal leader, of the Vitebsk Jewish community during the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries. The Parnas was an elected or appointed officer responsible for managing communal affairs, finances, and relations with civil authorities, making it one of the most powerful positions in Jewish communal governance.

Lipman Kissin was the brother of Rabbi Yisrael Kissin, the official Rav of Vitebsk and a student of the Vilna Gaon. Together, the two brothers represented a family with significant influence over both the religious and civic dimensions of Vitebsk Jewish life. Their family was also connected by marriage to the Alter Rebbe's household through the Segal family: Rebbetzin Sterna, the Alter Rebbe's wife, came from the Vitebsk Segal family which was related to the Kissins.

Lipman Kissin is primarily remembered in Chabad historical sources because of his son: Simcha Kissin became one of the most damaging informers (malshinim) against the Chabad movement in the 1820s. Simcha's testimony to Russian authorities contributed to the arrests and exile of significant Chabad figures. The transition from Lipman's position of communal respectability to his son's role as an informer reflects the complex dynamics within Vitebsk Jewish society — dynamics in which proximity to power, personal grievances, and ideological opposition could combine with devastating effects.

The Kissin family's trajectory illustrates a broader phenomenon in early nineteenth-century Russian Jewish communities: families that had been established and respectable in the eighteenth century sometimes found themselves caught up in the political and ideological conflicts of the Alexandrine and Nicholene periods, with individual members taking dramatically different positions toward the Chassidic movement.

## Significance

Lipman Kissin's importance is partly genealogical (as the father of Simcha Kissin) and partly structural (as an example of lay communal power in Vitebsk). Understanding his position helps explain the social context in which the Kissin family operated and why Simcha Kissin's later actions had the impact they did: he came from a family that genuinely had connections, standing, and credibility that made his testimony to Russian authorities dangerous. The contrast between his father's communal leadership and his own role as an informer is a small but revealing window into the pressures on Jewish communal life in early nineteenth-century Russia.
