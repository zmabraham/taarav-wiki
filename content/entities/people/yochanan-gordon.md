# R' Yochanan Gordon

**Shochet from Doksitz; central figure in the development of Chabad study and practice in New York**

## Overview

R' Yochanan Gordon was a shochet (ritual slaughterer) who came to America from the town of Doksitz (Dokshitsy, in the Minsk region). He became one of the most important figures in R' Yisrael Jacobson's efforts to build a Chassidic community and study culture in Brooklyn and the Bronx during the late 1920s and 1930s. He served as baal tefila (prayer leader), baal tokea (shofar blower), and teacher of Chassidus to multiple groups, including the first organized women's Chassidus study group in America.

## Background

R' Yochanan came to America with his family, initially wearing the traditional Russian caps (kasket) common in Eastern Europe. Upon arriving, he bought American-style hats (shlyappe) for his children. His son Shalom Dov once hid outside when arriving at Jacobson's home, embarrassed to enter wearing a fedora. Jacobson comforted the boy with a teaching about dress and context that he had heard from both the Rebbe Rashab and the Rayatz.

R' Yochanan's son R' Nissan Gordon and his son R' Shalom Dov Gordon (as well as R' Yisrael Gordon, who was a small child at the time) all became fixtures in the Chabad community that formed around Jacobson.

## Religious Role

R' Yochanan served as **baal tefila and baal tokea** for the special Yamim Noraim minyan that Jacobson organized for his student-disciples beginning around 5694 (1933–1934). This minyan, held initially in the Bikur Cholim hall on Osborn Street in Brownsville, later moved to the Anshei Azaritch synagogue at 885 Hopkins Avenue.

He also taught Chassidus regularly as part of Jacobson's study circuit. He led early classes before davening, read the Torah, and was a stabilizing presence in the growing Chabad minyan.

## Leil Shabbat and Weekly Gatherings

On Rosh Hashana, when Jacobson's group made the walking circuit after Tashlich to visit homes and celebrate, they would stop first at R' Yochanan's house in East New York, sit in Chassidic fellowship, study a Chassidic discourse, and then daven Maariv — creating a model of Chassidic home hospitality that helped define the community's culture.

## Teaching the Women's Group

Following the Rayatz's 1937 visit to Europe, where he urged Jacobson to organize women's Chassidus study groups (as had been done in Riga), R' Yochanan Gordon was designated one of the three "shepherds" (_ro'im_) instructed by the Rayatz to teach the women's group. The Rayatz's letter to Jacobson named R' Yochanan Gordon, R' Shlomo Aharon Kazarnovsky, and Jacobson himself as the teachers — the first organized women's Chassidus study group in America, which took the name "Achos Temimim" (Sisters of the Temimim).

## Relationship with Jacobson

R' Yochanan occasionally despaired about the challenge of maintaining a regular minyan. In one characteristic exchange, he complained to Jacobson: "What do you want from me with your dreams of making a minyan — we have to go upstairs at 10 o'clock in the morning to beg one or two people to come down for a minyan..." But the minyan eventually stabilized and grew.

The Gordon family — brothers R' Yochanan and R' Reicha Yitzchak (Reitzen) Gordon — were among the most devoted members of the Chabad circle Jacobson built.

## Significance

R' Yochanan Gordon represents the immigrant generation of Chabad-trained shochetim who formed the grassroots of the American Chabad community before the Rayatz's permanent arrival. His presence across multiple roles — shochet, baal tefila, baal tokea, Chassidus teacher — and his consistent participation in Jacobson's projects illustrates the commitment required to sustain Chabad religious life in 1930s New York.
