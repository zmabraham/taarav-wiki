# R' Moshe Akselrad

**Chabad rabbi who served in Zlobin and later Sorazh; founder of a small yeshiva in Sorazh; graduate of Tomchei Temimim**

## Biography

R' Moshe Akselrad was a graduate of Tomchei Temimim (he is described in the Shusterman memoir as a "tamim" — a Tomchei Temimim alumnus). He served as rabbi of Zlobin, White Russia, before being appointed rabbi of Sorazh — described as a more prestigious rabbinate than Zlobin, being an "ir gdola shel chachamim v'soferim" (a great city of scholars and scribes). A warm letter of blessing from the Rayatz is cited for R' Akselrad upon his appointment to Sorazh.

In Sorazh, R' Akselrad attempted to found a small yeshiva. He recruited seven students from Zlobin (including the young Shusterman) and three others from the surroundings, creating a group of ten students. The yeshiva functioned for at least one full term (summer 1927), with the students learning Tractate Gittin.

The yeshiva was disrupted when a contagious illness (scarlet fever) broke out in Zlobin, and the community of Sorazh refused to let the Zlobin students return for fear of spreading the illness.

R' Akselrad left Russia in the late 1920s or 1930s and settled in the United States.

## Significance

R' Moshe Akselrad represents the generation of Tomchei Temimim graduates who took on rabbinical positions throughout the former Soviet Union while continuing educational activities under Soviet pressure. His attempt to found a small yeshiva in Sorazh — teaching students in the midst of the Soviet anti-religious campaigns — demonstrates the determination of Chabad-trained rabbis to maintain Torah education regardless of the political environment.
