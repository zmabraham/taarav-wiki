# Rabbi Chaim Mordechai Aizik Hodakov

**Born**: 1902, Bobruisk, Belarus  
**Died**: 1993, Brooklyn, New York  
**Role**: Chief secretary and executive director of Chabad-Lubavitch institutions under the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]

---

## Overview

Rabbi Chaim Mordechai Aizik Hodakov was the most powerful administrative figure in Chabad-Lubavitch for over four decades. Serving as the chief of staff and executive director of the Rebbe's organizational apparatus, he translated the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe's]] visionary directives into institutional reality. He ran Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch (Chabad's central educational arm), Machne Israel, and Kehot Publication Society — the three pillars of Chabad's organizational infrastructure.

If the Rebbe was the mind and heart of the movement, Hodakov was the nervous system — connecting directives from 770 to thousands of emissaries, institutions, and communities worldwide.

---

## Early Life

Hodakov was born in Bobruisk, Belarus — a city with deep Chabad roots. He studied at the Tomchei Temimim yeshiva system, absorbing both the spiritual and organizational ethos of Chabad education. He was known from youth for his sharp intellect, organizational precision, and tireless work ethic.

---

## Service to the Rayatz

In 1927, Hodakov began serving as secretary to the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] during the Riga period (1927–1933). This was a formative experience: the Rayatz was rebuilding Chabad's institutional structure after fleeing the Soviet Union, and Hodakov was at the center of this reconstruction.

He continued serving the Rayatz after the move to Warsaw (1933) and then to America (1940). His transition from the Rayatz's administration to the seventh Rebbe's was seamless — a continuity of institutional memory that proved invaluable.

---

## Executive Director Under the Seventh Rebbe

After the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] assumed leadership in 1951, Hodakov became the primary administrative executor of the Rebbe's vision:

**Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch**: As director of Chabad's educational arm, Hodakov oversaw the establishment and coordination of Chabad schools, yeshivos, and educational programs worldwide. Every new Chabad school — from Brooklyn to Buenos Aires to Melbourne — passed through his office.

**Machne Israel**: The social services and outreach arm of Chabad. Hodakov managed the growing network of community programs, holiday campaigns, and individual welfare cases.

**Kehot Publication Society**: The publishing house responsible for disseminating the Rebbe's teachings, Chassidic texts, and educational materials in multiple languages.

---

## Working Style

Hodakov was legendary for his discipline, precision, and fierce devotion to the Rebbe's instructions. His office at 770 Eastern Parkway was the nerve center of Chabad operations:

- He maintained meticulous records and followed up relentlessly on every directive
- Meetings with him were known for their brevity and clarity — he did not tolerate vagueness
- He served as gatekeeper, ensuring that only matters requiring the Rebbe's personal attention reached the Rebbe
- His loyalty was absolute; he implemented the Rebbe's wishes with exactitude, never substituting his own judgment

Shluchim (emissaries) who corresponded with 770 often dealt with Hodakov as their primary contact. His letters — precise, formal, and authoritative — carried the weight of the Rebbe's office.

---

## Role in the Shlichus System

As the [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] network expanded globally, Hodakov played a critical role in the logistics of dispatching and supporting emissaries:

- Processing shlichus assignments
- Coordinating financial support
- Resolving territorial and jurisdictional questions between shluchim
- Maintaining communication between the Rebbe and the field

The organizational infrastructure he built enabled the explosive growth of Chabad from dozens of outposts in the 1950s to thousands by the 1990s.

---

## Legacy

Rabbi Hodakov served continuously from the Rayatz era through the seventh Rebbe's entire leadership — a span of nearly six decades of institutional service. He died in 1993, one year before the Rebbe's passing, having devoted his entire adult life to Chabad's organizational mission.

His contribution was not glamorous but it was indispensable. The global Chabad infrastructure that exists today — thousands of Chabad Houses, schools, and institutions on every continent — was built on the administrative foundation that Hodakov laid.
