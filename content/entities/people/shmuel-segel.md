# Rabbi Shmuel Segel — ר' שמואל זעגל

**Role**: Chabad shliach (emissary) in Berlin, Germany; partner of [[entities/people/yehuda-teichtel|Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel]] in the Berlin renewal  
**Location**: Berlin, Germany  
**Period**: Late 20th century – present

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shmuel Segel was one of the founding figures of the post-Holocaust Chabad renewal in Berlin, working alongside [[entities/people/yehuda-teichtel|Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel]] to establish the "Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin" — the major Chabad center that became a landmark of Jewish renewal in post-war Germany.

---

## The Berlin Renewal

The partnership of Segel and Teichtel in Berlin represented the culmination of the seventh Rebbe's instruction to maintain Jewish activity in both East and West Germany. Together they established:

- The "Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin" — a major Chabad center in central Berlin
- A synagogue seating 250 worshippers
- The "Yudishe Traditsyone Shule Ohr Avner" — a Jewish school housed in a former Nazi officers' club building
- A Jewish restaurant, mikveh, library, and youth center
- Shabbat minyanim that grew from 20 people to hundreds

Chancellor Angela Merkel acknowledged this achievement as evidence of genuine Jewish confidence in Germany's future.

---

## Significance

Rabbi Segel's work in Berlin represents the most dramatic irony in Chabad history: the movement whose fifth Rebbe (the Rashab) called Kaiser Wilhelm II "Amalek" and despised Germany — and whose leadership survived the Holocaust — ultimately established a thriving center in the capital of that same country.
