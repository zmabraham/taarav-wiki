# Rabbi Binyamin Zusman

**Born**: Early 20th century, Russia
**Role**: Soviet Chabad figure; underground activities

---

## Overview

Rabbi Binyamin Zusman was a Soviet-era Chabad chassid who maintained Jewish religious life under Communist persecution. As part of the underground network that kept Torah alive throughout the Soviet Union, Reb Binyamin participated in secret religious activities that required constant courage and commitment.

While detailed biographical information about Reb Binyamin is limited, his story is part of the broader narrative of Soviet-era Jewish resistance — the countless individual acts of faith and courage that prevented Judaism from being extinguished under Communist rule.

---

## Underground Activities

Reb Binyamin was active in the Chabad underground network that operated throughout the Soviet Union during the decades of religious suppression.

**Secret Minyanim and Services**: Reb Binyamin helped organize clandestine prayer services and Torah study sessions. These gatherings would meet in private homes, moving constantly to avoid detection by the authorities. Each gathering represented an act of defiance and spiritual commitment.

**Educational Activities**: Like other Soviet-era chassidim, Reb Binyamin participated in efforts to transmit Torah knowledge to the next generation. This included teaching children, preparing boys for bar mitzvah, and maintaining Jewish education under impossible conditions.

**Holiday Observances**: Jewish holidays posed particular challenges under Soviet rule, when public observances were impossible and even private celebrations were dangerous. Reb Binyamin helped organize secret seders, High Holiday services, and other holiday observances.

## Life Under Soviet Persecution

The experience of Soviet-era chassidim like Reb Binyamin reflects the broader story of Jewish survival under Communism.

**Constant Risk**: Participation in underground religious activities carried constant risk of arrest, interrogation, and imprisonment. The NKVD (later KGB) maintained extensive surveillance of religious activists.

**Resourcefulness**: Maintaining Jewish life under Soviet conditions required extraordinary resourcefulness — obtaining religious books that were officially banned, finding ways to perform circumcisions in secret, arranging for kosher food, and coordinating communications between scattered communities.

**Community Resilience**: Despite the pressures of persecution, Soviet Jewish communities demonstrated remarkable resilience. Chassidim like Reb Binyamin formed networks of mutual support that enabled Jewish life to continue even in the darkest periods.

## Legacy

Reb Binyamin Zusman is remembered as one of the many Soviet-era chassidim who kept Judaism alive through decades of persecution. Though his story may not be as widely documented as some of his contemporaries, his contribution to Jewish survival was no less significant.

**Oral Tradition**: Much of what we know about Soviet-era chassidim like Reb Binyamin comes through oral tradition — stories passed down from generation to generation. These stories preserve the memory of individual heroism and communal resilience.

**Inspiration**: The example of Reb Binyamin and others like him continues to inspire contemporary Jews. Their mesirus nefesh (self-sacrifice) for Torah and mitzvot demonstrates the power of faith and commitment even under the most difficult circumstances.
