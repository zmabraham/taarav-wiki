# Rabbi Yitzchak Nissim

Rabbi Yitzchak Nissim (25 Kislev 5656 – 9 Av 5741 / 1896–1981) was the Sephardic Chief Rabbi of Israel (Rishon LeTzion) from 5715–5733 (1955–1973), serving as the spiritual leader of the Sephardic Jewish community during Israel's formative decades.

## Biography

Born in Baghdad on the first day of Chanukah — hence the name "Nissim" (miracles) was added. At age ten he moved with his family to Jerusalem but soon returned alone to Baghdad to study Torah with the great Babylonian rabbis of the era. He became renowned throughout the region as "Hakham Nissim" — an ilui (prodigy) and gaon.

He married Victoria in Baghdad, then in 5685 (1925) ascended to Israel on a long journey by donkey lasting several months, settling in Jerusalem. He continued Torah study in his free time and maintained halachic correspondence with the Torah giants of the generation.

After the passing of Rishon LeTzion Rabbi Ben-Zion Meir Chai Uziel, he was elected in the Rabbinate elections of 6 Nissan 5715 (1955) as his successor, defeating Rabbi Yaakov Moshe Tolido by 42 to 27 votes. He served until 5733 (1973), when legislation changed the nature of the position and he was succeeded by Rabbi Ovadia Yosef.

He passed away on Tisha B'Av 5741 and was buried on Har HaZeitim in Jerusalem.

## Relationship with the Rebbe

During his tenure as Chief Rabbi, Rabbi Nissim maintained various contacts with the Rebbe, especially on public matters:

- After the murder at the Kfar Chabad trade school (5715/1955), he visited to offer condolences and encouraged the community's continued settlement
- In 5720 (1960), for the 200th anniversary of the Baal Shem Tov's passing, he wrote to figures under his influence encouraging them to honor the Baal Shem Tov's memory and Torah
- After a tour of Jewish diaspora communities in 5726 (1966), he wrote to the Rebbe with great admiration about the Chabad emissaries he encountered worldwide, expressing regret about places where there was no Chabad activity
- He cooperated with Chabad on Land of Israel issues and joined a Torah collection defending the prohibition of territorial concessions
- In 5728 (1968), the Rebbe spoke critically when Rabbi Nissim's decision in a family identity case was seen as undermining the Jewish identity laws
