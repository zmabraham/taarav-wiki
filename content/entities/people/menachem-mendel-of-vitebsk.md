# Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk

**Born**: c. 1730, Vitebsk, Belarus  
**Died**: 1 Iyar 5548 (May 12, 1788), Tiberias, Eretz Yisrael  
**Role**: Chassidic leader; led the first organized Chassidic aliyah to Eretz Yisrael (1777); settled in Tiberias

---

## Overview

Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk was one of the most important Chassidic leaders of the generation that came immediately after the [[entities/people/baal-shem-tov|Baal Shem Tov]]. He was a disciple of the [[entities/people/maggid-of-mezeritch|Maggid of Mezeritch]] and a close associate — and ultimately a leading critic — of the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] in the early years of Chabad's formation. Most significantly, he led the first organized Chassidic mass aliyah to Eretz Yisrael in 1777.

---

## Relationship with the Alter Rebbe

Rabbi Menachem Mendel was a major figure in the Chassidic world of White Russia and Belarus, and the Alter Rebbe (then known as Rabbi Schneur Zalman) initially operated under his leadership. When the Alter Rebbe began developing Chabad's distinct intellectual approach, some tension emerged with other Chassidic leaders, including — at certain points — with Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk.

Despite personal differences, Menachem Mendel maintained correspondence with the Alter Rebbe from the Land of Israel, and their relationship was ultimately respectful.

---

## The 1777 Aliyah

In 1777, Rabbi Menachem Mendel led a group of approximately 300 Chassidim from White Russia to Eretz Yisrael — one of the largest organized Jewish immigration waves to the Land of Israel in the pre-modern era.

The journey was extraordinarily difficult, with many travelers falling ill and dying en route. The group eventually split:

- Rabbi Menachem Mendel and his group settled in **Tiberias**, on the Sea of Galilee — one of the four holy cities of Jewish tradition
- [[entities/people/avraham-of-kalisk|Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk]] settled in **Tiberias** with him, and later co-led the community

The settlers were impoverished and dependent on fundraising (_chalukah_) from diaspora Jewish communities. They wrote frequent letters to the Alter Rebbe — who had not made aliyah — asking for support. These letters are among the earliest documents in Chabad's rich epistolary tradition.

---

## Legacy

Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk established one of the earliest organized Chabad-affiliated communities in Eretz Yisrael. The Tiberias community he founded persisted for generations. His decision to make aliyah — despite the extraordinary hardship — demonstrated the deep commitment to the Land of Israel that would characterize elements of the Chassidic movement.

He died in Tiberias in 1788, buried there. His grave remains a pilgrimage site.
