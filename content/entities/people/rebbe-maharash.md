# Rebbe Maharash — Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn

**כ״ק אדמו״ר מוהרש״ש נ״ע — The Fourth Rebbe**

**Born**: 2 Iyar 5594 (1834), Lubavitch  
**Died**: 13 Tishrei 5643 (1882), Lubavitch  
**Rebbe years**: 1866–1882  
**Father**: [[tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]]  
**Successor**: Son, [[rebbe-rashab|Rabbi Shalom DovBer (Rashab)]]

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn, the fourth Chabad Rebbe, is known for his vigorous diplomatic advocacy on behalf of Russian Jewry and for his distinctive teaching style and personal character. He led Chabad for sixteen years (1866–1882), a period of growing anti-Semitism in Czarist Russia, and made extensive trips to Western Europe to lobby for Jewish rights.

---

## Diplomatic Activity in Europe

The Maharash traveled repeatedly to Western European capitals — including Berlin, Vienna, and Paris — to intercede for Russian Jews suffering under Czarist oppression. He met with government ministers and influential Jewish community leaders. These efforts established a Chabad precedent of Rebbe-as-diplomat that his son the Rashab and grandson the Rayatz would continue.

---

## Key Teaching: "L'Chatchila Ariber"

The Maharash is famous for his aphorism: **"The world says: if you cannot go under [an obstacle], go around it. I say: L'chatchila ariber — from the outset, go over it."** This phrase became emblematic of Chabad's approach to overcoming difficulty directly rather than through accommodation or workarounds.

---

## Legacy

The Maharash's son [[rebbe-rashab|the Rashab]] was born in Lubavitch during his tenure, as was the entire next generation of Chabad leadership. His years consolidated Chabad's standing during the difficult post-Tzemach Tzedek period.
