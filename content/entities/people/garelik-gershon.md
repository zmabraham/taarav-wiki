# Rabbi Gershon Mendel Garelik

**Born**: 1935, Brooklyn, New York  
**Died**: 2014  
**Role**: Chabad shliach to Italy; founder of Chabad in Milan

---

## Overview

Rabbi Gershon Mendel Garelik was the founding shliach of Chabad in Milan, Italy, sent by the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]] in 1958. Over more than five decades of work, he built Chabad of Italy into one of the strongest Chabad communities in Western Europe, establishing schools, synagogues, a mikveh, and extensive communal infrastructure in Milan and beyond.

---

## Mission to Milan (1958)

Born in Brooklyn, Garelik was sent by the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]] to Milan in 1958. Italy at that time had a small but ancient Jewish community. Milan, as Italy's economic capital, had a growing Jewish population but limited traditional infrastructure. The Rebbe's vision was to establish a full Chabad presence that could serve both the existing Italian Jewish community and the many Jewish travelers and businesspeople who passed through Milan.

---

## Building Chabad of Italy

Garelik's achievements in Milan were comprehensive:

- **Synagogues** — established Chabad congregations serving the Milan Jewish community
- **Schools** — built educational institutions for Jewish children
- **Mikveh** — constructed a mikveh serving the broader community
- **Outreach programs** — reached unaffiliated Italian Jews and Jewish visitors
- **Community services** — kashrus supervision, holiday programming, and family services

Milan under Garelik's leadership became the anchor of Chabad in Italy and one of the premier Chabad centers in Western Europe.

---

## Legacy

Garelik's work in Milan laid the foundation for the broader expansion of Chabad across Italy. Today, Chabad centers operate in Rome, Florence, Venice, Turin, and other Italian cities — many of them offshoots or inspired by the model Garelik established in Milan. His son, Rabbi Berel Lazar, became the [[entities/people/berel-lazar|Chief Rabbi of Russia]], demonstrating the multigenerational impact of the shlichus enterprise.

He passed away in 2014 after more than fifty-five years of service in Italy.
