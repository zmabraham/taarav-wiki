# Rabbi Zusia of Anipoli (ר' זוסיא מאניפאלי)

**Beloved chassidic master and disciple of the Maggid of Mezeritch; gave approbation (haskama) to the first printing of the Tanya**

## Biography

Rabbi Meshulam Zusia of Anipoli — universally known simply as Reb Zusia — was one of the most beloved and celebrated disciples of Rabbi Dov Ber of Mezeritch (the Maggid), who was himself the primary successor to the Baal Shem Tov. Reb Zusia is famous throughout chassidic literature for his extraordinary humility, his simple piety, and the warm stories that grew up around his life and teachings.

Reb Zusia and his brother Rabbi Elimelech of Lizhensk were among the most influential early disciples of the Maggid. While Rabbi Elimelech became the primary transmitter of Chassidus to Polish Jewry, Reb Zusia remained more closely associated with the Ukrainian/Galician tradition. He served his master devotedly and became a figure of legend within the wider Chassidic world.

His connection to early Chabad history comes through his formal approbation (haskama) to the first printing of the Tanya. When the Alter Rebbe prepared his foundational work for publication in Slavuta in תקנ"ז (1796/7), he sought and received haskamot from senior chassidic leaders of his generation. Reb Zusia's haskama — alongside that of Rabbi Yehuda Leib HaKohen — served as an important endorsement, lending the work the authority of recognized spiritual masters outside the Chabad circle itself. This is historically significant because it positioned the Tanya not merely as a work of one school but as a contribution endorsed by the broader Chassidic world.

Reb Zusia passed away in תק"ס (1800). The town of Anipoli (Annopil in Polish, now in Ukraine) where he was based became a site of chassidic pilgrimage after his death. His grave is honored to this day.

## Significance

Reb Zusia's haskama on the Tanya represents a formal bridge between the broader Chassidic world and the emerging Chabad movement. By securing his endorsement, the Alter Rebbe both honored a beloved elder of the generation and gained the legitimating authority of a disciple in the direct chain from the Baal Shem Tov through the Maggid. Reb Zusia's universal popularity across chassidic groups meant that his endorsement carried weight beyond any particular school. His simple, story-rich life also stands as a foil to the intellectual rigor of Chabad's approach, illustrating that the Chassidic world encompassed radically different spiritual styles that nonetheless recognized each other.
