# Rebbetzin Rivka (מרת רבקה)

**Wife of the Maharash (4th Rebbe); grandmother of the Rebbe Rashab; revered matriarch of Lubavitch.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Husband** | Maharash — R' Shmuel Schneerson (4th Rebbe of Chabad) |
| **Son** | Rebbe Rashab (5th Rebbe) |
| **Grandson** | Rayatz (6th Rebbe) |
| **Residence** | Lubavitch court |
| **Passed away** | During Israel Jacobson's years at TT (~5674/1914 or earlier) |

## Overview

Rebbetzin Rivka was a beloved and highly respected figure at the Lubavitch court during the era of Tomchei Temimim. She maintained an open home where yeshiva students regularly ate meals at her table. Israel Jacobson notes that young students would receive 2–3 meals per week at Rebbetzin Rivka's table, as part of the informal welfare system for students studying far from home.

She was known for her compassion and her willingness to intervene with her son, Rebbe Rashab, on behalf of families seeking admission for their sons to the yeshiva. Poor mothers, widows, and others would weep and beg before her, and she would advocate for their children.

## In the Zachor Memoirs

Jacobson recounts two moving episodes of her intercessions:

1. Two brothers from Smilavichy (the "uncle and nephew" Arantchik family) — the uncle was accepted but the nephew was initially rejected. Eventually, through persistent pleading with Rebbetzin Rivka, both were accepted.

2. A widow from Zembin came with two sons (Moshe Reuven and Shraga Feivish). The yeshiva refused. She stayed in Lubavitch for a long time, crying daily at the doors of influential people. Only through Rebbetzin Rivka's tearful intercession with her son was admission granted. Moshe Reuven later became shochet in Kursk.

Also mentioned: Rebbetzin Rivka hosted the Rebbe Rashab's Shavuot discourses at the large table in her dining room. The first such occasion was Shavuot 5667, when the Rashab gave a brief accessible discourse that all students could repeat — then the following year gave a very deep discourse at the same occasion.
