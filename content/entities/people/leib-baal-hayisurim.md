# R. Leib Baal HaYisurim

A Chabad Chassid and early settler in the Holy Land. His sobriquet **"Baal HaYisurim"** (בעל היסורים — "Master of Suffering") reflects either a life of physical illness and hardship, or a known practice of accepting suffering as spiritual service. He is documented in *Toldos Chabad beEretz HaKodesh* as a notable early Chabad figure in the Holy Land.

## Timeline

- Lived in [[safed|Safed]] as part of the Chabad community there
- Eventually moved from Safed to [[hebron|Hebron]] (following the Mitteler Rebbe's general directive to the Holy Land Chassidim)
- **Died 15 Marcheshvan 5597** (c. October/November 1836) — just weeks before the great earthquake of 1 Shevat 5597 (January 1837) that devastated Safed

## Significance

His death before the earthquake meant he did not become one of its victims. His move from Safed to Hebron reflects the broader migration of Chabad Chassidim to Hebron as Chabad's primary Holy Land center during the 1820s–1840s.

## The Name "Baal HaYisurim"

In Chabad literature and tradition, individuals who accepted suffering or illness with equanimity were sometimes given honorific nicknames reflecting this quality. "Baal HaYisurim" (or "Baal haYisurin") appears occasionally in Chabad accounts as a mark of spiritual distinction.
