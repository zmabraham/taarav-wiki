# Rabbi Yosef Hartman of Kiryat Malachi

Rabbi Yosef Hartman (born 23 Tishrei 5702 / 1941, Tel Aviv) is an educator, lecturer, and author who has lived for decades in Nachalat Har Chabad (Kiryat Malachi). He served as a teacher and first principal of the Beit HaMelachah vocational school in Nachalat Har Chabad, and as rabbi of the Chabad girls school there. He is a seventh-generation descendant of the Alter Rebbe and has been active in the Tefillin campaign for decades.

## Biography

Born in Tel Aviv to Rabbi Nachman Shalom Shkna Hartman and Beila (née Shdami). His father was himself an educator who received the title "Yakir HaChinuch HaDati" in Israel.

In 5718 (1958) he began studies at Yeshivat Bnei Akiva Kfar HaRoeh under Rabbi Moshe Tzvi Neriah, volunteering to teach youth in surrounding communities. Through Tanya study sessions organized with mashpiah Rabbi Meir Shalom Blizinsky, Rabbi Rafael Amos Karniel was drawn to Chabad — a connection that later bore significant fruit.

After high school, guided by mashpiah Rabbi Reuven Donin, he transferred to Tomchei Tmimim Lod (1959), then to Tomchei Tmimim Kfar Chabad under mashpiah Rabbi Shlomo Chaim Kesselman.

In Elul 5722 he traveled to the Rebbe for Tishrei. He was sent by Tzeirei Chabad on a lecture tour across the United States, which was very successful. Upon returning to 770 for Shemini Atzeret, the Rebbe asked his secretary: "Where is Yosef Hartman?" — and consequently the gabbai honored him with carrying a Sefer Torah during Hakafot.

In 5724 he married Mathia Lerer (a granddaughter of the well-known Chassid Rabbi Avraham Paris). In 5730 (1970), on the Rebbe's shlichus, they moved to Nachalat Har Chabad in Kiryat Malachi, among its founding families. He established the Beit HaMelachah vocational school there for Soviet immigrants and at-risk youth.

From 5735–5765 he taught Bible and Oral Law at the regional religious high school. From 5736–5766 he served as rabbi of the Chabad girls school in Kiryat Malachi. After retiring, he launched daily Tefillin campaign activities in Kiryat Malachi and gives Torah classes in multiple venues.

Over nearly 60 years he taught and guided more than 7,000 students, as well as thousands of officers, soldiers, and kibbutz members he drew to Jewish heritage and identity.

## Connection to the Rebbe

He received personal recognition and acknowledgment from the Rebbe on several occasions, including the famous episode during Hakafot at 770. He also pioneered the publication of summaries of the Rebbe's talks in Israeli newspapers following the first broadcast of a farbrengen (10 Shevat 5730), an initiative that received the Rebbe's explicit encouragement and approval.
