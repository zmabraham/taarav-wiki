# Rabbi Shalom DovBer Levin

**Born**: c. 1940  
**Role**: Chabad archivist, historian, and editor; head librarian of the Agudas Chasidei Chabad Library at 770 Eastern Parkway

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shalom DovBer Levin is one of the most important Chabad historians and archivists of the modern era. As the head of the Chabad library and archive at 770 Eastern Parkway in Brooklyn, he has been the custodian and organizer of the vast Chabad documentary heritage. He is best known as the editor of the multi-volume _Mesibbv L'Chassidus_ series — the primary published collection of Chabad historical memoirs and documents.

---

## Family Background

Rabbi Levin is the son of the memoirist Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levin, whose memoirs appear in the _Zichronosai_ volume of the _Mesibbv L'Chassidus_ series. This family connection to the tradition of Chabad memoir-writing and historical preservation shaped his own life's work.

---

## Mesibbv L'Chassidus

Rabbi Levin edited the 13+ volumes of the _Mesibbv L'Chassidus_ series, which collects memoirs, documents, and historical materials related to the Chabad Rebbes and their communities. This series forms the basis of much primary-source research into Chabad history, including:

- Memoirs of chassidim who knew the Rebbes personally
- Historical documents from Chabad archives
- Correspondence and communal records
- Photographs and genealogical materials

The series is an indispensable resource for anyone studying Chabad history from the inside.

---

## Other Works

Beyond _Mesibbv L'Chassidus_, Rabbi Levin has authored major works on Chabad history, including a detailed biography of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn). His scholarly work combines archival rigor with deep insider knowledge of the Chabad tradition.

---

## The Chabad Library

As head of the Agudas Chasidei Chabad Library at 770, Rabbi Levin oversees one of the most important collections of Jewish manuscripts, rare books, and historical documents in the world. The library was the subject of a famous legal case in 1985-1987, which affirmed that the collection belonged to the Chabad movement rather than to any individual.
