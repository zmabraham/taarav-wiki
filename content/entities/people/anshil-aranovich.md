# R' Anshil Aranovich — ר' אנשיל אראנאוויטש

**Chabad Community Leader in Vilna; Martyred in the Holocaust**

**Full Name**: Rabbi Anshil Aranovich
**Dates**: (late 19th–1941/43)
**Position**: Chabad community leader in [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]]
**Affiliation**: Chabad-Lubavitch
**Death**: Martyred in the Holocaust (Ponary massacres, 1941–1943)

---

## Overview

Rabbi Anshil Aranovich was a Chabad communal leader in [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]] (Vilnius), Lithuania. He was one of the leaders of the Chabad community in Vilna during the interwar period, helping maintain the Chassidic presence in this historic Mitnagdic capital. He was martyred in the Holocaust, murdered in the Ponary massacres that destroyed Vilna's Jewish community between 1941 and 1943.

---

## Background

### Vilna's Chabad Community

Vilna's Chabad community in the interwar period:

- **Several synagogues**: A small but established presence
- **Historical weight**: Operating in the city historically most hostile to Chassidism
- **Diverse leadership**: Community had rabbis, mashpiim, and lay leaders like Aranovich

### Role in the Community

R' Aranovich's role as community leader:

- **Organizational leadership**: Helped manage the practical affairs of the Chabad community
- **Liaison**: Connected local Chabad members to the broader Chabad network
- **Community support**: Worked to support Chabad institutions in Vilna
- **Outreach**: Helped maintain Chabad presence in the broader Vilna Jewish world

---

## The Ponary Massacres

R' Anshil Aranovich perished in the Ponary massacres:

- **Ponary (Paneriai)**: Forest approximately 10 km from Vilna where Nazi Einsatzgruppen and Lithuanian collaborators murdered approximately 70,000 Jews
- **Timeline**: Mass killings began in July 1941, continued through 1943
- **Vilna Ghetto**: Vilna Jews were concentrated in a ghetto before being taken to Ponary
- **Total destruction**: Virtually all of Vilna's Jewish population was murdered

---

## Legacy

R' Anshil Aranovich is among the many Chabad leaders whose lives and work were cut short by the Holocaust. He represents:

- **Vilna Chabad's last generation**: Among the last leaders of a community with roots going back to the earliest period of Chassidism
- **The destroyed world**: The vibrant "Jerusalem of Lithuania" that was annihilated
