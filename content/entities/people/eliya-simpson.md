# R' Eliya (Eliyahu Yeichil) Simpson

**Temim, rabbi in Harlem, president of Agudas HaTmimim; pillar of early American Chabad**

## Overview

R' Eliya (Eliyahu Yeichil) Simpson was one of the founding pillars of Chabad in America in its earliest organized phase. He and his brother R' Gershon Simpson, both Temimim (graduates of Tomchei Temimim in Lubavitch) who had studied there for an extended period, were central figures in the small community of Chabad-connected Jews in New York in the 1920s.

## At Tomchei Temimim

R' Eliya and his brother R' Gershon studied for a long period at Tomchei Temimim in Lubavitch (the main yeshiva, not a branch). R' Yisrael Jacobson, who knew them both, described them as "pillars of our organization" — meaning the founding core of Agudas HaTmimim.

## Founding of Agudas HaTmimim

On Motzaei Shabbat Parshat Shemini 5686 (October 1925), a group of approximately twenty Tomchei Temimim graduates gathered at **R' Eliya Simpson's home at 116 West Street, No. 137, in New York** and formally founded Agudas HaTmimim — the Organization of Temimim, for students who had studied at Tomchei Temimim in America.

R' Eliya was elected as the first president of Agudas HaTmimim. His brother R' Gershon was elected treasurer. R' Yisrael Jacobson served as secretary. The meetings continued to be held at R' Eliya's home for the initial period of the organization's existence.

## Role as Rabbi

R' Eliya served as a rabbi in Harlem, New York — one of the neighborhoods that then housed a significant Jewish Orthodox population. As a rabbi in an American community, he was among the small group of Chabad-trained figures trying to maintain Chabad-style religious life in the difficult American environment of the 1920s.

When the Rayatz made his first visit to America in 5689–5690 (1929–1930), R' Eliya was among the approximately 30 Temimim who came for yechidus (private audience) before Rosh Hashana. This group represented the assembled Chabad community in America at that time.

## Agudas HaTmimim Activities

Under R' Eliya's leadership as president, and with R' Gershon as treasurer and Jacobson as secretary, the organization pursued several goals:

- Setting fixed times for Torah study and Chassidic learning (_limud haChassidus_)
- Discussing the _maamad_ (financial support fund for Chabad institutions)
- Attempting to establish a cheder (Jewish elementary school) for their children, in the spirit of Chabad
- Discussing the difficulties Temimim from Lubavitch faced adapting to the American environment
- Coordinating efforts to bring Temimim out of Soviet Russia to America

One of their earliest and most notable achievements was raising funds and organizing the printing of a new edition of _Likkutei Torah_ (the Alter Rebbe's Torah commentary) in Warsaw — a project initiated by Jacobson that was directly attributed to the efforts of Agudas HaTmimim. Five hundred copies were sent to America and sold for $5 for both volumes.

## Significance

R' Eliya Simpson represents the generation of Chabad figures who bridged the Old World yeshiva of Lubavitch and the New World challenges of American Jewish life. His home was the literal birthplace of organized Chabad activity in America — the first gathering point for what would eventually grow into the expansive Chabad movement in the United States. As the first president of Agudas HaTmimim, he embodied the determination to maintain Chabad identity and values in the face of American assimilation pressures.
