# Rabbi Yechezkel Feiglin (Faigin)

**Born**: 1875  
**Died**: 1937 (executed by Soviet authorities)  
**Role**: Senior Chabad activist during the Soviet period; student of Tomchei Temimim

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yechezkel Feiglin (also spelled Faigin) was a senior Chabad activist and organizer who played a critical role in maintaining Jewish life under the increasingly hostile conditions of the Soviet Union. A product of the Tomchei Temimim yeshiva founded by the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]], he became one of the key figures in the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] network of underground religious activities.

---

## Tomchei Temimim and Early Career

Rabbi Feiglin was among the students of the original Tomchei Temimim yeshiva in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], where he absorbed both the rigorous Talmudic curriculum and the deep Chassidic training that characterized the institution. This formation prepared him for a life of selfless dedication to Jewish communal needs.

---

## Soviet-Era Activities

After the Russian Revolution and the establishment of the Soviet Union, Rabbi Feiglin worked tirelessly to maintain Jewish religious life under conditions of severe persecution. The Yevsektsiya (Jewish Section of the Communist Party) and the Soviet secret police actively targeted religious leaders and institutions. Rabbi Feiglin was a crucial figure in the Rayatz's clandestine network, helping to organize:

- Underground Torah education (chadorim and yeshivos)
- Mikvaos (ritual baths)
- Shechita (kosher slaughter)
- Communication between scattered Jewish communities

---

## Arrest and Death

Like many Chabad activists of the Soviet period, Rabbi Feiglin was eventually arrested by Soviet authorities. He was executed in 1937 during the Great Purge (Yezhovshchina), when Stalin's regime carried out mass executions of perceived enemies. He is counted among the Chabad martyrs of the Soviet period.
