# Professor Avraham Yitzchak Katz

Professor Avraham Yitzchak Katz (1 Elul 5668 – 27 Tammuz 5758 / 1908–1998) was an American professor, bibliographer, and author. He founded the Department of Jewish Studies at New York University and maintained ongoing correspondence with the Rebbe regarding his scholarly works and the Chabad books held captive in the Soviet Union.

## Biography

Born in Slav, Poland, to Rabbi Reuven Katz (later Chief Rabbi of Petach Tikvah). He studied for a period at the yeshiva of Rabbi Shimon Shkop in Grodno.

At approximately age 17 he emigrated to the United States and studied at several American universities, receiving a PhD in philosophy and becoming a Professor of Hebrew Culture at NYU. He founded the Jewish Studies department at NYU and established a Jewish library of approximately 25,000 titles, many rare.

In 5716 (1956) he traveled to the Soviet Union to locate rare Jewish books in various libraries. During this trip he photographed thousands of manuscripts and rare documents, making them accessible to the West. He returned three more times to the USSR for this purpose.

He authored twelve books on various topics and approximately 300 articles, becoming a renowned expert in Jewish culture and literature.

## Connection to the Rebbe

Professor Katz maintained ongoing correspondence with the Rebbe, sending him his published works and meeting with him multiple times in yechidus.

In 5716, after visiting Russia, he sent the Rebbe a catalogue of Hebrew books found in Russian libraries. He also sent photographs of Chassidic manuscripts captive in Russia, for which the Rebbe repeatedly thanked him — referring to one manuscript as "the notebook of Prof. Katz."

On receiving his book "Einstein's Theory of Relativity," the Rebbe commented that he was pleased to see the author had not changed his name — "it says Katz — Kohen Tzedek."

The photographs and manuscripts Katz removed from Russia some twenty years later became the basis for the Rebbe's mission to locate and redeem the captive Chabad manuscripts and return them to the Chabad library.
