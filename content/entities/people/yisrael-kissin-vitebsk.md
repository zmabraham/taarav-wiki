# Rabbi Yisrael Kissin of Vitebsk

**Official Rav of Vitebsk; student of the Vilna Gaon; relative of the Alter Rebbe's family; briefly served in Lubavitch**

## Biography

Rabbi Yisrael Kissin was the official rabbi (Rav) of Vitebsk, one of the largest and most important Jewish communities in the Vitebsk gubernia of White Russia. He was a student of Rabbi Elijah of Vilna (the Vilna Gaon), placing him firmly within the Mitnagdic rabbinic tradition that was the principal ideological opponent of the Chassidic movement.

His relationship to the Alter Rebbe's household was through family ties: he was a close relative (cousin or nephew) of Rebbetzin Sterna, the Alter Rebbe's wife, whose family the Segals were prominent Vitebsk notables. This placed Rabbi Yisrael Kissin in an unusual position — he was related by family connection to the founder of Chabad Chassidus, yet served as an official representative of the traditional Mitnagdic rabbinic establishment.

Despite this tension, Rabbi Yisrael Kissin's career touched the Chabad world in a striking way: at some point he briefly held a rabbinic position in Lubavitch, the town that would become the headquarters of Chabad under the Alter Rebbe. The details of this brief tenure are not fully clarified in the sources, but it illustrates the way in which personal and family connections could temporarily bridge even deep ideological divides.

Rabbi Yisrael Kissin was also the brother of Lipman (Hananya) Kissin, who served as Parnas (lay leader) of the Vitebsk Jewish community. Lipman's son Simcha Kissin would later become one of the most damaging informers against Chabad in the nineteenth century, providing testimony that led to the arrest and exile of leading Chabad figures around 1825. The Kissin family's trajectory thus moves from proximity to the Alter Rebbe's household through the complex politics of Vitebsk communal life and ultimately to active opposition to Chabad.

## Significance

Rabbi Yisrael Kissin embodies the complex social reality of early Chabad history, in which personal family ties, communal politics, and ideological divisions created intricate relationships between individuals who were simultaneously connected and opposed. As a Gaon's student who was related to the Alter Rebbe's wife, he represents the interpenetration of the Mitnagdic and Chassidic worlds at the human level, even as their institutions clashed. His brief stint in Lubavitch and his brother's role in Vitebsk communal politics place the Kissin family at the center of the tensions that would eventually produce the tragic figure of Simcha Kissin the informer.
