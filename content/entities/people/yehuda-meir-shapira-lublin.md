# Rabbi Yehuda Meir Shapira of Lublin

Rabbi Yehuda Meir Shapira (7 Adar 5647 – 7 Cheshvan 5694 / 1887–1933) was a Torah genius and Chassid, one of the rabbis of Poland, head of the Chachmei Lublin Yeshiva, and the founder of the Daf Yomi daily Talmud study program.

## Biography

Born in Shatz (Suceava), Romania, to Rabbi Yaakov Shimshon Shapira and Margala. By age 9 he knew Yoreh De'ah with commentaries by heart. At his bar mitzvah he delivered a brilliant halachic discourse. His maternal grandfather, Rabbi Shmuel Yitzchak Shur, taught him the ways of halachic decision-making.

He also studied Kabbalah and Chassidus, immersing himself in the Toldot Yaakov Yosef and the Alter Rebbe's writings. At 16, after his grandfather's death, he was called "the Ilui (prodigy) of Shatz." Rabbi Shalom Mordechai HaKohen Shvadron (Maharsham of Brzezhany) was deeply impressed upon meeting him and blessed him: "Blessed is He Who created luminaries."

He was ordained in Galina and Sanok, then in 5683 (1923) was elected as a representative of Orthodox Jewry in the Polish Sejm (parliament). At the first Knessia Gedola of Agudath Israel in Vienna (also 5683), he proposed the idea of Daf Yomi — daily study of one Talmud page — which was accepted and continues to this day.

In 5684 (1924) he was elected rabbi of Piotrkow, where he laid the cornerstone of the Chachmei Lublin Yeshiva. The building was inaugurated on 28 Sivan 5690 (1930) with tens of thousands of guests from all over Europe, and he was simultaneously crowned rabbi of Lublin. Hundreds of students studied at the yeshiva, which provided fully for their material and spiritual needs.

He passed away on 7 Cheshvan 5694, having asked his students to drink "l'chaim" and sing his beloved melodies; he passed away during their singing.

## Connection to the Rebbe

Rabbi Shapira attended the Rebbe's wedding in Warsaw on 14 Kislev 5689 (1928). The Frierdiker Rebbe (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn) asked him to discuss Torah with the Rebbe (Menachem Mendel Schneerson). After an hour-long Torah discussion, Rabbi Shapira told the Frierdiker Rebbe: "The Lubavitcher Rebbe, you have taken a genius-of-the-world as a groom." The next day he brought Rabbi Shapira's responsa collection "Or HaMeir" as a wedding gift with a personal inscription.

The Rebbe cited Rabbi Shapira's "Or HaMeir" in various contexts over the years, including regarding intercalation of the year.
