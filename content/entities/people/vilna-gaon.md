# Vilna Gaon — Rabbi Elijah of Vilna

**Born**: 23 Nisan 5480 (April 23, 1720), Sielec, Lithuania  
**Died**: 19 Tishrei 5558 (October 9, 1797), Vilna, Lithuania  
**Role**: Greatest Lithuanian Torah authority of the eighteenth century; leader of the Mitnagdim (opponents of Chassidism); issued a ban (cherem) against the Chassidic movement

---

## Overview

Rabbi Elijah ben Solomon Zalman — universally known as the **Vilna Gaon** (the Genius of Vilna) or by the Hebrew acronym **GRA** (Gaon Rabbenu Eliyahu) — was the paramount Torah authority of Lithuanian Jewry in the eighteenth century. He is considered one of the greatest Talmudic scholars in Jewish history. His fierce opposition to the emerging Chassidic movement, including his issuance of a formal ban (_cherem_), made him the central figure in the most significant internal Jewish religious conflict of the modern era — the clash between the Mitnagdim (opponents) and the Chassidim.

---

## Opposition to Chassidism

The Vilna Gaon viewed Chassidism with deep alarm. His objections included:

**Religious grounds:**

- The Chassidic emphasis on emotional experience over Torah study seemed to undervalue scholarship
- The Chassidic practice of delaying prescribed prayer times (to prepare emotionally) violated halachic norms
- Various Chassidic customs appeared to deviate from established practice
- The elevated role of the tzaddik seemed to border on inappropriate intermediation between Jews and God

**Political grounds:**

- The Chassidic movement created parallel communal structures that threatened established Jewish community organization

The Gaon issued formal bans (_cherem_) against the Chassidic movement in 1772 and again in 1781. He reportedly refused to meet with the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] when the Alter Rebbe came to Vilna seeking dialogue — a refusal that deepened the divide.

---

## The Hoshana Raba 1798 Ban

The [[summaries/toldot-chabad-poland|Chabad history of Poland]] documents: on Hoshana Raba 5559 (1798), the Vilna Gaon's followers in Vilna — even after the Gaon's death — continued the campaign, arresting R' Meir Refael and other Chabad chassidim. The Gaon's influence extended beyond his death.

---

## Legacy

Despite the ferocity of his opposition to Chassidism, the Vilna Gaon is one of the most revered figures in all of Jewish history. His scholarly output was extraordinary, and his school — the Lithuanian or "Litvak" tradition of pure Torah scholarship — became one of the two dominant streams of Ashkenazic Jewry (alongside Chassidism).

Over time, the Mitnagdim-Chassidim conflict faded. Today, Chabad chassidim venerate the Gaon's Torah scholarship even while disagreeing with his anti-Chassidic stance.
