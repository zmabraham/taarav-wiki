# Rabbi Shmuel Gronem Estherman

**Born**: c. 1860  
**Died**: c. 1920s  
**Role**: First _mashpia_ (spiritual mentor) of [[events/tomchei-temimim-founding-1897|Tomchei Temimim]] yeshiva

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shmuel Gronem Estherman served as the first and foundational _mashpia_ (spiritual mentor/guide) of Yeshiva Tomchei Temimim — the flagship Chabad yeshiva founded by the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] in Lubavitch in 1897. In this role, he shaped the spiritual character of the first generation of _temimim_ (sincere students), establishing the unique pedagogical model that would define Chabad yeshiva education.

---

## The Role of Mashpia

Tomchei Temimim's radical innovation was placing the _mashpia_ — not the traditional _rosh yeshiva_ (head of Talmudic studies) — at the center of the yeshiva's educational mission. While a rosh yeshiva focuses on intellectual Talmudic instruction, the mashpia focuses on:

- Personal spiritual guidance (_avodah_)
- Chassidic philosophy (_Chassidus_)
- Character development and internalization of values
- Individual mentorship and relationship

Rabbi Shmuel Gronem's appointment as the first to hold this role established its centrality in the Chabad educational model.

---

## Educational Approach

Under Rabbi Shmuel Gronem's guidance, Tomchei Temimim students studied Chassidic texts for approximately 4 hours daily alongside 8 hours of Talmud/halacha. The mashpia's classes were not lectures but intimate explorations of Chassidic ideas aimed at transforming the student's inner life — producing a _penimi_ (internalized person) rather than merely a _gadol_ (great scholar).

---

## Legacy

The Tomchei Temimim model, with the mashpia at its center, went on to produce generations of Chabad emissaries who could withstand the pressures of Soviet persecution, Holocaust, and the challenges of outreach across the globe. Rabbi Shmuel Gronem's foundational work in establishing this model makes him one of the most significant (if lesser-known) figures in Chabad institutional history.
