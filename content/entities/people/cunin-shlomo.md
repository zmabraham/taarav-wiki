# Rabbi Shlomo Cunin

**Born**: 1936, Brooklyn, New York  
**Role**: Chabad shliach to the West Coast USA; founder of Chabad of California

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shlomo Cunin is the founding director of Chabad of California and one of the most dynamic shluchim of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]]. Sent from Brooklyn to California, he built Chabad's West Coast presence from nothing into a massive network of over fifty centers spanning the state, pioneering Chabad's outreach to college campuses and the entertainment industry.

---

## Mission to California

Born and raised in Brooklyn, Cunin was sent by the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]] to California to establish a Chabad presence on the West Coast. California in the mid-twentieth century represented a frontier for Jewish life — a large, rapidly growing Jewish population that was largely disconnected from traditional institutions and observance.

The Rebbe recognized California's strategic importance: it was the cultural engine of America, home to Hollywood, the emerging tech sector, and a massive university system. Establishing Chabad there was essential to the global shlichus vision.

---

## Building the Network

Under Cunin's leadership, Chabad of California grew into one of the largest regional Chabad operations in the world:

- **Over 50 Chabad centers** across California, from San Diego to San Francisco
- **College campus outreach** — Chabad Houses at UCLA, USC, UC Berkeley, Stanford, and numerous other California universities, pioneering a model later replicated nationwide
- **Hollywood outreach** — engagement with the entertainment industry and its Jewish population
- **Schools, synagogues, and community programs** serving Jews across the economic and religious spectrum

---

## Legacy

Cunin's California operation became a model for large-scale regional Chabad development. The sheer scale of what he built — dozens of centers across a single state — demonstrated that the shlichus model could work not only in individual communities but as a coordinated regional network.
