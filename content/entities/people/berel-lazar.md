# Rabbi Berel Lazar

**Born**: 1964, Milan, Italy  
**Role**: Chief Rabbi of Russia (Federation of Jewish Communities); prominent Chabad shliach

---

## Overview

Rabbi Berel Lazar is the Chief Rabbi of Russia and one of the most prominent Chabad figures in the post-Soviet world. Born in Milan to a Chabad family (his father is [[entities/people/garelik-gershon|Rabbi Gershon Mendel Garelik]], the founding shliach of Chabad in Italy), Lazar moved to Russia after the collapse of the Soviet Union and was appointed Chief Rabbi of Russia in 2000. He has built an extensive Chabad network across the Russian Federation and maintains a close working relationship with the Russian government that has enabled unprecedented Jewish communal activity.

---

## Early Life

Born in Milan, Italy in 1964, Lazar grew up in a Chabad shlichus household — his father had been sent to Milan by the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]] in 1958. This upbringing gave him firsthand experience of the shlichus enterprise and its demands. He studied in Chabad yeshivos and received his rabbinic ordination.

---

## Move to Russia

After the fall of the Soviet Union in 1991, a wave of Chabad shluchim entered the former Soviet states to rebuild Jewish life after seventy years of communist suppression. Lazar was among the earliest and most ambitious of these emissaries, arriving in Moscow and quickly establishing himself as a major communal leader.

---

## Chief Rabbi of Russia (2000)

In 2000, Lazar was appointed Chief Rabbi of Russia by the Federation of Jewish Communities of Russia (FJCR), a Chabad-affiliated umbrella organization. This appointment gave him official recognition as a representative of Russian Jewry and a platform for building Jewish infrastructure on a national scale.

Key achievements include:

- **Synagogues and community centers** across dozens of Russian cities
- **Jewish schools and educational programs** serving the post-Soviet Jewish population
- **The Moscow Jewish Community Center** (Marina Roscha) — one of the largest Jewish communal facilities in Europe
- **Kashrus and lifecycle services** for Jewish communities throughout Russia
- **Holocaust memorialization** projects and interfaith dialogue

---

## Government Relations

Lazar has maintained a close and publicly visible relationship with the Russian government, including with President Vladimir Putin. This relationship has been both a source of his effectiveness — enabling the construction of Jewish infrastructure, the return of communal property, and official recognition of Jewish communal needs — and a subject of controversy, particularly as Russia's international relations have become more fraught.

---

## Significance

Lazar's career represents the extraordinary post-Soviet chapter of the [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] story. That a Chabad rabbi could become the Chief Rabbi of Russia — a country that had persecuted Chabad for seventy years — is one of the most remarkable developments in modern Jewish history. It also demonstrates the multigenerational nature of shlichus: from his father's pioneering work in Milan to his own leadership in Moscow.
