# Avigdor ben Chaim (Accuser of Alter Rebbe)

## Overview
Avigdor ben Chaim was the primary accuser who denounced the Alter Rebbe to the Russian government, directly causing the Alter Rebbe's first arrest in 1798. He was a Mitnaged rabbi who had been removed from his position as rabbi of Pinsk.

## Details
Avigdor ben Chaim had served as the rabbi of Pinsk until Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berdichev was appointed in his stead. Bearing a deep grudge, he composed a detailed written denunciation (donos) against the Alter Rebbe and the Chassidic movement, submitting it to the Russian government in 5558 (1798).

His accusations included claims that the Chassidim were engaged in activities harmful to the state and that their religious practices were subversive. He filed his accusations in St. Petersburg with the government's Secret Chancery (the political police of that era).

His denunciation was the proximate cause of the Alter Rebbe's arrest in the summer of 5558 (1798) and his transport to St. Petersburg for interrogation. However, after the interrogations were completed and translated into Russian, Emperor Paul I personally reviewed the case and found that the Chassidim had done nothing harmful to the state, ordering their release on 19 Kislev 5559 (1798).

The Russian documents from this case name both "Avigdor ben-Chaim" and "Zalman ben-Baruch" (the Alter Rebbe) as the principal parties in the dispute.
