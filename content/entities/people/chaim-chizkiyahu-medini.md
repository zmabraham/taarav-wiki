# Rabbi Chaim Chizkiyahu Medini (Sdei Chemed)

Rabbi Chaim Chizkiyahu Medini (7 Cheshvan 5593 – 24 Kislev 5665 / 1832–1904), sometimes referred to as "HaChChaM" or "the Sdei Chemed" after his encyclopedic work, was the rabbi of Krasno-Bazar (Bilohirsk) in the Crimea Peninsula and later of Hebron. He is famous for the monumental Talmudic-halachic encyclopedia "Sdei Chemed."

## Biography

Born in Jerusalem to Rabbi Eliyahu Rafael Medini, from an ancient Jerusalem family. He received rabbinic ordination at age 13. Due to economic hardship he emigrated to Istanbul where he was appointed as a dayan. In 5626 (1866) he was called to serve as the chief rabbi of Krasno-Bazar in the Crimea, where he ministered to the Crimchak community (an ancient Jewish group speaking Tatar). He became greatly beloved by all in the region, including non-Jews.

During his time in the Crimea he contracted a serious illness in 5638 (1878), and as a healing segulah, the name "Chaim" was added to his given name of Chizkiyahu.

In 5619 Tammuz (1899), after 33 years in the Crimea, he returned to Eretz Yisrael. After two years in Jerusalem, he was appointed Chief Rabbi of Hebron, a position he held until his death. He established a yeshiva in Beit Romano and appointed Rabbi Menachem Mendel Naeh as its rosh yeshiva.

He was extremely respected even among Arabs, who considered him a "man of God." When he was brought to burial in the ancient Hebron cemetery, Arabs accompanied the procession with reverence; some attempted to secretly exhume his body to rebury it near a mosque, believing his grave would bless the area. The Jewish community placed guards at the site for a long time.

## Connection to Chabad

The Rebbe Rashab had a warm friendship with him. They corresponded on halachic matters and the Rebbe Rashab worked to help him when missionaries threatened to purchase a building in Hebron for a missionary school.

The young Avraham Chaim Naeh was so beloved by the Sdei Chemed that he was given access to his entire library, leading to Naeh's lifelong scholarly career. The Frierdiker Rebbe and the Rebbe both greatly esteemed the Sdei Chemed and encouraged Chassidim to study his works.
