# Yoel Smolianir

**18th-century Chabad emissary (shaliach) from the Tiberias community to Russia; early link between Eretz Yisrael and Chabad Diaspora**

## Biography

Yoel Smolianir was a Chabad emissary (shaliach, literally "messenger") from the community of Tiberias in Eretz Yisrael who traveled to Russia in the late 18th century to collect funds (chalukah) for the Jewish community in the Land of Israel. His travels as a shaliach placed him in contact with the early Chabad movement.

The Tiberias community of this period was part of the pioneering Chassidic settlement in Eretz Yisrael that had begun with the 1777 aliyah led by R' Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and R' Avraham of Kalisk. These Galilee communities depended heavily on financial support collected by emissaries sent to Diaspora communities.

As a shaliach from Tiberias traveling in Russia, Yoel Smolianir would have interacted with Chabad Chassidim and potentially with the Alter Rebbe's court. His role bridges the Eretz Yisrael Chassidic settlement and the emerging Chabad movement, documenting early connections between Tiberias and White Russia.

## Significance

Yoel Smolianir represents an important but understudied category of Chabad figures: the emissaries who maintained the financial and social connection between the Eretz Yisrael Chassidic communities and their brethren in Eastern Europe. These shluchim were the original "Chabad emissaries" in a geographic sense — traveling far from their base community to accomplish a mission. His 18th-century activity predates the more famous 20th-century shlichus network but represents an earlier form of the same concept.
