# Rabbi Dr. Bernard Revel — ר' ברנרד ריוול

**Born**: 1885, Lithuania  
**Died**: 1940  
**Role**: First president of Yeshiva University; architect of Torah u-Madda approach  
**Significance for Chabad**: Represented one of the established American Orthodox educational approaches at the time of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] arrival in America

---

## Overview

Rabbi Dr. Bernard Revel (born Dov Revel) was the founding president of **Yeshiva University** in New York, presiding over its development from Rabbi Isaac Elchanan Theological Seminary into a full university. He championed the _Torah u-Madda_ approach — the synthesis of traditional Torah learning with secular academic study — which became the defining philosophy of Modern Orthodoxy in America.

---

## American Orthodox Landscape

The _Undaunted_ biography of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] situates Revel and Yeshiva University as one of the two major Orthodox institutional forces (alongside [[entities/people/shraga-feivel-mendlowitz|Rabbi Mendlowitz]] and Torah Vodaath) when the Rayatz arrived in America in 1940. The Rayatz's aggressive plan to transform American Jewry into a Torah center represented a third model — more intensively Chassidic and less accommodating of secular university study.

The three models (YU's Torah u-Madda, Torah Vodaath's mesivta system, and Chabad's Tomchei Temimim approach) coexisted and competed for the American Orthodox community's direction in the 1940s and 1950s.

---

## Death in 1940

Revel died in December 1940 — the same year the Rayatz arrived in America. Thus the two major architects of competing American Orthodox educational visions never had the opportunity for the extended dialogue that Chabad's arrival might have prompted.
