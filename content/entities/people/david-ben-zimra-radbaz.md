# Rabbi David ben Zimra (Radbaz)

Rabbi David ben Zimra, known as the Radbaz (acronym of Rabbi David Ben Zimra), was one of the heads of the Egyptian Jewish community and author of the responsa work "Shut HaRadbaz." Born in Spain around 5240 (1479–1480) and passed away 21 Cheshvan 5334 (1573).

## Biography

Born in Spain to Rabbi Shlomo. During the Spanish Expulsion of 5252 (1492), his family moved to Eretz Yisrael and settled in Tzfat, then Jerusalem. Around 5272–3, due to economic hardship, he moved to Alexandria, Egypt, and then to Cairo.

In Cairo he joined the yeshiva of the Nagid Rabbi Yitzchak HaKohen Shulal and was appointed Av Beit Din. He became one of the leading scholars of Egyptian Jewry, refusing to take a salary from the rabbinate and earning his living through commerce. Among his important enactments was instituting the counting of years by the creation calendar (not the Seleucid era), and restoring silent prayer.

In 5313 (1553) he moved to Jerusalem, then to Tzfat. He wrote thousands of responsa, a commentary on Maimonides' Mishneh Torah ("Yikar Tiferet"), and kabbalistic works including "Magen David."

## Connection to Chabad

The Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch relies heavily on the Radbaz. In the letters found in the Kherson Geniza, Rabbi Avraham the Malach (son of the Maggid of Mezeritch) asked the Alter Rebbe to obtain volumes of Shut HaRadbaz. The Rebbe cited the Radbaz as one of "the pillars of halachic decisors in Israel," saying: "One cannot outmaneuver the Radbaz" (i.e., his opinions are authoritative and watertight), noting his authority in both Kabbalah and Rambam commentary.
