# Rabbi Yoel Kahn

**Born**: 1930, Leningrad (St. Petersburg), Soviet Union  
**Died**: August 15, 2021, Brooklyn, New York  
**Role**: Master _chozer_ (oral memorizer and transcriber) of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe's]] farbrengens; primary channel for the accurate transmission of the Rebbe's teachings

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yoel Kahn was the most important single figure in the preservation and transmission of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe's]] Torah teachings. As the Rebbe's chief _chozer_ — literally "repeater" — he possessed the extraordinary ability to memorize the Rebbe's hours-long Shabbos and Yom Tov farbrengens (delivered in Yiddish, without notes, often lasting four to six hours) and then transcribe them with remarkable fidelity.

His transcriptions form the textual foundation of _Likkutei Sichos_ (39 volumes), the Rebbe's collected discourses, and numerous other published works. Without Rabbi Kahn's prodigious memory and scholarly precision, the corpus of the Rebbe's oral teachings — delivered on Shabbos and holidays when recording was forbidden — would have been largely lost.

---

## Early Life

Kahn was born in Leningrad (St. Petersburg) in 1930. His family emigrated from the Soviet Union, and he grew up in the Chabad community that gathered around 770 Eastern Parkway in Brooklyn. From an early age, he demonstrated exceptional intellectual gifts — a photographic memory combined with deep Talmudic and Chassidic learning.

---

## The Role of _Chozer_

The institution of the _chozer_ has deep roots in Chabad history. Since the time of the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]], Chabad Rebbes delivered their most important teachings orally — as _maamarim_ (Chassidic discourses) and _sichos_ (talks) — often on Shabbos and Yom Tov when writing is prohibited. Designated listeners with exceptional memories would absorb the teachings and write them down after Shabbos.

Under the seventh Rebbe, this tradition reached its apex. The Rebbe's farbrengens were legendary in their scope: a single Shabbos farbrengen might include multiple maamarim and sichos covering Talmud, Halacha, Chassidus, Biblical commentary, and contemporary issues — all delivered extemporaneously in rapid Yiddish for hours on end.

Rabbi Kahn would sit at the Rebbe's farbrengen, listen with total concentration, and after Shabbos (at the _melaveh malka_), he would stand before a group of scholars and repeat the entire farbrengen from memory — a performance that itself could last hours. Other scholars would take notes, and the transcription process would begin.

---

## The Transcription Process

The system Rabbi Kahn developed was methodical:

1. **Farbrengen**: The Rebbe speaks for 4–6 hours on Shabbos
2. **Chazara (repetition)**: After Shabbos, Rabbi Kahn repeats the entire farbrengen from memory to a team of scholars
3. **Hanachah (written draft)**: Scholars write up the transcription
4. **Editing**: Rabbi Kahn reviews and corrects the written version for accuracy
5. **Publication**: The finalized text is published in pamphlet form (_kuntres_) and eventually collected into the volumes of _Likkutei Sichos_

This process continued for decades — week after week, holiday after holiday — producing the largest published corpus of any Chabad Rebbe.

---

## Likkutei Sichos

The 39 volumes of _Likkutei Sichos_ represent the Rebbe's most systematic Torah commentary, organized by parsha (weekly Torah portion) and holiday. These volumes are the primary text studied in Chabad yeshivos and communities worldwide.

Rabbi Kahn's role in their creation was irreplaceable. The Rebbe did review and edit the final versions, but the raw material — the initial transcription from oral delivery to written text — depended on Kahn's memory.

---

## As Scholar and Teacher

Beyond his role as chozer, Rabbi Kahn was a profound Chassidic scholar in his own right. He taught classes, trained younger scholars, and was regarded as one of the foremost authorities on the Rebbe's teachings. His understanding of the Rebbe's intellectual system — developed through decades of absorbing every word — gave him a unique vantage point.

He was often consulted on questions of interpretation: "What did the Rebbe mean by...?" — questions that only someone who had heard the original delivery, with its tone, emphasis, and context, could fully answer.

---

## Passing and Legacy

Rabbi Yoel Kahn passed away on August 15, 2021 (7 Elul 5781), at the age of 91. His death was mourned as the loss of an irreplaceable link in the chain of Chabad's oral tradition. No one else possessed his comprehensive, first-person knowledge of the Rebbe's spoken Torah.

The corpus he helped create — tens of thousands of pages of transcribed teachings — remains the central textual legacy of the seventh Rebbe's leadership. Every student who opens a volume of _Likkutei Sichos_ is reading text that passed through Yoel Kahn's extraordinary memory.
