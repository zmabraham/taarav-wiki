# Rabbi Chaim Ozer Grodzinski

Rabbi Chaim Ozer Grodzinski (9 Elul 5623 – 5 Av 5700 / 1863–1940) was one of the greatest Torah authorities in Lithuania in the pre-Holocaust era, a prominent posek throughout the Jewish world, and president of the Moetzet Gedolei HaTorah of Agudath Israel. He served as the rabbi of Vilna.

## Biography

Son of Rabbi Dovid Shlomo, he was educated at the Volozhin Yeshiva where he was known as "the Ilui (prodigy) from Ivye." His friend at Volozhin was Rabbi Chaim of Brisk, with whom he maintained a warm friendship all his life. At age 21 he married the granddaughter of Rabbi Yisrael of Salant; his father-in-law was the rabbi of Vilna, and he later became his successor.

He was a member of the Moetzet Gedolei HaTorah of Agudath Israel from its founding until his death, and was active in the Va'ad HaYeshivot organization. Although yeshivot were central to his world, he himself never delivered formal yeshiva lectures. He served as chief rabbi of Vilna until his death.

In the first year of World War II, with Vilna occupied by the Soviets, many yeshiva refugees gathered in the city. Rabbi Chaim Ozer continued his public activities ceaselessly despite his terminal illness during this final year of his life.

## Relationship with Chabad

Despite being a mitnaged (non-Chassidic) city, Rabbi Chaim Ozer was a close friend and ally of both the Rebbe Rashab and later the Frierdiker Rebbe, working together on behalf of Russian Jewry. He co-signed joint proclamations with the Rebbe Rashab and participated in rabbinic assemblies organized by the Rebbe Rashab.

He attended the wedding of the Frierdiker Rebbe's daughter Sheina Horenstein, an exceptional act of friendship since he normally avoided attending weddings.

Near his death, the Frierdiker Rebbe's emissary Rabbi Shmuel Zalmanow visited him and showed him the journal "HaTamim." Rabbi Chaim Ozer read through the Chassidus sections and told Zalmanow: "Reading HaTamim, I am becoming a little bit of a Chassid myself." He asked to keep the volume and sent warm regards to the Frierdiker Rebbe, praising the impression HaTamim made on him.
