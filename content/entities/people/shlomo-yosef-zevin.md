# Rabbi Shlomo Yosef Zevin

**Born**: 1888, Kazimirov, Belarus  
**Died**: 1978, Jerusalem, Israel  
**Role**: Leading halachic authority; Talmudic encyclopedist; Chabad-affiliated scholar

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shlomo Yosef Zevin was one of the most important Torah scholars of the twentieth century, known primarily as the founder and editor of the **Encyclopedia Talmudit** (Talmudic Encyclopedia), a monumental multi-volume Hebrew work that systematically catalogues and analyzes the legal discussions of the Talmud. Though he studied in Lithuanian yeshivos, he was closely aligned with Chabad and maintained deep ties to the Chabad Rebbes.

---

## Early Life and Education

Born in 1888 in Kazimirov, Belarus, Zevin received a traditional yeshiva education in the Lithuanian style. Despite this Lithuanian training, he was drawn to Chabad Chassidus and developed a lifelong connection to the Chabad movement. This dual formation — Lithuanian Talmudic rigor combined with Chassidic depth — characterized his scholarly output.

---

## The Encyclopedia Talmudit

Zevin's magnum opus was the **Encyclopedia Talmudit**, which he founded and edited. This multi-volume Hebrew encyclopedia systematically presents the halachic topics discussed in the Talmud, organizing the vast sea of Talmudic law into accessible, alphabetically arranged entries. Each entry traces a topic from its biblical source through the Talmudic discussion and into the later codes and responsa.

The Encyclopedia Talmudit remains in publication to this day — decades after Zevin's death — and is considered one of the greatest achievements of modern Torah scholarship.

---

## Other Works

- **L'Ohr HaHalacha** — essays on halachic topics
- **Ishim V'Shitot** — biographical and analytical sketches of major Talmudic authorities and their methodologies
- **Ha-Mo'adim Ba-Halacha** — a classic work on the festivals as viewed through halachic literature
- **Sippurei Chassidim** — collections of Chassidic stories

---

## Connection to Chabad

Zevin lived in Tel Aviv and later Jerusalem, but maintained a close connection to the Chabad leadership, corresponding with both the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] and the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]]. His Chassidic works and his personal connection to Chabad made him an important bridge between the Lithuanian and Chassidic scholarly worlds.
