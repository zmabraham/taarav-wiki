# R' Reuven Liberman of Pleshchenitsy

**Shoichet (ritual slaughterer) and Chabad community leader in Pleshchenitsy; maintained the Chassidic community during the absence of R' Avraham Chaim Razenboim**

## Biography

R' Reuven Liberman served as shoichet in Pleshchenitsy (Pleszczenice), a town in the Minsk gubernia of White Russia. He was a dedicated Chabad Chassid who played a central role in the communal life of the local Chassidic circle.

His most notable period of leadership came during the years 1888–1892, when R' Avraham Chaim Razenboim — the leading figure of the Pleshchenitsy Chassidic community — was imprisoned and then living in exile. During this time, R' Reuven established a regular study session (kevias itim) for learning Chassidic texts (Divrei Elokim Chaim — d'a"ch) at his home, keeping the community spiritually active.

Toldos Avraham Chaim records that R' Avraham Chaim wrote from his imprisonment in Smolensk specifically mentioning R' Reuven's efforts with warm approval: "This gives me great pleasure, that there are found a small number of those who desire to conduct themselves in Chassidus and with fixed times and place at R' Reuven the shoichet's for studying d"a"ch — may God help them." After R' Avraham Chaim emigrated from Russia (c. 1893), R' Reuven continued as the de facto head of the Pleshchenitsy Chassidic circle, writing to R' Avraham Chaim in Amsterdam and America.

## Significance

R' Reuven Liberman exemplifies the shoichet-mashpia type that sustained Chabad community life in small White Russian towns when more prominent figures were absent. His home served as the informal beit midrash for the remaining Chassidim, demonstrating the resilience of Chabad communal infrastructure even under adverse conditions. The correspondence preserved in the Toldos Avraham Chaim archives provides rare documentation of daily Chassidic life in a small provincial community.
