# Rabbi Mordechai Dubin

**Born**: 1889, Latvia  
**Died**: 1956 (in Siberian exile or shortly after release)  
**Role**: Latvian MP; Jewish community leader; devoted Chabad Chasid; key diplomatic intermediary for the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]

---

## Overview

Rabbi Mordechai Dubin was the most politically powerful Jew in interwar Latvia and one of the most significant figures in the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] exile years. As a member of the Latvian parliament (_Saeima_) and chairman of the Riga Jewish community, he wielded influence that extended across borders — enabling him to act as the Rayatz's primary diplomatic channel with Soviet and Latvian authorities.

---

## Political Career

Dubin served as a member of the Latvian Saeima (parliament) as a representative of the Jewish community. He was a swing vote whose support was actively courted by competing political factions, giving him leverage disproportionate to the Jewish community's numbers. As chairman of the Riga Jewish community, he controlled significant institutional resources and commanded personal respect from Latvian leaders.

He was also a devoted Chabad Chasid — maintaining the dual identity of modern politician and traditional religious Jew with unusual success.

---

## Service to the Rayatz

When the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] arrived in Riga in October 1927 after his expulsion from the Soviet Union, Dubin immediately became his primary ally and diplomatic representative.

**Soviet Negotiations:** Dubin served as the Rayatz's channel for negotiations with Soviet authorities — using his parliamentary standing and diplomatic connections to press for better treatment of Jewish religious communities inside the USSR.

**The 1929 Matzah Campaign:** Dubin played a central role in the extraordinary 1929 Passover Matzah Campaign — an international effort to ship matzah into starving Soviet Russia despite the famine and official Soviet hostility to religion. Dubin negotiated with the Soviet ambassador in Riga, coordinating a diplomatic solution that allowed matzah to enter. This represented the intersection of religious necessity, Cold War-era diplomacy, and Chabad institutional creativity at its most sophisticated.

See [[events/matzah-campaign-1929|Matzah Campaign (1929)]].

---

## Arrest and Fate

When Soviet forces occupied Latvia in 1940, Dubin's political career ended. He was arrested by Soviet authorities — the same system he had spent years negotiating with on behalf of Jews. He was deported to Siberia.

Dubin died in 1956, either in Siberian exile or shortly after his release — a tragic end for a man who had devoted his career to protecting Jewish interests. His fate illustrated the personal risk taken by those who engaged Soviet authorities, even in ostensibly protective roles.

---

## Significance

Dubin exemplifies a distinctive type in modern Jewish history: the traditional Jew who masters the secular political world without abandoning religious identity. His ability to move between Chabad court and Latvian parliament, between Hebrew prayer and parliamentary speech, made him uniquely valuable in the interwar period.
