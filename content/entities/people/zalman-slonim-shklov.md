# Zalman Reizes Slonim of Shklov

**Lived**: early 19th century, Shklov, Russia  
**Role**: Senior Chabad chassid of Shklov; witness in the Mitteler Rebbe's 1825–1826 investigation; related by marriage to the Rebbe's family

---

## Overview

R' Zalman Reizes Slonim (also spelled Reuzes; also known by the family name Slonim) was a prominent Chabad chassid of Shklov. His family name "Reizes" came from his mother-in-law, the Rebbetzin Roiza [Berlin], making him a brother-in-law of [[entities/people/pinchas-shik|R' Pinchas Reizes Shik]]. He was also a mechutan (co-parent-in-law) of the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]], as his son Yaakov Kuli Slonim married the Rebbe's daughter Menucha Rochel (mentioned in the distribution document as "the betrothed Menucha Rochel").

---

## Role in the 1825 Investigation

R' Zalman was brought from Shklov to Vitebsk by investigators [[entities/people/loginov|Polkovnik Loginov]] and [[entities/people/markov-investigator|Court Counselor Markov]] to testify as a witness regarding the Mitteler Rebbe's financial support system (maavad). He was explicitly noted as "not an accused in this case, and testified."

His recorded testimony (5 October 1825) states:

- The Rebbe arrived in Shklov in spring 1813 with his family and gathered the most distinguished community members — the late Pinchas Shik, Shlomo Fridman, himself, and others — and informed them of his dire situation following the French looting and burning of Lyady.
- He asked them to help collect donations from the local chassidim; they spread through the community and all donated generously.
- Similar collections were made in other towns along the Rebbe's journey from Kremenchug to Shklov.
- No fixed salary was established, but each year a sum was collected; each chassid donated according to his means.
- He believed the Rebbe organized such collections in the Mogilev and Vilna governorates.
- Regarding the distribution document shown to the governor, he confirmed it was written by the Rebbe, that the sum was genuinely distributed to the family members mentioned, and that he (Zalman) was only a witness at the time of distribution, not knowing the document was being drafted.
- He also noted that besides the Rebbe, R' Aharon of Staroselye also received support from chassidim, and each person supported whichever of the two rebbes he preferred.

He signed: "Zalman Leizerovitch Slonim."

---

## Historical Significance

R' Zalman's testimony is one of the most detailed first-person accounts of how the chassidic financial support system (maavad) worked in practice. His description of the 1813 fundraising tour — the Rebbe personally gathering prominent men and explaining his situation, then they dispersing to collect donations community-wide — provides unique insight into early Chabad organizational practice.
