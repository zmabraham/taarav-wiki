# Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levin

Author of the memoir *Zichronos* (זכרונותי — My Memoirs), a first-person account of Chabad life under Soviet rule in Ukraine and Russia from 1919 to 1963.

## Biography

Yisrael Yehuda Levin was born in Zhivitiov, a small shtetl near Titov and Dashov in Vinytsia Province (Podolia), Ukraine. His father R' Efraim Shmuel was a melamed who ran a traditional *cheder* (Torah school) and was deeply devoted to Jewish observance.

As a young child (around age 8), Levin witnessed the Haidamak and other gang attacks during the civil war following WWI (winter 5680/1919–1920), including the near-complete massacre of the neighboring town of Titov. His family fled from Zhivitiov to Lipovitz just before Passover 5680.

In Lipovitz, Levin came of age during the rise of the Yevsektsiya (Jewish Section of the Communist Party) and witnessed the systematic dismantling of Jewish religious life. His family, refusing to send children to Soviet schools, were designated "lishentsyes" (deprived of civil rights).

In summer 5687 (1927), Levin heard about the Tomchei Temimim yeshiva network in Kiev and began studying there. In spring 5688 (1928), he traveled to Vitebsk to join the underground yeshiva. Over the next ten years, he studied in clandestine Tomchei Temimim yeshivas across Soviet Russia, including Vitebsk, Pereyaslav, Kiev, Baku, Kutaisi, Kolash, Moscow, Chernigov, Malachovka, Leningrad, Voronezh, Krivoy Rog, and Kharkov.

He was arrested multiple times by the NKVD/GPU. A miraculous conscription exemption in 5696 (1935) came through wearing glasses on the advice of the Rebbe (Rayatz), enabling him to falsely appear to have poor eyesight.

In 5698 (1938) he married Ita Devora Gorkow in Kharkov. During WWII, the couple fled to Samarkand, where their young son Efraim Shmuel died on 9 Kislev 5702 (1941). In Samarkand, Levin worked in the underground, obtaining forged Polish identity papers as "Naphtali Bat" to avoid Soviet conscription, and helped establish a clandestine Tomchei Temimim yeshiva.

In summer 5706 (1946), Levin was among the first Chabad families to leave Soviet Russia, using his Polish papers. After three years of wandering, the couple settled in Kfar Chabad in summer 5709 (1949).

The memoir concludes with his first visit to the 7th Lubavitcher Rebbe (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson) in New York in Tishrei 5723 (1962).

## The Memoir

The memoir was begun around 5720 (1960) and written over 35 years during free hours, as Levin recalled memories from his Soviet past. It was signed and dedicated on 18 Elul 5755 (1995) in Kfar Chabad.

The work was published in the *Meisaviv LeChassidus* series by the Chabad Library of Lubavitch (chabadlibrary.org). His son R' Shalom DovBer Levin and grandson R' Yosef Yitzchak Keller edited and prepared it for publication.

Portions of the memoir had previously appeared in *Toldos Chabad beRussia haSovietit* (Kehos, 5749/1989).

## Correspondence with the Rebbe (Rayatz)

A distinctive feature of the memoir is the verbatim quotation of letters exchanged with the 6th Lubavitcher Rebbe (Rayatz), including:
- Letters about writing down memories of Soviet-era activities (5709)
- Guidance on applying for exit visas to Eretz Yisrael or Riga
- The miraculous advice to wear glasses to avoid conscription (5696)
- Blessings for the Levin family preserved in the Rebbe's archive
