# Rabbi Shmuel Levitin

**Born**: 1896, Russia
**Died**: 1976, France
**Role**: Senior Chabad mashpia; spiritual leader of French Chabad community; student of the Rashab

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shmuel Levitin was a senior Chabad _mashpia_ (spiritual mentor) who survived the Soviet underground and eventually settled in France, where he became a major spiritual figure for the French Chabad community. A student of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]], Reb Shmuel represented the direct transmission of pre-Soviet Chabad spirituality to postwar Europe.

His long life spanned the final years of Czarist Russia, the entire Soviet period, the Holocaust, and the rebuilding of European Jewry after the war. Through all these upheavals, he maintained the warmth, wisdom, and spiritual depth that characterized the Chabad of earlier generations.

---

## Early Life and Education

Reb Shmuel was born in Russia in 1896 into a Chabad family. He came of age in the final years of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]]'s leadership and received his spiritual education directly from the fifth Rebbe.

**Student of the Rashab**: As a young man, Reb Shmuel studied in Tomchei Temimim and had direct access to the Rashab's teachings. This connection to the pre-Soviet Chabad world would shape his entire approach to Torah and Chassidus.

**The Rayatz's Leadership**: When the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] assumed leadership in 1920, Reb Shmuel became one of his devoted followers. He absorbed the Rayatz's emphasis on mesirus nefesh (self-sacrifice) and the need to maintain Jewish life under Soviet persecution.

---

## Soviet Underground Activities

During the Soviet period, Reb Shmuel was active in the underground network that maintained Jewish religious life throughout the USSR.

**Teaching and Mentorship**: As a _mashpia_, Reb Shmuel taught and mentored younger chassidim, transmitting the Chabad tradition despite Soviet restrictions. His teachings emphasized the inner dimension of Torah service and the importance of serving G-d with joy and depth.

**Secret Religious Activities**: Like other Soviet-era chassidim, Reb Shmuel participated in clandestine religious observances — secret minyanim, Torah classes, and holiday celebrations. Each of these activities required ingenuity and courage given the constant threat of arrest.

---

## Escape from the Soviet Union

Reb Shmuel was among the Soviet chassidim who managed to escape the USSR after World War II, often as part of the "Great Escape" operation orchestrated by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] from Brooklyn.

**The Escape Route**: The exact details of Reb Shmuel's escape are not fully documented, but he likely used the Polish passport route that many Soviet Jews used in 1946-1947. This dangerous journey involved forged documents, border crossings, and constant risk of arrest.

**Reaching the West**: After escaping, Reb Shmuel made his way to Western Europe, eventually settling in France.

---

## Building French Chabad

In France, Reb Shmuel became a central figure in the postwar Chabad community, helping to rebuild Jewish life after the devastation of the Holocaust.

**The Marseille Community**: Reb Shmuel settled in Marseille, which had a significant Jewish population including many Holocaust survivors and North African immigrants. He became a spiritual mentor to the community, teaching Torah and providing guidance.

**Mashpia to Generations**: As a respected elder, Reb Shmuel served as a mashpia to generations of French chassidim. His connection to the Rashab and the Rayatz gave him special authority, and his wisdom was sought by students and community leaders alike.

**Teaching Style**: Those who knew Reb Shmuel described his teaching as characterized by warmth, depth, and practical guidance. He emphasized that true Chassidus wasn't merely intellectual but had to transform one's character and daily conduct.

---

## Relationship with the Seventh Rebbe

Reb Shmuel maintained a connection with the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]], who valued him as a link to the earlier generations of Chabad.

**Correspondence**: The Rebbe corresponded with Reb Shmuel, seeking his counsel on matters relating to French Jewry and European Chabad communities. This correspondence demonstrated the Rebbe's respect for Reb Shmuel's judgment and experience.

**Visits to New York**: On several occasions, Reb Shmuel visited New York and participated in farbrengens with the Rebbe. His presence at these gatherings was always noted with respect by younger chassidim who recognized him as a living connection to pre-Soviet Chabad.

---

## Final Years and Legacy

Reb Shmuel Levitin passed away in France in 1976 at the age of 80. His funeral and burial were attended by the French Chabad community who honored him as a spiritual elder.

**The Link to Earlier Generations**: Reb Shmuel was one of the last living students of the Rashab. His passing represented the severing of a direct link to the pre-Soviet Chabad world. Through his students and the teachings he transmitted, that connection continues.

**Influence on French Chabad**: The French Chabad community that Reb Shmuel helped build has grown tremendously, with shluchim (emissaries) serving throughout France and North Africa. The spiritual foundation he helped lay continues to bear fruit.

**Oral Tradition**: Stories about Reb Shmuel's wisdom, warmth, and spiritual insight continue to be told in Chabad communities, preserving his legacy for future generations.
