# R' Yeruchamiel Binyaminzon

**Chabad rabbi who served in Zlobin, White Russia; noted peacemaker; later moved to Riga after leaving the Soviet Union**

## Biography

R' Yeruchamiel Binyaminzon (also spelled Binyaminzon) was a Chabad rabbi who served in Zlobin, White Russia during the 1920s, succeeding the earlier rabbi R' Moshe Akselrad (who had moved to Sorazh). He was known for his exceptional character as a "man of peace" — a description his own community confirmed.

His arrival in Zlobin was marked by a notable act of reconciliation: the town had been divided by factional disagreement between supporters of two rabbis. R' Binyaminzon, before accepting the position, insisted that he would not come to a town with such divisions. He went personally to the older rabbi (the "Alter Rav," Rabbi Vilda) to secure agreement and eliminate the factions. This reconciliation was celebrated by the community and mentioned in a letter of blessing from the Rayatz.

His rabbinate in Zlobin continued through the Soviet period. According to the Shusterman memoir, during the time of the Rayatz's 1927 arrest and liberation, R' Binyaminzon and his community in Zlobin were unaware of the events due to Soviet censorship.

Eventually, in the 1930s, R' Binyaminzon left the Soviet Union (like many Chabad rabbis who found ways to emigrate) and moved to Riga, Latvia, where the Rayatz had also been based. His departure left Zlobin's Chabad community without a formal rabbi.

## Significance

R' Binyaminzon exemplifies the ideal of the Chabad rabbi as peacemaker and communal leader. His success in reconciling the factions in Zlobin, praised by the Rayatz himself, reflects a core Chabad value of communal harmony. His subsequent emigration to Riga follows the pattern of Chabad rabbis navigating their way out of the increasingly hostile Soviet Union.
