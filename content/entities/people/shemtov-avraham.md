# Rabbi Avraham Shemtov

**Born**: c. 1930, Riga, Latvia  
**Role**: Chabad shliach to Philadelphia; national Chabad political liaison in Washington, D.C.

---

## Overview

Rabbi Avraham Shemtov is one of the most prominent shluchim of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]], serving simultaneously as the head of Chabad of Philadelphia and as Chabad's primary political representative in the United States. His unique dual role — community rabbi and Washington lobbyist — has made him one of the most influential figures in American Jewish public life.

---

## Early Life

Born in Riga, Latvia, Shemtov escaped the devastation of World War II and eventually made his way to the Chabad community in the United States. He became a devoted chassid of the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]] and was entrusted with major responsibilities in both outreach and public affairs.

---

## Chabad of Philadelphia

Shemtov led Chabad of Philadelphia for decades, building a network of synagogues, schools, and outreach programs that served the Jewish community across the Philadelphia metropolitan area.

---

## Washington and National Public Affairs

Shemtov's most distinctive contribution has been his role as Chabad's liaison to the American political establishment. Key achievements include:

- **White House Chanukah Menorah Lightings** — Beginning in 1979, Shemtov organized the lighting of a public menorah near the White House, eventually becoming the iconic National Menorah on the Ellipse near the National Mall. This became a landmark event in American Jewish public life.
- **Presidential Access** — Shemtov has personally met with every sitting US president from Gerald Ford onward, representing Chabad and Jewish communal interests at the highest levels of government.
- **Legislative Advocacy** — He has been instrumental in securing various proclamations, resolutions, and legislative recognitions related to Jewish education, religious freedom, and related causes.

---

## Significance

Shemtov represents a distinctive dimension of the [[concepts/shlichus|shlichus]] enterprise: the Rebbe's vision extended not only to individual Jews and communities, but to the public square and the corridors of political power. Shemtov's work ensured that Chabad's message — particularly regarding Jewish education and public religious expression — reached the highest levels of American governance.
