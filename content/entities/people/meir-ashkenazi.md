# Rabbi Meir Ashkenazi

**Born**: c. 1891, Russia  
**Died**: 1954  
**Role**: Chabad Chasid; Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Shanghai during WWII; facilitated the stay of refugee yeshiva students

---

## Overview

Rabbi Meir Ashkenazi was a Chabad Chasid who served as the Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Shanghai before and during WWII. When Jewish refugees began arriving via the [[events/shanghai-rescue-1940|Curacao/Japan route]] in 1940–1941, Rabbi Ashkenazi was positioned to facilitate their reception and integration into Shanghai Jewish life.

---

## Shanghai Jewish Community

Shanghai in the 1930s–1940s was uniquely positioned as one of the few places in the world with no immigration restrictions — neither Chinese authorities nor the Japanese occupiers (after 1937) required formal visas. This made Shanghai a refuge of last resort for European Jews.

The Ashkenazi Jewish community of Shanghai was relatively small but established. Rabbi Ashkenazi served as its chief rabbi, providing religious and communal leadership.

---

## Reception of Jewish Refugees (1940–1941)

When approximately 40 Chabad yeshiva students arrived via the Sugihara/Zwartendijk visa route, along with approximately 200 students from the Mir Yeshiva (essentially the entire institution), Rabbi Ashkenazi was the senior Jewish religious figure to receive them.

As a Chabad Chasid himself, he was particularly aligned with the Chabad students' needs and worldview. He helped:

- Facilitate their housing in Shanghai's overcrowded refugee conditions
- Maintain religious life (kosher food, Shabbat, Jewish education) under difficult conditions
- Coordinate with the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in Brooklyn, who was directing the operation from afar

The Rayatz in Brooklyn — coordinating through intermediaries — secured US Embassy negotiations and raised funds for the students' ongoing support.

---

## Wartime Shanghai

The Japanese occupied Shanghai in full from 1941. The situation for European refugees became more difficult — particularly after 1943, when the Japanese required all "stateless refugees" (a category that included most Jewish refugees) to move into a designated area in Hongkew (Hongkou) district, essentially a ghetto.

Rabbi Ashkenazi navigated these conditions as a spiritual and communal leader, maintaining the integrity of Jewish religious life under occupation while working to protect the refugees in his community.

---

## Legacy

Rabbi Meir Ashkenazi represents the often-overlooked role of established diaspora communities in rescue efforts. Without his presence and leadership in Shanghai, the arrival of hundreds of European Jewish refugees would have been far more chaotic and dangerous. His Chabad identity gave him a natural connection to the Chabad students — making him a uniquely appropriate figure to be in Shanghai at that precise moment.
