# Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levi

**Born**: 7 Tammuz 5672 (1912), Zviitov, Ukraine  
**Died**: c. 1990s, Kfar Chabad, Israel  
**Role**: Underground yeshiva student; author of [[summaries/zichronosai|*Zichronosai*]]; settler in Kfar Chabad

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levi's life story exemplifies the Chabad underground experience of the Soviet period. Born in 1912, he spent over a decade moving through Soviet cities as part of the clandestine Tomchei Temimim network, surviving multiple arrests, evading military conscription, and ultimately escaping the Soviet Union after WWII to settle in Kfar Chabad, Israel. His son, Rabbi Shalom DovBer Levin, became the editor of the entire _Mesibbv L'Chassidus_ series.

---

## Underground Yeshiva (1927–1938)

R' Yisrael Yehuda spent over a decade in the clandestine yeshiva network, moving through twelve Soviet cities to evade GPU surveillance:

**Vitebsk → Kiev → Baku → Kutaisi → Kolash → Moscow → Chernigov → Malakhovka → Leningrad → Voronezh → Krivoy Rog → Kharkov**

The Caucasian cities (Baku in Azerbaijan, Kutaisi in Georgia) represent the unusual geographic reach of the underground network — pushing into Soviet republics where the secret police had less penetration.

---

## Arrests and the Eyeglasses Miracle

R' Yisrael Yehuda was arrested multiple times during this period. He survived through courage, wit, and forged papers with false identities.

The most remarkable episode of his memoirs concerns military conscription. Facing induction into the Red Army — which would have ended his religious life — he sought guidance from the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]. The Rebbe's advice was specific: **wear glasses**. R' Yisrael Yehuda obtained and wore glasses, and was exempted from military service on vision grounds. He understood this as a direct miracle facilitated by the Rebbe's spiritual insight.

---

## World War II: Samarkand

When Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, R' Yisrael Yehuda fled eastward to Samarkand in Soviet Uzbekistan. The Central Asian city became home to a significant concentration of Chabad chassidim during the war years. There, his **young son died** — a devastating personal loss amid communal crisis.

---

## Escape and Aliyah

In 1946, R' Yisrael Yehuda escaped the Soviet Union using forged Polish papers under the name "**Naftali Bat**" — assuming a Polish Jewish identity to join the repatriation movement. This was the same strategy documented in the broader [[events/great-escape-1946|Great Escape]].

Rather than going to Western Europe or America, he chose Eretz Yisrael, arriving in **1949** and settling in **Kfar Chabad** — the village established for Soviet-era survivors near Tel Aviv.

---

## First Visit to the Rebbe (1962)

After more than a decade in Kfar Chabad, R' Yisrael Yehuda made his first trip to Brooklyn for the Tishrei holidays in 5723 (1962). His memoir conveys the overwhelming emotional weight of a chassid finally reaching his Rebbe after decades of separation, persecution, and loss.

---

## The Memoirs

R' Yisrael Yehuda wrote his memoirs — _Zichronosai_ — over 35 years beginning approximately 1960, driven by the desire to record everything accurately. His son, R' Shalom DovBer Levin (who became the editor of the entire _Mesibbv L'Chassidus_ series), edited the work for publication.
