# Rabbi Chaim David HaLevi

Rabbi Chaim David HaLevi (18 Shevat 5684 – 12 Adar 5758 / 1924–1998) was a posek and thinker, serving as Sephardic Chief Rabbi of Rishon LeTzion (5711–5733) and Chief Rabbi of Tel Aviv-Yafo (5733–5758). He received the Israel Prize for Torah literature and the Rabbi Kook Prize.

## Biography

Born and raised in Jerusalem's Ohel Moshe neighborhood, his father Moshe had emigrated from Bursa, Turkey at age 16. He studied at Porat Yosef Yeshiva and received rabbinic ordination from Rabbi Ezra Attia. He served as rosh midrash and personal secretary to Rishon LeTzion Rabbi Ben-Zion Chai Uziel.

In 5711 (1951) he was elected rabbi of Rishon LeTzion. In 5724 (1964) he was elected to the Chief Rabbinical Council. In 5733 (1973) he became Chief Rabbi of Tel Aviv-Yafo, serving until his death.

He composed the five-volume "Mekor Chaim HaShalem" (complete Kitzur Shulchan Aruch), which serves as a basic halachic textbook in religious Zionist education.

He passed away on 12 Adar 5758.

## Connection to Chabad

Over the years he maintained close contact with the Rebbe and was received for yechidus. He aided Chabad's activities in Tel Aviv extensively.

In 5753 (1993) he received the title "Yadid Chabad" (Friend of Chabad) from the Chabad Center of Tel Aviv for his friendship and substantial assistance to Chabad in the city.

At the dedication of a Sefer Torah to the "Geulat Yisrael Chabad" synagogue in Tel Aviv, Rabbi HaLevi said: "This Sefer Torah is dedicated to the Rebbe — and not for the elevation of his soul — and I deduce from this that Chassidim recognize the Rebbe as alive and present... I tell you that I feel that the Rebbe is alive! One cannot say about him the expressions used for tzaddikim who are no longer among the living! Though personally I find it difficult to say 'shlita' — I feel that he is alive!"
