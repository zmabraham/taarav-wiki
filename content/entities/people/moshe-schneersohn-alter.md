# Moshe Schneersohn

## R' Moshe — Son of the Alter Rebbe (late 18th–early 19th century)

**Also known as:** R' Moshe Schneersohn, Moshe of the Alter Rebbe
**Born:** Late 18th century (c. 1780s)
**Died:** Early 19th century
**Father:** The [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] (R' Schneur Zalman of Liadi)
**Mother:** Sterna Segal
**Brothers:**

- [[entities/people/chaim-avraham-schneersohn|R' Chaim Avraham]] (eldest)
- [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|R' DovBer]] (the Mitteler Rebbe)

---

## Overview

Rabbi **Moshe Schneersohn** was a son of the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]], founder of Chabad Hasidism. While less well-known than his brother the Mitteler Rebbe (who became the second Chabad Rebbe), Moshe was part of the Alter Rebbe's immediate family and the founding generation of Chabad.

Historical sources about Moshe are limited, but his place in the family tree is significant for understanding the Alter Rebbe's family dynamics and the early Chabad community.

---

## Family Context

### The Alter Rebbe's Children

The Alter Rebbe and his wife Sterna had several children, including:

1. **R' Chaim Avraham** — eldest son, who did not become Rebbe
2. **R' DovBer** — the Mitteler Rebbe, second Chabad Rebbe
3. **R' Moshe** — the subject of this page
4. **Daughters** — who married into prominent Chassidic families

### The Significance of Being a Schneersohn

Bearing the name "Schneersohn" (son of Schneur) meant being part of the founding family of Chabad. Even those who did not become Rebbe carried:

- The prestige of their lineage
- The expectations of their community
- The responsibility of their name
- The connection to Chabad's founding

## Life and Role

### Limited Historical Information

Unfortunately, historical sources provide limited information about Moshe's life and activities. What we know includes:

- He was the son of the Alter Rebbe
- He lived during the founding period of Chabad
- He did not assume a leadership role comparable to his brother DovBer
- He was part of the extended Chabad community

### Possible Roles

Based on what we know about the families of Chabad Rebbes, Moshe likely:

- Studied Torah and Chassidus under his father
- Participated in the Chabad community's activities
- May have taught or guided other chassidim
- May have had communal responsibilities
- Did not establish his own court or claim independent authority

## Historical Context

### The Founding Generation

Moshe belonged to the **founding generation of Chabad** — those who witnessed the Alter Rebbe build the movement from its earliest days. This generation experienced:

- The excitement of new spiritual teachings
- The opposition from Mitnagdim
- The arrests and persecutions of 1798 and 1800
- The flight from Napoleon in 1812
- The trauma of the Alter Rebbe's death

### Life After the Alter Rebbe's Passing

After the Alter Rebbe's death in 1812, Moshe would have been part of the community that:

- Turned to his brother DovBer (the Mitteler Rebbe) for leadership
- Rebuilt after the disruptions of war
- Re-established the Chabad center in Lubavitch
- Preserved and transmitted the Alter Rebbe's teachings

---

## Family Legacy

### The Schneersohn Dynasty

Although Moshe did not become Rebbe, he was part of the family dynasty that would lead Chabad for seven generations:

- His father: Alter Rebbe (first Rebbe)
- His brother: Mitteler Rebbe (second Rebbe)
- His nephew: Tzemach Tzedek (third Rebbe)
- Through his brother's line: All subsequent Rebbes

### Descendants

Moshe likely married and had children, continuing the Schneersohn family line. His descendants would have been:

- Part of the broader Schneersohn family
- Connected to Chabad communities
- Carriers of the family name and legacy

## Historical Significance

### The Question of Leadership

Moshe's life raises questions about how Chabad leadership was determined:

- Why did DovBer become Rebbe and not Chaim Avraham (the eldest)?
- What about other sons like Moshe?
- How did the family navigate succession?

The peaceful succession to DovBer suggests that:

- There was agreement about who should lead
- Family unity was maintained
- The community recognized DovBer's unique qualifications
- Other family members accepted their roles

### The Schneersohn Family Network

The Alter Rebbe's descendants formed an extensive network that:

- Provided leadership for Chabad communities
- Maintained family connections across regions
- Preserved the founding teachings
- Created a dynasty that continues today
