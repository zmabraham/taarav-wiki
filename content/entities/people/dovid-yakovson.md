# R' Dovid Yakovson

**Rabbi of Lubavitch during the era of the Rashab (5th Rebbe) and into the early Rayatz era**

## Biography

R' Dovid Yakovson served as the communal rabbi of Lubavitch during the lengthy leadership of R' Shalom DovBer Schneersohn (the Rashab, 5th Rebbe, who led from 1882 to 1920). His tenure as town rabbi spanned one of the most consequential periods in Chabad history — the establishment of Tomchei Temimim (1897), World War I, the Russian Revolutions, and the final years of Lubavitch as the Chabad capital.

During the Rashab's era, Lubavitch grew further in prestige and institutional complexity. The founding of Tomchei Temimim in 1897 brought hundreds of students to the town, creating a new layer of communal life. R' Yakovson as town rabbi would have overseen the day-to-day halachic functioning of this expanded community.

He likely remained in the position during the transition to the Rayatz (6th Rebbe) in 1920, following the Rashab's death in Rostov. The fate of Lubavitch itself changed dramatically in this period: Soviet rule brought increasing pressure, and the Rayatz eventually relocated the Chabad center to Rostov and then Leningrad, before leaving Russia entirely in 1927.

## Significance

R' Dovid Yakovson's rabbinate bridges the classic Lubavitch era (under the Rashab) and the beginning of its Soviet decline. His role documents how the institutional infrastructure of the Lubavitch community — separate from the Rebbe's court — continued to function through the upheavals of the early 20th century. The 1921 fire that devastated the Lubavitch courtyard occurred during or near the end of his tenure.
