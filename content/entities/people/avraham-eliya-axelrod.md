# R' Avraham Eliya Axelrod

**Temim from Kobylnik; founding member of Agudas HaTmimim; rabbi in Baltimore; man of extraordinary piety**

## Overview

R' Avraham Eliya Axelrod was from Kobylnik (Narach, Belarus). He was among the founding members of Agudas HaTmimim in New York and later served as a rabbi in Baltimore. He was renowned for an extraordinary degree of yiras shamayim (fear of heaven) combined with great personal kindness and selflessness.

## Dedication to Tomchei Temimim

R' Avraham Eliya's devotion to Tomchei Temimim is documented through a letter he wrote to R' Yisrael Jacobson at the time of Agudas HaTmimim's founding (around 5687/1927). He studied at Tomchei Temimim for approximately fourteen years — from summer 5666 (1906) to approximately 5681 (1921) — progressing through its various divisions and branches:

- Summer 5666: "The Bobruisk Room" (der Bobroisker cheder), under R' Aryeh Leib Haft
- Winter 5667: under R' Meir Lobak of Lyaznie
- Summer 5667: under R' Hershel Kavner [Gotlib], Rosh Yeshiva
- Winter 5668: under R' Yechiel from Shchedrin [Komisar], Rosh Yeshiva
- Summer and winter 5669: in Babinowitz under R' [Zalman] Yissachar Ber Dribiner [Shechter], and under the influence of R' Shaul Dov Zislin (mashpia)
- Summer 5669 in Lubavitch: under R' Mendel from Zlabin [Dubravsky] and R' the Rabbi from Kachanovke
- 5670: in Shchedrin under R' Yissachar Ber from Dribin [Shechter] and R' Moshe Mordechai Epstein [Poharelsky/Amrami] from Pochep
- From 5671 onward: in the Great Hall (Zal), and afterward in Poltava, Kremenchug, and Rostov until 5681

Most remarkably, when Axelrod's parents emigrated to America while he was still young, **he chose to remain in Lubavitch** even though this meant he might never see his parents again. This choice — separating himself from his family to continue Torah study in Lubavitch — was considered a remarkable act of mesiras nefesh (self-sacrifice) for Torah.

## Piety and Character in America

R' Yisrael Jacobson describes R' Avraham Eliya with particular warmth:

**Otherworldly detachment:** When the Rayatz visited America in 5689–5690 (1929–1930), R' Avraham Eliya's congregants in Baltimore complained to the Rebbe that he refused to wear a (dress) shirt and was excessively disconnected from worldly matters. The Rebbe prevailed upon him to wear a shirt — but R' Avraham Eliya insisted that his kippah (skullcap) should be visible protruding above his hat.

**Personal kindness:** Jacobson found it difficult to adequately describe his goodness to others — he would sometimes give up his own bed for a friend in need.

**Contentment with little:** He lived with minimal personal needs, and this was not poverty forced upon him but a genuine preference for simplicity.

**Yiras Shamayim:** His fear of Heaven pervaded every aspect of his conduct — Jacobson writes that it was "impossible to describe" the particularity of his religious observance.

## After Arriving in America

After the upheavals of the Civil War and early Soviet period in Russia, R' Avraham Eliya arrived in America in Chanukah 5684 (winter 1923) and settled in Baltimore in summer 5684. Initially he received $5 per week to teach at a shul (Tzemach Tzedek, Nusach HaArizal); this subsequently grew to $8 per week when his congregants began asking him halachic questions informally, effectively serving as a rabbi without a formal letter of appointment. He also received $10 a month from the Ladies Auxiliary and $15 a month from the rabbinical fund (Kufat HaRabbanim).

He had received rabbinic ordination (semicha) from the rabbis of Minsk, including R' Eliezer Rabinowitz and the av beis din of Lubavitch.

## Founding of Agudas HaTmimim

R' Avraham Eliya Axelrod was among the founding members of Agudas HaTmimim when it was established in October 1925 at R' Eliya Simpson's home. Jacobson describes him as "among the first members of Agudas HaTmimim."

## Connection to R' Zev Kazinitz

Jacobson notes that R' Avraham Eliya had been the one who encouraged R' Zev Kazinitz to go study in Lubavitch. When R' Kazinitz asked him before emigrating to America what he should bring, R' Avraham Eliya answered: "Bags of mesiras nefesh" (_zek mit mesiras nefesh_).

## Significance

R' Avraham Eliya Axelrod embodies the Temim who gave fourteen years to Tomchei Temimim, sacrificed family proximity, and then carried the Lubavitch spirit into American rabbinic life. His life in Baltimore — modest compensation, genuine learning, and extraordinary personal piety — represents the best of what the early American Chabad community produced from its yeshiva-trained rabbinate. His detailed self-account in the Jacobson archive preserves an unusually complete record of Tomchei Temimim's study framework in its various branches.
