# R' Shalom Yehuda Gribaow

**Chabad rabbi from Nevel; served briefly as rabbi of Zlobin after R' Binyaminzon's departure; arrested by Soviet authorities**

## Biography

R' Shalom Yehuda Gribaow was originally from Nevel, one of the most important Chabad towns in White Russia. He served as rabbi in Zlobin, White Russia, after R' Yeruchamiel Binyaminzon left for Riga in the early 1930s.

According to the Shusterman memoir, R' Gribaow served as rabbi of Zlobin but "did not last long" because the Soviet anti-religious campaign began arresting "kli kodesh" (sacred vessels — meaning rabbis and religious functionaries) in the region. He was arrested by Soviet authorities. His fate after arrest is unclear from the sources, though the context suggests he may have perished.

The memoir notes that during the same period, other Chabad rabbis in White Russia were also arrested: R' Chaim Leib Zaks (rabbi of Ozda) and R' Shmuel Notik (rabbi of Kopust), among others.

## Significance

R' Gribaow represents the final wave of Chabad rabbis who attempted to maintain formal Jewish leadership in the Soviet Union through the early 1930s. As the Stalinist terror intensified, the systematic arrest of religious leaders — documented in accounts like the Shusterman memoir — eliminated organized Jewish religious leadership throughout Soviet White Russia.
