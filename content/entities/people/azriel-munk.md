# Rabbi Azriel Munk — ר' עזריאל מונק

**Born**: 1867  
**Died**: 1940  
**Role**: Rabbi of Adath Yisrael congregation, Berlin; Orthodox communal leader  
**Family**: Nephew of [[entities/people/azriel-hildesheimer|Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer]]; father of Rabbi Meir Munk (Berlin)

---

## Overview

Rabbi Azriel Munk served as rabbi of the Orthodox Adath Yisrael congregation in [[entities/places/berlin|Berlin]], one of the important separate Orthodox kehillos in the German capital. He was a nephew of [[entities/people/azriel-hildesheimer|Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer]], the founder of the Berlin Rabbinical Seminary, placing him within the inner circle of Berlin Neo-Orthodox leadership. He died in 1940 — during the early Nazi period — likely in Berlin.

---

## The Adath Yisrael Congregation

The Adath Yisrael congregation in Berlin was a separate Orthodox community established along the model of Frankfurt's Adath Yeshurun — rejecting the unified community model (the _Einheitsgemeinde_) in favor of independent Orthodox kehilla life. This congregation represented the more separatist wing of Berlin Orthodoxy, in contrast to those who remained within the general community structure.

## Connection to Chabad History

The Chabad-Germany volume documents a meeting between the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] and Rabbi **Meir Munk** (son of Rabbi Azriel Munk) during the Rayatz's Berlin years. The encounter took place in Shvat 5688 (early 1928) — shortly after the Rayatz arrived in Berlin from Riga.

This meeting reflects the Rayatz's systematic cultivation of relationships with Berlin Orthodox leaders during his years in Germany (1928–1933). The Munk family's connection to the Hildesheimer circle meant that meeting with them bridged the Chabad-German Orthodox network that the Rashab had built over the preceding decades.

---

## The Hildesheimer Connection

Rabbi Azriel Munk's relationship to his uncle [[entities/people/azriel-hildesheimer|Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer]] placed him within the most significant institutional network of German Orthodoxy:

- Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer had founded the Berlin Rabbinical Seminary (1873)
- The seminary was continued by Hildesheimer's son [[entities/people/meir-hildesheimer|Rabbi Meir Hildesheimer]]
- Rabbi Azriel Munk served the congregational (rather than seminary) side of this network
- His son Rabbi Meir Munk carried the name forward into the next generation

This overlapping set of Hildesheimer connections — uncle (Azriel Hildesheimer), nephew (Azriel Munk), and grandson-namesake (Meir Munk) — illustrates the tight family and institutional web of Berlin's Neo-Orthodox community.
