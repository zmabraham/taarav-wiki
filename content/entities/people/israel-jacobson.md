# Rabbi Israel Jacobson (ר' ישראל דזייקאבסאן)

**Chabad activist, memoirist, and founding leader of American Chabad organizations.**

Rabbi Israel Jacobson was a student of Tomchei Temimim during the era of the Rebbe Rashab, a witness to key events in Lubavitch and Rostov, and one of the principal founders of organized Chabad in America. His memoirs, written 1957–1959 at the instruction of the Lubavitcher Rebbe (7th), constitute one of the most detailed firsthand accounts of early 20th-century Chabad life.

## Biography

Born into a family with four generations of shochtim (ritual slaughterers) in Zuravichy, Jacobson showed early scholarly ability. After debating between yeshivos in Lida (Yeshivas Reines) and Lubavitch, he chose Lubavitch and arrived at Tomchei Temimim in 5668 (1907/1908), just before his bar mitzvah. He was placed under the supervision of R' Gershon Chen.

He studied under multiple melamdim at TT, advanced through several classes, and was present at many significant moments — including the Rebbe Rashab's discourses in both the small and large halls, and the fire that burned the small hall.

After World War I disrupted the yeshiva's presence in Lubavitch, he moved to Homel (Gomel) where he lived 1920–1925 and helped found a Chabad yeshiva. He emigrated to New York around 1925/1926.

In America, he was a central figure in founding **Agudas HaTmimim** (at the founding meeting Motzaei Shabbat Shemini Atzeret 5686) and managing **Agudas Chasidei Chabad**. He played a key role during the Rayatz's first visit to America (13 Elul 5689 – 20 Tammuz 5690 / 1929–1930), including arranging High Holiday prayers and community logistics.

He also helped found **Achei Temimim** and worked to send American-born youth to study at Tomchei Temimim in Otwock, Poland.

He wrote his memoirs in 1957–1959 at the instruction of the Lubavitcher Rebbe. Excerpts were published in "Di Idishe Heim" quarterly from 5724–5735. The complete memoirs were published posthumously in ~5752 (1992).

During the winter of 5700 (1939/1940), he was involved in rescuing the Rayatz from Nazi-occupied Warsaw — a topic covered in a separate (as yet unpublished) companion volume.
