# R' Meir Gorkow

Father-in-law of the memoirist R' Yisrael Yehuda Levin; Chabad Chassid and author of his own memoirs of Soviet-era Chabad life.

## Biography

R' Meir Gorkow was a significant Chabad Chassid who spent time imprisoned under the Soviet regime. After his release, he arrived in Samarkand in summer 5704 with his wife Piga Rivka, joining the growing Chabad community there that included his son-in-law R' Yisrael Yehuda Levin.

He wrote memoirs of his experiences in Soviet Russia, which were praised by the Rayatz in a letter dated 22 Marcheshvan 5710 (5710 = 1950). The Rebbe described the "kuntres of memories" as "well-received and appropriate" and stated that "it is right and proper that all elder students should write down their memories worthy of remembrance."

His memoirs were eventually published as *Sefer haZichronos Divrei haYomim* in New York in 5736 (1976).

The Rayatz's instructions for Chabad members to write down their Soviet-era memories (letters of 18 Tevet 5709 and 24 Shvat 5709, cited in the introduction to *Zichronos*) were the direct inspiration for both Gorkow's memoirs and those of his son-in-law Yisrael Yehuda Levin.
