# R' Avraham Zeligson

**Rabbi of Lubavitch during the era of the Maharash (4th Rebbe); community leader of the Chabad capital**

## Biography

R' Avraham Zeligson served as the official communal rabbi (mara d'asra) of Lubavitch during the era of R' Shmuel Schneersohn, known as the Maharash (the 4th Rebbe, who led Chabad from 1866 to 1882). This position was distinct from the role of Rebbe — while the Maharash provided spiritual leadership and Chassidic guidance, R' Zeligson administered the practical halachic and communal affairs of the town itself.

Lubavitch at this time was the undisputed center of Chabad: thousands of Chassidim made pilgrimages there throughout the year, particularly for the High Holidays and other significant occasions. As rabbi of the town, R' Zeligson oversaw the kashrut supervision, the town's beit din (rabbinical court), and the welfare of the permanent residents of Lubavitch, as distinct from the transient Chassidic visitors.

His tenure coincided with the prosperous Maharash era, when Chabad maintained relative stability despite broader anti-Jewish pressures in Russia. The Maharash was known for his engagement with the aristocracy and business world — this created an atmosphere where Lubavitch remained a functioning Chassidic center with substantial resources.

## Significance

The position of town rabbi in Lubavitch, distinct from the Rebbe's role, illustrates the dual institutional structure of Chabad's central city. R' Zeligson's tenure helps document the administrative infrastructure that allowed Lubavitch to function as a major Jewish communal center. His story complements the more famous figures of the Rebbes by filling in the practical communal layer of the Chabad capital.
