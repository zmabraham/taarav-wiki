# Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev

**Born**: c. 1740, Husakov, Galicia  
**Died**: 25 Tishrei 5570 (October 5, 1809), Berditchev, Ukraine  
**Role**: Major Chassidic leader; disciple of [[entities/people/maggid-of-mezeritch|Maggid of Mezeritch]]; known for his passionate love of Israel and advocacy before God on behalf of the Jewish people

---

## Overview

Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev was one of the most beloved figures in all of Chassidic history — known for his extraordinary love of Israel (the Jewish people), his habit of defending Jews before the Heavenly Court, and his ability to see the good in every Jew regardless of their religious standing. He served as the Chassidic rebbe of Berditchev in Ukraine for over two decades.

---

## Character and Teachings

Unlike the intellectually-oriented [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] (Chabad), Rabbi Levi Yitzchak represented the emotional and devotional stream of Chassidism. He was known for:

- Stopping prayers to address God directly on behalf of Jews
- Finding merit in even the most apparently irreligious Jew
- His famous Kaddish prayer in which he argued with God about the sufferings of Israel
- His composition of numerous Yiddish prayers and melodies

His major work, _Kedushas Levi_, remains widely studied in Chassidic circles.

---

## Connection to Chabad History

The connection between Rabbi Levi Yitzchak and Chabad appears in [[summaries/lemaan-yedau|*Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu*]]: memoirist Shusterman's hometown of Zlobin was the site of a famous wedding between the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe's]] daughter and a grandson of Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev — demonstrating the intermarriage between Chabad's dynasty and other major Chassidic families.
