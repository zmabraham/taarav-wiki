# Rabbi Meir Lehman — ר' מאיר לעהמאן

**Born**: 1831, Verden an der Aller, Hanover  
**Died**: 1890, Mainz  
**Role**: Rabbi of Mainz; founder and editor of _Der Israelit_; Orthodox Jewish journalist and novelist

---

## Overview

Rabbi Meir Lehman (known in German as Marcus Lehmann) was the rabbi of Mainz and the founder of the influential Orthodox weekly newspaper _Der Israelit_ ("The Israelite"), which he edited from 1860 until his death in 1890. As the primary Orthodox Jewish newspaper in the German-speaking world, _Der Israelit_ was an essential platform for traditional Jewish expression against the dominant Reform movement. Lehman was also a prolific writer of Jewish historical novels aimed at educating and inspiring Orthodox Jewish youth.

---

## Der Israelit

Founded in 1860 in Frankfurt, _Der Israelit_ moved with Lehman to Mainz and became one of the most widely read Jewish newspapers in Germany. The paper:

- **Advocated for traditional Orthodoxy** against Reform Judaism's growing dominance
- **Covered Jewish communities worldwide**, including news from Eastern Europe
- **Published Torah commentary** and halakhic discussions
- **Featured Lehman's historical fiction** — serialized novels set in medieval Jewish history

The newspaper continued after Lehman's death and remained a major Orthodox voice until the Nazi period.

---

## Jewish Historical Novels

Lehman's novels — including _Rabbi Joselmann_, _The Slave of Saragossa_, and others — combined historical research with religious storytelling to portray Jewish heroism, martyrdom, and steadfast faith. They were widely read in Orthodox homes and translated into multiple languages, including Hebrew and Yiddish. Their influence on Orthodox Jewish cultural self-understanding in the late 19th and early 20th centuries was considerable.

---

## Role in German Orthodoxy

Rabbi Lehman represented a current within German Orthodoxy that engaged with modernity through journalism and popular literature — seeking to strengthen Orthodox identity through accessible, compelling narratives rather than withdrawal from culture. This approach differed from, though was not opposed to, the more academic approach of [[entities/people/azriel-hildesheimer|Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer]] and the institutional approach of [[entities/people/shimshon-raphael-hirsch|Rabbi Shimshon Raphael Hirsch]].

## Connection to Chabad History

Rabbi Meir Lehman is mentioned in the survey of German rabbis in the Chabad-Germany historical volume (Book 13 of the Mesibbv L'Chassidus series). His inclusion reflects his significance as a representative of the broader German Orthodox world that the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] engaged with during his visits to Germany.
