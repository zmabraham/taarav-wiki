# R' Arye Leib Sheinin — ר' אריה לייב שיינין

**Chabad Rabbi of Doksitz; Traveling Mashpia; Martyred 1942**

**Full Name**: Rabbi Arye Leib Sheinin
**Dates**: (late 19th–1942)
**Position**: Chabad rabbi of [[entities/places/doksitz|Doksitz]]; traveling mashpia for surrounding communities
**Affiliation**: Chabad-Lubavitch
**Death**: Martyred in the Holocaust, 1942

---

## Overview

Rabbi Arye Leib Sheinin served a dual role in the Chabad world of the eastern Polish borderlands: he was the dedicated Chabad rabbi of [[entities/places/doksitz|Doksitz]] (a community with four Chabad synagogues) and also an itinerant mashpia who traveled to other communities in the region to provide Chassidic teaching and spiritual guidance. He was martyred in the Holocaust in 1942, together with the Jewish community of Doksitz.

---

## Role: Rabbi and Traveling Mashpia

### Stationary Role in Doksitz

As Chabad rabbi of Doksitz:

- **Halachic authority**: Provided rabbinical rulings according to Chabad tradition
- **Community leader**: Led the four Chabad synagogues in Doksitz
- **Spiritual guide**: Served as the local mashpia for Doksitz Chassidim
- **Community management**: Oversaw Chabad institutions — kosher food, mikvah, education

### Traveling Mashpia

Beyond his local role, R' Sheinin was known for traveling:

- **Itinerant teaching**: Visited smaller communities in the surrounding region
- **Bringing Chassidus**: Brought Chabad teachings to communities too small to have their own mashpia
- **Farbrengens**: Led Chassidic gatherings in the communities he visited
- **Personal guidance**: Individual consultations with Chassidim seeking spiritual direction
- **Network building**: His travels strengthened the connections among the small Chabad communities of the eastern borderlands

---

## The Region He Served

The eastern borderlands where R' Sheinin worked:

- **Dense Jewish settlement**: Dozens of small Jewish communities within a small geographic area
- **Mixed character**: Communities with Chabad, other Chassidic, and Mitnagdic elements
- **Relative isolation**: Small communities often lacked formal Chabad leadership
- **His contribution**: R' Sheinin's visits brought the "big picture" of Chabad to communities that might otherwise feel disconnected

---

## The Traveling Mashpia Tradition

R' Sheinin embodied an important Chabad tradition:

- **Chabad has always valued personal contact**: The Rebbe-Chassid relationship requires real connection
- **Emissary work**: Traveling teachers were crucial to maintaining the network
- **Self-sacrifice**: Travel in that era — by horse-drawn vehicle, poor roads, harsh weather — required real dedication
- **Spiritual courage**: Entering hostile or indifferent communities required confidence in one's mission

---

## Martyrdom (1942)

The German occupation of Belarus/eastern Poland:

- **Occupation**: Germany occupied the eastern borderlands after invading the Soviet Union (June 1941)
- **Doksitz destruction**: The Jewish community of Doksitz was murdered in 1942
- **R' Sheinin's death**: He was killed with his community
- **Loss**: His unique dual role — local rabbi and traveling mashpia — was irreplaceable

---

## Legacy

R' Arye Leib Sheinin is remembered as:

- **A martyr of Lithuanian/Polish Chabad**: One of the many dedicated leaders who perished
- **The traveling mashpia model**: His work demonstrates the importance of mobile spiritual leadership
- **Doksitz connection**: The primary human face of the four-synagogue community at Doksitz
