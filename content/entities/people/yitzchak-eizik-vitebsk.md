# Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik Barad of Vitebsk

Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik Barad (c. 5528–5627 / c.1768–1867) was one of the earliest and most prominent Chassidim of the Alter Rebbe, serving as rabbi for 75 years — first in Ostrovnah and then for 60 years in Vitebsk. He was a living link between the earliest generation of Chabad Chassidus and the era of the Maharash.

## Biography

Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik was initially a mitnaged (opponent of Chassidus). He was converted to Chassidus following the famous Minsk debate of 5543 (1783), in which the Alter Rebbe demonstrated the authenticity and depth of Chassidic teaching. From that point, he became a devoted disciple of the Alter Rebbe.

He received rabbinic ordination from the Alter Rebbe in the Alter Rebbe's own handwriting — a mark of special distinction. He served as rabbi of Ostrovnah for 15 years, then as rabbi of Vitebsk for an extraordinary 60 years, for a total rabbinic career of 75 years.

He was one of the last living eyewitnesses to the era of the Alter Rebbe and his court. The Maharash (Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn) spent 23 years (5603–5626 / 1843–1866) listening to his firsthand stories and recollections of the Alter Rebbe's generation, and these accounts became a significant historical source for Chabad tradition.

His responsa were collected in "She'ut Pnei Yitzchak," published in Vilna in 5669 (1909).

## Connection to Chabad

As a direct student of the Alter Rebbe who received ordination in the Alter Rebbe's hand, Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik was a living embodiment of the first generation of Chabad. His long life allowed him to transmit the oral tradition and personal recollections of the Alter Rebbe's era to subsequent generations.

The Maharash's sustained effort to record his memories — spanning more than two decades — reflects the high historical and spiritual value Chabad placed on preserving these early traditions.
