# R' Shlomo Chaim Kutan (ר' שלמה חיים קוטאין)

**Shochet (ritual slaughterer) in Lubavitch; teacher of shechita to Tomchei Temimim students.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Shochet in Lubavitch; informal shechita teacher for TT students |
| **Active** | At least 5668 (1908) and surrounding years |
| **Relation** | Uncle of R' Alter Beylin (brother of his mother); father-in-law of R' Shlomo Sadovsky |

## Overview

It was the established practice at Tomchei Temimim for students to apprentice in the craft of shechita, in addition to their regular studies. Since R' Shlomo Chaim Kutan was both a skilled shochet and a resident of Lubavitch, students would come to him in the afternoons (around 3–4pm, after prayer and lunch) to learn.

At any given time approximately ten students would be studying shechita under him. The apprenticeship typically took 2–3 years to complete. The Rebbe Rashab reportedly said of him: "Only the teacher remained in him" — suggesting his entire being was devoted to his craft as a teacher.

The Rayatz himself was among those who trained in shechita under R' Shlomo Chaim. Israel Jacobson stayed for a time in the home of R' Shlomo Chaim's relative, R' Yishaaya Beylin.

R' Shlomo Chaim's son-in-law, R' Shlomo Sadovsky, studied at TT and later became a rabbi in Rochester, NY.
