# Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (Rayatz)

Sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe (1880–1950). Son of the Rebbe Rashab (R' Shalom Dov Ber Schneersohn). Led the Chabad movement through some of its most turbulent decades, including Soviet persecution, relocation to Poland, and the Holocaust.

## Rescue from Warsaw (1939–1940)

When Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, Rayatz was in Warsaw. Despite initial refusal to leave, he was eventually persuaded to seek escape. The rescue involved:

**German contacts**: Klaus Shenk and Ernst Bloch, German officers of partial Jewish ancestry, facilitated his passage. Admiral [[entities/people/wilhelm-canaris]] of German military intelligence played a key role.

**US diplomatic intervention**: Attorney Asher Rabinowitz and Attorney Sam Kremer worked in the USA. Judge Louis Brandeis, Congressman Sol Blum, Congressman Emanuel Celler, James Roosevelt (son of President Roosevelt), Secretary of State Cordell Hull, and State Department official Robert Pell all intervened on his behalf.

**Latvian connection**: Alfred Bilmanis, Latvian envoy to the USA, and Alexander Kirk, US Ambassador to Berlin, facilitated the transit through Germany to Riga.

**Secretaries**: R' [[entities/people/yechezkel-feigin]] and R' [[entities/people/chaim-lieberman]] accompanied him.

## Library

Rayatz built the Lubavitch Library, beginning with the purchase of R' [[entities/people/shmuel-weiner]]'s collection in Leningrad in 5685 (1924). The library grew to some 13,000–23,000 volumes including rare manuscripts and early printed books. It traveled with him from Leningrad to Riga to Warsaw to Otwock. Upon his escape from Europe, 100 boxes of books were sent via Stockholm to New York (arriving Sivan 5701 / 1941). See [[entities/events/lubavitch-library-rescue]].

## Relief Activities

Upon arriving in the USA, Rayatz established the [[entities/organizations/vaad-pidyon-shvuyim]] (Committee for Captive Redemption) to assist Jews trapped in Europe. He coordinated lists of families in Lithuania (~100 families, ~500 souls) and Nazi Poland (163 persons) and arranged food packages and visas.
