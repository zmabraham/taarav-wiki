# Rabbi Yoel Sirkes (the Bach)

Rabbi Yoel Sirkes (5321–20 Adar 5400 / 1561–1640), known as "the Bach" (an acronym for his major work "Beit Chadash"), was one of the greatest Acharonim, a proofreader of the Talmud, and a leading posek of his era.

## Biography

Born in Lublin to Rabbi Shmuel, his family name was originally Yaffe; he was called "Sirkes" after his righteous mother Sirkah. He studied under Rabbi Shlomo Leibush of Lublin (a colleague of Rabbi Eliyahu Baal Shem of Worms).

He served as rabbi in Pozna, Lubomla, Medzibizh, Belz, and Brest-Litovsk (where he was appointed Av Beit Din in 5375/1615), and finally moved to Kraków as Av Beit Din in 5379 (1619), serving there until his death.

He was among the heads of the Va'ad Arba Aratzot (Council of Four Lands), and his name appears frequently in its records, particularly regarding education taxes. He was wealthy and supported many students in his large yeshiva.

Remarkably, he avoided learning foreign languages and external wisdom, with the exception of mathematics. His students included the Taz (his son-in-law), Rabbi Yehoshua Charif (Maginei Shlomo), Rabbi Menachem Mendel Krokhmal (Tzemach Tzedek HaKadmon), and Rabbi Yoel Baal Shem.

## Scholarship and Kabbalah

In his halachic works he demonstrates deep knowledge of the Zohar and Kabbalistic texts. He wrote a commentary on the Kabbalistic work of the Ramak (Rabbi Moshe Cordovero), "Pardes Rimonim." Despite this, he ruled that Kabbalah should not override Talmudic law or accepted practice.

## Connection to Chabad

The Alter Rebbe rules in his Shulchan Aruch in many instances according to the Bach.

The Tzemach Tzedek related a remarkable story: once the Alter Rebbe issued a ruling permitting a bird that the Bach would have declared forbidden. After the Alter Rebbe's passing, the Bach appeared to him in the upper world requesting him to review the case. On that same day, the Alter Rebbe came to his grandson, the Tzemach Tzedek, asking him to provide justification for this ruling — the reason for the delay.

The Tzemach Tzedek said of the Bach: "The Bach is a reliable authority — all tzaddikim must pass through Gehinnom, but when the Bach departed, they cooled Gehinnom for sixty days in his honor."

The Rebbe quoted multiple times the Bach's remarkable passage on Torah study — that the intention of Heaven was for Torah to spiritually unite one's soul with its Divine source, and that deviation from this causes the Shechina to withdraw from the earth. The Rebbe noted: "Were we not seeing this explicitly written by the Bach in revealed Torah, one might think only a Chassidic Rebbe could have written it."

## Works
- **Beit Chadash** — extensive commentary on all sections of the Arba'ah Turim
- **She'ut HaBach** — responsa
- **Commentary on Pardes Rimonim** of the Ramak
- **Chiddushim on the Talmud** (printed in Vilna Shas margins)
