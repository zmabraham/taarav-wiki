# Rabbi Yosef Dov Soloveitchik (Beit HaLevi)

Rabbi Yosef Dov Soloveitchik (5580–4 Iyar 5652 / 1820–1892) was the head of the Volozhin Yeshiva and the Av Beit Din (chief rabbi) of Slutsk and Brisk. He composed the responsa work Beit HaLevi.

## Biography

Born in Nissvizh. His father Rabbi Yitzchak Zev was a Torah scholar and communal leader, grandson of Relvke (daughter of Rabbi Chaim of Volozhin, founder of the Volozhin Yeshiva and disciple of the Vilna Gaon).

He studied at Volozhin Yeshiva and in 5613 (1853) was appointed Mashne (deputy head) of the Volozhin Yeshiva under the Netziv. He published his responsa "Beit HaLevi" in 5623 (1863). In 5625 (1865) he was appointed as rabbi of Slutsk; in 5639 (1879) he became rabbi of Brisk, serving until his death.

He was known for extraordinary physical and mental endurance — famously undergoing surgery without anesthesia, having promised the doctors not to move during the procedure, and keeping his word without uttering a sound.

He passed away on 4 Iyar 5652, the same day his son Rabbi Chaim was crowned rabbi of Brisk at his funeral.

## Connection to Chabad

His son Rabbi Chaim Soloveitchik was very close friends with the Rebbe Rashab and the Frierdiker Rebbe. The Frierdiker Rebbe related how Rabbi Chaim quoted his father as saying that when he delivered deep lectures on Taharot (ritual purity laws), it raised the level of Torah study throughout the community — down to the simplest Jews. The Rebbe Rashab heard this and responded: "Indeed, this is the order of descent through levels from above to below."
