# R' Yaakov Leib Jacobson (ר' יעקב ליב דזייקאבסאן)

**Shochet, mohel, communal leader in Zuravichy; father of memoir author Israel Jacobson.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | ~5630 (1870) |
| **Origin/Residence** | Zuravichy (זוראוויץ), Belarus |
| **Role** | Shochet, mohel, communal leader; 4th generation shochet in Zuravichy |
| **Father** | R' Avraham Shlomo Jacobson (shochet, follower of Tzemach Tzedek and Maharash) |
| **Wife** | Frieda Malka, daughter of R' Avraham Nachum of Alkav |
| **Emigrated** | Before Purim 5683 (1923) to the USA |

## Overview

R' Yaakov Leib was the fourth in a lineage of four generations of shochtim in Zuravichy. Beyond his formal religious role, he was a central civic figure: he built the new synagogue in Zuravichy, and separately built a mikveh and bathhouse (the only stone building in town). He established and grew a gemach (free loan society) through the JCA (Jewish Colonization Association). During WWI he organized cooperative shops for the poor. He served as a referee and arbitrator in disputes, and was a certified expert mohel for the entire region.

When it came to his son Israel's education, he traveled personally with him to scout between Yeshivas Reines in Lida and Tomchei Temimim in Lubavitch, ultimately choosing the latter after chance encounters on the train and at a train station.

He emigrated to America before Purim 5683 (1923) with his wife, sons Shlomo Tzvi, Nissan, and Avraham Yosef, and daughter Rivka Leah. The emotional departure from Zuravichy, where his family had lived for four generations, is movingly described in chapter 20 of the memoirs.

In America, he found it difficult to adapt — going from being a respected expert mohel and arbiter in his hometown to working in the NY slaughterhouses under new management, causing him great suffering.
