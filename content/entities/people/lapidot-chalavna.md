# Lapidot Chalavna

A Chabad Chassid and settler in [[hebron|Hebron]] in the 19th century. The word "Chalavna" (חלבנא) refers to a dairy/milk seller, suggesting his occupation. *Toldos Chabad beEretz HaKodesh* notes that there were **two men with the name Lapidot Chalavna** in the Hebron Chabad community, reflecting the common practice of giving the same nickname to men in the same family or trade.

## Identity and Period

Both men named Lapidot Chalavna were part of the Chabad community in Hebron during the period when Hebron served as the primary Chabad center in the Holy Land (from 5581/1821 onward, following the [[mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]]'s directive to relocate from Safed and Tiberias to Hebron).

## Significance

The Lapidot Chalavna family represents the broader category of Chabad artisans and tradespeople who settled in Hebron — not just scholars or rabbis, but ordinary Jews who maintained religious life while earning a livelihood. Their presence indicates that the Hebron Chabad community was socially diverse.

## 1837 Earthquake Context

The great earthquake of 5597 (1837) struck the Galilee primarily (Safed and Tiberias suffered most), but the entire Eretz Yisrael community was affected. Chabad families who remained in Safed after most had relocated to Hebron suffered casualties; the Lapidot families were in Hebron and may have been less directly affected.
