# Rabbi Yehuda Leib Segal of Vitebsk

**Prominent Vitebsk communal notable; father-in-law of the Alter Rebbe**

## Biography

Rabbi Yehuda Leib Segal was one of the most respected Jewish communal leaders in Vitebsk during the second half of the eighteenth century. He belonged to the distinguished Segal (a Levitic designation, short for Segan Levi'ah) family, a family of standing within White Russian Jewry.

His most historically significant role was as the father of Rebbetzin Sterna, who became the wife of Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi — the founder of Chabad Chassidus. Through this match, Rabbi Yehuda Leib became the father-in-law of the Alter Rebbe, and his family became intertwined with the leadership of the nascent Chabad movement.

Rabbi Yehuda Leib's family connections in Vitebsk were broad and influential. His nephew or close relative Rabbi Yisrael Kissin served as the official Rav of Vitebsk and was himself a student of the Vilna Gaon, placing Rabbi Yehuda Leib's family at the intersection of both the Mitnagdic rabbinic establishment and the Chassidic world through his son-in-law. This dual connection created both social opportunities and social tensions for the extended family.

The Vitebsk community to which Rabbi Yehuda Leib belonged was a major Jewish center in the Russian Empire, with an active communal life, established kehila (community) institutions, and ongoing tensions between traditional rabbinic leadership and the rising Chassidic movement. That the Alter Rebbe's wife came from this milieu suggests that the marriage brought the young Shneur Zalman into a socially prominent and well-connected family, lending his subsequent religious leadership additional legitimacy in the eyes of White Russian Jewry.

## Significance

Rabbi Yehuda Leib Segal's importance lies primarily in his family role: as father-in-law to the Alter Rebbe, he represents the established Vitebsk Jewish society that the Alter Rebbe entered through marriage. His family's connections to both the traditional rabbinic world (through the Kissin relatives) and the Chassidic court (through his daughter) illustrate the complex social landscape in which early Chabad operated. The Vitebsk Segal family's networks helped shape the Alter Rebbe's geographic and social reach during the formative years of Chabad.
