# Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka Schneerson

**Born**: 25 Adar 5661 (March 16, 1901), Babinovitch, Russia  
**Died**: 22 Shevat 5748 (February 13, 1988), Brooklyn, New York  
**Role**: Wife of the seventh Rebbe; daughter of the Rayatz

---

## Overview

Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka Schneerson — universally called by her childhood nickname "Moussia" — was the daughter of [[entities/people/rayatz|Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn]] (the sixth Rebbe) and wife of [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson]] (the seventh Rebbe). She played a pivotal role in Chabad history not through public leadership but through decades of devoted personal support for her husband and quiet, dignified presence at the center of Chabad life.

---

## Early Life

Chaya Mushka was born to the Rayatz and [[entities/people/nechama-dina|Nechama Dina]] in 1901 in Babinovitch. She grew up in Lubavitch amid the royal atmosphere of the Rebbe's court, absorbing the values and culture of the Chabad dynasty. As the Rayatz's daughter — and eventually the one who would marry the next Rebbe — she occupied a central place in the dynasty.

She received a thorough Jewish education uncommon for women of her era, becoming deeply learned in both Chassidic teachings and broader Torah literature.

---

## Marriage

The wedding of Chaya Mushka to Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson took place in Warsaw on **14 Kislev 5689 (November 27, 1928)**. The Rayatz invested enormous significance in this match, recognizing in his son-in-law qualities that would make him the next Rebbe. The wedding was attended by thousands and celebrated throughout the Chabad world.

See [[events/chaya-mushka-wedding-1928|Chaya Mushka's Wedding (1928)]] for a full account.

After the wedding, the couple moved to Berlin where Rabbi Menachem Mendel pursued studies at the University of Berlin. They later moved to Paris.

---

## Escape from Europe

As Nazi Germany occupied France in 1940, the couple fled Europe — arriving in the United States in June 1941. The Rebbetzin joined her husband as he took on increasingly central roles in Chabad under the Rayatz.

---

## Life at 770

After Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson became Rebbe in 1951, Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka led a deliberately private life. She was known for her sharp intelligence, wit, warmth toward individuals, and fierce protection of her husband's time and health. She managed their household affairs and served as an anchor for the Rebbe through the enormous pressures of his leadership.

The couple had no children — a source of private sorrow. The Rebbetzin channeled her maternal energy into care for others.

---

## Passing and Legacy

Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka passed away on 22 Shevat 5748 (February 13, 1988). The Rebbe was devastated by her loss and spoke of her repeatedly in subsequent years with deep emotion. He established institutions in her memory, and her yahrtzeit (22 Shevat) became a significant day in the Chabad calendar.

After her passing, the Rebbe's public activities intensified dramatically — as if he was accelerating his mission without the anchor of her presence.
