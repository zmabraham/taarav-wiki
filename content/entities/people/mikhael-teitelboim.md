# Mikhael Teitelboim

A close companion and fellow Chabad chassid of R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin, particularly during the Samarkand years. He was the driving force behind establishing the underground Tomchei Temimim yeshiva in Samarkand, and was present to greet the author at 770 when he first arrived in New York in 5722 (1962).

## Description
During the early Samarkand period (5702/1941), Mikhael Teitelboim took the initiative to organize an underground Tomchei Temimim with three orphaned students: Sholom Levertov and Zelig Katzman (whose parents had just died on the streets of Samarkand), and Yeishke (Yehoshua) Dobravsky (son of Manie Dobravsky). He approached the author to take the students for one day a week each, feeding them and learning with them. Mikhael would also host them.

At a Tu B'Shvat 5702 farbrengen at R. Yehoshua Karf's home, Mikhael spoke about the need to arouse mercy for anash and for all Israel.

The memoir's final chapter notes that when the author arrived at 770 for the first time, Mikhael came to greet him and took him to his home with his luggage.
