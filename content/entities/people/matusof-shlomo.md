# Rabbi Shlomo Matusof

**Born**: 1910, Russia  
**Died**: 1984  
**Role**: Chabad shliach to Morocco; founder of Chabad in North Africa

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shlomo Matusof was one of the most successful shluchim of the early [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]] era. Sent to Morocco, he built an extensive network of Chabad schools and institutions across the country that preserved Jewish education and identity during a critical and tumultuous period in North African Jewish history.

---

## Early Life and Escape from Russia

Born in Russia in 1910, Matusof grew up under Soviet anti-religious persecution. He escaped the Soviet Union and came under the guidance of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (sixth Rebbe) and subsequently the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]], who directed him to Morocco.

---

## The Morocco Mission

Morocco in the mid-twentieth century was home to one of the largest Jewish communities in the Muslim world, numbering in the hundreds of thousands. However, this ancient community faced enormous challenges: poverty, limited access to Jewish education, pressure from both French colonial culture and Arab nationalism, and the looming question of mass emigration to Israel.

The Rebbe recognized that Morocco's Jews needed immediate educational infrastructure to preserve their Jewish identity and connection to Torah, regardless of whether they remained in Morocco or emigrated.

---

## Building the Network

Matusof established Chabad schools and institutions across multiple Moroccan cities:

- **Casablanca** — the largest Jewish community in Morocco
- **Rabat** — the capital
- **Marrakech** — a historic Jewish center
- **Fes** — home to one of Morocco's oldest Jewish communities

These schools provided Jewish education to thousands of children who might otherwise have received little or no Torah instruction. The network combined traditional Jewish learning with modern education, preparing students for life in Morocco or for emigration.

---

## Historical Significance

Matusof's work in Morocco represents one of the most important chapters in the global shlichus story. At a time when North African Jewry was undergoing a massive demographic upheaval — with hundreds of thousands emigrating to Israel, France, and elsewhere — Matusof's schools ensured that the last generation of Moroccan Jews received a strong Jewish education. Many graduates carried their Chabad education with them to Israel and France, seeding Chabad communities in those countries.
