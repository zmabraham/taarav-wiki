# Rabbi Yitzchak Kaduri

Rabbi Yitzchak Kaduri (16 Tishrei 5659 – 29 Tevet 5766 / 1898–2006), known as "Zaken HaMekubalim" (Elder of Kabbalists) in his generation, served as head of the Kabbalistic yeshiva "Yeshivat Nachalat Yitzchak" in Jerusalem and its rabbinical court.

## Biography

Born in Baghdad on the second day of Sukkot — the day of the ushpizin of Yitzchak Avinu — he was named accordingly. His father, a spice merchant, sent him to study at the local "Midrash Beit Zalcha." As a child he encountered the Ben Ish Chai (Rabbi Yosef Chaim of Baghdad), who was deeply impressed by him. By age 17 he was proficient in large portions of the Talmud.

In 5682 (1922) he emigrated to Eretz Yisrael and entered the kabbalistic yeshiva "Porat Yosef" in the Old City of Jerusalem. When the Old City fell in the War of Independence, he moved to the yeshiva "Beit El," working as a bookbinder for his livelihood. He later founded his own kabbalistic yeshiva "Nachalat Yitzchak" (with his son Rabbi David Kaduri) following the tradition of the Ari and the Rashash.

He was known for blessing visitors with verses about peace, and daily reviewed the newspaper to find names of sick people to pray for. He gave away large sums to charity and various institutions, typically redirecting donors to give to others before they donated to him.

He passed away on Saturday night, 29 Tevet 5766, at 108 years old. His funeral drew hundreds of thousands from all backgrounds, constituting a massive Kiddush Hashem.

## Connection to the Rebbe

Rabbi Kaduri visited the Rebbe in the dollars-distribution line with his children and grandchildren, informing the Rebbe about his yeshiva and mikveh project. The Rebbe blessed him that the building would be a preparation for the Third Temple, and added that his name "Kaduri" (from "kadur," a sphere/globe) hints that his light influences the entire globe. The Rebbe blessed him to see Mashiach's coming — "not only according to Kabbalah but also according to revealed Torah."

In early Av 5743 (1983), he jointly published (with Rabbi Yisrael Abuchatzeira — the Baba Sali) a proclamation against those who attacked Chassidim teaching the Tanya in Williamsburg, defending "the Torah of the Baal Shem Tov and the Alter Rebbe of Lubavitch."
