# Rabbi Chaim Chaykl of Hamdura (ר' חיים חייקל מהמדורא)

**Chassidic master and disciple of the Maggid of Mezeritch; an independent leader of Belarusian Chassidim; his son later came under the Alter Rebbe's leadership**

## Biography

Rabbi Chaim Chaykl of Hamdura (also spelled Amdur or Indura — a town in present-day western Belarus, near Grodno) was a major disciple of Rabbi Dov Ber of Mezeritch, the Maggid and primary successor to the Baal Shem Tov. After the Maggid's death in תקל"ג (1772/3), Rabbi Chaim Chaykl emerged as one of the independent chassidic leaders in the White Russian-Lithuanian borderlands.

The Hamdura (Indura) chassidic tradition was known for its combination of fervent prayer and social engagement. Rabbi Chaim Chaykl attracted a significant following in the Grodno region, establishing Hamdura as a notable chassidic center alongside — but independent of — the emerging Chabad movement of the Alter Rebbe to the east.

Rabbi Chaim Chaykl died in the winter of תקמ"ז (1787), a significant date in the history of White Russian Chassidism. The sources in Toldos Chabad BeRussia note his death as part of the narrative of how various independent chassidic followings eventually came under greater Chabad influence. Following his father's death, Rabbi Chaim Chaykl's son approached the Alter Rebbe and submitted to his authority, acknowledging the Alter Rebbe as the preeminent leader of White Russian Chassidism. This transition reflected the growing consolidation of Chabad's influence in the region during the late 1780s and 1790s.

The Hamdura town and community represent a type of small but spiritually vibrant chassidic center that characterized the landscape of White Russian Jewry in the late eighteenth century — dozens of such communities, each with its own rebbe, creating a rich but decentralized Chassidic world.

## Significance

Rabbi Chaim Chaykl's life and death illustrate the process by which independent chassidic lineages gradually came under Chabad's umbrella in White Russia. His death in 1787 and his son's subsequent submission to the Alter Rebbe is a data point in tracing how the Alter Rebbe came to exercise increasingly broad authority, even beyond the Chabad circle proper. It also illustrates the respect that non-Chabad Chassidic leaders had for the Alter Rebbe's standing during his own lifetime.
