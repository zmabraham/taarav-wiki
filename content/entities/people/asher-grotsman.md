# R' Asher Grotsman of Nikolaev

**Chabad shochet, mashpia, and scholar in Nikolaev (southern Ukraine); proofreader of the 1900 Vilna Tanya edition**

## Biography

R' Asher Grotsman served as shochet (ritual slaughterer) and mashpia (spiritual mentor) in Nikolaev, a major port city on the southern Ukrainian steppe near the Black Sea. He was a disciple of R' Baruch Tamrish, a prominent Chabad mashpia in Kremenchug. Under R' Baruch's guidance, R' Asher developed into a significant figure in the Chabad community of southern Russia.

His most historically notable contribution came around 1900, when he served as a proofreader for the Vilna edition of the Tanya — the foundational text of Chabad Chassidus authored by the Alter Rebbe. The 1900 Vilna printing was one of the important early 20th-century editions that helped standardize the text of the Tanya. R' Asher's careful scholarship in this role demonstrates the high level of Torah learning maintained by Chabad shochetim, who in many communities served simultaneously as teachers, scholars, and mashpi'im.

R' Asher was part of the broader Nikolaev Chabad community that also included R' Avraham David Lavot, the city's rabbi and author of the halachic work Kav Naki.

## Significance

R' Asher Grotsman represents the important but often overlooked stratum of Chabad society — the learned shochet-mashpia who combined practical communal service with deep Chassidic scholarship. His work on the Tanya text connects the local Nikolaev community to the central literary heritage of Chabad. His lineage from R' Baruch Tamrish of Kremenchug illustrates the transmission chain of Chabad maspi'im in southern Russia.
