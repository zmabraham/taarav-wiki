# Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik Safrin of Komarno

Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik Yehuda Yechiel Michel Safrin (25 Shevat 5566 – 10 Iyar 5634 / 1806–1874) was the second Rebbe of the Komarno dynasty and the central figure in Komarno Chassidic history. An extraordinary Kabbalist and prolific author, he wrote extensive commentaries on the Torah and Zohar combining the teachings of the Ari and the Baal Shem Tov.

## Biography

Born in Komarno. Orphaned from his father at age 12, he was raised by his uncle Rabbi Tzvi Hirsh Eichenstein of Zhidachov. His teachers included the Seer of Lublin, Rabbi Naftali Tzvi of Ropshitz, Rabbi Avraham Mordechai of Pintshov (his father-in-law), Rabbi Moshe of Uhel (Yismach Moshe), and others. He became one of the most prolific Chassidic writers, viewing his life's mission as composing books that integrated Lurianic Kabbalah with Baal Shem Tov teachings.

## Relationship with Chabad

Rabbi Yitzchak Eizik maintained mixed relations with Chabad. On the positive side: in his work "Ketam Ofir" on Megillat Esther he wrote that he had seen the tefillin boxes of the Alter Rebbe (in a dream vision) and described the Alter Rebbe as "mighty warrior, lamp of Israel who illuminates the land." In his "Shulchan HaTahor" he called the Alter Rebbe "mighty warrior, lamp of Israel." However, when the Rebbe Rashab obtained his works and studied them daily, he stopped when he reached places where the Komarno Rebbe disputed the Alter Rebbe — and put the books away permanently.

The Tzemach Tzedek said of him: "He revealed secrets from the inner chamber." When the first volume of Heichal HaBracha was published, the Tzemach Tzedek studied it continuously from start to finish and declared: "I did not know there was still another person in this generation so expert in Kabbalah." The book never left the Tzemach Tzedek's table for the rest of his life.

The Frierdiker Rebbe told his grandson Rabbi Chaim Yaakov Safrin of Komarno: this was the Tzemach Tzedek's reaction to Heichal HaBracha.

## Works
- **Heichal HaBracha** — Kabbalistic-Chassidic commentary on the five books of Torah
- **Zohar Chai** — commentary on the Zohar (7 vols.)
- **Megilat Setarim** — mystical diary with visions and stories
- **Notzer Chesed** — commentary on Pirkei Avot
- **Shulchan HaTahor** — on Shulchan Aruch
