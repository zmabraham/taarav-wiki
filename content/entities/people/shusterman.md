# M. Shusterman

**Born**: 1 Elul 5674 (1914), Zlobin, Belarus  
**Died**: c. 1980s–1990s, USA  
**Role**: Underground yeshiva student; Soviet-era survivor; printer of Chabad publications at 770 Eastern Parkway; author of _Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu_

---

## Overview

M. Shusterman's life arc — from Soviet-era underground yeshiva student through WWII survival in Samarkand, escape to Paris, and final settlement in America as a printer of Chabad texts — represents one of the most complete trajectories of the generation born in revolutionary Russia who rebuilt Chabad in the West.

---

## Zlobin and Historical Context

Shusterman was born in Zlobin — a town between Gomel and Bobruisk in Belarus with significant Chabad historical resonance. A famous wedding of the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe's]] daughter to a grandson of R' Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev had taken place there — an event that anchors Shusterman's memoir in Chabad dynastic history from its opening pages.

---

## Underground Yeshiva

Shusterman studied in the clandestine Tomchei Temimim network, moving through:

- **Vitebsk** — Chabad's historical heartland
- **Mogilev** — significant Jewish city in Belarus
- **Kherson** — the southern yeshiva center on the Black Sea

The experience of constant movement, forged documents, and the ever-present GPU threat was shared by an entire generation of underground students. See also [[entities/people/yisrael-yehuda-levi|Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levi's]] parallel memoir.

---

## World War II: Moscow and Samarkand

When Germany invaded in 1941, Shusterman was in Moscow and later fled to Samarkand — one of the Central Asian cities that became a refuge for Chabad chassidim fleeing the Nazi advance into Soviet territory.

---

## Escape and Paris (1947)

After the war, Shusterman used the strategy employed by many Chabad chassidim: claiming Polish citizenship to join the repatriation movement. He reached **Paris in 1947** — one of a significant number of Chabad refugees who gathered in post-war Paris before further emigration decisions.

---

## Work in America: Printing the Tanya

In America, Shusterman found his calling as a **Linotype typesetter** for Chabad publications at **770 Eastern Parkway** (Chabad's world headquarters in Brooklyn). His most notable achievement was participating in the printing of **the Tanya** — the Alter Rebbe's foundational Chassidic masterwork, central to all Chabad study and life.

That a man who had studied the Tanya underground in Soviet Russia now physically set its type in Brooklyn represented a powerful completion of a life's journey.

---

## The Memoirs

Shusterman wrote his memoirs — [[summaries/lemaan-yedau|*Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu*]] — as both personal testimony and legacy for future generations. The title (from Psalms 78:6) means "So that children yet to be born will know."
