# Rabbi Elazar Abuchatzeira (Baba Elazar)

Sephardic rabbi, kabbalist, and miracle worker from the Abuchatzeira dynasty. Grandson of the Baba Sali (Rabbi Yisrael Abuchatzeira) and son of Rabbi Meir Abuchatzeira (Baba Meir). Murdered by a supplicant in 2011.

## Biography

Born in Rissani in the Tafilalt region of Morocco to a family of revered Moroccan Kabbalists. His grandfather the Baba Sali and his father Rabbi Meir oversaw Chabad institutions in Erfoud together. Rabbi Elazar studied at Chabad yeshiva in Erfoud (his name appears in the student lists published in the book on the history of Chabad in Morocco).

In 5723 (1963) he studied for about a year at the Chabad yeshiva in Casablanca. The shaliach and Rosh Yeshiva Rabbi Shalom Eidelman recalled: "Rabbi Meir Abuchatzeira sent me a special letter with instructions on how to educate his son."

In 5726 (1966) he immigrated to Israel with his family and settled in Ashdod.

After the passing of the Baba Sali, he assumed leadership in Be'er Sheva, living in a house connected by a tunnel to the synagogue. In his later decades he rarely left his home, receiving thousands of supplicants for blessings and counsel.

### Murder
On the night of 27 Tammuz 5771, Rabbi Elazar was stabbed to death by a man who had been coming to him for blessings and counsel while he was reading a petition during an audience.

His funeral procession started from his Beit Midrash in Be'er Sheva, then went to Yeshivat Porat Yosef in Jerusalem, and he was buried on the Mount of Olives. Hundreds of thousands attended. At the funeral, his eldest son Rabbi Pinchas was crowned as his successor.

## Chabad Connection

Rabbi Elazar had a profound connection to the Lubavitcher Rebbe. He frequently said "He is the Holy of Holies" about the Rebbe and spoke of him continuously. He would conduct a festive meal on 19 Kislev (the festival of the Alter Rebbe's liberation). He reportedly knew the entire book of Tanya by heart.

He possessed a special shechita knife that the Rebbe had sent to his father Rabbi Meir when he was still in Morocco. Each eve of Passover he would take it from its special cabinet and show it to his family while extolling the Rebbe.

He visited the Ohel (grave) of the Frierdiker Rebbe in New York in 2010, spending about an hour there.

He regularly said: "Only thanks to Chabad was Moroccan Jewry saved."
