# Vilna Gaon (Rabbi Elijah ben Shlomo Zalman)

## Overview
Rabbi Elijah ben Shlomo Zalman of Vilna (1720–1797), known as the Vilna Gaon or HaGra, was the leading Torah scholar of his generation and the foremost opponent of the Chassidic movement. His opposition to Chassidism formed the ideological basis for the Mitnagdim's persecution of the Alter Rebbe and other Chassidic leaders.

## Details
The Vilna Gaon issued a formal ban against the Chassidim in 5532 (1772), which was cited by subsequent Mitnagdim as justification for their attacks on the Alter Rebbe and his community. The Alter Rebbe himself references this, writing in 5565 (1805): "all the troubles that befell us in the year 5559 [1798-1799] were based on the signature of the Gaon of Vilna from the year 5532."

The Alter Rebbe attempted to meet with the Vilna Gaon on at least two occasions to discuss and clarify points of theological difference, but the Gaon refused to receive him.

The Gaon's refusal to hear the Alter Rebbe's explanations contributed to the escalating conflict, as his opposition gave the Mitnagdim of Shklov, Mohilev, and Vilna religious license to pursue the Chassidim through the Russian government.
