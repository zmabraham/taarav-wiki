# Ibrahim Pasha — איבראהים פאשה

**Born**: 1789  
**Died**: 1848  
**Role**: Egyptian military commander; governor of Greater Syria (including Eretz Yisrael) 1831–1840  
**Significance for Chabad**: His rule over Eretz Yisrael coincided with the [[events/hebron-pogrom-1834|Hebron Pogrom of 1834]]

---

## Overview

Ibrahim Pasha was the son of Muhammad Ali, the Ottoman viceroy of Egypt, and served as commander of the Egyptian forces that conquered and governed Greater Syria (including Palestine/Eretz Yisrael) from 1831 to 1840. His rule was a period of political upheaval that directly affected the Jewish communities in the Holy Land, including the Chabad community in Hebron.

---

## Rule Over Eretz Yisrael

Ibrahim Pasha conquered Greater Syria from Ottoman control in 1831 and governed the region with a modernizing agenda, reducing some traditional religious privileges while introducing conscription and other reforms that generated widespread resentment among both Arab and Jewish populations.

In **1834**, a large-scale Arab revolt broke out against Ibrahim Pasha's rule (the "Arab Revolt of 1834"). The rebels targeted Jewish communities in Hebron, Tzfat (Safed), and other cities. The [[events/hebron-pogrom-1834|Hebron Pogrom of 1834]] — in which the Chabad and Sephardic communities suffered devastating losses — occurred within this context of regional violence.

Ibrahim Pasha's forces eventually suppressed the revolt, but not before serious damage to Jewish life in Hebron.

---

## Significance for Chabad History

The political instability of Ibrahim Pasha's rule frames the violent backdrop against which the early Chabad community in Hebron struggled to establish itself. The 1834 pogrom nearly destroyed the young Hebron Chabad community that had been founded just over a decade earlier. Recovery required decades and significant external support from [[entities/people/moses-montefiore|Sir Moses Montefiore]] and others.
