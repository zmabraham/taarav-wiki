# Rabbi Yehuda Krinsky

**Born**: 1933, Boston, Massachusetts  
**Role**: Personal secretary to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]; chairman of Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch and Machne Israel; one of the primary institutional leaders of Chabad-Lubavitch after 1994

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yehuda Krinsky served as a personal secretary and close aide to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] for over forty years. After the Rebbe's passing in 1994, Krinsky emerged as one of the most important institutional leaders of the global Chabad-Lubavitch movement, serving as chairman of both Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch (the educational arm) and Machne Israel (the social services arm).

His career arc — from young Boston-born chassid drawn to the Rebbe's court to senior institutional leader of a worldwide movement — mirrors Chabad's own transformation from an insular Hasidic group to a global Jewish organization.

---

## Early Life and Connection to the Rebbe

Krinsky was born and raised in Boston in a Chabad-affiliated family. As a young man, he was drawn to 770 Eastern Parkway in Brooklyn and became closely attached to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]]. His devotion, reliability, and organizational competence were recognized early, and he was brought into the Rebbe's inner circle as a personal secretary.

---

## Secretary to the Rebbe

As one of the Rebbe's primary secretaries (along with [[entities/people/groner-leibel|Rabbi Leibel Groner]]), Krinsky handled a wide range of responsibilities:

- Managing the Rebbe's vast correspondence
- Coordinating audiences (yechidus) and public events
- Serving as a public liaison and spokesperson
- Driving the Rebbe to and from the Ohel (the resting place of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]) — these regular visits were a central part of the Rebbe's spiritual practice
- Accompanying the Rebbe on public occasions

Krinsky was known for his public-facing role — he often served as the voice of the Rebbe's office to the media, government officials, and the broader Jewish community.

---

## After the Rebbe's Passing (1994)

The Rebbe's death on 3 Tammuz 5754 (June 12, 1994) created an unprecedented leadership vacuum. No successor was appointed, and Chabad's institutional continuity depended on the administrative structures the Rebbe had built during his lifetime.

Krinsky stepped into a leading institutional role:

**Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch**: As chairman, he oversees the educational operations of Chabad worldwide — including the coordination of thousands of Chabad Houses, schools, and programs.

**Machne Israel**: As chairman, he directs the social services, outreach, and community welfare operations.

**Kehot Publication Society**: Continues the publication of the Rebbe's teachings and Chassidic literature.

---

## Institutional Leadership

In the decades since 1994, Krinsky has been one of the key figures maintaining Chabad's organizational coherence and growth. The movement has continued to expand dramatically — from approximately 2,000 emissary couples at the time of the Rebbe's passing to over 6,000 today — and Krinsky's institutional leadership has been part of this continuity.

He has navigated complex questions of authority, jurisdiction, and direction in a movement that lost its charismatic leader but not its institutional momentum.
