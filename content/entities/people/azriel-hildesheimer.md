# Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer

**Born**: May 11, 1820, Halberstadt, Germany  
**Died**: July 12, 1899, Berlin, Germany  
**Role**: Founder of the Neo-Orthodox "Adass Jisroel" Berlin congregation; founder of the Berlin Rabbinical Seminary; first significant German Orthodox contact of the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]]

---

## Overview

Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer was the preeminent Orthodox rabbi of late nineteenth-century Germany — the founder of the Adass Jisroel congregation in Berlin and the Berlin Rabbinical Seminary (Rabbinerseminar), the first Orthodox rabbinical seminary in Germany. He represented a form of "Torah Im Derech Eretz" Orthodoxy that combined strict halachic observance with secular academic training and engagement with modernity.

---

## The Adass Jisroel and Berlin Rabbinical Seminary

Hildesheimer founded the **Adass Jisroel** (Community of Israel) congregation in Berlin in 1869 as an Orthodox alternative to the dominant Reform and Liberal Jewish movements. It was a separationist Orthodox community that refused compromise with religious reform.

His **Berlin Rabbinical Seminary** (founded 1873) was the first institution to train Orthodox rabbis in Germany using modern academic methods alongside traditional Torah study. It served as a model for combining rigorous Orthodox education with modern scholarship.

---

## Contact with the Rashab (1885)

In 1885, the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] (Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn) visited Berlin at age 24 and called on Rabbi Hildesheimer. This was the Rashab's first visit to Berlin and his first significant engagement with German Orthodox Jewry.

Hildesheimer received the young Rashab with great respect — honoring the memory of the Rebbe Maharash (the Rashab's father) through his son. This meeting established the Rashab's relationship with German Orthodox Jewry and opened the channel through which Chabad would engage with German Jewish communities over the following decades.

See [[events/wurzburg-year-1906|Würzburg Year (1906)]] for how this relationship later deepened.

---

## Significance

The Hildesheimer-Rashab meeting represents a significant moment of contact between Eastern European Chassidic leadership and Western Orthodox Judaism. Despite their different worlds — the Rashab coming from the mystical, enclosed Chabad tradition of Russia; Hildesheimer representing modern, academic German Orthodoxy — they found common ground in their commitment to halachah and their opposition to Reform Judaism.

Hildesheimer's son, **Rabbi Meir Hildesheimer**, continued the relationship with the Rashab in subsequent years.
