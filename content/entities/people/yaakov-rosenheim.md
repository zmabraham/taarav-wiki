# Rabbi Yaakov Rosenheim

**Born**: February 12, 1870, Frankfurt am Main, Germany  
**Died**: June 5, 1965, New York, USA  
**Role**: Founder and president of Agudas Yisrael; collaborated with [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] on German Orthodox organization

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yaakov Rosenheim was the founder and long-serving president of **Agudas Yisrael** — the major Orthodox political and religious organization that sought to unite Torah-observant Jews worldwide in opposition to both Zionism and Reform Judaism. He was one of the most significant Orthodox organizational figures of the twentieth century, and a key collaborator with the Chabad leadership.

---

## Agudas Yisrael

Rosenheim founded Agudas Yisrael in 1912 as an international Orthodox organization. Its founding represented the Orthodox world's attempt to create an institutional counterweight to the World Zionist Organization (founded by Herzl in 1897). Agudas Yisrael's position was that Jewish national aspirations must be grounded in Torah — not secular nationalist ideology.

The organization brought together Chassidic rebbes (including Chabad leadership), Lithuanian Torah giants, and German Orthodox leaders under one umbrella — a remarkable achievement given the substantial differences between these communities.

---

## Connection to the Rashab

During the [[events/wurzburg-year-1906|Rashab's year in Würzburg (1906–1907)]], Rosenheim collaborated with the Rashab and [[entities/people/shlomo-zalman-breuer|Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Breuer]] to draft the _"HiTaadus HaYereim"_ organizational plan for German Orthodox Jewry.

The Rashab's correspondence documented in [[summaries/chabad-germany|the Germany volume]] includes sustained exchange with Rosenheim about supporting Russian Jewry through Agudas Yisrael's channels, and about the proper Orthodox response to the challenges of modernity.

---

## Legacy

Rosenheim lived to the age of 95, dying in New York in 1965 — having witnessed the rise of Nazism, the Holocaust, the creation of the State of Israel (which Agudas Yisrael initially opposed but eventually engaged with), and the postwar rebuilding of Orthodox life. His longevity allowed him to bridge the pre-war Orthodox world and the postwar Orthodox revival.
