# Rabbi Shraga Feivel Mendlowitz — ר' שרגא פייוול מענדלאוויטש

**Born**: 1886, Hungary  
**Died**: 1948  
**Role**: Founder and principal of Mesivta Torah Vodaath (Brooklyn); architect of American Torah education  
**Significance for Chabad**: Represented one of the established American Orthodox educational approaches when the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] arrived in America in 1940

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shraga Feivel Mendlowitz was the driving force behind **Mesivta Torah Vodaath** — one of the first and most influential yeshivas in America. Often called "Mr. Mendlowitz" (his preferred title), he transformed American Orthodox education, pioneering the high school yeshiva (mesivta) model and emphasizing mussar and Chassidic thought alongside Talmud.

---

## American Orthodox Landscape

When the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] arrived in America in 1940, the _Undaunted_ biography describes the American religious landscape as including Torah Vodaath (under Mendlowitz) and Yeshiva University (under [[entities/people/bernard-revel|Rabbi Bernard Revel]]) as the two major existing Orthodox educational institutions. The Rayatz's strategy of transforming American Jewry necessarily had to contend with and complement these existing institutions.

Mendlowitz himself was broadly sympathetic to Chassidic thought — he was one of the rare American Orthodox educators who taught Tanya (the Chabad foundational text) and incorporated Chassidic spirituality into his curriculum. This gave him a natural affinity for Chabad's mission.

---

## Relationship to Chabad

While not a Chabad chassid, Mendlowitz's educational philosophy had significant overlap with Chabad's emphasis on internalized Jewish identity over mere academic achievement. He reportedly expressed respect for the Rayatz and the Chabad approach to Torah education.
