# Freydel Stein

The wife of [[chaim-stein-telz|R. Chaim Stein]], Mashgiach Ruchani of Telshe Yeshiva, and a key figure in rescuing the narrator's family from the post-war refugee limbo. Through her personal initiative and connections, she arranged a **"professor" visa** (permanent immigrant visa) for the narrator and his family, enabling their immigration to the United States.

## The Visa Rescue

When the narrator was in [[paris|Paris]] in summer 5707 (1947) with no clear path to legal immigration, Freydel Stein — using her husband's connection to Telshe Yeshiva — arranged a permanent immigrant visa (categorized under "professor" status, a provision sometimes available to religious scholars and educators). This visa enabled the narrator and his family to settle permanently in America in 5708 (1948).

## Her Role

In the memoir, the narrator credits Freydel Stein specifically: she is the one who acted, used her connections, and navigated the paperwork. Her husband R. Chaim Stein is named as the institutional connection (Telshe Yeshiva), but Freydel Stein is credited as the agent who made it happen.

## Significance

Freydel Stein's actions exemplify the informal rescue networks in the post-war Jewish world, where women often served as practical organizers, using personal relationships to navigate bureaucratic obstacles that formal institutions could not easily address. She ensured that the Telshe-Chabad connection was translated into a concrete life-saving act.
