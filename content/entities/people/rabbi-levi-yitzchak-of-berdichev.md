# Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berdichev

## Overview
Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berdichev was one of the greatest disciples of the Maggid of Mezeritch, renowned for his love of every Jew. He served as the chief rabbi of Berdichev (in Volhynia) for many years.

## Details
He served as chief rabbi of Pinsk from approximately 1775-1785, when the Mitnagdim expelled him, after which he moved to Berdichev in Volhynia.

In the debates between Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk and the Alter Rebbe regarding Chabad philosophy (after the publication of the Tanya in 1797), Rabbi Levi Yitzchak defended the Alter Rebbe. He wrote a strong letter to Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk arguing that the Alter Rebbe's approach was in fact in keeping with the teachings of the Maggid of Mezeritch.

This is significant because Rabbi Levi Yitzchak was not initially listed by Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk as one of the three designated leaders of Chassidim in Russia (the three were the Alter Rebbe, Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk, and Rabbi Yissachar Ber of Lubavitch), and yet he later became one of the Alter Rebbe's strongest defenders.
