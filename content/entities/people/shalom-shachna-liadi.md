# Rabbi Shalom Shachna of Liadi

**Son-in-law of the Alter Rebbe; involved in the first printing of the Tanya in Slavuta**

## Biography

Rabbi Shalom Shachna was the son-in-law of Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the Alter Rebbe and founder of Chabad Chassidus. Through his marriage into the Alter Rebbe's family, he occupied a position of both familial closeness and communal responsibility within the Chabad leadership circle.

His most documented historical contribution was his involvement in coordinating the first printing of the Tanya in Slavuta in תקנ"ז (1796/7). The publication of the Tanya was a major undertaking: the Alter Rebbe had circulated the text in manuscript form for years, and numerous imprecise or unauthorized copies had proliferated among Chabad communities. The decision to finally commit the work to print was intended to establish an authoritative, edited version and prevent the further spread of corrupted manuscripts.

Rabbi Shalom Shachna's role in the Slavuta printing connected the Alter Rebbe's household directly to the practical logistics of publishing. The Shapiro printing house in Slavuta — run by Rabbi Pinchas of Slavuta, grandson of the Baal Shem Tov — was one of the most prestigious Hebrew publishing houses in the region, and securing their involvement lent the printing additional authority and legitimacy.

Beyond the Tanya printing, Rabbi Shalom Shachna appears in the sources as part of the extended family network that helped sustain the Chabad movement during the Alter Rebbe's lifetime. The Alter Rebbe's household in Liadi was a major institutional center, receiving thousands of Chassidim for guidance, and family members like Rabbi Shalom Shachna shared in the practical management of that reality.

## Significance

Rabbi Shalom Shachna's involvement in the Tanya's first printing illustrates that the publication of this foundational Chabad text was a family and communal project, not a solo act of authorship. The cooperation of the Alter Rebbe's son-in-law in organizing the printing reflects the distributed nature of Chabad's early institutional operations, where the Rebbe's household, disciples, and family all contributed to the movement's practical functioning.
