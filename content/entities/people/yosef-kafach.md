# Rabbi Yosef Kafach

Rabbi Yosef Kafach (12 Kislev 5678 – 18 Tammuz 5760 / 1917–2000) was one of the greatest Yemenite rabbis in Israel, a dayan on the Supreme Rabbinical Court, a translator and editor of the Rambam's works and other Rishonim, and one of the foremost scholars of Maimonidean texts.

## Biography

Born in Sanaa, Yemen, to Rabbi David Kafach. Orphaned from his father at age one and from his mother at age five, he was raised in the home of his grandfather, Rabbi Yichya Kafach. At age 14 his grandfather passed away, and he took over teaching classes in the beit midrash.

He married his cousin Bracha at age 16 and worked as a goldsmith. In 5703 (1943) he emigrated to Israel and entered Merkaz HaRav yeshiva. In 5710 (1950) he received ordination for the rabbinate and began serving as a dayan. In 5730 (1970) he was appointed dayan of the Supreme Rabbinical Court and member of the Chief Rabbinical Council of Israel. Though offered the position of Chief Rabbi of Israel multiple times, he consistently declined.

He passed away on 18 Tammuz 5760 and was buried at Har HaMenuchot in Jerusalem.

## Scholarship: Maimonides Editions

His central life's project was producing a critical edition of the Rambam's Mishneh Torah based on accurate Yemenite manuscripts. According to Yemenite tradition, their community maintained close contact with Maimonides, periodically sending emissaries to have their texts corrected by the Rambam himself, meaning their manuscripts represent the most accurate final version.

He also edited and translated Peirush HaMishnayot (Commentary on the Mishnah), Moreh Nevuchim (Guide for the Perplexed), Sefer HaMitzvot, and other Rambam works.

## Relationship with the Rebbe

The Rebbe maintained an extensive written correspondence with Rabbi Kafach, with letters delivered by personal messenger rather than ordinary mail. Rabbi Kafach sent his books to the Rebbe, and the Rebbe reciprocated by sending all Rambam-related books published by Kehot.

In Elul 5744 (1984), the Rebbe wrote to him: "I was glad to hear that your honor is publishing the Yad HaChazakah according to manuscripts." As a contribution to the work, the Rebbe sent him the book "Marei Mekomot l'Sefer Mishneh Torah."

The Rebbe addressed the question of multiple Mishneh Torah manuscript versions in a discourse, affirming the value of Yemenite manuscripts while noting that halachic decisions should not be altered based on variant versions, since previous authorities relied on standard texts. He concluded by praising "those engaged in this holy work."

In Tishrei 5751 (1991), Rabbi Kafach requested an approbation from the Rebbe; the Rebbe declined (explaining he did not give approbations) but had a personal letter included in volume 6 of the edition.

Rabbi Kafach supported the Rebbe's positions on various matters including Jewish identity laws, Land of Israel, and the prohibition of wigs. He vigorously opposed the Oslo Accords, saying he would have established a second Tisha B'Av on the day of their signing.
