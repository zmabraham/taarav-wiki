# Ita Dvora Levin (Gorkow)

Wife of R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin (the memoir author); daughter of R. Meir Gorkow. She married the author in late winter 5698 (1938) in Kharkov. She passed away (indicated by the notation "ע"ה" — may her memory be blessed) at some point after the Samarkand years.

## Description
Ita Dvora was a devoted and courageous partner throughout the most difficult years described in the memoir. During the Samarkand period, she was the family's primary breadwinner: she went to the market daily to buy and resell goods to Uzbeks, often in freezing cold and rain. She also went to register at a factory to obtain knitting work, bringing home 600 grams of bread per day for the completed work.

When WWII began, she went to the police before fleeing Kharkov and obtained a joint identity card with a five-year military exemption for the author. This document was crucial until it was left behind during a military inspection in Samarkand.

In winter 5705 (1944-1945) she fell gravely ill with typhus but recovered. After her illness she was moved to her parents' home. The author notes prayers they made together for children after years of childlessness following the death of their first son.
