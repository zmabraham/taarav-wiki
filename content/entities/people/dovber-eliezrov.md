# Rabbi Dov Ber Eliezrov

Chabad rabbi who served as rabbi of the British Mandate detention camp at Latrun and later as rabbi of the Katamon neighborhood in Jerusalem.

## Biography

Born in Hebron to a distinguished Chabad family. His family had settled in Hebron following the immigration of the family matriarch Menucha Rachel Slonim. Educated from youth by his grandfather Rabbi Shlomo Yehuda Leib Eliezrov.

During World War I, his family fled to Egypt. After the war they settled in the Beit Yisrael neighborhood of Jerusalem. He studied at Talmud Torah Etz Chaim with Rabbi Isser Zalman Meltzer and received rabbinic ordination from Meltzer, Rabbi Avraham Yitzchak Kook, and Rabbi Shimshon Aaron Polansky. A classmate and lifelong friend was Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Auerbach.

He also served as Ram (instructor) at Yeshivat Torat Emet and as Rosh Yeshiva of the Radzin Yeshiva in Jerusalem.

### Meeting with the Frierdiker Rebbe (1929)
During the Frierdiker Rebbe's 1929 visit to the Holy Land, Eliezrov joined him and accompanied him during his visit to Hebron. In his diary from those days he recorded: "Despite the Rebbe being weak in health and his speech very quiet, the Hasidic discourses he delivered were... wondrous light."

### Latrun Detention Camp
During the British Mandate period, he was appointed (upon the advice of Chief Rabbi Ben Tzion Uziel) as rabbi of the Latrun detention camp, responsible for the religious needs of the Jewish detainees. He spent every Shabbat at the camp. His activities there are described in detail in the introduction to his book Sha'ali Tzion.

### Katamon
During the War of Independence his home in the Mea Shearim area was destroyed by shelling. He moved to the Katamon neighborhood, where refugees from the Old City welcomed him as their rabbi, a position he held until his death.

He was a member of the Chabad Rabbinical Court in the Land of Israel from approximately 5736 (1976).

He once asked the Rebbe whether to change his family name back to Kazernovsky; the Rebbe instructed him not to change it.

In his will he requested no eulogies, not at the funeral or afterward. He only permitted his tombstone to read: "He was the rabbi who visited the prisoners at Latrun."

## Works
- **Sha'ali Tzion** — responsa on topics arising from his service at the Latrun camp
- **Moreh BeHalacha** — laws of niddah and mikvaot
- **Dvar Tzion** — Torah commentary and essays on the festivals
