# Czar Alexander I of Russia

**Born**: December 23, 1777, Saint Petersburg  
**Died**: December 1, 1825, Taganrog  
**Role**: Russian Emperor during the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]'s final years and the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]]'s early leadership

---

## Overview

Czar Alexander I ruled the Russian Empire from 1801 to 1825, a period that encompassed the Napoleonic Wars, the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]'s death, and most of the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]]'s leadership. His reign was characterized by an initial period of relative liberalism that gradually gave way to conservatism and reaction.

---

## The Napoleonic War and Chabad

Alexander I led Russia's resistance to [[entities/people/napoleon|Napoleon]]'s 1812 invasion — the conflict in which the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] actively supported the Russian side. Russia's ultimate victory vindicated the Alter Rebbe's position, though the Alter Rebbe did not live to see Napoleon's final defeat.

---

## Jewish Policy

Alexander I's reign saw fluctuating Jewish policies:

- **Early reign (1801-1812)**: Relatively liberal measures, including the 1804 _Statute Concerning Jews_ that, while restrictive in some areas, also opened educational and economic opportunities
- **Later reign (1815-1825)**: Growing conservatism and mystical religiosity led to harsher attitudes toward minorities, including Jews

---

## The Mitteler Rebbe's Arrest

The [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]]'s arrest on 1 Tishrei 5586 (October 1, 1825) — prompted by denunciations from [[entities/people/simcha-kissin|Simcha Kissin]] — occurred in the final months of Alexander I's reign. Alexander died just two months later (December 1825), and his successor [[entities/people/nicholas-i|Nicholas I]] brought a dramatically harsher regime.
