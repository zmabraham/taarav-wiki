# Rabbi Yaakov ben Moshe Molin (Maharil)

Rabbi Yaakov Halevi ben Moshe Molin, known as the Maharil (also Mahari Segel), was the rabbi of Mainz and head of its yeshiva, a celebrated halachic decisor of the Rishonim era in Ashkenaz. Many halachic rulings and customs of Ashkenazic Jewry are founded upon him. Born in Mainz around 5120–5125 (c.1360–1365) and passed away 22 Elul 5187 (1427).

## Biography

Son of Rabbi Moshe Segel, grandson of Rabbi Yekutiel who was a great leader of the Rhine communities. A child prodigy, he married the daughter of Rabbi Moshe Neumerk, rabbi of Vienna. He studied in Wiener Neustadt under his main teacher Rabbi Shalom of Neustadt, and also under Rabbi Avraham Kloisner. In 5147 (1387) he assumed his father's position as rabbi of Mainz and established a large yeshiva there. Among his students was the Mahari Weil.

He was considered one of the Rishonim, with the title of "Gadol HaDor" (greatest of his generation). He was the pioneer of Ashkenazic custom (minhag), establishing or transmitting the liturgical customs of Ashkenazic prayer and synagogue practice. He and his teacher Rabbi Shalom of Vienna were the first to receive the honorific "Morenu" (our teacher). His rulings follow the Sefer HaAguda.

He passed away at 62 and was buried in Worms.

## Connection to Chabad

The Alter Rebbe's Shulchan Aruch cites the Maharil extensively, as many Ashkenazic customs come through him. The Tzemach Tzedek discusses his customs in his responsa. The custom of spitting during "Aleinu Leshabe'ach" originates with the Maharil. Many Chabad customs are grounded in the Maharil's practices.

The Rebbe cited him frequently, once noting: "The Maharil's Minhagim was recorded by his students... the Rebbe Rashab's tradition that 'the custom of our fathers is Torah' draws, among other sources, from the Maharil." The custom of the rabbi giving a public sermon on Shabbat HaGadol before Pesach also originates from the Maharil.
