# Senator James F. Byrnes

**Born**: 1882, Charleston, South Carolina  
**Died**: 1972  
**Role**: US Senator; Supreme Court Justice; Secretary of State; key figure in the rescue of the Rayatz from Nazi-occupied Poland

---

## Overview

James F. Byrnes was one of the most powerful American politicians of the mid-twentieth century, serving as a US Senator from South Carolina, Associate Justice of the Supreme Court, Secretary of State under President Truman, and Governor of South Carolina. In the context of Chabad history, he played a key role in the diplomatic effort to rescue the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe) from Nazi-occupied Warsaw in 1939-1940.

---

## Political Career

Byrnes's career spanned virtually every branch of the American government:

- **US Senator** from South Carolina (1931-1941)
- **Associate Justice, US Supreme Court** (1941-1942)
- **Director of War Mobilization** (1943-1945) — so powerful in this role he was called "Assistant President"
- **Secretary of State** (1945-1947)
- **Governor of South Carolina** (1951-1955)

---

## Role in the Rayatz's Rescue (1939-1940)

When the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] was trapped in Nazi-occupied Warsaw after the German invasion of Poland in September 1939, an extraordinary American diplomatic rescue effort was launched. This effort required intervention at the highest levels of the US government — including the State Department, the military, and Congress.

Senator Byrnes was one of several prominent American officials who used their influence to facilitate the Rayatz's extraction from occupied territory. The rescue involved complex negotiations with German military authorities and required American diplomatic pressure at a time when the United States was still officially neutral.

The Rayatz was eventually evacuated through Berlin and Riga, arriving in the United States on **9 Adar II 5700 (March 19, 1940)**, where he settled in Brooklyn and began rebuilding the Chabad movement in America.

---

## Significance for Chabad History

The involvement of figures like Byrnes in the Rayatz's rescue demonstrates the extraordinary network of political contacts that Chabad had cultivated in America, even before its center of gravity shifted to the United States. The rescue was not merely a humanitarian act — it preserved the leadership of the Chabad movement at a critical moment, enabling the Rayatz to continue directing the global Chabad network and eventually to pass the mantle to the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Seventh Rebbe]].
