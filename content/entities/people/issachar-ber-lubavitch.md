# Rabbi Issachar Ber of Lubavitch

**Co-appointed leader of Russian Chassidim alongside the Alter Rebbe; later submitted to the Alter Rebbe's sole authority**

## Biography

Rabbi Issachar Ber was a chassidic leader based in Lubavitch during the late eighteenth century. He occupies a significant but often overlooked place in early Chabad history as one of the figures jointly appointed by Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk (also known as R. Menachem Mendel of Horodok) to lead the Chassidic community of Russia and White Russia after the great aliyah of 1777.

When Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk led a large group of chassidim to the Land of Israel in תקל"ז (1777), he recognized that the Chassidic communities of Russia needed continued leadership in his absence. In a formal letter written on 4 Adar תקמ"ב (1782), Rabbi Menachem Mendel appointed three leaders to share responsibility for Russian Jewry: Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (the Alter Rebbe), Rabbi Issachar Ber of Lubavitch, and Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk. Each was to serve in their area of influence, with the understanding that the Chassidic community would continue to thrive despite the physical distance of its senior leadership.

In practice, as the Alter Rebbe's stature grew — through his authorship of the Tanya, his expanding network of disciples, and his increasingly systematized approach to Chabad philosophy — the other co-appointees gradually recognized the Alter Rebbe's primary authority. Rabbi Issachar Ber, according to the sources, submitted to the Alter Rebbe's leadership, acknowledging the Alter Rebbe as the primary figure among the three co-appointees. This shift did not represent a political defeat but rather an organic recognition of the Alter Rebbe's unique intellectual and spiritual contributions to the movement.

Rabbi Issachar Ber's base in Lubavitch is historically significant: Lubavitch would become the headquarters of Chabad beginning in the early nineteenth century under the Mitteler Rebbe, and the town's prior association with a Chassidic leader may have facilitated its emergence as the movement's center.

## Significance

Rabbi Issachar Ber illustrates that the early Chabad movement was not monolithic from the beginning. The formal tri-partite appointment of 1782 reveals that Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk envisioned a distributed leadership for Russian Chassidim, not a single successor. The gradual consolidation of authority in the Alter Rebbe represents an important developmental moment in Chabad's institutional history. Rabbi Issachar Ber's willingness to submit to the Alter Rebbe also models the kind of voluntary deference that was idealized within Chassidic leadership culture.
