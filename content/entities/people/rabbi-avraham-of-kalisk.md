# Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk

## Overview
Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk was a major disciple of the Maggid of Mezeritch who made aliyah to Eretz Yisrael together with Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk in 1777. He became a central figure in the Chassidic community in Eretz Yisrael and had significant disputes with the Alter Rebbe regarding Chabad philosophy.

## Details
Rabbi Avraham accompanied Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk to Eretz Yisrael, eventually settling in Tiberias.

After Rabbi Menachem Mendel's passing in 1788, he became the leader of the Chassidic community in Eretz Yisrael and continued to collect and distribute funds (ma'amadot) sent from Russia.

His relationship with the Alter Rebbe was complex. Starting around 1798, Rabbi Avraham objected to the publication of the Tanya, disagreeing with its approach of providing individual guidance to every Chassid through a book rather than through direct contact with the Tzaddik. He championed the approach "v'tzaddik be'emunato yikhye" (the righteous lives by his faith in the Tzaddik).

The Alter Rebbe wrote a detailed letter in summer 1805 addressing this dispute, in which he discusses the founding of Chabad as a distinct movement. In that letter, the Alter Rebbe notes that by winter 1803, those who opposed the Alter Rebbe referred to his followers as "Chabad."

Despite these disputes, they also exchanged warm letters and Rabbi Avraham ultimately reconciled with the Alter Rebbe.
