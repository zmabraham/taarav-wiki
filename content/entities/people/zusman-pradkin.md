# R. Zusman Pradkin

A Chabad Chassid from **[[shumyatz|Shumyatz]]** (Shumyache), Belarus, who served as both **shochet** (ritual slaughterer) and **de facto rabbi** of his community during the Soviet era. He is the father-in-law of the unnamed narrator of *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu*.

## Background

- **Hometown**: [[shumyatz|Shumyatz]] (Shumyache), a small town in the Mogilev region of Belarus
- **Occupation**: Shochet (ritual slaughterer) — one of the few Jewish religious occupations that could still function, somewhat covertly, under Soviet rule
- **Role**: De facto community rabbi of Shumyatz — as an ordained shochet with Torah knowledge, he provided religious guidance even without an official rabbinic title
- **Education**: He had studied at the **Slobodka Yeshiva** (the Musar yeshiva in Kovna/Kaunas, Lithuania), indicating serious traditional learning before the Soviet period

## Marriage into This Family

The narrator's engagement to R. Zusman's daughter came about through the matchmaking of [[zusha-paz|R. Zusha Paz]] of Moscow. The tena'im (engagement) took place in **Nisan 5700 (1940)** in Moscow. R. Zusman's father-in-law — the narrator's grandfather-in-law — was [[moshe-leib-paz|R. Moshe Leib Paz]] of Klimovitch.

## Family

- **Father-in-law**: [[moshe-leib-paz|R. Moshe Leib Paz]] of Klimovitch (shochet, mohel, Chabad Chassid)
- **Daughter**: The narrator's wife (unnamed in the memoir)

## Slobodka Connection

Slobodka Yeshiva (officially Knesset Yisrael) in Kovna was the flagship institution of the Musar movement. Its students were known for their ethical rigor. R. Zusman's having studied there, despite eventually becoming a Chabad Chassid, illustrates the movement of Jewish youth between different yeshiva worlds in early 20th-century Eastern Europe.
