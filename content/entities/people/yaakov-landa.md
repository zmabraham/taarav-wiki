# R' Yaakov Landa — ר' יעקב לנדא

**Rabbi of Libau (Latvia); Then 50-Year Rabbi of Bnei Brak (Israel)**

**Full Name**: Rabbi Yaakov Landa
**Dates**: (early 20th century – late 20th century)
**Position**: Rabbi of Libau (Liepāja), Latvia; Rabbi of Bnei Brak, Israel
**Affiliation**: Chabad-Lubavitch

---

## Overview

Rabbi Yaakov Landa was a Chabad rabbi who served the Jewish community of [[entities/places/libau|Libau (Liepāja)]], Latvia, during the interwar period, before escaping the Holocaust and becoming the rabbi of [[entities/places/bnei-brak|Bnei Brak]], Israel, where he served for approximately 50 years. His remarkably long tenure in Bnei Brak made him one of the most enduring rabbinical figures in Israeli Orthodox life — a man who bridged the pre-Holocaust world of Latvian Chabad with the rebuilding of Jewish life in the Land of Israel.

---

## Biography

### Background and Early Career

- **Training**: Received rabbinical education in the Chabad tradition
- **Libau appointment**: Appointed as Chabad rabbi of Libau, Latvia's major port city
- **Latvian period**: Served during the era when Latvia was an independent republic (1918–1940)
- **Network**: Part of the Latvian Chabad network directed by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] from [[entities/places/riga|Riga]] (1927–1933)

### Service in Libau

In Libau, R' Landa:

- **Led the Chabad community**: Provided rabbinical leadership for Libau's Chabad synagogue and community
- **Halachic guidance**: Rendered decisions according to Chabad tradition
- **Community building**: Maintained Chabad institutions in this cosmopolitan port city
- **Interwar activity**: Active during the period of relative Jewish freedom under Latvian democracy

### Escape from the Holocaust

When Nazi Germany occupied Latvia in 1941:

- **Survival**: R' Landa managed to escape — the exact circumstances are not fully documented here
- **Departure**: Left Latvia before or during the destruction of the Jewish community
- **Loss**: The Libau Jewish community he had served was largely annihilated

## Rabbinate in Bnei Brak

### The 50-Year Tenure

R' Landa's Israeli career was extraordinary:

- **Appointment**: Became rabbi of Bnei Brak after arriving in Israel
- **Duration**: Served as rabbi of Bnei Brak for approximately 50 years — one of the longest rabbinical tenures in Israeli history
- **Scope**: Bnei Brak, founded in 1924, grew into one of Israel's most important Orthodox cities; to be its rabbi for 50 years was a position of enormous influence

### Role in Bnei Brak

As rabbi of this growing city:

- **Halachic authority**: Rendered decisions on the many complex questions arising from modern life
- **Communal leader**: Navigated the diverse Orthodox and Chabad communities in Bnei Brak
- **Torah teaching**: Continued his Chabad-influenced teaching
- **Elder statesman**: As decades passed, became a senior figure in Israeli Orthodox rabbinics
- **Connection to roots**: Maintained his Chabad identity and connection to Lubavitch even while serving a broad community

---

## Significance

### Bridge Between Worlds

R' Yaakov Landa represents:

- **Pre-Holocaust continuity**: One of the few Chabad rabbis from the Baltic region who survived and continued his work
- **Israel building**: Part of the generation that built up Israeli Orthodox institutions from the ground
- **Long service**: 50 years as a city rabbi — an extraordinary commitment and continuity
- **Chabad in Israel**: Demonstrated that Chabad-trained rabbis could serve broad Orthodox communities, not just Chabad-specific ones
