# Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk

**Co-appointed leader of Russian Chassidim alongside the Alter Rebbe; traveled to the Land of Israel and died there**

## Biography

Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk was a chassidic leader associated with the town of Polotsk in the Vitebsk gubernia of White Russia. He is best known as one of three leaders formally appointed by Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk to guide the Chassidic communities of Russia after the latter's emigration to the Land of Israel in תקל"ז (1777).

In a formal letter of appointment written on 4 Adar תקמ"ב (1782), Rabbi Menachem Mendel designated Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (the Alter Rebbe), Rabbi Issachar Ber of Lubavitch, and Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk as co-leaders of Russian Chassidim. Each was recognized as a spiritual authority within his area of influence, with the collective responsibility to sustain the movement in the absence of the senior leadership that had emigrated.

Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk, however, did not remain in Russia. He eventually traveled to the Land of Israel, where he died before any potential return. This meant that in practice, the formal tri-partite leadership arrangement resolved itself naturally into the Alter Rebbe's sole stewardship of Russian Chassidim, with Rabbi Issachar Ber of Lubavitch submitting to his authority and Rabbi Yisrael having departed permanently.

The sources in Toldos Chabad BeRussia note Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk's death in the Land of Israel as a historical fact relevant to understanding how the Alter Rebbe came to exercise undivided leadership. His connection to Polotsk links him to one of the important White Russian Jewish communities of the period.

## Significance

Rabbi Yisrael of Polotsk's significance is primarily structural: his appointment as one of three co-leaders reveals the distributed nature of early Chassidic leadership in Russia, and his subsequent emigration and death in Israel helps explain how the Alter Rebbe came to be the sole authority over Russian Chassidim. His trajectory also reflects the broader phenomenon of aliyah among Chassidic leaders of the period, following the example set by Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk and Rabbi Avraham of Kalisk.
