# Rabbi Binyamin Gorodetsky

**Born**: c. 1900  
**Died**: c. 1990s  
**Role**: [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] European representative; primary organizer of the [[events/great-escape-1946|Great Escape (1946)]]

---

## Overview

Rabbi Binyamin Gorodetsky served as the Rayatz's chief representative in Europe during the critical postwar years, functioning as the operational commander on the ground for one of the most audacious rescue operations in Jewish history — the "Great Escape" of approximately 1,000 Chabad Chasidim from the Soviet Union in 1946–1947.

---

## European Representative

As the Rayatz's representative in Europe, Gorodetsky was the bridge between the Rebbe in Brooklyn and the Chabad communities scattered across liberated Europe, occupied territories, and the Soviet Union. He operated in the chaotic postwar environment when borders were fluid, displaced persons camps were overflowing, and the fate of survivors was uncertain.

---

## The Great Escape (1946–1947)

Gorodetsky's central role was coordinating the logistics of the mass escape of Chabad Chasidim from the Soviet Union:

**The Operation's Elements:**

- Chabad Chasidim in Uzbekistan (where they had fled the Nazi advance) acquired Polish identity papers on the black market
- Skilled counterfeiters modified names and details
- Women served as messengers (less conspicuous to Soviet authorities)
- Cattle cars transported groups from Lvov across the Polish border
- A secret rabbinical court of 23 judges in Lvov set guidelines and pooled resources

Gorodetsky coordinated the European side — receiving escapees, managing transit through Poland, and organizing onward transport to France, Western Europe, and ultimately resettlement destinations across the world.

The Rayatz in Brooklyn communicated with Gorodetsky in code through intermediaries, providing $7,000 bribes to free arrested Chasidim and directing the overall strategy.

See [[events/great-escape-1946|The Great Escape (1946)]] for the full account.

---

## Dispersion Coordination

After the escape, Gorodetsky helped choreograph the Rayatz's vision of global dispersion — placing Chabad families strategically in France, Australia, Canada, South Africa, and other countries. Each family was understood not as a refugee finding haven but as an emissary sent to transform their new community.
