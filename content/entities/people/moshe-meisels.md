# Rabbi Moshe Meisels

**Born**: c. 1780  
**Died**: c. 1845  
**Role**: Chabad chassid who served as interpreter for Napoleon's army; instrumental in the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]'s escape during the 1812 invasion

---

## Overview

Rabbi Moshe Meisels is one of the most dramatic figures in Chabad history. A chassid of the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]], he found himself at the intersection of Napoleonic geopolitics and Chassidic loyalty. His story is one of espionage, courage, and devotion to his Rebbe during the most dangerous period of the Alter Rebbe's life.

---

## Role During Napoleon's 1812 Invasion

When [[entities/people/napoleon|Napoleon Bonaparte]] invaded Russia in June 1812, Moshe Meisels — who was fluent in French — was pressed into service as an interpreter for the French army. This placed him in a unique position: he had access to French military communications and troop movements.

According to Chabad tradition, Meisels used his position to secretly relay intelligence to the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]], informing him of the French army's movements and enabling the Rebbe and his family to flee eastward ahead of the advancing forces.

---

## The Alter Rebbe's Flight

The Alter Rebbe had taken a firm stance in support of the Russian Empire against Napoleon. He reasoned that while Napoleon's liberal policies might improve Jewish material conditions, they would erode Jewish spiritual life and religious observance. Russian oppression, paradoxically, kept Jews closer to their traditions.

When the French army advanced toward the Alter Rebbe's location, Meisels' intelligence reports became crucial. The Alter Rebbe and his family undertook an arduous flight eastward — a journey that ultimately cost the Alter Rebbe his life. He died on 24 Tevet 5573 (December 1812) in the village of Piena, exhausted from the flight.

---

## Significance

Moshe Meisels represents the intersection of Chabad history with the great events of European history. His story illustrates:

- The deep personal loyalty that bound chassidim to their Rebbe
- The willingness to risk one's life for the Chassidic community (a form of [[concepts/mesirus-nefesh|mesirus nefesh]])
- The way in which world-historical events directly shaped the course of Chabad's development
