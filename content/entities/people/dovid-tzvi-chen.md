# R' Dovid Tzvi Chen (Rad"tz)

**Rabbi and Gaon of Chernigov; one of the outstanding Chabad elder chassidim of his generation**

## Overview

R' Dovid Tzvi Chen — known by the acronym Rad"tz (ר"ד"צ) — was the rabbi of Chernigov (Chernihiv, Ukraine) during the late Czarist and early Soviet period. He was widely regarded as one of the gedolei ha-poskim of his era, a man of extraordinary piety, and a revered figure among Chabad chassidim. The Rebbe Rashab would send Tomchei Temimim students to him to advance in practical halachic adjudication (horo'a).

## Scholarship and Halachic Eminence

R' Dovid Tzvi was described in his generation as a man who was simultaneously a great Torah scholar (gaon) and a devout chassid — a combination that the memoirist Jacobson found unusual enough to remark upon. He was known for his expertise in halachic responsa and is the subject of the book _LeManchamei_. When students of Tomchei Temimim needed to refine their halachic skills, the Rebbe Rashab sent them specifically to Chernigov to study under him.

## Daily Practices and Character

R' Yisrael Jacobson, who lived in Chernigov twice during the years 5676–5678 (1916–1918), left a detailed portrait of R' Dovid Tzvi's daily schedule and character:

**Prayers:** R' Dovid Tzvi's prayer schedule was extraordinary. After immersion in the mikveh and synagogue attendance in the morning, he would return home and pray privately — not until 2 PM in the afternoon. He would study from the _Siddur Tefillah LeMoshe_ (of the Ramak), moving his finger carefully along each word, clearly intending the mystical concentrations written there. His davening from Pesukei DeZimra through Nefillat Apayim typically took an hour and forty minutes or more.

**Two days per week (Mondays and Thursdays):** He would immerse in the mikveh. The students would accompany him and then walk with him to synagogue for Kedusha, Barchu, and the Torah reading.

**Wonder at the world:** R' Dovid Tzvi had a childlike sense of wonder at modern technology. Each time an automobile passed, he would look up and say: "Wonders of the Creator — a wagon travels at speed without horses!" The shochtim told the students that thirty years earlier he had said the same thing about bicycles: "Wonders of the Creator — a person rides on two wheels without falling!"

**Handling money:** He was so removed from worldly concerns that he would sometimes wrap tobacco in banknotes (one or three rubles), treating them as mere paper. He refused to look at the small paper coins the government issued because they bore images.

**Avoidance of lashon hara:** Once when a guest in his home began speaking about another person, R' Dovid Tzvi turned his face away and covered his ears.

**Chassidic teaching:** He would occasionally teach Chassidus to the students — sometimes from _Likkutei Torah_ or _Tanya_ — but would first spend long periods in preparation. Students might wait twelve hours or more, and then he would expound for an hour or more on just a few lines of text, and conclude: "Now go learn."

**Generosity:** He received people who came with kvitlach, accepting their petitions with warmth.

**Extraordinary equanimity:** Despite genuine poverty (his rebbetzin was partially paralyzed and unable to speak clearly, though she could recite blessings and bentch with clarity), R' Dovid Tzvi was never visibly dispirited about material conditions. The one time Jacobson saw him truly downcast was apparently over concerns about his living situation and appearance — not spiritual matters.

## Family

His son was the gaon R' Menachem Mendel Chen, who served as rabbi in Nizhyn (Nezhin). R' Menachem Mendel was a scholar of extraordinary caliber — Jacobson, who spent a night in Nizhyn on his father's behalf, observed him studying Mishnah, Rashi, Gemara, and Tosafot late into the night, explaining every word carefully "as one teaches a young child," with complete intellectual freshness. The Rebbe Rashab held R' Menachem Mendel in high regard and sent Temimim students to him to be examined in practical halacha.

Tragically, R' Menachem Mendel Chen of Nizhyn was killed by Denikin's forces in the pogroms that accompanied the Russian Civil War — one of the many Torah scholars martyred in those years.

## Role in Helping Tomchei Temimim Students

During WWI, when young Temimim who held blue military exemption cards (raz'ryad 2) were being recalled by the Russian military, the Rebbe Rashab advised Jacobson to travel to Chernigov to R' Dovid Tzvi. R' Dovid Tzvi used his connections with local notables (particularly R' Mordechai Paliy, who had relationships with government officials) to secure the release of students from military detention. Jacobson himself was arrested in Chernigov, held for four days, and was freed through R' Dovid Tzvi's intercession with a full white discharge card (bilyet lavan).

On another occasion, when Jacobson was detained and facing a military commission, R' Dovid Tzvi awaited news with genuine concern and greeted Jacobson's release with heartfelt joy — opening the door immediately upon hearing the knock, which indicated he had been waiting.

## Significance

R' Dovid Tzvi Chen represents the older generation of Chabad elder chassidim — figures who bridged the intellectual world of halachic scholarship with the devotional world of Chassidic practice. The Rebbe Rashab's confidence in him as a place to send students reflected his stature in both worlds. His portrayal in the Zachor memoirs is one of the most detailed firsthand accounts of an elder Chabad gadol in the pre-Soviet period.
