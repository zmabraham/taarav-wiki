# Rabbi Moshe Gourarie

**Born**: Early 20th century  
**Died**: Late 20th century  
**Role**: Pioneer Chabad shaliach to Morocco; builder of Jewish institutions in North Africa

---

## Overview

Rabbi Moshe Gourarie was among the first generation of shluchim (emissaries) dispatched by the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] to establish Chabad presence in North Africa. His work in Morocco, Tunisia, and other North African countries laid the foundation for what would become a thriving Chabad community serving hundreds of thousands of Jews.

Operating in challenging conditions — post-colonial transition, political instability, and the mass exodus of North African Jewry to Israel and France — Rabbi Gourarie built institutions that would serve both those remaining and those passing through on their way to new lives.

---

## Historical Context: North African Jewry in the 1950s

The Moroccan Jewish community was one of the largest and oldest in the Jewish world, dating back to Roman times. By the 1950s, it numbered approximately 250,000–300,000 people. However, the establishment of the State of Israel and growing nationalism in North Africa triggered a massive migration.

Between 1948 and 1968, more than 200,000 Moroccan Jews emigrated to Israel, with tens of thousands more moving to France and Canada. This created a paradox: the community was shrinking, yet the need for Jewish institutions was urgent — both for those remaining and for those in transition.

It was into this complex environment that the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe]] sent shluchim, with Rabbi Gourarie among the pioneers.

---

## Arrival in Morocco

Rabbi Gourarie arrived in Morocco in the early 1950s, part of the first wave of Chabad shluchim to North Africa. The Rebbe had identified North Africa as a priority region, both for its immediate Jewish population and for its role as a transit point for Jews emigrating to Israel.

Operating under difficult conditions — limited infrastructure, unfamiliar customs, political uncertainty — Rabbi Gourarie set about building the foundations of Chabad activity. He worked alongside other pioneer shluchim including [[entities/people/matusof-shlomo|Rabbi Shlomo Matusof]] and [[entities/people/garelik-gershon|Rabbi Gershon Garelik]].

---

## Building Institutions

Rabbi Gourarie's work took several forms:

### Educational Institutions

He established schools and yeshivos that provided both Jewish and secular education. These were crucial at a time when the French colonial school system was scaling back and many families were preparing for emigration to Israel. The schools taught Hebrew, Torah, and Chassidus alongside general studies, preparing students for life as Jews wherever they would end up.

### Synagogues and Mikvaot

Rabbi Gourarie built synagogues that followed the Chabad nusach (prayer style) and mikvaot (ritual baths) that met the highest standards of Jewish law. These institutions served as anchors for Jewish life in communities that were otherwise in flux.

### Community Services

He provided a range of community services including kosher food supervision, circumcision (_brit milah_), wedding officiation, and burial services. For many Jews — especially those preparing to leave for Israel — these services were their last formal connection to Moroccan Jewish life before departure.

---

## Relationship with the Rebbe

Rabbi Gourarie maintained close correspondence with the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe]] throughout his tenure in Morocco. The Rebbe took a personal interest in North African Jewry, frequently asking about the situation on the ground and offering specific guidance.

In several published letters, the Rebbe encouraged Rabbi Gourarie and his colleagues to strengthen their work, emphasizing the importance of reaching every Jew regardless of their background or level of observance. This guidance reflected the Rebbe's broader philosophy of outreach (_mivtzoim_) that would soon be formalized into the Mitzvah Campaigns.

---

## Challenges and Obstacles

Rabbi Gourarie's work was not without challenges:

- **Political instability**: Morocco gained independence from France in 1956, leading to uncertainty about the future of Jewish institutions
- **Emigration pressure**: As Jews left in large numbers, maintaining community institutions became increasingly difficult
- **Cultural differences**: The Chassidic approach was unfamiliar to many Moroccan Jews, who had their own rich religious traditions
- **Resource constraints**: Financial support from abroad was limited, especially in the early years

Despite these challenges, Rabbi Gourarie persevered, building institutions that would outlast his tenure and serve future generations.

---

## Legacy

Rabbi Gourarie's work in Morocco laid the foundation for what would become a vibrant Chabad presence in North Africa. The institutions he established continued to operate long after his departure, serving both the remaining Jewish community and Jewish visitors from around the world.

Today, Chabad has a significant presence in Morocco, with centers in Casablanca, Marrakech, Tangier, and other cities. These institutions trace their origins to the pioneering work of Rabbi Gourarie and his colleagues in the 1950s and 1960s.

Moreover, Rabbi Gourarie's work in North Africa exemplified the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe's]] global vision: that every Jewish community, no matter how remote or threatened, deserved access to authentic Torah Judaism and Chassidic warmth.
