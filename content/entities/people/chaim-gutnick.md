# Rabbi Chaim Gutnick

**Born**: c. 1920  
**Died**: c. 2004, Melbourne, Australia  
**Role**: Sent to Australia by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in 1941; pioneer of Chabad in Australia

---

## Overview

Rabbi Chaim Gutnick was sent to Australia by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in 1941 to prepare the ground for the reception of Chabad families who would eventually be directed there as part of the Rayatz's global dispersion strategy. He became a pioneering figure in Australian Jewish life and one of the most significant Chabad shlichim of his generation.

---

## The Mission (1941)

In 1941, the Rayatz dispatched Rabbi Gutnick to Australia — a country then at the far periphery of the Jewish world in terms of Chabad presence. The timing was deliberate: the Rayatz was already thinking beyond the immediate crisis of WWII, planning for where Chabad communities could be established globally after the war.

Australia offered a stable, democratic society with a small but established Jewish community. The Rayatz understood that Australia could become a significant Chabad center if proper foundations were laid.

---

## Building Australian Chabad

Rabbi Gutnick settled in Melbourne, which had the larger and more established of Australia's two major Jewish communities (the other being Sydney). He:

- Established Chabad presence in Melbourne
- Built synagogues and educational institutions
- Received and integrated the Chabad families sent from Europe after the war
- Mentored the next generation of Australian Chabad leaders

His son, Joseph Gutnick, became a major figure in Australian business and philanthropy, funding Chabad institutions globally — representing the second-generation flowering of what Rabbi Gutnick planted.

---

## Significance in the Global Plan

The Rayatz's instruction to send Gutnick to Australia in 1941 — before the war was even decided — reflects the extraordinary strategic vision of the Chabad leadership. The Rayatz was simultaneously:

1. Fighting to save Jews from the Holocaust
2. Planning the postwar global reconstruction of Jewish life

Australia was one of six Chabad families the Rayatz directed specifically to Melbourne and surroundings after the [[events/great-escape-1946|Great Escape]], creating the nucleus of what would become a significant community.
