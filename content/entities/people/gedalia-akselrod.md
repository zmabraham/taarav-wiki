# Rabbi Gedalia Akselrod

Rabbi Gedaliahu Akselrod (born 5 Elul 5701 / 1941) is one of the prominent Chabad rabbis in Israel, currently serving as a rabbi of the Chabad community in Haifa and a member of "Machon Halacha Chabad." He formerly served as a member of the Chabad rabbinical court, the Ramat Gan rabbinate, and as Av Beit Din (presiding judge) of the rabbinical court in Haifa.

## Biography

Born in Ramat Gan to Rabbi Moshe Akselrod and his mother Chaya Sara. He studied in Chinuch Atzmai's Talmud Torah in Bnei Brak, then at Tomchei Tmimim in Lod, under the influence of Rabbi Shlomo Chaim Kesselman. He also studied at Ponevezh Yeshiva. When he was 18 his father passed away.

In 5720 (1960), the Rebbe arranged for him to be appointed rabbi of Ramat Gan as his father's successor. He became a member of the city's rabbinate and served as rabbi of the Chabad synagogue "Sukat Shalom" that his father had established.

For Purim 5726 (1966), he came to the Rebbe for the first time. The Rebbe received him twice in yechidus with great warmth. When he entered, the Rebbe stood up and said "I was waiting for you, Rabbi Gedalia." When he asked at the end of the yechidus "What comes next?", the Rebbe closed his eyes and replied in the melody of a Chassidic discourse, explaining the concept of the Beinoni.

In 5737 (1977) he was appointed to the Chabad rabbinical court in Israel, serving until 5741 (1981). He then served as a dayan in Haifa's rabbinical court, later becoming its Av Beit Din, and retired in 5761 (2001). He has since served as rabbi of the Chabad community in Haifa.

He was among the first rabbis to sign the ruling declaring the Rebbe to be Melech HaMashiach (Iyar 5751/1991). After Gimmel Tammuz (1994) he continued publishing his belief in the Rebbe's continued presence and participated in Geulah conferences.

From 5765–5770 (2005–2010) he served as Rosh Yeshiva of Tomchei Tmimim Be'er Sheva. In 5776 (2016) he joined Machon Halacha Chabad.

Known for his public campaigns on Jewish identity issues, land of Israel, and Jewish burial laws — including vigorous opposition to "stacked burial," for which he organized demonstrations and published halachic materials.

## Works
- **Sha'ut Migdal Tzofim** — 9 volumes of responsa (from 5752/1992)
- **Migdal Tzofim al HaTorah** — Chassidic insights on the Torah
- **Shalheivot HaTanya** — explanations of Tanya with Chassidic stories (from 5783/2023)
