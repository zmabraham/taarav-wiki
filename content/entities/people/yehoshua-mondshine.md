# Rabbi Yehoshua Mondshine

Rabbi Yehoshua Mondshine (9 Shevat 5707 – 2 Tevet 5775 / 1947–2014) was a Chabad researcher and historian who worked as a senior librarian at the National Library in Jerusalem. He published hundreds of articles and books on Chabad history and Chassidic literature, defending the accuracy of rabbinic sources with rigorous scholarship.

## Biography

Born in Tel Aviv to Mordechai Shmuel and Chaya Mondshine, from a religious Zionist family. Before his bar mitzvah he wrote his first letter to the Rebbe, asking for a blessing — the Rebbe replied with his standard bar mitzvah blessing. Upon noticing the differences in text between the Tehillat Hashem siddur and others, he wrote to the Rebbe about it, and the Rebbe directed him to the relevant sources.

He studied at the Yishuv HaChadash Yeshiva in Tel Aviv, then transferred to Tomchei Tmimim Kfar Chabad in 5724 (1964), where he developed deeply under the mashpia Rabbi Shlomo Chaim Kesselman. In 5728 (1968) he studied at Tomchei Tmimim Central — 770 in Brooklyn — during the "year of the group."

After his marriage in 5729 (1969) to Rachel Leah (daughter of Rabbi David Brooman, KehOT Europe director), the Rebbe suggested he settle in Crown Heights and work at the Lubavitch library and publishing staff, but this did not materialize. In 5737 (1977) he was hired as a librarian at the National Library in Jerusalem.

He formed a close bond with librarian Rabbi Chaim Lieberman, from whom he absorbed much knowledge and method, and later published a tribute article about him and helped publish his books.

His personality combined two seemingly contradictory traits: a deeply contemplative inner spiritual life — daily prayer with kavana, constant engagement with Chassidic ideas — alongside sharp critical scholarship where nothing was accepted without rigorous examination. His writing voice was forceful and clear, though his spoken voice was barely audible in synagogue. He published under pen names such as "Yehoshua D. Levonon" and "Y. Levononi."

From 5765–5773 (2005–2013) he published the series "Sipurim VeGilguleihem" (Stories and Their Transformations) on Shturem.net, examining how Chassidic stories changed across their various versions. This sometimes led to controversy, as critics felt he was too quick to "debunk" stories passed down from the Rebbeim. He maintained that accuracy was itself a service to truth.

He passed away on "Zot Chanukah" (2 Tevet 5775) after a difficult illness, at age 67. His funeral was held the same day. After his passing, his family and researcher Rabbi Menachem Mendel Kastel established the "Kramim" institute to publish his writings and books.

## Selected Works
- **Shivchei HaBesht** — photographic edition of the sole manuscript of Shivchei HaBesht (5742/1982)
- **Beit Krem Chabad** — journal, 4 volumes (5747–5752)
- **Otzar Minhagei Chabad** — 2 volumes (5755, 5765)
- **HaMa'asar HaRishon** — documents from the Alter Rebbe's first imprisonment (5772/2012)
- **HaMasa HaAcharon** — on the Alter Rebbe's journey during the Napoleonic war (5772/2012)
