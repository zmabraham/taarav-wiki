# Rabbi Mordechai (Mottel) of Kozlin

**Born**: Late 19th/Early 20th century
**Role**: Soviet-era Chabad chassid

---

## Overview

Rabbi Mordechai of Kozlin, known as "Reb Mottel," was a Soviet-era Chabad chassid who maintained Jewish religious life under Communist persecution. His story, like those of many Soviet-era chassidim, exemplifies the concept of _mesirus nefesh_ — self-sacrifice for Torah and the Jewish community.

The designation "of Kozlin" indicates his connection to the town of Kozlin (also spelled Kožlin or similar variations), a Jewish community in Eastern Europe. Like many chassidim of his generation, Reb Mottel's identity was tied to both his spiritual commitment and his communal roots.

---

## Life Under Soviet Rule

Reb Mottel came of age during a period of tremendous upheaval — the transition from Czarist rule to the Bolshevik Revolution to the establishment of the Soviet Union.

**The Transition to Soviet Power**: The Bolshevik Revolution of 1917 brought the Communists to power, initiating a campaign against religion that would intensify over the coming decades. Jewish religious life, which had thrived under the Czars despite occasional persecution, now faced systematic suppression.

**Maintaining Observance**: Despite the new restrictions, Reb Mottel and other chassidim refused to abandon Jewish observance. They found ways to maintain religious life underground — secret minyanim, clandestine schools, hidden ritual baths, and covert circumcisions.

---

## Underground Activities

Reb Mottel participated in the Chabad underground network that kept Jewish life alive throughout the Soviet Union.

**Secret Minyanim**: Reb Mottel helped organize and maintain secret prayer quorums, meeting in private homes and moving constantly to avoid detection. These minyanim were essential for community life and represented acts of spiritual resistance.

**Torah Education**: Like other Soviet-era chassidim, Reb Mottel participated in efforts to transmit Torah knowledge. This included teaching children, mentoring younger chassidim, and organizing study groups.

**Holiday Observances**: Jewish holidays posed particular challenges under Soviet rule. Reb Mottel helped organize secret seders, High Holiday services, and other celebrations — each representing an act of defiance and spiritual commitment.

---

## Challenges and Hardships

Life as a religious Jew in the Soviet Union involved constant challenges and dangers.

**Risk of Arrest**: Participation in underground religious activities carried the constant risk of arrest. Many chassidim spent years in Soviet prisons and labor camps for the "crime" of maintaining Jewish observance.

**Economic Hardship**: The Soviet economy, particularly in its early decades, was marked by scarcity and hardship. Religious Jews often faced additional economic discrimination.

**Social Pressure**: Beyond official persecution, Soviet Jews faced cultural pressure to assimilate and abandon religious observance. Maintaining Jewish commitment required inner strength and community support.

---

## Legacy

Reb Mottel of Kozlin is remembered as one of the many Soviet-era chassidim who kept Judaism alive through decades of persecution. Though his individual story may be less documented than some of his contemporaries, his contribution to Jewish survival was significant.

**The Oral Tradition**: Like many Soviet-era chassidim, much of what we know about Reb Mottel comes through oral tradition — stories passed down from generation to generation. These stories preserve the memory of individual heroism.

**Inspiration for Future Generations**: The example of Reb Mottel and others like him continues to inspire. Their mesirus nefesh for Torah and mitzvot, even under conditions of persecution, sets a standard of commitment that continues to guide the Chabad-Lubavitch movement.
