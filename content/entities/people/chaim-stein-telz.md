# R. Chaim Stein (Telshe Yeshiva)

The **Mashgiach Ruchani (spiritual supervisor)** of **Telshe Yeshiva** — the famous Lithuanian yeshiva that relocated from Telz (Telsiai, Lithuania) to Cleveland, Ohio during World War II. He appears in the memoir *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* as the husband of [[freydel-stein|Freydel Stein]], through whose efforts the narrator received an immigration visa to America.

## Life and Career

- Served as Mashgiach Ruchani (director of spiritual affairs) at Telshe Yeshiva
- The yeshiva was founded in Telz, Lithuania in 1875 and became one of the premier Lithuanian yeshivos before WWII
- During WWII, the yeshiva escaped the Holocaust and reestablished itself in Cleveland
- R. Chaim Stein became a major figure at the American Telshe Yeshiva

## Connection to Samarkand and Paris Refugees

During or after WWII, R. Chaim Stein was in [[samarkand|Samarkand]] (Central Asia), where many Jewish refugees had fled during the German advance. He married a cousin of the narrator's uncle ([[zusman-pradkin|R. Zusman Pradkin]]'s family), creating a personal connection between the Lithuanian yeshiva world and the Chabad Chassidic community.

## The "Professor" Visa

Through Telshe Yeshiva, the narrator received a **"professor" visa** (a category of permanent immigrant visa) to enter the United States. This was arranged largely through the personal efforts of his wife [[freydel-stein|Freydel Stein]], who used her connections to obtain the visa documentation. The visa allowed the narrator and his family to permanently settle in America from [[paris|Paris]] in 5708 (1948).

## Significance

R. Chaim Stein's role illustrates the informal rescue networks that operated between different segments of Orthodox Jewry — Lithuanian yeshivos helping Chabad Chassidim obtain the papers needed to reach America, based on personal connections formed in the wartime diaspora.
