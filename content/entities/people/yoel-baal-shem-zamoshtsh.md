# Rabbi Yoel Baal Shem of Zamoshtsh

Rabbi Yoel Helperin of Zamoshtsh (died c. 5474 / 1714) was the second leader of the hidden tzaddikim (nistarim) after the death of his teacher Rabbi Eliyahu Baal Shem of Worms. He was the teacher of Rabbi Adam Baal Shem, who in turn taught the Baal Shem Tov. He served as rabbi and Av Beit Din in multiple important communities.

## Biography

Born to Rabbi Moshe, himself described as an extraordinary Torah scholar and hidden righteous man. Rabbi Yoel joined the yeshiva of Rabbi Eliyahu Baal Shem in Prague as a young man and in 5394 (1634) was accepted into the nistarim circle. On Rabbi Eliyahu's instruction, he returned to Zamoshtsh in Poland in 5399 (1639) where he was crowned as a Baal Shem. He led the yeshiva there for approximately 50 years.

He served as rabbi and Av Beit Din in Loytzk (Lutsk), then in Pinsk, and in 5452 (1692) was appointed to serve in Ostroh after his predecessor Rabbi Naftali Kohen moved to Poznan. He was subsequently chosen as Av Beit Din of Lvov but died in Ostroh on 4 Tishrei 5474 before taking that seat.

His learning methodology for revealed Torah (nigleh) followed the approach of his teacher Rabbi Yoel Sirkes (the Bach), which emphasized deep analytical understanding of each Talmudic passage in context.

Like all the kabbalist-baalei shem in his tradition, he was expert in divine names, amulets, and exorcisms, healing physical and spiritual ailments. He left behind written segulot and amulets.

In his generation, the hidden tzaddikim renewed the practice of drawing common people close to Torah through storytelling, awakening the spark of their souls.

## Connection to Chabad

Rabbi Yoel Baal Shem is a figure in the chain of spiritual transmission leading to Chassidus. His student Rabbi Adam Baal Shem taught the Baal Shem Tov. The Frierdiker Rebbe's Memoirs (Sefer HaZikronot) document the history of this circle, which forms part of the prehistory of the Chassidic movement.
