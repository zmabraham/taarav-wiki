# Rabbi Baruch Shalom Schneersohn (Maharabash)

**Born**: 1823, Lubavitch  
**Died**: 1869, Lubavitch  
**Known by**: The acronym "Maharabash" (Moreinu HaRav Baruch Shalom)  
**Father**: [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]]

---

## Overview

Rabbi Baruch Shalom Schneersohn was the eldest son of the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] (third Chabad Rebbe). Despite being the firstborn, he did not succeed his father as the primary Rebbe of Lubavitch — that role passed to the youngest son, [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Rabbi Shmuel (Maharash)]]. Rabbi Baruch Shalom served as a Chabad Rebbe in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] alongside his brothers during the brief period before his passing.

---

## The Succession Question

When the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] passed away in 1866, the question of succession was complicated by the fact that he had seven sons, several of whom were accomplished Torah scholars and Chassidic leaders in their own right. Rather than a single successor, multiple sons established their own courts:

- **Rabbi Baruch Shalom** (the eldest) — remained in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]]
- [[entities/people/yehuda-leib-kopust|Rabbi Yehuda Leib]] — established a court in Kopust
- [[entities/people/chaim-schneur-zalman-liadi|Rabbi Chaim Schneur Zalman]] — settled in Liadi
- [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Rabbi Shmuel (Maharash)]] — became the primary successor in Lubavitch
- [[entities/people/menachem-nachum-niezhin|Rabbi Menachem Nachum]] — established a court in Niezhin
- Yisrael Noach — established a court in Niezhin (later moved)
- [[entities/people/avraham-schneersohn|Rabbi Avraham]] — settled in Kishinev

Despite being the eldest, Rabbi Baruch Shalom's early death in 1869 — only three years after his father — meant that the question of his role was largely overtaken by events. The [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Maharash]] consolidated the Lubavitch leadership.

---

## Legacy

Rabbi Baruch Shalom is remembered within Chabad tradition as a deeply pious and scholarly figure. His early passing prevented him from leaving the kind of institutional or literary legacy that some of his brothers achieved. However, his lineage continued — he had descendants who remained part of the broader Schneersohn family.
