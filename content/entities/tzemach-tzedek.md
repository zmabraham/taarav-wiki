# Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (Tzemach Tzedek)

**Third Lubavitcher Rebbe; grandson and son-in-law of the Mitteler Rebbe; led Chabad during Czar Nicholas I's harsh decrees against Jewish life.**

---

## Overview

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (1789–1866), known as the Tzemach Tzedek after his famous responsa work of the same name, was the third Lubavitcher Rebbe. He was the grandson of the Alter Rebbe (his mother was the Alter Rebbe's daughter) and also the son-in-law of the Mitteler Rebbe (he married his cousin, the Mitteler Rebbe's daughter). This dual connection to both of his predecessors through family ties was unusual and gave him a particular authority in the Chabad succession.

He led Chabad through one of its most challenging external periods: the reign of Czar Nicholas I (1825–1855), who undertook systematic efforts to force Jews to abandon traditional life. The most notorious of these was the cantonist system — the forced conscription of Jewish boys into the Russian army for 25 years, often beginning at age 12, with the goal of converting them to Christianity. The Tzemach Tzedek devoted enormous energy to combating these decrees, traveling to St. Petersburg multiple times to petition the government. He also led Chabad's resistance to the Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment), which he viewed as a spiritual threat comparable to the physical threats of forced conscription.

He was a prodigiously prolific author, with works spanning halacha (the Tzemach Tzedek responsa, for which he is named), Chassidic discourse, and Kabbalistic commentary. He had seven sons, each of whom led a separate Chassidic court — a remarkable dynastic branching that spread Chabad influence across a wide region.

## Life and Role

The Tzemach Tzedek's leadership spanned over forty years, making him the longest-reigning of the Chabad Rebbes. His tenure encompassed the full arc of Nicholas I's anti-Jewish policies, and his responses to these challenges — both practical (petitioning the government, advising individuals on how to navigate the cantonist decrees) and ideological (strengthening Chassidic commitment against the Haskalah) — shaped Chabad's orientation for generations.

His halachic work is exceptional in scope. The Tzemach Tzedek responsa (She'elot u-Teshuvot Tzemach Tzedek) runs to multiple volumes and covers virtually every area of Jewish law. This prolific responsorial writing, combined with equally prolific Chassidic works, made him a figure of authority across a broad spectrum of traditional Jewish communities, not only within Chabad.

His seven sons' branching into separate courts created a network of Chabad-affiliated communities throughout the Pale of Settlement. The youngest son, Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn (the Maharash), remained in Lubavitch and became the fourth Rebbe. The others — including Rabbi Yehuda Leib of Kopust and others — created what became known as the "branches" (anafim) of Chabad, some of which persist as separate Chassidic courts to this day.
