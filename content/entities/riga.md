# Riga (Exile Base 1927–1933)

**Capital of Latvia; base for the Rayatz's Chabad leadership after his expulsion from the Soviet Union in October 1927; six-year period of relative safety and international activity.**

---

## Overview

Riga, the capital of Latvia, served as the Rayatz's primary base of operations for six years (1927–1933) following his expulsion from the Soviet Union. After the dramatic events of 1927 — the arrest, the death sentence, the international rescue campaign, and the eventual commutation and expulsion — the Rayatz settled in Riga in October 1927. Latvia, as an independent republic between the wars, offered far greater religious freedom than the Soviet Union and allowed the Rayatz to lead Chabad openly, maintain correspondence with communities worldwide, and organize international activities.

Riga's Jewish community was substantial and vibrant in the interwar period. The city was a center of Jewish cultural and religious life in the Baltic region, with active Zionist, Orthodox, and communal organizations. The Rayatz's presence elevated it further as a Chassidic center.

From Riga, the Rayatz directed the network of Chabad communities that had survived or fled the Soviet Union, maintained contact with the underground network that continued to operate inside Soviet territory, and managed international correspondence and organizational work. The Riga period was one of the most productive in terms of institutional organization — schools, publications, and outreach were all developed from this base.

## Significance in Chabad History

The Riga period represents a recovery after the traumatic year of 1927. The Rayatz arrived in Riga with his reputation as a man who had faced a Soviet death sentence and maintained his dignity throughout — a status that commanded enormous moral authority in the Jewish world. From this position, he worked to rebuild and strengthen Chabad's networks.

The six years in Riga produced significant organizational and literary output. The Rayatz continued to write and teach; his disciples came to visit; emissaries were sent to communities across Europe and beyond. The Riga period established patterns of leadership — international correspondence, systematic educational work, publishing — that the Rayatz would carry forward to Warsaw and eventually to America.

In 1933, the Rayatz moved from Riga to Warsaw, which he deemed a more central location for reaching the large Jewish communities of Poland. The Riga chapter thus closed as a successful if limited period — a bridge between the Soviet persecution and the Polish years.
