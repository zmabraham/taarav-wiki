# Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneersohn

**Chief rabbi of Yekaterinoslav (Dnepropetrovsk), Ukraine; renowned Kabbalist; father of Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (the 7th Rebbe); arrested by the Soviets and died in exile in Kazakhstan in 1944.**

---

## Overview

Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneersohn was born in 1878 and served as the chief rabbi of Yekaterinoslav (later renamed Dnepropetrovsk, today Dnipro), one of Ukraine's major cities with a large Jewish community. He was a scholar of both revealed Torah and Kabbalah, and his major work — Likkutei Levi Yitzchak — demonstrates a deep mastery of Kabbalistic and Chassidic thought. He was also the father of Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn, the future seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe.

Despite the Soviet regime's suppression of religion, Rabbi Levi Yitzchak continued to serve as a rabbi and maintain Jewish religious life in Yekaterinoslav. This defiance eventually cost him his freedom. In 1939, the Soviet secret police (NKVD) arrested him. He was convicted on charges related to his religious activities and sentenced to exile in Kazakhstan — the remote Soviet practice of forcing prisoners to live in harsh, distant regions. He died in exile in the Kazakh city of Alma-Ata (today Almaty) in 1944, having never been reunited with his son who had escaped to America.

## Life and Role

Rabbi Levi Yitzchak's career as a rabbi in Ukraine unfolded during one of the most dangerous periods for Jewish religious leadership in modern history. The Soviet regime systematically dismantled Jewish communal infrastructure, arrested rabbis, and persecuted religious observance. Rabbi Levi Yitzchak continued his rabbinic work despite these dangers, maintaining the Jewish community of Yekaterinoslav through decades of Soviet pressure.

His scholarly output reflects an extraordinary breadth of learning. The Likkutei Levi Yitzchak contains insights on Torah, Talmud, Midrash, and Kabbalistic texts, and is studied in Chabad circles as a work of exceptional depth. He also composed poems and prayers that were preserved by his family and community. His wife Rebbetzin Chana, who shared his exile in Kazakhstan, was herself a remarkable woman who preserved his manuscripts and teachings.

The 7th Rebbe was deeply affected by his father's suffering and eventual death in exile. The Rebbe's profound knowledge of Kabbalah — rare even among Chassidic masters — is often connected to his father's legacy and to the manuscripts of Likkutei Levi Yitzchak that his mother preserved and eventually brought out of the Soviet Union. Rabbi Levi Yitzchak's passing in 1944, while his son was in Brooklyn working to rebuild Jewish life in America, represents one of the hidden personal tragedies behind the public narrative of Chabad's postwar revival.
