# Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (Ramash / 7th Rebbe)

**The seventh and final Lubavitcher Rebbe; son-in-law of the Rayatz; transformed Chabad into a global movement with emissaries in over 100 countries.**

---

## Overview

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn, known as the Ramash (an acronym of his name) and later simply "the Rebbe," was born on 11 Nissan 5662 (April 18, 1902) in Nikolaev, Ukraine, to Rabbi Levi Yitzchak Schneersohn and Rebbetzin Chana. He was a prodigious scholar from childhood, immersed in both Talmud and Chassidic texts. He married Chaya Mushka Schneersohn, the second daughter of the Rayatz (Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn), on 14 Kislev 5689 (November 27, 1928) in Warsaw — a wedding celebrated throughout the Chabad world as a "royal" marriage uniting two dynasties.

During the years before he accepted the Rebbiship, he studied engineering and science in Berlin and Paris, reflecting a breadth of secular learning unusual for someone of his background. He escaped Nazi Europe and arrived in New York on June 23, 1941, joining his father-in-law at 770 Eastern Parkway in Brooklyn. For nearly a decade, he served as head of the Chabad publishing house (Kehot) and the educational arm (Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch), all while remaining close to the Rayatz. After the Rayatz's passing on 10 Shvat 5710 (January 28, 1950), the Ramash was the consensus choice of Chabad leadership to assume the Rebbiship, which he formally accepted on 10 Shvat 5711 — exactly one year after the Rayatz's passing.

His forty-four years as Rebbe (1950–1994) saw Chabad transformed from a wounded movement rebuilding from the catastrophes of Soviet persecution and the Holocaust into a worldwide presence with thousands of emissary (shliach) families stationed in over 100 countries. His approach — sending young couples to serve as emissaries in every corner of the world — became one of the most successful Jewish outreach models of the modern era. He passed away on 3 Tammuz 5754 (June 12, 1994) in New York.

## Life and Role

As son-in-law of the Rayatz, the Ramash occupied a unique position even before becoming Rebbe — trusted, learned, and deeply connected to his father-in-law's leadership. During the Rayatz's final years in Brooklyn, he was the engine behind Chabad's institutional expansion in America. He edited and published Chassidic texts, organized educational programs, and was the conduit through which the Rayatz's vision reached a new generation of American-born and immigrant students.

After accepting the Rebbiship, the seventh Rebbe began an era of proactive outreach unprecedented in Chabad's history. His weekly public discourses (farbrengens), distributed in transcript form globally, became a primary vehicle for Chassidic teaching. He wrote thousands of letters (collected in the Igrot Kodesh series) addressing individuals' personal, spiritual, and communal questions. He established the network of Chabad Houses — homes and centers opened by young emissary couples — that spread Chabad's model of warmth, inclusivity, and Jewish identity renewal to nearly every Jewish community on earth.

The Rebbe placed enormous emphasis on education, publishing, and the sanctity of every individual Jew regardless of background or observance level. His outreach philosophy — that every Jew is infinitely valuable and that no community is too small or too remote to deserve a Chabad presence — drove the global shlichus network. By the time of his passing, over 1,500 emissary families were stationed worldwide; that number grew to several thousand in subsequent decades.

## Connection to the "Undaunted" Biography

In the biography of the Rayatz, the Ramash appears as a key figure: son-in-law, trusted aide, and eventual successor. The celebrated wedding of the Ramash and Chaya Mushka in Warsaw (1928) is a landmark event in the narrative. His presence beside the Rayatz during the escape from Nazi-occupied Warsaw (1940) and the rebuilding years in Brooklyn frames the story's conclusion. The Rayatz's passing and the Ramash's acceptance of leadership on 10 Shvat 5711 closes the biographical arc of the "Undaunted" period.
