# Liadi (Alter Rebbe's Town)

**Town in Belarus where the Alter Rebbe (1st Rebbe) established his court; associated with his flight and passing during Napoleon's invasion in 1812.**

---

## Overview

Liadi (also spelled Lyady) is a town in the Vitebsk region of Belarus that became the seat of Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi — the Alter Rebbe — for the latter portion of his leadership. The Alter Rebbe had moved there from his previous base in Liozna, and Liadi became so associated with him that "of Liadi" became part of his standard name.

Liadi is connected to the final chapter of the Alter Rebbe's life. When Napoleon invaded Russia in 1812, the Alter Rebbe — who had aligned himself spiritually and politically with the Russian cause against the French (despite Napoleon's offers of religious freedoms to Jews) — fled eastward with his family and followers to avoid falling under French occupation. He fled from Liadi, and his flight took him eastward through winter conditions. He passed away on 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812) in the town of Pyena, in present-day Kursk Oblast, Russia, during this flight. His passing in exile, on the road from Liadi, gave his final chapter a quality of redemptive self-sacrifice — fleeing not from enemies but toward an ideal.

The town also gives the Alter Rebbe his name. In Chabad literature and prayer, he is consistently referred to as "Rabbeinu HaZaken — the Alter Rebbe — of Liadi."

## Significance in Chabad History

Liadi marks the end of the first era of Chabad — the period of the Alter Rebbe's direct leadership. When the Alter Rebbe fled Liadi in 1812 and died on the road, his son Rabbi DovBer (the Mitteler Rebbe) assumed leadership and relocated the movement's center to Lubavitch. Liadi itself ceased to be a Chabad center after the Alter Rebbe's time.

The name "of Liadi" attached to the Alter Rebbe serves as a permanent geographical anchor in Chabad consciousness — connecting the movement's founder to a specific place in the Belarusian landscape that was, for decades, the home of Chabad's founder. The Alter Rebbe's love for Russia and his alignment with the Russian cause against Napoleon is symbolized by his departure from Liadi and his death on Russian soil, never to see the outcome of the war he had chosen.
