# Rostov-on-Don

**City in southern Russia where the Rashab (5th Rebbe) passed away on 2 Nissan 1920; where the Rayatz assumed leadership of Chabad.**

---

## Overview

Rostov-on-Don is a major city in southern Russia, situated on the Don River near the Sea of Azov. In Chabad history, it holds significance as the city where the Rashab (Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn, the fifth Rebbe) passed away on 2 Nissan 5680 (March 21, 1920), and where his son Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz) assumed leadership of Chabad as the sixth Rebbe.

The family had relocated to Rostov from Lubavitch during the disruptions of World War I (1915). What began as a temporary relocation became permanent when the war gave way to revolution and then civil war. The Rashab died in Rostov in 1920 — not in Lubavitch, where generations of Rebbes had lived and led, but in exile from the movement's historic home. This displacement was itself a harbinger of the even greater displacements that would characterize the Rayatz's subsequent leadership.

## Significance in Chabad History

The Rashab's passing in Rostov on 2 Nissan 1920 was a moment of profound transition. The fifth Rebbe had led Chabad through the final decades of relative stability in the Pale of Settlement, the founding of Tomchei Temimim (1897), the upheavals of World War I, and the beginning of the Bolshevik era. His death came at the moment when the full weight of Soviet anti-religious policy was beginning to fall on Jewish life.

The Rayatz — then 40 years old — assumed leadership in Rostov. He had already been deeply involved in his father's work and had been learning at the Rashab's side for decades. The transition from son to leader, from student to Rebbe, occurred in this displaced city rather than in Lubavitch, reflecting the changed circumstances the movement now faced.

Rostov itself was not a center of Chabad activity in the way Lubavitch had been. The Rayatz's subsequent leadership moved northward — to Moscow, Leningrad, and the broader Soviet network — before being driven into exile. Rostov's significance in Chabad history is concentrated in this single transitional moment: the end of the Rashab's leadership and the beginning of the Rayatz's.
