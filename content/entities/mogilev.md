# Mogilev (Historically Significant City)

**City in eastern Belarus; historically significant to early Chabad history; part of the Pale of Settlement; connected to early Chabad communities.**

---

## Overview

Mogilev (also known as Mahilyow in Belarusian, or Mohilev in older transliterations) is a major city in eastern Belarus on the Dnieper River. As part of the former Pale of Settlement — the region of the Russian Empire where Jews were legally required to reside — Mogilev had a substantial Jewish population for centuries. In the context of Chabad history, the city and its region are connected to the early decades of the movement, when Chabad was establishing itself in the Pale of Settlement under the Alter Rebbe and his successors.

The broader region of Belarus — Belorussia in Russian — was the heartland of early Chabad. Towns like Liozna (birthplace of the Alter Rebbe), Lubavitch (the movement's center for 103 years), Vitebsk, and others were all in this general territory. Mogilev, as one of the region's larger cities, had its own Chabad and Chassidic community and played a role in the regional network of Jewish life in which Chabad operated.

## Significance in Chabad History

Mogilev's significance in the "Undaunted" narrative is primarily as part of the broader geographic and cultural context of Belarusian Jewish life — the world in which the Chabad movement was born and developed. The Pale of Settlement, of which Mogilev was a part, was the world of the Alter Rebbe's community, the Mitteler Rebbe's court, and the subsequent generations of Chabad development.

The city's Jewish community experienced the full trajectory of Eastern European Jewish history: relative stability under the Czars, the upheavals of World War I and the Russian Revolution, the Soviet period of religious suppression, and ultimately the Holocaust. Like virtually every other Jewish community in the region, the Jewish population of Mogilev was devastated during the Nazi occupation.
