# Otwock (Polish Court 1930s)

**Town near Warsaw where the Rayatz established his court and residence during the Polish period (1930s); where he was living when Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939.**

---

## Overview

Otwock (also transliterated Otvotsk) is a town approximately 23 kilometers southeast of Warsaw, situated in a forested area that made it a popular summer resort for Warsaw's Jewish community. During the 1930s, the Rayatz established his primary residence and Chassidic court in Otwock, using it as a base from which to lead the movement while maintaining proximity to Warsaw's Jewish community and organizational infrastructure.

The Tomchei Temimim yeshiva was also relocated to Otwock during this period. Having the yeshiva near the Rebbe — as it had been in Lubavitch — was consistent with the Chabad tradition and allowed close supervision and regular contact between the Rebbe and his students.

Otwock holds particular significance as the place where the Rayatz was living on September 1, 1939, when Germany invaded Poland. The town, being near Warsaw, was quickly caught up in the chaos of the invasion. The subsequent weeks — trapped in an Otwock that was soon in Nazi-occupied territory, waiting for the diplomatic rescue to materialize — were among the most terrifying of the Rayatz's life.

## Significance in Chabad History

Otwock's significance is primarily as the setting for the opening of the Nazi chapter in the Rayatz's biography. The pleasant resort town, where Chassidim gathered around their Rebbe and where Tomchei Temimim students learned, became a trap within days of September 1, 1939. The Rayatz and his family were now in Nazi-occupied territory, unable to leave through normal means.

The rescue effort that eventually freed the Rayatz involved American diplomats, German military contacts who were willing to provide safe passage (acting through channels that included the personal intervention of the German Foreign Ministry at American request), and a journey through occupied territory to safety. The image of the Rayatz being spirited out of Otwock and Warsaw — a man who had already survived a Soviet death sentence now facing the Nazi genocide — is the culminating tension of the "Undaunted" narrative.

The Otwock community that the Rayatz left behind shared, in large part, the fate of Polish Jewry. The forests that had made Otwock a pleasant resort became, in some cases, sites of mass murder during the German occupation.
