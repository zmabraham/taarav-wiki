# Max Rhoade (American Lawyer / Rescuer)

**American lawyer and advocate whose intervention was pivotal in securing the release and exile of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn from Soviet imprisonment in 1927.**

---

## Overview

Max Rhoade was an American lawyer who played a pivotal role in the diplomatic rescue of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz) from Soviet imprisonment in 1927. When the Rayatz was arrested, sentenced to death, and then facing a three-year internal exile, Rhoade worked through diplomatic channels and international pressure to secure first the commutation of the death sentence and then the Rebbe's release and permission to leave the Soviet Union entirely.

Rhoade represented the practical arm of a broader international effort: Jewish organizations, American Chabad representatives, and diplomatic figures from multiple countries were mobilized to apply pressure on the Soviet government. The Soviets, while ideologically committed to their policies, were sensitive to international opinion in these early years of the Communist regime when they still sought international legitimacy. Rhoade's legal and diplomatic work helped translate this international pressure into the specific outcome: the Rayatz's expulsion from the Soviet Union in October 1927 and his arrival in Riga, Latvia.

## Life and Role

The international campaign to free the Rayatz in 1927 involved multiple actors working on different fronts: American Chabad representative Israel Jacobson working with the State Department; European Jewish organizations lobbying their governments; and lawyers like Rhoade working through legal and diplomatic channels. The Soviet government received representations from German, American, and other foreign officials, all arguing for the Rebbe's release.

Rhoade's specific legal expertise was in navigating the formal aspects of this pressure — understanding what arguments were likely to resonate with Soviet authorities and how to frame the case in terms that went beyond purely religious pleading (which would have been counterproductive) to arguments about international law, Soviet reputation, and the political costs of maintaining a death sentence against a religious leader who had attracted international attention.

The success of the campaign — the death sentence reduced to exile, and then exile converted to expulsion from the USSR entirely — was one of the rare victories against Soviet repression in the 1920s. Rhoade's contribution to this outcome made him a figure of gratitude in Chabad history, an example of a non-Chassidic ally whose professional skills and advocacy made a life-saving difference.
