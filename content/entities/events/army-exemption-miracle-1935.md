# Miraculous Army Exemption (1935)

On 11 Tishrei 5696 (1935), R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin received a complete military exemption from the Soviet army through a combination of the Rebbe's advice, personal courage, and what he describes as an open miracle.

## Key Facts
| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Date | 11 Tishrei 5696 (October 1935) |
| Location | Yegarovsk (military induction center, ~100 km from Moscow) |
| Method | Wore reading glasses to deceive eye doctor |
| Rebbe's instruction | "Wear glasses, and God will help him for good" |
| Result | Complete permanent exemption from Soviet military service |

## Description
Background: The author needed to present himself for military induction at Yegarovsk. Service would have meant being forced to eat treif food and violate Shabbos; one known case of a chassid who refused treif in the army ended in a death sentence for "undermining the Red Army." Additionally, the NKVD was seeking a young man matching the author's description, and his situation was desperate.

The Rebbe's written response (conveyed via R. Yaakov Maskalik): "(1) For the delusions and imaginings — subject your thoughts to thoughts of Torah or an intellectual matter, and do not be alone; God will help you. (2) Wear glasses, and God will help him for good."

The author consulted R. Nissan Nemenov and R. Yaakov Maskalik, who both said to proceed if the Rebbe said so. He obtained reading glasses (prescribed for elderly people) from a medical clinic by pretending he had poor vision.

At the induction, during the eye examination, he wore the glasses and misread a letter on the eye chart. When the doctor was about to test him with the clinic's own glasses (which would have exposed the deception), the doctor suddenly changed his mind and said "you have your own glasses, put those on." The author repeated the misidentification. The doctor wrote something and dismissed him.

When military service booklets were distributed, the author's booklet showed he was completely unfit — not even subject to first-year service. He then brazenly entered the commander's office and obtained all four required official stamps through an act of extraordinary chutzpah.

He immediately fled Yegarovsk and traveled to Moscow for the final days of Sukkos (Hoshana Rabbah and Shemini Atzeres), then fled to Voronezh.
