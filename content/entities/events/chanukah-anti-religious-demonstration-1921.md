# Chanukah Anti-Religious Demonstration in Lipovetsh (1921)

A public anti-religious demonstration organized by the Yevsektsia in Lipovetsh during Chanukah 5682 (winter 1921), specifically targeting the Levin family and R. Efraim Shmuel Levin's cheder. Considered a turning point when the family first felt threatened not just physically but spiritually.

## Description
During Chanukah 5682, the Yevsektsia organized a large public parade through the streets of Lipovetsh against religion. Old men wore red neckties; students, young people and children marched. The demonstrators carried caricatures mocking R. Efraim Shmuel Levin — depicting him teaching students — and made fun of the cheder, the Torah, and all religious matters.

This was the first time the family felt threatened spiritually, not just physically. Leah Beila Levin (the author's mother) responded with equanimity, saying: "If they've coupled our family with God and the Torah and make fun of all of us together, that's a great honor for us."

The demonstration was part of a broader Yevsektsia campaign including mock trials, anti-religious theater performances on Yom Kippur, and pressure on families to send children to Soviet schools.
