# Trans-Siberian Escape of Chabad Refugees (1940–1941)

A mass escape of Jewish refugees — including many Chabad students, rabbis, and families — from Nazi-threatened Europe via a remarkable route through the Soviet Union, Japan, and ultimately to America or Shanghai.

## The Route

**Vilna/Kovna → Trans-Siberian Railroad (Soviet Union) → Vladivostok → Kobe, Japan → Shanghai or USA**

## Background

After Germany invaded Poland in September 1939, thousands of Jewish refugees fled east to Vilna (then under Lithuanian, later Soviet control). Vilna became a gathering point for those seeking escape.

## The Visa Solution: Chiune Sugihara

In 1940, [[entities/people/chiune-sugihara]], the Japanese Vice-Consul in Kovna (Kaunas), Lithuania, defied instructions from his government and issued thousands of Japanese transit visas to Jewish refugees. These visas enabled travel through the Soviet Union on the Trans-Siberian Railroad to Japan.

## The Journey

1. **Vilna/Kovna** — Gather, obtain visas
2. **Trans-Siberian Railroad** — Cross the Soviet Union (~7 days)
3. **Vladivostok** — Board ships to Japan
4. **[[entities/places/kobe]], Japan** — First stop; temporary refuge
5. From Kobe: Most proceeded to **[[entities/places/shanghai]]**, China; some (including Rayatz's secretaries) continued to the USA

## Chabad Personnel Who Used This Route

- R' [[entities/people/moshe-leib-rothstein]], R' [[entities/people/shmuel-zalmanow]], R' [[entities/people/yitzchak-dov-aushpal]] — Rayatz's secretaries; arrived New York Parshas Noach, Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan 5701 via San Francisco
- Numerous Tomchei Temimim yeshiva students and faculty

## Shanghai Yeshiva

Those who ended up in Shanghai maintained a functioning Tomchei Temimim yeshiva throughout the war years, under R' [[entities/people/yitzchak-meir-gurary]] and others.
