# Rescue of the Lubavitch Library from Europe (1940–1941)

The effort to save the Lubavitch Library — the personal collection of [[entities/people/rayatz-yosef-yitzchak-schneersohn]] (Rayatz) — from Nazi-occupied Europe during WWII.

## The Library

The [[entities/organizations/lubavitch-library]] comprised some 13,000–23,000 volumes including rare manuscripts and early printed books, built over 15+ years beginning with the 1924 purchase of R' [[entities/people/shmuel-weiner]]'s collection. It was stored in Otwock, Poland (Rayatz's base until 1939) at the time of the German invasion.

## Rescue Operation

Attorney [[entities/people/aharon-blum]] (Warsaw) הי"ד agreed to arrange the export for **$1,000** total: $500 paid before Rayatz departed, $500 upon completion.

**What was rescued**: 100 boxes of books were shipped from Warsaw via Stockholm to New York. They arrived in **Sivan 5701** (summer 1941). These books eventually became the core of the library at 770 Eastern Parkway.

## What Was Lost

| Loss | Details |
|------|---------|
| 17 boxes | Left in Warsaw; disappeared; fate unknown |
| 3 boxes of manuscripts | Deposited at the US Embassy in Warsaw; lost when the US entered WWII (Dec 1941) and the embassy closed |

## Post-War Recovery

After the war, R' [[entities/people/shlomo-zilberberg]] and R' [[entities/people/pinchas-klein]] — survivors in Warsaw — located some of the library's books that had survived. These were held by the Jewish Central Committee's historical division. Some books were eventually returned to Chabad.

## Attorney Blum's Fate

Attorney Aharon Blum himself perished in the Holocaust (הי"ד), never receiving the second $500 payment.
