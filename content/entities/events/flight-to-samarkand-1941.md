# Flight to Samarkand (1941)

When Nazi Germany invaded the Soviet Union in summer 1941, a large portion of the underground Chabad community fled eastward to Samarkand in Soviet Central Asia (present-day Uzbekistan). R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin and his family were among those who made this journey.

## Description
The German invasion of June 1941 sent millions of Soviet citizens fleeing eastward. The Chabad underground community seized the opportunity to relocate en masse to Samarkand, where they would be far from the front lines and — crucially — in a region where local authorities were less familiar with them and where enforcement of anti-religious laws was less rigorous.

The Levin family, including R. Yisrael Yehuda, his wife Figa Rivka, and their young son Efraim Shmuel, made this journey. The conditions were harsh — overcrowded trains, scarce food, and the uncertainty of wartime displacement.

In Samarkand, the community established a functioning underground yeshiva (Tomchei Temimim) and maintained communal life. This period saw the arrival of many significant Chabad personalities who would shape the postwar community, including R. Mendel Futerfas, R. Abba Pliskin, and others.
