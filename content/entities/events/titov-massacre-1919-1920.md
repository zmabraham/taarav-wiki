# Massacre of Titov (1919-1920)

One of the most catastrophic events of the Ukrainian Civil War pogroms in the Vinnytsia region. The bandit leader Sokolov and his forces massacred nearly the entire Jewish population of Titov — approximately 6,000 of 8,000 Jews — burning the synagogue with those who had taken refuge inside.

## Description
When Sokolov's forces arrived at Titov, the young men of the town chose to resist rather than submit. This fatal decision led to a massacre. The townspeople who had not been killed in the initial fighting sought refuge in the synagogue; Sokolov's men burned it down with the Jewish refugees inside.

The smoke from the burning synagogue was visible from Zhivotov, miles away, where R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin (then about eight years old) and his family were fleeing through the fields.

The surviving orphans — mostly small children — were subsequently gathered and brought to Lipovetsh and other nearby cities. The Soviet Yevsektsia then took control of these orphans, placing them in anti-religious orphanages (Priyutin) and denying them any connection to Judaism, including the right to say Kaddish for their murdered parents.
