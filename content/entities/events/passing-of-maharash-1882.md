# Passing of the Maharash (1882)

Three days after Yom Kippur 5643 (October 1882), Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn — the Maharash, fourth Lubavitcher Rebbe — passed away in Lubavitch at age forty-eight. His passing left young Yosef Yitzchak without a grandfather and set in motion a decade-long interregnum before the Rashab formally assumed leadership.

## Description

Before his passing, the Maharash reportedly signaled to his wife that his death was imminent, telling her he would "rise up thirty-two steps in a spiritual way." He passed away three days after Yom Kippur at the relatively young age of forty-eight.

In the weeks following his death, Chabad elders convened a leadership conclave in Lubavitch, imploring the Rebbe's sons to assume leadership. The sons initially refused but consented to recite maamarim (chasidic discourses) privately. The Rashab would not formally accept the position of Rebbe until Rosh HaShanah 1893, eleven years after his father's passing.

Two-year-old Yosef Yitzchak (the future Rayatz) was present in Lubavitch at this time. One account describes him being brought to his father's study during mourning, where he was moved by intense praying.
