# Escape from the USSR Using Polish Identity (1946)

R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin escaped from the Soviet Union in 5706 (1946) by using forged Polish identity documents under the name "Naftali Bat." The escape was part of a larger organized exodus of Chabad chassidim who used the postwar Polish repatriation agreement to leave Soviet territory.

## Description
After years of living underground in Samarkand, R. Yisrael Yehuda — along with many other Chabad chassidim — seized the opportunity created by the Soviet–Polish repatriation agreement. Poles who had been in Soviet territory were permitted to return to Poland, and a number of Soviet Jews obtained Polish identity documents to take advantage of this window.

R. Yisrael Yehuda's Polish identity ("Naftali Bat") had been obtained from a Polish refugee named Waldman who was living in Samarkand during the war. This alias had served him earlier as cover from Soviet military draft authorities who were searching for him.

The escape required extraordinary courage and careful preparation. Many members of the Chabad underground community made this journey, which took them through war-ravaged Poland on their way westward toward eventual aliya to Eretz Yisrael. The introduction to the memoir records that the author passed through Samarkand, Tashkent, Moscow, Kyiv, Riga, and Vilna, and mentions Baku, Kutais, and Kulash as other cities he lived in over the years.
