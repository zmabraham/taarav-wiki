# Founding of Tomchei Temimim (1897)

On September 15, 1897 (18 Elul 5657), the Rashab officially opened Yeshivas Tomchei Temimim in Lubavitch — the revolutionary institution that would train generations of Chabad emissaries and become the backbone of the movement's survival through Soviet persecution and the Holocaust.

## Description

The Rashab announced his revolutionary plan at the wedding reception for his son Yosef Yitzchak (the Rayatz) and Nechama Dina on September 12, 1897. The yeshivah would integrate rigorous Talmud study with chasidic philosophy — a novel combination that ran counter to both the traditional Lithuanian yeshivah model and to the growing secularist movements.

Three days later, on 18 Elul, studies officially began for the first group of students. At Simchat Torah 1898, the institution was officially named and students given the title "temimim" (complete ones). The young Rayatz was appointed executive director in September 1898 at age eighteen, a role that launched his lifelong career in organizational leadership.

The yeshivah's students — drilled in both halachic learning and chasidic farbrengens — would become the primary vehicle for Chabad's underground operations during Soviet persecution. During the 1920s and 1930s, Tomchei Temimim branches operated covertly across the Soviet Union. Branches also opened in Warsaw, Hebron (as Toras Emes), and eventually the United States (1940).

## Significance

Tomchei Temimim represented a paradigm shift in Chabad education: producing activist-scholars ("soldiers," in the Rashab's metaphor at Simchat Torah 1900) rather than purely scholarly figures. This organizational model was critical to Chabad's 20th-century expansion.
