# Petliura Pogroms in the Vinnytsia Region (1919-1920)

A series of devastating pogroms carried out by various armed bands during the Ukrainian Civil War (1919-1920), primarily affecting the shtetlach of the Vinnytsia region including Zhivotov, Titov, Dashov, and surrounding areas. Multiple competing armed factions — Petliura forces, Denikin forces, and independent bandit gangs — all committed atrocities against the Jewish population.

## Description
The pogroms in this region began in earnest in summer 5679 (1919) when Haidamak (pogromist) bands arrived in Zhivotov. At first bands would extort money, food, and labor but avoided mass killings. As the civil war intensified between Denikin and Petliura forces, the violence escalated.

**Zhivotov:** The town repeatedly suffered raids from different bands. The community hid in a secret loft space ("Makif") under the synagogue roof. A local hero named Yankl, who slept in the synagogue, refused under beatings and threat of death to reveal the hiding place to raiders, saving the community. One named bandit, "Sokolov," extorted the town but was appeased by the community going out to meet him with bread, water, and a Torah scroll. He still had ten men killed, including the rabbi's wife.

**Titov:** When Sokolov reached Titov, the townspeople's young men resisted him. He massacred nearly the entire population. The remaining survivors fled into the synagogue; the synagogue was burned with the people inside. Of approximately 8,000 Jews, fewer than 2,000 survived — mostly small orphaned children. The smoke of the burning synagogue was visible from Zhivotov.

**Displacement:** After Purim 5680, the Levin family and the entire community of Zhivotov fled westward by wagon to Lipovetsh. The roads were deep in mud and the family experienced extreme hardship.
