# NKVD Raid on Kyiv Yeshiva Students

A raid by plainclothes NKVD agents on a group of Chabad yeshiva students studying in the women's gallery of a Kyiv synagogue. Most senior students escaped; younger students were detained and interrogated.

## Key Facts
| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Location | Women's gallery of a Kyiv synagogue |
| Timing | After Simchas Torah (autumn), specific year in the early 1930s |
| Perpetrators | Two plainclothes NKVD agents; uniformed officers at the door |
| Detained | Moshe Binyamin Kaplan, Moshe Aharon Geisinsky, and others; the author's luggage confiscated |
| Escaped | R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin and others |

## Description
The students were studying Gemara in the women's gallery of the synagogue when two well-dressed, clean-shaven men entered and approached the younger students. They examined the Gemara and began testing the students. Then they announced that everyone was under arrest; armed uniformed officers were already at the door.

Several of the senior students managed to escape through windows. The author himself escaped but left all his luggage — containing all his identity papers, including his birth certificate and Kyiv passport. The younger students (Kaplan, Geisinsky, and others) were taken to the NKVD (Nekvid).

The NKVD interrogators — apparently Jewish and sympathetic — kept the passports but returned other documents and effectively told the students to flee immediately. A lawyer they consulted said each of them deserved not less than five years imprisonment. The author was advised by fellow students to flee immediately by boat down the Dnieper to Chernigov, which he did.

Moshe Aharon Geisinsky later published his memoirs of this incident in "Kfar Chabad" journal #379 (5749/1989).
