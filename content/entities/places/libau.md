# Libau (Liepāja) — ליבאוו

**Latvian Port City; Chabad Synagogue; Home of R' Yaakov Landa**

**Location**: Western Latvia (now Liepāja, third-largest city in Latvia)
**Jewish Population**: Approximately 7,000–9,000 Jews in early 20th century
**Significance**: Chabad synagogue; served by R' Yaakov Landa who later became rabbi of Bnei Brak for 50 years
**Holocaust**: Jewish community largely annihilated 1941

---

## Overview

Libau (Latvian: Liepāja; Russian: Либава / Libava; Yiddish: ליבאַוו / Libav) was one of Latvia's most important cities — a major Baltic port with a substantial and diverse Jewish community. Within Chabad history, Libau is especially notable as the city where [[entities/people/yaakov-landa|R' Yaakov Landa]] served before moving to Israel to become rabbi of Bnei Brak for approximately 50 years.

---

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Setting

Libau's location made it distinctive:

- **Baltic port**: Latvia's most important ice-free port, connecting the country to world trade
- **Location**: Southwestern Latvia on the Baltic Sea
- **Cosmopolitan**: Port cities attract diverse populations and are generally more open
- **Access**: Emigration point for many Eastern European Jews heading to America and elsewhere

### Jewish Community

Libau had one of Latvia's oldest and most established Jewish communities:

- **Population**: Approximately 7,000–9,000 Jews by the early 20th century
- **History**: Jewish presence from at least the 17th century
- **Diversity**: Mix of religious, Zionist, and secular Jews; various Chassidic groups
- **Commercial prominence**: Jews heavily involved in Libau's port commerce and trade
- **Education**: Multiple Jewish schools, yeshivos, and cultural organizations

## Chabad in Libau

### Chabad Synagogue

Libau had a dedicated Chabad synagogue:

- **Established community**: A permanent Chabad presence in the city
- **Nusach Ari**: Prayer according to the Chabad liturgical tradition
- **Community services**: Full range of Jewish communal services within the Chabad community

### R' Yaakov Landa in Libau

The most historically significant Chabad figure associated with Libau:

- **Service**: Served as Chabad rabbi of Libau in the interwar period
- **Role**: Provided rabbinical leadership, halachic guidance, and community organization
- **Escape**: Survived the Holocaust — escaped before or during the German invasion
- **Later career**: After reaching Israel, served as rabbi of [[entities/places/bnei-brak|Bnei Brak]] for approximately 50 years — an extraordinary tenure
- **Legacy**: One of the longest-serving rabbis in Israeli history; his Libau experience shaped his later leadership

---

## Libau During the Interwar Period (1918–1940)

### Under Latvian Independence

Libau's Jewish community flourished:

- **Constitutional protections**: Latvia's minority rights laws protected Jewish communal life
- **Commercial activity**: Port commerce sustained the Jewish community economically
- **Cultural life**: Active Jewish press, theater, and educational institutions
- **Political participation**: Jewish community leaders participated in Latvian civic life

### Connection to Riga Chabad Headquarters

When the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] lived in [[entities/places/riga|Riga]] (1927–1933):

- **Accessibility**: Libau Chassidim could travel to Riga relatively easily
- **Network**: Part of the Latvian Chabad network under Rayatz's direction
- **Communications**: Maintained regular contact with Chabad leadership

---

## The Holocaust

### Soviet Occupation (1940–1941)

- **Annexation**: Soviet Union occupied Latvia (June 1940)
- **Restrictions**: Religious life suppressed; Jewish institutions closed
- **Arrests**: Jewish leaders among those arrested

### Nazi Occupation (1941–1944)

- **Invasion**: Germany captured Libau in late June 1941
- **Immediate terror**: Massacres began within days of occupation
- **Jewish fate**: Most of Libau's Jews were murdered in 1941, with some used for forced labor before being killed
- **Destruction**: The Jewish community was largely annihilated
- **R' Landa's survival**: R' Yaakov Landa was among the fortunate who escaped before the catastrophe

---

## Modern Liepāja

Today Liepāja is Latvia's third-largest city:

- **Population**: Approximately 70,000 (tiny Jewish minority)
- **Memory**: Holocaust memorials; some acknowledgment of Jewish history
- **Jewish Community**: Minimal present Jewish presence

---

## Libau's Place in Chabad History

### A Survivor Who Led

The most important Chabad connection to Libau is through R' Yaakov Landa:

- **Libau origin**: His rabbinical career began in this Latvian port city
- **Israeli legacy**: His 50-year tenure in Bnei Brak made him one of the most influential Orthodox rabbis in Israel
- **Bridge**: He bridged the pre-Holocaust world of Latvian Chabad with the Israeli Orthodox community
