# Crown Heights, Brooklyn — קראון הייטס

**Chabad World Headquarters Since 1940**

**Location**: Brooklyn, New York (approximately 5 km southeast of Manhattan)  
**Chabad Headquarters**: 1940–present — 770 Eastern Parkway  
**Significance**: Home of 770, the Rebbe's residence, and the global Chabad-Lubavitch movement  
**Population**: Approximately 150,000 (approximately 10–15% Jewish in broader area; Chabad community approximately 10,000–20,000)

---

## Overview

Crown Heights is a neighborhood in central Brooklyn, New York, that has served as the world headquarters of the Chabad-Lubavitch movement since 1940. It was here that the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] established himself after escaping war-torn Europe, and it was here that the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe (Menachem Mendel Schneerson)]] led the global expansion of Chabad from 1951 to 1994.

At the heart of Crown Heights is 770 Eastern Parkway — the iconic building known simply as "770." This building houses the main synagogue, the Rebbe's office and study, and has been replicated in miniature form in Chabad centers worldwide.

Crown Heights was also the site of the 1991 Crown Heights riots, a tragic episode that tested the community but ultimately demonstrated its resilience.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Crown Heights is located in Brooklyn:

- **Location**: Central Brooklyn, approximately 5 km southeast of Manhattan
- **Boundaries**: Roughly Atlantic Avenue to the north, Empire Boulevard to the south, Washington Avenue to the west, Ralph Avenue to the east
- **Terrain**: Generally flat, typical Brooklyn urban landscape
- **Climate**: Humid subtropical — hot summers, cold winters

### Neighborhood History

- **Development**: Developed in late 19th and early 20th centuries
- **Architecture**: Brownstones, apartment buildings, and institutional buildings
- **Demographics**: Historically mixed; has had Jewish, African-American, and Caribbean communities

### The Chabad Community

- **Population**: Approximately 10,000–20,000 Chabad chassidim live in Crown Heights
- **Institutions**: Dozens of synagogues, schools, and community institutions
- **Growth**: Community grew significantly under the Rebbe's leadership
- **Global Hub**: Central point of worldwide Chabad movement

## 770 Eastern Parkway

### The Building

770 Eastern Parkway is the iconic headquarters of Chabad:

- **Address**: 770 Eastern Parkway, Brooklyn, New York
- **Architecture**: Gothic-inspired brick building constructed in 1920s
- **Purchase**: Acquired by the Rayatz in 1940
- **Expansion**: Building expanded and modified over the decades

### Spaces Within 770

The building contains:

- **Main Synagogue**: Large sanctuary used for daily prayers, Shabbos, and Yom Tov
- **The Rebbe's Study**: Office and study where the Rebbe worked and received visitors
- **Small Synagogue (Zal)**: Used for smaller gatherings and study
- **Library**: Chabad library with rare manuscripts and books
- **Offices**: Administrative offices for Chabad organizations
- **Living Quarters**: The Rebbe's residence (historically)

### Global Symbolism

770 has become a symbol replicated worldwide:

- **Miniature 770s**: Many Chabad centers have buildings modeled on 770
- **Recognition**: The building is instantly recognizable to Chabad chassidim globally
- **Pilgrimage**: Chassidim worldwide visit 770 when in New York
- **Central Focus**: The building is the spiritual center of global Chabad

## The Rayatz in Crown Heights (1940–1950)

### Arrival in America

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] arrived in America in March 1940:

- **Escape Route**: Fled [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]] → [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]] → [[entities/places/riga|Riga]] → Sweden → America
- **Establishment**: Settled in Crown Heights and established headquarters at 770
- **Challenge**: Rebuilding Chabad after the Holocaust
- **Vision**: Laying groundwork for future expansion

### Establishing Institutions

The Rayatz founded:

- **Merkos L'Inyonei Chinuch**: Central educational organization (1942)
- **Kehot Publication Society**: Publishing house for Chabad texts (1942)
- **Machne Israel**: Social services organization (1941)
- **Beth Rivkah**: Schools for girls
- **Tomchei Temimim**: Yeshiva system reestablished in America

### WWII and Holocaust

The Rayatz's years in Crown Heights coincided with:

- **Holocaust Unfolding**: Destruction of European Jewry
- **Anxiety for Soviet Chassidim**: Concern for those trapped behind Iron Curtain
- **Uncertainty**: Fate of family and followers unknown
- **Resilience**: Despite tragedy, planning for future rebuilding

## The Rebbe's Leadership (1951–1994)

### Assumption of Leadership

The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe (Menachem Mendel Schneerson)]] became the 7th Rebbe in 1951:

- **Reluctance**: Initially refused leadership; accepted only after pressure from chassidim
- **Vision**: Transform Chabad from insular chassidic group to global movement
- **Innovation**: Pioneered shluchim (emissary) system

### Global Expansion from Crown Heights

From Crown Heights, the Rebbe directed:

- **Shluchim System**: Sent emissaries to Jewish communities worldwide
- **Institutions**: Schools, synagogues, and centers established globally
- **Outreach**: Innovated approaches to Jewish outreach and education
- **Correspondence**: Voluminous correspondence with followers worldwide

### Sunday Dollars

The Rebbe's Sunday gatherings became legendary:

- **Distribution**: Every Sunday, the Rebbe distributed dollars for charity
- **Crowds**: Thousands of people lined up to see the Rebbe and receive dollars
- **Blessings**: The Rebbe gave blessings and brief words of encouragement
- **Global Reach**: Visitors from around the world attended

## The 1991 Crown Heights Riots

### The Incident

August 19–21, 1991, Crown Heights experienced three days of racial unrest:

- **Car Accident**: A car in the Rebbe's motorcade struck and killed a young Caribbean-American child (Gavin Cato)
- \*\*Yankel Rosenbaum': Shortly after, a Jewish man (Yankel Rosenbaum) was stabbed to death by a mob
- **Riots**: Three days of unrest, violence, and property damage
- **Tension**: Longstanding tensions between Black and Jewish communities erupted

### The Rebbe's Response

The Rebbe's response became famous:

- **Call for Calm**: The Rebbe called for peace and calm
- **No Retaliation**: Urged no revenge or retaliation
- **Positive Action**: Called for increased Torah study and good deeds as response
- **Rebuilding**: After riots, urged community to rebuild and strengthen

### Aftermath

- **Healing**: Gradual healing between communities
- **Institutions**: Community institutions rebuilt and strengthened
- **Memory**: The riots remain a difficult but important memory
- **Resilience**: Community emerged stronger

- **Continued Center**: 770 remains the spiritual center
