# Lubavitch (ליובאוויטש)

**Small town in Smolensk Governorate, Russia; seat of the Chabad dynasty from 1813 to 1915; home of Tomchei Temimim.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Country** | Russia (now in Smolensk Oblast, Russia) |
| **Period as Chabad seat** | 5573–5675 (1813–1915) |
| **Rebbes based here** | Mitteler Rebbe through Rebbe Rashab (Rebbes 2–5) |
| **Key institution** | Tomchei Temimim yeshiva (founded 5657/1897) |
| **Abandoned** | ~5675 (1914/1915) due to WWI |

## Overview

Lubavitch was the central town of Chabad Chasidus for over a century, from when the Mitteler Rebbe established the court there in 5573 (1813) until WWI forced the Rashab to flee. The word "Lubavitch" became synonymous with the entire Chabad movement (hence "Lubavitcher Rebbe").

The physical layout included:
- The Rebbe's residence
- The "Kleiner Zal" (small hall / beis medrash) — where the Rashab gave his Chassidic discourses, standing in the southeast corner
- The "Ulam Gadol" (large hall) — where older TT students studied, and where the Rashab began giving maamarim after the 5668 fire
- Multiple residential buildings for the Rebbe's family
- Yeshiva classrooms (the "Shilem's rooms," various residential facilities)
- "Binyamin's Shtiebel" — a small shul used for farbrengens
- Two water wells in the courtyard

## Tomchei Temimim at Lubavitch

Tomchei Temimim was founded in Lubavitch in 5657 (1897). It had multiple divisions:
- The large Ulam (hall) for advanced students
- Chadarim (classrooms) for younger students, opened ~5666 (1906)
- Evening/night learning culture — students often stayed awake through Friday nights for farbrengens

## Life in Lubavitch

Israel Jacobson's memoirs paint a vivid picture of student life:
- Students lived with local families who provided room and board ("essen teg" / eating days)
- Rebbetzin Rivka hosted students for regular meals
- Rebbetzin Sterna Sarah provided daily soup for younger students
- Students apprenticed in shechita at R' Shlomo Chaim Kutan's home
- Farbrengens ran from 11pm until near dawn on Friday nights
- High Holidays drew 1,000–1,500 visitors from across White Russia and Ukraine
- Seasonal migrations: many students went home for Passover, some stayed for Sukkos

## Key Events Here

- 5668 winter: Fire in the small hall; students fought the fire with buckets; Rashab and Rayatz came to survey after
- 5668 Shavuot: Rashab's discourse at Rebbetzin Rivka's Shavuot meal
- 5669 Elul: Widow from Zembin with two sons pleads for yeshiva admission
- 5673 Purim Katan (approx.): Famous farbrengen of R' Shmuel Gronem with the Arantchik student
