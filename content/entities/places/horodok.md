# Horodok (Haradak / Horodets)

**Small town near Vitebsk; base of Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk (1773–1777); residence of the Alter Rebbe (1781–1783)**

## Overview

Horodok (also spelled Haradak, Gorodok, or Horodets; Belarusian: Гарадок) is a small town located approximately 35–40 kilometers north of Vitebsk in present-day Belarus. During the late eighteenth century it was within the Vitebsk gubernia of the Russian Empire.

## Chabad History

Horodok's significance in early Chabad history derives from two distinct phases:

**1. Base of Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk (1773–1777)**

After the Rovna Assembly of 1772, when the Chassidic leadership gathered in the face of Mitnagdic opposition, Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk — who would become the most senior Chassidic leader in White Russia — settled near Vitebsk and used Horodok as his principal base of operations. For four years (1773–1777), it served as the de facto center of White Russian Chassidism, drawing disciples and petitioners from across the region.

Rabbi Menachem Mendel's tenure in Horodok was the period during which he came to know the young Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (later the Alter Rebbe), who had joined his circle after studying under the Maggid of Mezeritch. The relationship between them, forged partly in the Horodok years, would become foundational to Chabad's institutional development.

In תקל"ז (1777), Rabbi Menachem Mendel led a major group of White Russian Chassidim on aliyah to the Land of Israel, leaving Horodok behind.

**2. Residence of the Alter Rebbe (1781–1783)**

After Rabbi Menachem Mendel's departure to Israel, the Alter Rebbe eventually settled in Horodok himself from approximately תקמ"א to תקמ"ג (1781–1783). This was a transitional period in the Alter Rebbe's leadership: he was consolidating his role as the primary leader of Russian Chassidism, developing his distinctive approach to Chabad philosophy, and building the institutional structures that would define the movement. Horodok served as his residence before he moved to Lazna (Liozna), which would become his principal base for the next two decades.

## Later History

After the early Chassidic era, Horodok became an ordinary small town in the Vitebsk region. It suffered during both World Wars and the Holocaust, in which its Jewish community was destroyed.
