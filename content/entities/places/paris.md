# פאריז — Paris

**Country**: France (capital)  
**Chabad significance**: Post-war gathering point for Chabad refugees (1946–1950s); the seventh Rebbe's studies (1931–1941); major European Chabad center

---

## Overview

Paris occupies a distinctive place in twentieth-century Chabad history as both the place where the future seventh Rebbe studied for a decade and as the primary staging point in Western Europe where Chabad chassidim gathered after escaping the Soviet Union following WWII.

---

## The Seventh Rebbe in Paris (1931–1941)

After completing his studies at the University of Berlin (1928–1931), Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson — the future [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] — continued his studies in **Paris**, reportedly at the École Spéciale des Travaux Publics (engineering). He lived in Paris from 1931 until 1941.

The German occupation of France (June 1940) forced him and [[entities/people/rebbetzin-moussia|Rebbetzin Moussia]] to flee. They escaped to Lisbon and then to the United States, arriving in 1941 — shortly after the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] had arrived in 1940.

---

## Post-War Gathering of Chabad Chassidim

A major feature of the [[events/great-escape|Great Escape (1946–47)]] was that Paris became the first major Western staging ground for Chabad chassidim escaping the Soviet Union. The memoir of M. Shusterman (_Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu_) records **Paris (1947)** as a gathering point before further emigration decisions — to Israel, America, or elsewhere.

The _Undaunted_ biography of the Rayatz notes that the Chabad refugees who gathered in France after the war "became the nucleus of the largest Jewish community in Europe" — a reference to the eventual size of the French Jewish community, augmented significantly by post-war immigration.

---

## European Chabad Center

Paris today hosts one of the largest Chabad communities outside Israel and the United States. The post-war refugee community laid the foundation for this subsequent growth.
