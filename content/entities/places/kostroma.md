# קוסטרומה — Kostroma

**Location**: Northeast of Moscow, on the Volga River  
**Character**: Provincial Russian city  
**Chabad significance**: City designated as the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] place of internal exile (1927) before his full liberation

---

## Overview

Kostroma is a city approximately 350 km northeast of Moscow, situated on the Volga River. It is historically significant as one of the old Russian cities of the "Golden Ring." In Chabad history, it is known as the designated destination for the Rayatz's internal exile in 1927 — the third and final commutation of his sentence before international pressure secured his full release and expulsion from the Soviet Union.

---

## The Rayatz's Exile Sentence

After the Rayatz's **death sentence** was commuted to **10 years in [[entities/places/solovki|Solovki]]**, continuing international pressure resulted in a further commutation: **3 years of internal exile in Kostroma**. This meant he would be confined to Kostroma without freedom of movement, under Soviet supervision — a milder form of punishment than the Arctic labor camp.

The Rayatz arrived in **Malakhov** (a dacha village near Moscow) as a transitional point before potentially being sent to Kostroma. At Malakhov, continued diplomatic pressure succeeded in securing his full release and the right to leave the Soviet Union entirely. Thus the Rayatz never actually served his Kostroma exile — it was the sentence _from_ which he was liberated on 12-13 Tammuz 5687 (July 1927).

See [[events/rayatz-liberation-1927|Rayatz's Liberation and Exile (1927)]] for the full account.

---

## Historical Context

Internal exile (_ssylka_) was a standard Soviet (and earlier Czarist) punishment — less severe than a labor camp but still involving forced residence in a designated location, separation from family and community, and restrictions on movement. The choice of Kostroma — distant, cold, provincial — reflected the regime's intent to neutralize the Rayatz's influence while avoiding the international attention that outright imprisonment or execution would draw.
