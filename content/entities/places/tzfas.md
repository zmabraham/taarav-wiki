# Tzfat (Safed) — צפת

**Mystical City with Chabad Presence**

**Location**: Upper Galilee, northern Israel (approximately 900 meters above sea level)  
**Significance**: One of Judaism's Four Holy Cities; center of Kabbalah  
**Jewish Population**: Approximately 30,000 (mixed religious and secular)  
**Chabad Presence**: Active community, institutions, and outreach

---

## Overview

Tzfat (also spelled Safed or Tzfas; Hebrew: צפת / Tzfat; Arabic: صفد / Safed) is one of Judaism's four holy cities (along with [[entities/places/jerusalem|Jerusalem]], [[entities/places/hebron|Hebron]], and [[entities/places/tiberias|Tiberias]]). Located in the Upper Galilee at an elevation of approximately 900 meters, Tzfat has been a center of Jewish mysticism (_Kabbalah_) since the 16th century.

For Chabad, Tzfat has both historical and contemporary significance. It was the destination of the 1777 aliyah led by [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]] — an early Chassidic immigration that predated the formal Chabad movement. Today, Tzfat has an active Chabad community serving residents and the many tourists drawn to the city's mystical heritage.

**Important Clarification**: [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]], who led the 1777 aliyah, is actually buried in [[entities/places/tiberias|Tiberias]], not Tzfat, though the two cities are closely connected.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Tzfat is located in northern Israel:

- **Location**: Upper Galilee, approximately 130 km north of [[entities/places/jerusalem|Jerusalem]]
- **Elevation**: Approximately 900 meters (2,950 feet) above sea level
- **Terrain**: Mountainous with stunning views of the Galilee
- **Climate**: Mountain climate — cool to cold winters; hot, dry summers (less humid than coastal areas)

### The Holy City

Tzfat is one of Judaism's four holy cities:

1. **Jerusalem** — Holiest city; site of the Temple
2. **Hebron** — Tomb of the Patriarchs
3. **Tzfat** — Center of Kabbalah
4. **Tiberias** — Center of Torah learning; location of many rabbinic graves

## Jewish History in Tzfat

### Ancient and Medieval Period

Tzfat has ancient Jewish roots:

- **Biblical**: Mentioned in Tanach as a fortified city
- **Talmudic**: Jewish community in Talmudic times
- **Crusader Period**: Jewish community rebuilt after Crusader conquest
- **Mamluk Period**: Growing Jewish community

### The Golden Age (16th Century)

The 16th century was Tzfat's golden age:

- **Spanish Expulsion (1492)**: Jewish refugees from Spain settled in Tzfat
- **Kabbalistic Center**: Tzfat became the world center of Kabbalah
- **Great Kabbalists**:
  - [[entities/people/arizal|Rabbi Isaac Luria (the Ari / Arizal)]] — greatest Kabbalist
  - Rabbi Moshe Cordovero (Ramak) — Kabbalah teacher
  - Rabbi Yosef Caro — author of _Shulchan Aruch_ (code of Jewish law)
- **Institutions**: Yeshivos, synagogues, printing presses
- **Influence**: Tzfat's Kabbalah influenced all subsequent Jewish mysticism

### Decline and Renewal

- **17th–18th Centuries**: Decline due to epidemics, earthquakes, and economic difficulties
- **1777 Aliyah**: Renewal with arrival of Chassidic immigrants
- **19th Century**: Gradual rebuilding
- **20th Century**: Growth continued
- **1948**: Tzfat captured by Israel; Jewish community expanded

## The 1777 Aliyah

### Historical Context

The 1777 aliyah was a major Chassidic immigration:

- **Leaders**: Led by [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]] and [[entities/people/avraham-of-kalisk|Abraham of Kalisk]]
- **Numbers**: Approximately 300 Chassidim immigrated
- **Settlement**: Initially settled in Tzfat

### Connection to Chabad

- **Pre-Chabad**: This aliyah occurred before the formal Chabad movement (the Alter Rebbe was the Mitteler Rebbe's student)
- **Early Chassidism**: These early Chassidim were precursors to organized Chabad
- **Relocation**: Over time, many relocated to [[entities/places/tiberias|Tiberias]]

### Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk's Burial

**Important**: Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk is buried in [[entities/places/tiberias|Tiberias]], not Tzfat:

- **Burial Site**: His grave in Tiberias is a pilgrimage site
- **Confusion**: Often associated with Tzfat due to the 1777 aliyah's initial Tzfat settlement
- **Connection**: Tzfat and Tiberias are closely connected geographically and historically

## Modern Tzfat

### Contemporary Jewish Community

Today's Tzfat:

- **Population**: Approximately 30,000 residents
- **Religious Character**: Mix of religious (including Chabad, Breslov, other Hasidic groups) and secular
- **Artistic Colony**: Artist quarter with galleries and studios
- **Tourism**: Major tourist destination for Jewish and Israeli visitors

### Jewish Life

- **Synagogues**: Numerous synagogues, including historic ones
- **Yeshivos**: Torah and Kabbalah study institutions
- **Mikvaos**: Ritual baths
- **Kosher**: Abundant kosher food options
- **Mystical Tourism**: Many visitors drawn to Kabbalah and mysticism

## Chabad in Tzfat

### Historical Presence

Chabad has had a presence in Tzfat for centuries:

- **Early Settlers**: Descendants of early Chassidic immigrants
- **Continuous Community**: Chabad families maintained presence
- **Institutions**: Synagogue, mikvah, and communal services

### Modern Chabad Presence

Today, Chabad is active in Tzfat:

- **Institutions**: Chabad House, synagogue, educational programs
- **Outreach**: Activities for residents and tourists
- **Kabbalah Connection**: Chabad's inner dimension (_Chassidus Chabad_) shares Kabbalah's mystical approach
- **Pilgrimage**: Chabad visitors come to experience Tzfat's spiritual atmosphere

## Sacred Sites in Tzfat

### Ancient Synagogues

Tzfat has historic synagogues:

- **Ari Synagogue**: Associated with the Ari (Rabbi Isaac Luria)
- **Abuhav Synagogue**: 16th-century synagogue with ancient Torah scroll
- **Yosef Caro Synagogue**: Associated with the author of _Shulchan Aruch_

### Other Sacred Sites

- **Ancient Cemetery**: Historic Jewish cemetery with graves of rabbis and Kabbalists
- **Mikvaos**: Ancient ritual baths
- **Kabbalah Centers**: Centers for Kabbalah study
