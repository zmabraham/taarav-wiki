# Kobe, Japan — קובה / 神戸

**Transit Point for Jewish Refugees Including Chabad Students (1940–1941)**

**Location**: Honshu island, Japan (approximately 500 km west of Tokyo)  
**Chabad Significance**: Transit point for Chabad yeshiva students escaping Europe  
**Period**: 1940–1941 — approximately one year of Jewish refugee transit  
**Jewish Population**: Temporary community of approximately several thousand refugees

---

## Overview

Kobe (Japanese: 神戸; Hebrew: קובה / Kobe) was an unlikely transit point for Jewish refugees fleeing Nazi Europe in 1940–1941. Located in Japan, the city briefly hosted a diverse Jewish community including Chabad yeshiva students escaping from [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]] and elsewhere in Poland.

The Kobe refugee episode represents a remarkable chapter in Holocaust history — when Japanese-occupied China and Japan provided temporary refuge for Jews fleeing Nazi genocide. The Chabad students who passed through Kobe eventually reached [[entities/places/shanghai|Shanghai]] and, after the war, [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Israel]] or America.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Kobe is located on Honshu island:

- **Location**: Approximately 500 km west of Tokyo, on Osaka Bay
- **Region**: Kansai region of Honshu
- **Port City**: Major port with connections to Asia and beyond
- **Terrain**: Coastal city with mountains nearby
- **Climate**: Humid subtropical — hot, humid summers; mild winters

### Historical Context

- **Imperial Japan**: Japan was expanding its empire in Asia in the 1930s–1940s
- **Alliance with Germany**: Japan was allied with Nazi Germany (Anti-Comintern Pact, 1936)
- **Unexpected Refuge**: Despite alliance with Germany, Japan briefly allowed Jewish refugees

## Jewish Refugees in Kobe (1940–1941)

### How Refugees Reached Kobe

The journey to Kobe was remarkable:

- **Visas**: Japanese diplomat [[entities/people/chiune-sugihara|Chiune Sugihara]] issued transit visas in Kaunas, Lithuania (1940)
- **Sugihara Visas**: Thousands of Jews received Japanese transit visas allowing passage through Japan
- **Trans-Siberian Railway**: Refugees traveled on Trans-Siberian Railway across Soviet Union to Vladivostok
- **Ship to Japan**: From Vladivostok, refugees took ships to Japan
- **Arrival in Kobe**: Many arrived in Kobe, Japan's main port for foreign ships

### The Kobe Jewish Community

For approximately one year (1940–1941), Kobe hosted:

- **Refugees**: Approximately several thousand Jewish refugees
- **Diversity**: Refugees from Poland, Lithuania, Germany, and elsewhere
- **Temporary**: Kobe was transit point, not final destination
- **Community**: Brief but vibrant Jewish community life

## Chabad Students in Kobe

### Chabad Yeshiva Students

Among the refugees were Chabad students escaping from:

- **Warsaw**: Students from Chabad yeshivas in [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]]
- **Otwock**: Students from [[entities/places/otwock|Otwock]] yeshiva
- **Elsewhere**: Chabad families and individuals fleeing Nazi advance

### Journey to Kobe

The Chabad students' journey:

1. **Escape from Poland**: Fled [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]] as Nazis invaded (September 1939)
2. **Vilna**: Reached [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]] (then under Lithuanian control)
3. **Sugihara Visas**: Obtained Japanese transit visas from Chiune Sugihara
4. **Trans-Siberian**: Traveled across Soviet Union
5. **Vladivostok**: Port in Soviet Far East
6. **Ship to Japan**: Sailed to Kobe

### Life in Kobe

- **Temporary Stay**: Kobe was transit point, not permanent home
- **Community**: Brief community life with other Jewish refugees
- **Religious Observance**: Maintained Jewish observance despite challenges
- **Planning**: Arranged next destination (usually [[entities/places/shanghai|Shanghai]])

## The Polish Government-in-Exile Connection

### Diplomatic Context

- **Polish Embassy**: Polish government-in-exile had representation in Japan
- **Support**: Polish diplomats helped Jewish refugees
- **Visas**: Polish passports and documents helped refugees

### Japanese Policy

- **Unexpected Tolerance**: Japan briefly allowed Jewish refugees despite alliance with Germany
- **Business Interests**: Some Japanese saw potential benefits from Jewish refugees
- **Limited Duration**: This policy lasted only until late 1941

## Transit to Shanghai

### Final Destination

Most Jewish refugees in Kobe intended to reach [[entities/places/shanghai|Shanghai]]:

- **Shanghai Ghetto**: Shanghai required no visas for entry
- **International Settlement**: Shanghai's International Settlement offered safety
- **Jewish Community**: Existing Jewish community in Shanghai
- **Japanese Control**: Shanghai was under Japanese control but relatively safe

### Departure from Kobe

- **Ships**: Regular ships sailed from Kobe to Shanghai
- **Family Groups**: Many Chabad students traveled with families
- **Final Departure**: Most left Kobe by late 1941

---

## End of the Kobe Refugee Episode

### Pearl Harbor and Aftermath

- **December 7, 1941**: Japan attacked Pearl Harbor; war with United States and Britain
- **End of Refugee Flow**: No more Jewish refugees could reach Japan
- **Suspicion**: Remaining refugees in Japanese-controlled Asia faced increased suspicion
- **Shanghai Hardship**: Those in Shanghai faced difficult conditions for remainder of war

---

## Chabad Students After Kobe

### Shanghai Period (1941–1945)

- **Hongkew Ghetto**: Refugees in Shanghai lived in Hongkew district under Japanese control
- **Hardship**: Difficult conditions but survived Holocaust
- **Community Life**: Maintained Jewish observance and community life
- **Mir Yeshiva**: The Mir yeshiva was also in Shanghai; Chabad students had contact with them

### Postwar

- **Liberation**: Shanghai liberated (1945)
- **Displacement**: Refugees in displaced persons camps
- **Emigration**: Most eventually reached [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Israel]] or America
- **Chabad Continuity**: Chabad students who survived continued their tradition
