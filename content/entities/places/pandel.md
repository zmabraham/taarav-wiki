# Pandel (Pandėlys) — פּאַנדעל

**Early Lithuanian Chabad Community from the 18th–19th Century**

**Location**: Northeastern Lithuania (now Pandėlys)
**Jewish Population**: Small Jewish community
**Significance**: One of the earliest Chabad communities in Lithuania, established in the period of Mitnagdic opposition
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated during WWII

---

## Overview

Pandel (Yiddish: פּאַנדעל; Lithuanian: Pandėlys) was a small town in northeastern Lithuania with one of the earliest Chabad communities in the region. Like [[entities/places/vidz|Vidz]] and [[entities/places/birzh|Birzh]], Pandel represents the courageous establishment of Chassidism in hostile Mitnagdic territory in the late 18th and early 19th centuries.

---

## Geography

Pandel is located in the Rokiškis district of northeastern Lithuania, near [[entities/places/rakishok|Rakishok]]. The area was part of the dense network of small Jewish communities in northeastern Lithuania.

---

## Chabad in Pandel

### Early Establishment

Pandel's Chabad community was established in the early period of Chabad's expansion into Lithuania:

- **Late 18th / early 19th century**: Among the first wave of Chassidim to settle in Lithuanian territory
- **Courageous presence**: Established community in the face of Mitnagdic opposition
- **Connection to early Chabad**: Community linked to the early generations of the Alter Rebbe's followers

### Character of the Community

- **Small but dedicated**: A community of committed Chassidim in a hostile environment
- **Persistence**: Maintained Chabad character through generations despite Mitnagdic pressure
- **Part of the network**: Connected to other early Lithuanian Chabad communities

---

## The Holocaust

Pandel's Jewish community was murdered during the Nazi occupation of Lithuania in 1941, as part of the systematic destruction of all Lithuanian Jewish communities.

---

## Pandel's Place in Chabad History

Pandel is one of the small early Lithuanian communities that demonstrate the spread of Chassidism into Mitnagdic strongholds — a remarkable achievement given the social, religious, and sometimes governmental hostility that early Lithuanian Chassidim faced.
