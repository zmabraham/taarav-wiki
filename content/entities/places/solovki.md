# סולובקי — Solovki

**Location**: Solovetsky Islands, White Sea, Arctic Russia  
**Character**: Soviet labor camp (GULAG); one of the first and most notorious  
**Chabad significance**: The sentence to which the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] was initially condemned after his 1927 arrest

---

## Overview

The Solovetsky Islands (Solovki) in the White Sea, just south of the Arctic Circle, were the site of one of the first and most notorious camps in the Soviet GULAG (Glavnoe Upravlenie Lagerei — Main Camp Administration) system. Effectively a death sentence for those without the constitution to survive Arctic conditions and forced labor, Solovki was the initial destination to which the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] was sentenced in 1927 after the death penalty was commuted.

---

## The Rayatz's Sentence

After the Rayatz's arrest in **June 1927**, his death sentence was reduced to:

1. **10 years** in the **Solovki** labor camp

International pressure — including diplomatic intervention by [[entities/people/mordechai-dubin|Mordechai Dubin]] and worldwide Jewish organizations — succeeded in further commuting this sentence to: 2. **3 years** exile in **[[entities/places/kostroma|Kostroma]]** (a city northeast of Moscow)

The Rayatz was ultimately fully released and expelled from the Soviet Union on 12-13 Tammuz 5687 (July 1927), never having served time in Solovki itself.

---

## Historical Significance

The fact that Solovki was the intended destination communicates how seriously the Soviet regime viewed the Rayatz's activities — this was not a light punishment but one that constituted a near-death sentence. The international pressure that prevented him from being sent there represents one of the most significant episodes of international intervention in Soviet internal Jewish affairs.
