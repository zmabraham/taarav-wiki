# Lazna (Liozna)

**Town in the Vitebsk gubernia; the Alter Rebbe's principal base of operations for nearly two decades; site of the Tanya regulations**

## Overview

Lazna — also known by its more common transliteration Liozna (Belarusian: Ліёзна; Russian: Лиозно) — is a town in the Vitebsk region of present-day Belarus, located approximately 60 kilometers south of Vitebsk along the road toward Mogilev. During the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries it was situated within the Vitebsk gubernia of the Russian Empire.

## The Alter Rebbe's Residence (1783–1801)

Lazna is one of the most important towns in early Chabad history because it served as the Alter Rebbe's primary residence and the movement's headquarters for approximately eighteen years, from תקמ"ג (1783) until תקס"א (1801), when the Alter Rebbe moved to Liadi.

During the Lazna period, the Alter Rebbe:

- **Systematized Chabad philosophy**: The intellectual framework of Chabad — the analysis of the soul's faculties, the interrelationship of Chochma, Bina, and Da'as (ChaBaD), and the distinctive approach to divine service — was developed and taught primarily from Lazna.
- **Issued the Tanya regulations (Hanhagos HaTanya)**: From Lazna, the Alter Rebbe issued formal guidelines governing the study and conduct expected of Chabad Chassidim. These regulations shaped Chabad practice for generations.
- **Attracted thousands of disciples**: Lazna became a major pilgrimage center for Chassidim from across White Russia, Ukraine, and Lithuania. Tens of thousands of Chassidim traveled to Lazna over the years to receive guidance, hear Torah from the Rebbe, and conduct personal consultations (yechidus).
- **Authored the Tanya**: The Tanya — the foundational text of Chabad philosophy — was composed primarily during the Lazna years and first printed in תקנ"ז (1796/7).

The town's significance was such that the Alter Rebbe's household became a major economic and social force in the local region. Chabad sources describe the logistical challenges of housing and feeding the thousands of visitors who came to Lazna.

## After 1801

In תקס"א (1801), the Alter Rebbe relocated to Liadi, a somewhat larger town further south, which became his final base until the Napoleonic invasion of 1812 forced the community to flee. Liadi eclipsed Lazna as the primary Chabad center, and the Alter Rebbe's title — "the Alter Rebbe of Liadi" — reflects his more famous later residence.

Lazna itself continued as a Jewish community through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. The town's Jewish population was destroyed in the Holocaust.
