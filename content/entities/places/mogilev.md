# מאהילעוו — Mogilev

**Location**: Eastern Belarus, on the Dnieper River  
**Character**: Major Belarusian city; historical Jewish community  
**Chabad significance**: Underground Tomchei Tmimim yeshiva location in Soviet era

---

## Overview

Mogilev (Yiddish: מאהילעוו / Mohilev) is a major city in eastern Belarus on the Dnieper River, with a long Jewish history. It appears in Chabad historical sources as one of the cities where the underground Tomchei Tmimim yeshiva operated during the Soviet persecution period.

---

## Chabad in Mogilev

The memoir _Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu_ of M. Shusterman lists **Mogilev** among the cities where he studied in the clandestine underground yeshiva network, alongside [[entities/places/vitebsk|Vitebsk]] and [[entities/places/kherson|Kherson]]. The network moved through multiple cities to evade GPU (secret police) surveillance.

Mogilev was a natural location for Chabad activity — it lay in the heart of Chabad's traditional heartland (White Russia / Belarus), with significant Jewish population and proximity to other Chabad centers like [[entities/places/vitebsk|Vitebsk]] and [[entities/places/homel|Homel]].

---

## Historical Jewish Community

Mogilev had one of the larger Jewish communities in the Pale of Settlement. The city's rabbinic tradition was significant; the Mogilev rabbinate was historically important in the governance of Belarusian Jewish communities. The city suffered severely during both WWI and WWII; the Holocaust virtually destroyed its Jewish community.
