# Birzh (Birzai) — בירז

**Lithuanian Chabad Community Since the Mitteler Rebbe Era**

**Location**: Northern Lithuania (now Biržai)
**Jewish Population**: Significant Jewish community through the 19th–early 20th century
**Significance**: Chabad presence dating to the Mitteler Rebbe's era; notable agreement with Mitnagdim
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated during WWII

---

## Overview

Birzh (Yiddish: בירז; Lithuanian: Biržai) was a town in northern Lithuania with a Chabad community dating to the era of the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (Rabbi Dovber Schneuri, 1773–1827). The town is notable in Chabad history for an agreement reached around 1820 between the local Chassidim and Mitnagdim regarding the appointment of a separate Chabad shochet (ritual slaughterer) — an important practical accommodation that helped formalize the Chassidic community's status.

---

## Geography

Birzh is located in northern Lithuania, near the Latvian border. Its position made it part of a network of communities connecting Lithuanian and Latvian Chabad. The town lay in a predominantly agricultural region with a mixed Jewish, Lithuanian, and Latvian population.

---

## Chabad in Birzh

### Establishment

The Chabad community in Birzh was established during or shortly after the era of the Mitteler Rebbe:

- **Early 19th century**: Chassidim settled in Birzh and established a minyan following Nusach Ari
- **Mitteler Rebbe's influence**: Community connected to the court of the Mitteler Rebbe in Lubavitch
- **Gradual growth**: Community grew through the 19th century

### The Birzh Agreement (c. 1820)

A landmark moment in Lithuanian Chabad history occurred in Birzh:

- **Problem**: Chassidim and Mitnagdim in Birzh were in conflict over ritual slaughter, as Mitnagdim questioned the validity of knives sharpened according to Chabad practice
- **Negotiations**: Community leaders from both sides negotiated an arrangement
- **Agreement**: The two communities agreed to appoint a separate Chabad shochet who would serve the Chassidic community
- **Significance**: This practical accommodation formalized the Chassidic community's right to maintain its own religious standards
- **Model**: The Birzh arrangement served as a model for other Lithuanian towns where Chassidim and Mitnagdim coexisted

### Later Development

Throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries:

- **Established community**: Birzh Chabad maintained its identity with a synagogue and community institutions
- **Interwar period**: Community continued functioning during Lithuanian independence (1918–1940)
- **Connections**: Links with Chabad leadership in [[entities/places/riga|Riga]] (Rayatz, 1927–1933) and [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]]

---

## The Holocaust

Birzh's Jewish community, including its Chabad members, was murdered in 1941 during the Nazi invasion and occupation of Lithuania. Lithuanian collaborators assisted the Einsatzgruppen in murdering the Jewish population shortly after the German occupation began in late June 1941.

---

## Birzh in Chabad History

Birzh represents:

- **Normalization**: The process by which early Lithuanian Chabad communities achieved communal legitimacy
- **Practical coexistence**: The Birzh agreement showed how Chassidim and Mitnagdim could reach practical accommodations
- **Mitteler Rebbe era**: An example of how the Mitteler Rebbe's leadership extended into Lithuania
