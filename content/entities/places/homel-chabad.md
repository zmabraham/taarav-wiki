# Homel / Gomel (הומיל)

**Major Belarusian city; Chabad community where Israel Jacobson lived and founded a yeshiva, 1920–1925.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Country** | Russia/Soviet Union (now Belarus) |
| **Period of Jacobson's residence** | 5680–5685 (1920–1925) |
| **Jewish community** | Large; had organized Chabad kehilla |

## Overview

Homel (now Gomel) was one of the larger cities in the Mogilev/Minsk region with a significant Jewish population. During the Soviet period, it became increasingly difficult to maintain Jewish religious life.

Israel Jacobson lived in Homel from 5680 (1920) — when the Rebbe Rashab passed away and the Rayatz assumed leadership — through 5685 (1925). During this period he was active in the local Chabad community and helped found a local yeshiva.

## Key Personalities Mentioned

- **R' Shmuel Yitzchak Luria** — Chairman of the Homel Jewish community during Jacobson's time; a key organizational figure
- **The Chafetz Chaim** (R' Yisrael Meir Kagan) — Made a notable visit to Homel while Jacobson was there; a significant event for the local community

## Yeshiva Founded Here

Israel Jacobson helped found a yeshiva in Homel during the 5680–5685 (1920–1925) period. This was part of broader Chabad efforts to maintain Jewish education in Soviet Russia despite increasing governmental opposition.

## Road from Lubavitch

Davsk, a small town near Zuravichy, stood on the crossroads of a stone road leading to Homel, Mogilev, Rogatchev, and Smolensk — indicating Homel's centrality in the regional Jewish geography.
