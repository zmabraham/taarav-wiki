# Shanghai, China

Major Chinese port city. Served as a wartime refuge for thousands of Jewish refugees, including a significant Chabad community and a functioning Tomchei Temimim yeshiva.

## WWII Refugee Community

After the Trans-Siberian escape route from Europe, most Jewish refugees who passed through Kobe, Japan, continued to Shanghai. Shanghai's International Settlement (and later the Hongkew district under Japanese control) accepted Jewish refugees without requiring visas.

## Tomchei Temimim in Shanghai

A Tomchei Temimim yeshiva (Chabad's central yeshiva) continued functioning in Shanghai throughout the war years. R' [[entities/people/yitzchak-meir-gurary]] was among the senior figures maintaining the yeshiva.

The yeshiva students and faculty endured difficult conditions — crowding, poverty, disease — but maintained Torah study and Jewish life throughout the war.

## End of the War

After Japan's defeat (1945), the Shanghai refugee community was able to make aliya to Eretz Yisroel or emigrate to the USA. The yeshiva and community eventually relocated to New York.
