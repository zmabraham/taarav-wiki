# Titov

A shtetl near Zhivotov in the Vinnytsia region of Ukraine. Titov was a deeply religious community — largely Twersky Chassidim (Chernobyler) with some from other disciples of the Maggid of Mezeritch. It suffered one of the most devastating massacres of the pogrom era.

## Description
Titov is described in the memoir as an exceptionally pious town: "all its inhabitants were God-fearing and meticulous in Torah and mitzvos." Most were Chernobyler (Twersky) Chassidim, with some from other branches of the Maggid's disciples.

When the bandit Sokolov arrived at Titov, the young men of the town stood up to resist him — unlike the Jews of Zhivotov who submitted and survived. Sokolov massacred nearly the entire population. The townspeople had gathered in the synagogue for refuge; Sokolov's forces burned the synagogue with the Jews inside. The smoke of the burning was visible from Zhivotov, where R. Yisrael Yehuda Levin (then about eight years old) and his family were fleeing through the fields.

Of a pre-pogrom population of roughly 8,000, fewer than 2,000 survived — primarily small orphaned children. These orphans were subsequently gathered and brought to Lipovetsh and other nearby towns, where the Yevsektsia took control of them.
