# Kfar Chabad — כפר חב"ד

**The Chabad Village in the Land of Israel**

**Location**: Central Israel, approximately 5 km southeast of Lod (near Ben Gurion Airport)  
**Founded**: 1949 (5709) by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]  
**Population**: Approximately 5,000–6,000 residents  
**Significance**: Spiritual center of Chabad in Israel; village founded by Soviet-era survivors

---

## Overview

Kfar Chabad ("Chabad Village") is a religious village in central Israel, founded in 1949 by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] as a home for Chabad chassidim escaping the Soviet Union after World War II. Located near Lod and Ben Gurion Airport, Kfar Chabad has grown from a small agricultural settlement to a major Chabad center with yeshivos, schools, and communal institutions.

The village is famous for the 1956 terror attack and the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe's]] famous response: _"Venach ve'harbeh zeh"_ — "And you shall rebuild and you shall continue to build this place." This response exemplified the Rebbe's approach to rebuilding in the face of tragedy.

Today, Kfar Chabad serves as the spiritual center of Chabad in Israel, with dozens of institutions serving residents, visitors, and the broader Israeli public.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Kfar Chabad is located in central Israel's coastal plain:

- **Location**: Approximately 5 km southeast of Lod, 15 km southeast of Tel Aviv, 5 km from Ben Gurion Airport
- **Region**: Central District, Shephelah (foothills) region
- **Terrain**: Flat to gently rolling agricultural land
- **Climate**: Mediterranean — hot, dry summers; mild, wet winters

### Village Layout

Kfar Chabad is organized as:

- **Residential Areas**: Family homes on quiet streets
- **Institutional Zone**: Schools, yeshivos, and community buildings
- **Commercial Area**: Shops, restaurants, and services
- **Industrial Zone**: Light industry and workshops

### Population

- **Total Population**: Approximately 5,000–6,000 residents
- **Demographics**: Primarily Chabad chassidim; includes Israeli-born and immigrants
- **Growth**: Village has grown steadily since founding
- **Diversity**: Includes families from varied backgrounds (Russian, American, Israeli, etc.)

## Founding of Kfar Chabad (1949)

### The Rayatz's Vision

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] envisioned Kfar Chabad as:

- **Home for Refugees**: Place for Chabad survivors of the Soviet underground to settle
- **Agricultural Settlement**: Combining Torah study with agricultural work
- **Spiritual Center**: Base for Chabad activities in the Land of Israel
- **Model Community**: Exemplifying Chabad values in practice

### The Founders

Kfar Chabad was founded by:

- **Soviet Survivors**: Chabad chassidim who escaped the Soviet Union via the Great Escape (1946–47)
- **WWII Refugees**: Families who had fled to [[entities/places/samarkand|Samarkand]] and Central Asia
- **Early Immigrants**: Pioneering families willing to build a new village from scratch

### Establishment

- **Land Acquisition**: Land purchased from Jewish National Fund or other sources
- **Early Living Conditions**: Difficult conditions in temporary housing
- **Agricultural Beginnings**: Initial focus on farming and agriculture
- **Spiritual Life**: From the beginning, Torah study and religious observance were central

## Growth and Development

### 1950s: Building the Village

The early years were challenging:

- **Housing**: Building permanent homes
- **Agriculture**: Establishing farms and orchards
- **Institutions**: Founding first schools and yeshivos
- **Community**: Building communal infrastructure

### The 1956 Terror Attack

A tragic event tested the young village:

- **April 11, 1956**: Arab terrorists infiltrated the village
- **Attack**: Terrorists entered the village and opened fire
- **Victims**: Five residents killed, including a pregnant woman and children
- **Shock**: The young village was traumatized by the attack

### The Rebbe's Famous Response

The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|Rebbe (Menachem Mendel Schneerson)]] responded with the famous words:

- **"Venach ve'harbeh zeh"**: "And you shall rebuild and you shall continue to build this place"
- **Source**: Quoting from Genesis (Genesis 26:22) regarding Isaac's wells
- **Meaning**: Not just rebuild, but expand and grow even more
- **Impact**: The response transformed tragedy into renewed determination

### Rebuilding and Expansion

After the attack:

- **Resilience**: Village rebuilt quickly
- **Expansion**: New buildings and institutions added
- **Growth**: Population increased
- **Symbolism**: The village became a symbol of Jewish resilience

## Institutions in Kfar Chabad

### Educational Institutions

Kfar Chabad is home to numerous educational institutions:

- **Yeshivos**: Several yeshivos for different ages and levels
- **Schools**: Elementary and high schools for boys and girls
- **Seminary**: Beit Chaya Mushka seminary for girls
- **Kollel**: Advanced Torah study for married men

### Community Institutions

- **Synagogues**: Multiple synagogues for various groups
- **Mikvah**: Ritual bath facilities
- **Library**: Chabad library with rare texts
- **Community Center**: Cultural and community events

### Visitor Center

- **Kfar Chabad Visitor Center**: Museum and exhibits about Chabad history
- **770 Model**: Replica of 770 Eastern Parkway
- **Exhibits**: Displays about Chabad in Israel, Soviet underground, and village history
- **Events**: Hosts events for visitors from Israel and abroad

### Publishing and Media

- **Printing Press**: Chabad texts printed and distributed
- **Media**: Chabad media operations based in or near Kfar Chabad

## Economic Life

### Agriculture

Agriculture remains important:

- **Orchards**: Fruit orchards
- **Vineyards**: Grape production
- **Farms**: Agricultural operations
- **Combining Work and Study**: Traditional Chabad model

### Industry and Services

- **Light Industry**: Workshops and small factories
- **Services**: Shops, restaurants serving village and visitors
- **Education**: Many residents work in education
- **Shlichus**: Many residents serve as shluchim (emissaries) throughout Israel

## Kfar Chabad's Role in Israeli Chabad

### Spiritual Center

Kfar Chabad serves as:

- **Pilgrimage Site**: Israelis visit for inspiration and blessing
- **Learning Center**: Yeshivos attract students from across Israel
- **Event Hosting**: Major Chabad events held here
- **Model**: Exemplifies Chabad values in Israeli context

### Shlichus Training

The village trains shluchim for service throughout Israel:

- **Preparation**: Training for emissaries heading to Israeli communities
- **Support**: Base for shluchim serving across Israel
- **Network**: Hub connecting Chabad activities nationwide

## Modern Kfar Chabad

### Contemporary Village

Today's Kfar Chabad:

- **Growth**: Continued expansion of housing and institutions
- **Diversity**: Includes immigrants from many countries
- **Visitor Destination**: Tourists and visitors come to learn and pray
- **Integration**: Part of broader Israeli society while maintaining distinct character

### Challenges

- **Housing**: Housing shortages and rising costs
- **Integration**: Balancing insular community with broader society
- **Economic Pressures**: Challenges of maintaining religious lifestyle in modern economy
