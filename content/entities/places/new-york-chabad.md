# New York — Early Chabad Community

**Hub of American Chabad from the 1920s onward; home of Israel Jacobson, Agudas HaTmimim, and later 770 Eastern Parkway.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Israel Jacobson arrived** | ~5685/5686 (1925/1926) |
| **Rayatz first visit** | 13 Elul 5689 – 20 Tammuz 5690 (1929–1930) |
| **Rayatz settled permanently** | 9 Adar II 5700 (1940) |
| **770 Eastern Parkway** | Established as HQ in 1940 |

## Key Locations

### Bronsville (Brownsville), Brooklyn
The primary neighborhood for Chabad and religious Eastern European Jews in the 1920s–30s. About 10 shuls in Nusach Ari (Chasidic rite).

- **"Anshei Lubavitch" / Anshei Lubavitch Beis Midrash** — 195 Watkins Street, Brownsville; large building; ~1,200 members; where the Rayatz prayed for High Holidays 5690. Rabbi: R' Tzvi Dakhavich.
- **"Anshei Bobruisk" congregation** — in Brownsville; R' Aharon Siminovosky was a leader; Dr. Akin (doctor from Rogatchev) also affiliated; provided housing for Rayatz during 5690 High Holidays (at Dr. Akin's home, 332 Stone Ave., corner Glenmore)

### Crown Heights, Brooklyn
Mentioned in chapter 30 as the area where the Rayatz's apartment was located during 5690.

### Manhattan
- 137 West 116th Street — home of R' Eliyahu Simpson; site of Agudas HaTmimim founding meeting

## The Chabad Community in New York (~1920s)

The Chabad community in early 1920s New York was small and scattered, made up mostly of immigrants from White Russia and Ukraine. The Zachor memoirs note:
- Many TT alumni had left Russia and were working in difficult manual labor (laundries, slaughterhouses)
- R' Gershon Aichorn worked in a laundry for $30/week but gave regular Gemara classes
- Most Russian Chasidim had NOT emigrated to America, having heeded Chabad Rebbes' discouragement of emigration
- The community needed organization and leadership

## Rayatz's 1929–1930 Visit

The Rayatz's first American visit was a turning point. During his approximately one year here (13 Elul 5689 to 20 Tammuz 5690), he:
- Prayed at Anshei Lubavitch on High Holidays
- Founded or strengthened Agudas Chasidei Chabad branches
- Made outreach trips to smaller cities/towns
- Worked with Israel Jacobson and others to build infrastructure
