# Vilna (Vilnius) — וילנע

**The Jerusalem of Lithuania and Center of Mitnagdic Opposition**

**Location**: Lithuania (historically part of Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth; interwar Poland; now Lithuania)  
**Jewish Population**: Approximately 60,000 Jews (over 30% of city population) before WWII  
**Significance**: Major center of Jewish learning; center of Mitnagdic opposition to Chassidism  
**Holocaust**: Site of Ponary massacres; Jewish community virtually annihilated

---

## Overview

Vilna (Lithuanian: Vilnius; Polish: Wilno; Yiddish: ווילנע / Vilne) was known as the "Jerusalem of Lithuania" — one of the great centers of Jewish learning, culture, and spirituality in European history. Before the Holocaust, Vilna had approximately 60,000 Jews and was home to the [[entities/people/vilna-gaon|Vilna Gaon]] (Elijah ben Solomon Zalman), the foremost opponent of the early Chassidic movement.

Vilna's relationship with Chabad was complex. As the epicenter of Mitnagdic opposition to Chassidism, Vilna was initially hostile to the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] and the nascent Chabad movement. Over time, however, a small Chabad community developed in Vilna, and the city played a role in the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] 1939 escape from Poland.

The Holocaust devastated Vilna's Jewish community. The Ponary massacres (1941–1943) saw the murder of approximately 70,000 Jews, including virtually all of Vilna's Jewish population. The vibrant "Jerusalem of Lithuania" was destroyed, leaving only memories and writings.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Vilna is located in southeastern Lithuania:

- **Location**: On the Vilnia and Neris rivers
- **Region**: Historic Lithuania; part of Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth
- **Terrain**: Rolling hills with river valleys
- **Climate**: Continental — cold winters, moderate summers

### Political Changes

Vilna's political status changed frequently:

- **Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth** (until 1795)
- **Russian Empire** (1795–1917)
- **German Occupation** (1915–1918, WWI)
- **Interwar Poland** (1920–1939)
- **Lithuanian Control** (1939–1940)
- **Soviet Occupation** (1940–1941)
- **Nazi Occupation** (1941–1944)
- **Soviet Lithuania** (1944–1991)
- **Independent Lithuania** (1991–present)

### Jewish Population Before the Holocaust

Vilna had one of Eastern Europe's most important Jewish communities:

- **Population**: Approximately 60,000 Jews (over 30% of city population) by 1939
- **Culture**: Center of Hebrew and Yiddish culture, literature, and scholarship
- **Institutions**: Yeshivos, libraries, schools, theaters, museums
- **Politics**: Diverse Jewish political and religious movements
- **Economy**: Jews prominent in trade, professions, and crafts

## Vilna as Jewish Center

### The "Jerusalem of Lithuania"

Vilna earned this title because:

- **Scholarship**: Unparalleled center of Torah learning
- **Printing**: Major center of Hebrew printing
- **Culture**: Hub of Jewish intellectual and cultural life
- **Spirituality**: Deep religious commitment across diverse movements
- **Yiddish**: Center of Yiddish literature and culture

### The Vilna Gaon and His Legacy

[[entities/people/vilna-gaon|The Vilna Gaon]] (1720–1797) defined Vilna's Jewish character:

- **Scholarship**: Foredestined Torah scholar of his generation
- **Opposition to Chassidism**: Led the Mitnagdic opposition to early Chassidic movement
- **Persecution**: Vilna Jewish community persecuted early Chassidim
- **Legacy**: His influence continues to shape Lithuanian Jewish tradition

## Chabad in Vilna

### Early Hostility

Vilna was initially hostile to Chabad:

- **Vilna Gaon's Opposition**: The Gaon excommunicated Chassidim
- **Persecution**: Vilna community actively persecuted early Chassidim
- **Informers**: Vilna authorities reported Chassidim to government
- **Alter Rebbe's Arrests**: The Alter Rebbe's 1798 and 1800 arrests originated from denunciations in the Vilna region

### Gradual Change

Over time, relations improved:

- **Tolerance**: Extreme opposition diminished after the Vilna Gaon's death
- **Small Community**: A small Chabad community developed in Vilna
- **Acceptance**: Chabad became one of many Jewish religious streams in Vilna

### Interwar Period

By the interwar period (1920s–1930s):

- **Presence**: Small but established Chabad community
- **Institutions**: Chabad synagogue, mikvah, and communal services
- **Relations**: Generally peaceful coexistence with other Jewish groups

## The Rayatz and Vilna (1939)

### Escape Route Stop

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] passed through Vilna during his 1939 escape:

- **September 1939**: Fled [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]] and [[entities/places/otwock|Otwock]] as Nazis invaded Poland
- **Journey to Vilna**: Traveled eastward to Vilna (then under Lithuanian control)
- **Temporary Stay**: Spent time in Vilna while arranging further journey
- **From Vilna to Riga**: Left Vilna for [[entities/places/riga|Riga]], then Sweden and America

### Vilna as Transit Point

Vilna played a crucial role:

- **Safety**: Briefly under Lithuanian control, not yet occupied by Nazis or Soviets
- **Diplomatic Options**: Vilna had diplomatic missions that could help with travel documents
- **Jewish Community**: Local Jewish community helped the Rayatz
- **Launch Point**: From Vilna, the Rayatz continued his journey to safety

## Vilna's Jewish Community and Culture

### Religious Diversity

Vilna hosted diverse Jewish religious streams:

- **Mitnagdim**: Followers of the Vilna Gaon's tradition
- **Chassidim**: Various Chassidic groups, including Chabad
- **Secular Jews**: Bundists, Zionists, and others
- **Institutions**: Synagogues representing all streams

### Cultural Centers

Vilna was a cultural hub:

- **YIVO**: Yiddish Scientific Institute (Yidisher Visnshaftlikher Institut) founded 1925
- **Strashun Library**: Major Jewish library
- **Theater**: Yiddish theater companies
- **Press**: Hebrew and Yiddish newspapers and journals

### Educational Institutions

- **Yeshivos**: Major Torah study institutions
- **Schools**: Religious and secular Jewish education
- **Vocational Training**: ORT and other vocational schools

## The Holocaust in Vilna

### Soviet Occupation (1940–1941)

- **Annexation**: Soviet Union annexed Lithuania (1940)
- **Religious Suppression**: Jewish religious life restricted
- **Arrests**: Jewish political and religious leaders arrested
- **Economic Changes**: Businesses nationalized

### Nazi Occupation (1941–1944)

- **Invasion**: Germany invaded Soviet Union (June 1941); captured Vilna (June 24, 1941)
- **Immediate Terror**: Einsatsgruppen and Lithuanian collaborators began murdering Jews immediately
- **Ghetto**: Vilna Ghetto established September 1941
- **Forced Labor**: Jews used for forced labor
- **Cultural Life**: Underground cultural and educational activities in ghetto

### Ponary Massacres

The Ponary massacres (1941–1943) were the destruction of Vilna Jewry:

- **Location**: Ponary (Paneriai) forest approximately 10 km from Vilna
- **Victims**: Approximately 70,000 Jews murdered, including most of Vilna's Jewish population
- **Method**: Shot in pits by Einsatzgruppen and Lithuanian collaborators
- **Vilna Ghetto Liquidation**: Ghetto liquidated September 1943
- **Resistance**: Some Jews escaped to join partisans

### Liberation

- **Soviet Liberation**: Red Army captured Vilna (July 1944)
- **Survivors**: Very few Vilna Jews survived
- **Destruction**: The "Jerusalem of Lithuania" was destroyed

## Postwar Vilna (Vilnius)

### Soviet Era (1944–1991)

Under Soviet rule:

- **Tiny Community**: Very few Jews returned to Vilnius
- **Suppression**: Religious life severely restricted
- **Memory**: Soviet regime minimized Jewish history
- **Emigration**: Most survivors emigrated to Israel or America

### Post-Soviet Lithuania

Since 1991, independent Lithuania:

- **Democracy**: Lithuania became democratic
- **Jewish Community**: Small community of approximately 2,000–3,000 Jews
- **Historical Recognition**: Lithuania has acknowledged its Jewish history
- **Memory**: Museums and memorials commemorate prewar Jewish community

## Modern Vilnius

### Contemporary Jewish Life

Today's Vilnius:

- **Population**: Approximately 540,000 (tiny Jewish minority)
- **Jewish Community**: Small but active community
- **Institutions**: Synagogue, community center, Jewish museum
- **Chabad Presence**: Limited Chabad activity today

### Historical Memory

- **Former Sites**: Locations of synagogues and Jewish institutions
- **Ponary Memorial**: Memorial at Ponary massacre site
- **Jewish Museum**: Vilna Gaon Jewish State Museum
- **Ghetto Streets**: Former ghetto area marked

## Vilna's Place in Jewish and Chabad History

### Center of Opposition

Vilna represented:

- **Mitnagdic Center**: Heart of opposition to early Chassidism
- **Persecution**: Vilna authorities persecuted early Chassidim
- **Historical Tension**: Early chapter in Chabad-Mitnagged relations

### Cultural and Intellectual Capital

Despite early tensions, Vilna contributed to Jewish civilization:

- **Scholarship**: Unparalleled Torah learning
- **Culture**: Yiddish and Hebrew cultural production
- **Printing**: Major center of Jewish publishing

### Holocaust Tragedy

Vilna also represents:

- **Destruction**: The vibrant Jewish community murdered
- **Lost Civilization**: The "Jerusalem of Lithuania" destroyed
- **Memory**: Lives on in writings and memories
