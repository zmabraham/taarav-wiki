# Homel (Gomel) — הומל / גומל

**One of the Strongest Chabad Communities in Russia**

**Location**: Mogilev Governorate (now Gomel Region, Belarus) — approximately 300 km southeast of Minsk  
**Chabad Significance**: Home of R' Yitzchak Aizik Epstein (Aizik Homiler); major Chabad community  
**Current status**: Regional capital in Belarus; small Jewish community

---

## Overview

Homel (Belarusian: Гомель / Homiel; Russian: Гомель / Gomel; Yiddish: הומל / Homel) was one of the most important and vibrant Chabad communities in the Russian Empire. As home to [[entities/people/yitzchak-aizik-epstein|Rabbi Yitzchak Aizik Epstein]] (known as the "Aizik Homiler"), a prominent Chabad leader and disciple of the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] and [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]], Homel developed into a major center of Chabad scholarship and communal life.

The city's Chabad community was renowned for its scholarship, strong institutions, and resistance to Soviet persecution in the underground period. Throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries, Homel was consistently recognized as one of the "strongest Chabad towns" in Russia — second only to [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] itself in importance to the movement.

---

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Homel is located in southeastern Belarus:

- **Location**: On the Sozh River (tributary of the Dnieper), approximately 300 km southeast of Minsk
- **Region**: Historic Mogilev Governorate; now Gomel Region
- **Border Region**: Close to the border with Ukraine
- **Terrain**: River valley with mixed forest and agricultural land

### Historical Jewish Community

Homel had a large and dynamic Jewish population:

- **Population**: By late 19th century, approximately 20,000+ Jews (over 50% of city population)
- **Economy**: Jews dominated trade, crafts, and manufacturing
- **Political Diversity**: Zionist, socialist, Bundist, and religious groups all active
- **Pogroms**: Homel experienced antisemitic violence (1903, 1905–06)

---

## Chabad's Arrival and Growth

### Early Chabad Presence

Chabad established itself in Homel in the early 19th century:

- **First Chassidim**: Followers of the Alter Rebbe settled in Homel
- **Community Growth**: Under the Mitteler Rebbe and Tzemach Tzedek, the community expanded
- **Institutions**: Synagogue, mikvah, cheder, and later yeshiva established
- **Mashpiim**: Spiritual mentors guided the community's growth

### R' Yitzchak Aizik Epstein — The Aizik Homiler

#### Biography

[[entities/people/yitzchak-aizik-epstein|Rabbi Yitzchak Aizik Epstein]] (1770s–1857) was the most famous Chabad figure associated with Homel:

- **Background**: Born in nearby Bobruisk; learned in Vilna under the Vilna Gaon (initially a Mitnaged)
- **Conversion to Chassidus**: Became a follower of the Mitteler Rebbe
- **Relocation**: Settled in Homel and became its spiritual leader
- **Reputation**: Known as one of the greatest Chabad scholars of his generation

#### Teachings and Influence

- **Scholarship**: Authored Chabad works on _Tanya_ and Chassidus
- **Correspondence**: Maintained close relationship with the Tzemach Tzedek
- **Students**: Trained many Chabad scholars who spread across Russia
- **Halacha**: Served as halachic authority for the community

#### Historical Significance

The Aizik Homiler represented:

- **Intellectual Bridge**: Combined Mitnagdic learning (Vilna) with Chassidic warmth
- **Community Leadership**: Model of local rabbinic leadership in Chabad
- **Literary Legacy**: His commentaries on Tanya remain studied today

## The Chabad Community in Homel

### Institutional Life

By the mid-19th century, Homel had a complete Chabad infrastructure:

- **Great Synagogue**: The main Chabad synagogue, one of several in the city
- **Beis Midrash**: Study hall open day and night
- **Mikvah**: Ritual bath following Chabad customs
- **Yeshiva**: Advanced Torah study institution
- **Cheder**: Elementary education
- **Chevra Kadisha**: Burial society

### Economic and Social Structure

Chabad chassidim in Homel included:

- **Wealthy Merchants**: Timber, grain, and textile traders who supported the community
- **Craftsmen**: Skilled workers in various trades
- **Scholars**: Full-time Torah study supported by community
- **Mixed Economy**: Diverse occupational structure similar to other shtetls

### Relations with Other Jewish Groups

Homel's diverse Jewish community included:

- **Mitnagdim**: Opponents of Chassidism, though tensions were less severe in Homel than in Lithuania
- **Other Chassidic Groups**: Karlin, Chernobyl, and other courts had followers
- **Zionists**: Early Zionist movement attracted many Homel Jews
- **Socialists**: Bundists and others promoted Jewish secular socialism
- **Chabad's Position**: Strong but one of many religious streams

## Notable Events and Figures

### The Pogroms (1903, 1905–06)

Homel experienced major antisemitic violence:

- **1903 Pogrom**: Severe violence against Jewish community
- **1905–06 Pogroms**: Following the Russo-Japanese War and 1905 Revolution
- **Jewish Self-Defense**: Homel Jews organized armed self-defense groups
- **Chabad Response**: Chabad families participated in defense and relief efforts

### Soviet Era

Under Bolshevik rule:

- **Religious Suppression**: Synagogues closed, religious education banned
- **Persecution**: Rabbis arrested, religious leaders targeted
- **Underground**: Chabad families maintained observance in secret
- **Gulags**: Many Homel chassidim were imprisoned or exiled

### World War II

During the Holocaust:

- **Nazi Occupation**: Germany captured Homel (August 1941)
- **Ghetto**: Jews forced into ghetto
- **Massacres**: Thousands of Homel Jews murdered
- **Resistance**: Some Jews escaped to join partisans
- **Liberation**: Red Army recaptured Homel (November 1943)

## Soviet Underground Period

### Continuing Chabad Presence

Despite Soviet persecution, Homel maintained an underground Chabad community:

- **Secret Minyanim**: Private prayer services in apartments
- **Underground Education**: Torah taught to children in secret
- **Coded Communication**: Correspondence with Rebbes abroad using coded language
- **Mohalim and Shochtim**: Ritual circumcisers and slaughterers operated clandestinely

### Connection to the Rayatz

Homel's underground maintained contact with the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]:

- **Letters**: Coded correspondence sent via couriers
- **Financial Support**: Funds sent from abroad to support underground activities
- **Guidance**: The Rayatz provided spiritual leadership from abroad

### The Great Escape (1946–47)

Homel chassidim participated in the mass escape:

- **Polish Passports**: Many obtained forged Polish passports
- **Moscow Hub**: Gathered in Moscow before leaving USSR
- **Refugee Camps**: Transited through Poland to displaced persons camps
- **Final Destinations**: Most reached [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Israel]] or America

---

## Modern Homel

### Post-Soviet Belarus

Since 1991, Homel is part of independent Belarus:

- **Population**: Approximately 500,000 (small Jewish minority)
- **Jewish Community**: Small remnant community with synagogue and cultural activities
- **Chabad Presence**: Some Chabad activity today but not the major center of the past

### Historical Memory

- **Former Sites**: Locations of synagogues and Jewish institutions marked
- **Memorials**: Holocaust memorials commemorate the destroyed community
- **Historical Recognition**: Homel's importance in Chabad history acknowledged

## Homel's Place in Chabad History

### A Model Community

Homel exemplified the ideal Chabad community:

- **Scholarship**: High level of Torah learning
- **Institutions**: Complete religious infrastructure
- **Leadership**: Strong local rabbinic figures like the Aizik Homiler
- **Resilience**: Survived persecution, pogroms, and communism

### Literary and Intellectual Contributions

Homel contributed to Chabad thought:

- **Commentaries on Tanya**: The Aizik Homiler's writings
- **Halachic Works**: Local rabbinic decisions with broader influence
- **Correspondence**: Letters to/from the Rebbes preserved

### Underground Heroism

In the Soviet period, Homel demonstrated:

- **Resistance**: Continued observance despite persecution
- **Sacrifice**: Risked arrest to maintain Jewish life
- **Community Solidarity**: Supported each other through difficult times
