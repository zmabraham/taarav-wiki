# Bukhara / Central Asia

**Ancient Jewish Communities and WWII Refuge**

Full documentation: [[summaries/chabad-bukhara|אדמו"רי חב"ד ויהדות בוכרה]]

---

## Overview

Central Asia — comprising present-day Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and surrounding regions — contains some of the world's oldest Jewish communities. Bukharan Jews (speaking Bukhori/Judeo-Tajik) maintained Jewish life for over a millennium largely separated from Ashkenazic and Sephardic developments. Chabad's contact with this community deepened dramatically during WWII when thousands of Chabad chassidim fled the Nazi invasion eastward.

---

## The Bukharan Jewish Community

Bukharan Jews are distinct in:

- Language: Bukhori (Judeo-Tajik)
- Customs: unique Sephardic-influenced practices
- History: continuous presence predating the Islamic conquest of Central Asia
- Geographic isolation from mainstream Jewish centers

---

## Chabad-Bukharan Contact

### Rashab Period (1882–1920)

The Rashab's leadership coincided with Russian Imperial consolidation of Central Asia, bringing Russian Jews and Bukharan Jews into closer contact. Initial Chabad-Bukharan relations established.

### WWII: Cities of Refuge (1941–1945)

When Nazi Germany invaded the Soviet Union (June 1941), Chabad chassidim fled eastward. **Samarkand** became the primary refuge — a major Central Asian city with an established Jewish (including Bukharan) community. Thousands of Chabad chassidim spent the war years there.

Key memoirs documenting this period:

- [[summaries/zichronosai|Zichronosai]] — R' Yisrael Yehuda Levi's account of Samarkand
- [[summaries/lemaan-yedau|Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu]] — another Samarkand memoir

The encounter between Eastern European Chabad chassidim and Bukharan Jewish communities created lasting bonds and cultural exchange.

### Post-War

Many Bukharan Jews emigrated to Israel and New York, where they encountered Chabad institutions and the movement's contemporary outreach programs.
