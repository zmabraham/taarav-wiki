# Dvinsk (Daugavpils) — דווינסק

**Major Chabad Center in Latvia and Home of the Rogatshover Gaon**

**Location**: Latvia (southeastern Latvia, on the Daugava River)
**Jewish Population**: Large Jewish community — approximately 11,000–13,000 Jews in early 20th century
**Significance**: One of the most important Jewish cities in Latvia; Chabad presence across several generations; home of the Rogatshover Gaon
**Holocaust**: Jewish community largely annihilated 1941

---

## Overview

Dvinsk (Latvian: Daugavpils; Russian: Двинск / Dvinsk; Yiddish: דווינסק / Dvinsk; earlier name: Dineburg) was the second-largest city in Latvia and home to one of the most significant Jewish communities in the Baltic region. With tens of thousands of Jews, the city supported multiple Chabad synagogues, produced distinguished rabbis, and was home to one of the greatest Torah scholars of the 20th century — the [[entities/people/rogatshover-gaon|Rogatshover Gaon]] (R' Yosef Rozin).

Dvinsk had a continuous Chabad presence from the early 19th century through the Holocaust, with a succession of distinguished Chabad rabbis who served the community and maintained links to the Chabad leadership. The city was also an important node in the Chabad network directed from [[entities/places/riga|Riga]] by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] during 1927–1933.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Dvinsk is located in southeastern Latvia:

- **Location**: On the Daugava River, near the borders of Lithuania, Belarus, and Russia
- **Region**: Historically part of Livonia, then Courland and Vitebsk province; became part of independent Latvia 1920
- **Transportation**: Major railway junction connecting Russia, Lithuania, and the Baltic
- **Size**: Second-largest city in Latvia

### Jewish Community

Dvinsk had one of Latvia's largest and most diverse Jewish communities:

- **Population**: Approximately 11,000–13,000 Jews, comprising 25–40% of the city's population
- **Diversity**: Mix of Mitnagdim, various Chassidic groups (Chabad prominent), and secular Jews
- **Institutions**: Multiple synagogues, yeshivos, schools, and cultural organizations
- **Economy**: Jews prominent in trade, crafts, and professional life
- **Languages**: Yiddish, Russian, and later Latvian

## Chabad in Dvinsk

### First Chabad Rabbi: R' Zalman Zezvmir

The first dedicated Chabad rabbi of Dvinsk:

- **Period**: Early 19th century
- **Background**: Came from the early generation of Lithuanian Chassidim
- **Role**: Served the Chabad minyan in Dvinsk
- **Legacy**: Established the Chabad community on a firm footing

### Second Chabad Rabbi: R' Yehuda Leib Zlkind Batlan

A distinguished scholar and leader:

- **Period**: Mid-19th century
- **Scholarship**: Torah scholar who authored the sefer *Zecher Yehuda*
- **Title "Batlan"**: A term of honor indicating he devoted himself entirely to Torah study without commercial concerns
- **Legacy**: The book *Zecher Yehuda* preserved his Torah insights and remained a reference in Dvinsk

### Later Rabbis

Dvinsk's Chabad rabbinical succession continued:

- **Third and fourth rabbis**: Chabad rabbis who maintained the community's character
- **Fifth Rabbi — Rogatshover Gaon**: R' Yosef Rozin (1858–1936), an extraordinary Torah genius, served as rabbi of Dvinsk for decades (see below)

### The Rogatshover Gaon in Dvinsk

[[entities/people/rogatshover-gaon|R' Yosef Rozin]] (1858–1936), known as the Rogatshover Gaon, was one of the greatest Talmudic scholars of the 20th century:

- **Arrival**: Came to Dvinsk as rabbi in the late 19th century
- **Extraordinary genius**: Known for encyclopedic knowledge and radical analytical method
- **Major work**: *Tzafnas Paneach* — revolutionary Talmudic responsa and novellae
- **Chabad background**: Though not strictly a Chabad Chassid, he served the Chabad community in Dvinsk
- **Respect**: Held in awe by scholars across all Jewish communities
- **Death**: Died in Dvinsk 1936, before the Holocaust

## Chabad Institutions in Dvinsk

### Synagogues

Dvinsk had multiple Chabad synagogues:

- **Main Chabad synagogue**: Large synagogue for the main Chabad community
- **Additional minyanim**: Smaller groups meeting for prayer in various locations
- **Distinctive prayer**: Nusach Ari (Chabad liturgical rite) used in these synagogues

### Educational Institutions

- **Cheder**: Elementary Torah education for Chabad children
- **Yeshiva connections**: Links to Tomchei Temimim yeshiva network
- **Mashpiim**: Spiritual mentors who guided the community

## Interwar Period (1920–1940)

Under Latvian independence:

- **Religious freedom**: Latvia's constitution guaranteed religious autonomy
- **Continued activity**: Chabad institutions functioned openly
- **Connections to Riga**: When the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] lived in [[entities/places/riga|Riga]] (1927–1933), Dvinsk Chassidim had relatively easy access to their Rebbe
- **Community life**: Active Jewish political, cultural, and religious life

---

## The Holocaust

### Soviet Occupation (1940–1941)

- **Annexation**: Soviet Union occupied Latvia in 1940
- **Restrictions**: Religious life suppressed; community leaders arrested
- **Nationalization**: Jewish-owned businesses expropriated

### Nazi Occupation (1941–1944)

- **Invasion**: Germany captured Dvinsk shortly after invading the Soviet Union (July 1941)
- **Massacres**: Dvinsk Jews were murdered in mass shootings in late 1941
- **Ghetto**: Briefly concentrated in a ghetto before liquidation
- **Destruction**: The large and vibrant Jewish community was annihilated

## Modern Daugavpils

Today:
- **Population**: Approximately 85,000 (with tiny Jewish minority)
- **Jewish Community**: Small remnant community
- **Chabad presence**: Chabad-Lubavitch activity in the city
- **Memory**: Holocaust memorials and some acknowledgment of Jewish history

## Dvinsk's Place in Chabad History

### Succession of Leaders

Dvinsk demonstrates the multi-generational character of Chabad communities:

- **Early roots**: Established in first generation of Lithuanian/Latvian Chabad
- **Scholarly tradition**: Produced distinguished Torah scholars like R' Yehuda Leib Zlkind
- **Great guest**: The presence of the Rogatshover Gaon elevated the community's Torah standing

### Strategic Location

- **Junction point**: Dvinsk's railway connections made it a hub for Chabad emissaries and visitors
- **Latvia network**: Key node in Latvian Chabad community network
- **Soviet border**: Proximity to Soviet territory made Dvinsk important for underground communications
