# ירושלים — Jerusalem

**Country**: Israel (capital); previously Ottoman Palestine, then British Mandate Palestine  
**Chabad significance**: Center of Chabad activity in Eretz Yisrael; home of Toras Emes yeshiva (from 1922); headquarters of Israeli Chabad institutions

---

## Overview

Jerusalem, the holiest city in Judaism and one of the four "holy cities" of Eretz Yisrael (along with Hebron, Tzfas, and Tiberias), became the primary center of Chabad institutional life in the Land of Israel after WWI. The city's role in Chabad history spans from early nineteenth-century settlement to the founding of key institutions in the twentieth century.

---

## Early Chabad Presence

While [[entities/places/hebron|Hebron]] was the primary Chabad center in Eretz Yisrael during the nineteenth century, Chabad maintained a presence in Jerusalem as well. The city attracted Chabad chassidim as pilgrims and settlers, though the organized Chabad community was less developed there than in Hebron until the early twentieth century.

---

## Toras Emes Yeshiva (from 1922)

The key institutional development was the relocation of **Yeshiva Toras Emes** to Jerusalem. The yeshiva had been founded in [[entities/places/hebron|Hebron]] in 1911 when the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] purchased Beit Romano and sent [[entities/people/zalman-havlin|Rabbi Zalman Havlin]] to establish a Chabad yeshiva branch in Eretz Yisrael. WWI forced the Russian students to return to Russia.

After the Rashab's death in 1920, Rabbi Havlin reconstituted the yeshiva in Jerusalem in **1922** — finding the capital a more practical location for institutional development under the British Mandate.

See [[events/toras-emes-jerusalem|Toras Emes Jerusalem (1922)]].

---

## Chabad and Jerusalem's Old Yishuv

The Chabad community in Jerusalem was part of the **Old Yishuv** — the pre-Zionist traditional Jewish settlement — and navigated the complex politics of Jerusalem's many Jewish communities: Sephardic, various Chassidic groups, Lithuanian yeshiva world, and eventually Zionist newcomers.

---

## Modern Chabad in Jerusalem

Jerusalem became home to major Chabad institutions in the twentieth century, including:

- Yeshivot and kollelim
- A significant Chabad neighborhood presence
- Connections to the central Chabad institutions in [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway]]
