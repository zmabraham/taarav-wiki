# Rakishok (Rokiškis) — ראָקישאָק

**Lithuanian Chabad Community Visited by the Rayatz in 1931**

**Location**: Northeastern Lithuania (now Rokiškis)
**Jewish Population**: Approximately 2,000–3,000 Jews in early 20th century
**Significance**: Chabad community served by R' Shmuel Levitan; visited by the Rayatz in Adar 5691 (1931)
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated 1941

---

## Overview

Rakishok (Yiddish: ראָקישאָק; Lithuanian: Rokiškis) was a town in northeastern Lithuania with an established Chabad community. Within Chabad history, Rakishok is notable for two reasons: it was served by [[entities/people/shmuel-levitan|R' Shmuel Levitan]], one of the rare Lithuanian Chabad rabbis who survived the Holocaust and continued his work in the United States; and it was visited by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in Adar 5691 (February/March 1931) — part of the Rayatz's first significant journey to Lithuania.

---

## Geography

Rakishok is located in northeastern Lithuania, in a region historically populated by Jews, Lithuanians, and some Belarusians. The town served as a market center for the surrounding agricultural region.

---

## Chabad in Rakishok

### The Chabad Community

Rakishok had an established Chabad community:

- **Synagogue**: A Chabad synagogue following Nusach Ari
- **Community life**: Regular prayer, Chassidic gatherings, and communal services
- **Part of Lithuanian Chabad network**: Connected to the broader Lithuanian and Latvian Chabad structure

### R' Shmuel Levitan

The Chabad rabbi of Rakishok:

- **Service**: Served as the Chabad rabbi of the community
- **Leadership**: Provided rabbinical guidance and spiritual direction
- **Survivor**: Unlike most Lithuanian Chabad leaders, R' Levitan survived the Holocaust
- **America**: After the Holocaust, R' Levitan continued his work in the United States
- **Legacy**: One of the few direct links to pre-Holocaust Lithuanian Chabad who could transmit that tradition personally

---

## The Rayatz's Visit to Rakishok (1931)

### Context of the Visit

The Rayatz's visit to Rakishok occurred during a broader trip to Lithuania:

- **Date**: Adar 5691 (February/March 1931)
- **First major Lithuanian visit**: This was one of the first significant trips the Rayatz made to Lithuania after settling in [[entities/places/riga|Riga]] in 1927
- **Journey**: The Rayatz traveled to several Lithuanian communities on this trip

### The Visit Itself

- **Warm reception**: The community received the Rayatz enthusiastically
- **Torah discourse**: The Rayatz delivered teachings and maamarim to the assembled Chassidim
- **Strengthening**: The visit strengthened the local community's connection to Chabad leadership
- **Community meetings**: The Rayatz met with local leaders, assessed needs, and gave guidance

### Significance

The visit to Rakishok:

- **Direct connection**: Gave the local Chassidim a personal encounter with their Rebbe
- **Morale boost**: Particularly important given the isolation of Lithuanian Chabad from the main Chabad center
- **Organization**: Helped organize the Chabad network in northeastern Lithuania

---

## Interwar Period (1918–1940)

Under Lithuanian independence:

- **Constitutional freedoms**: The Lithuanian constitution provided religious freedoms for minorities
- **Community activity**: Chabad institutions functioned openly
- **Network connections**: Connections to the Rayatz's base in Riga and later Warsaw

---

## The Holocaust

When Germany invaded Lithuania in June 1941:

- **Rapid destruction**: Lithuanian Jews were among the first to be systematically murdered
- **1941 massacres**: Rakishok's Jews were murdered in the summer/fall of 1941
- **R' Levitan's escape**: R' Shmuel Levitan managed to escape before the destruction
- **Community annihilated**: The Chabad community built over generations was destroyed
