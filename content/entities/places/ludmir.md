# Ludmir (Volodymyr-Volynsky) — לודמיר

**Volhynian Town Visited by the Rayatz in 1932**

**Location**: Volhynia, eastern Poland (interwar period; now Volodymyr, Ukraine)
**Jewish Population**: Approximately 10,000–12,000 Jews in early 20th century
**Significance**: Visited by the Rayatz in 1932; historic Chassidic center
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated 1942

---

## Overview

Ludmir (Yiddish: לודמיר; Polish: Włodzimierz Wołyński; Ukrainian: Володимир-Волинський / Volodymyr-Volynsky) was one of Volhynia's oldest and most historically significant Jewish communities. The city is famous in broader Jewish history as the home of the "Maiden of Ludmir" (Chana Rochel Verbermacher, 1805–1892), a rare female Chassidic "Rebbe" figure. Within Chabad history, Ludmir is notable for a visit by the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] in 1932.

---

## Geography and Demographics

### Historical Setting

Ludmir is one of the oldest cities in the Volhynia region:

- **Location**: Northwestern Ukraine, on the Lug River
- **History**: Ancient city dating to at least the 10th century; one of Volhynia's oldest settlements
- **Region**: Historic center of Volhynia; later a district capital in Russian-ruled Ukraine, then interwar Poland

### Jewish Community

Ludmir had a large and historically significant Jewish community:

- **Population**: Approximately 10,000–12,000 Jews by the early 20th century
- **History**: Jewish presence since at least the 15th century
- **Chassidic traditions**: Various Chassidic groups active in the city
- **Culture**: Center of Jewish learning, commerce, and culture in Volhynia

---

## The Maiden of Ludmir

Ludmir is famous in broader Jewish history for:

- **Chana Rochel Verbermacher (1805–1892)**: Known as the "Maiden of Ludmir" (Ludmirer Moyd)
- **Unique figure**: One of the only women in Jewish history to have a following as a Chassidic leader
- **Her court**: Conducted Tish, gave Torah teachings, and had followers
- **Later life**: Eventually settled in the Land of Israel

---

## Chabad in Ludmir

### Chabad Presence

Ludmir had a Chabad community:

- **Part of Volhynian Chabad**: One of many Chabad-connected communities in Volhynia
- **Network connections**: Connected to the Polish Chabad network directed from Warsaw

### The Rayatz's Visit (1932)

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] visited Ludmir in 1932:

- **Date**: 1932 (during the Rayatz's years in Warsaw, 1933, or possibly Riga)
- **Purpose**: Visiting Jewish communities, strengthening Chabad institutions, and meeting followers
- **Reception**: The community welcomed the Rayatz
- **Significance**: One of the Rayatz's visits to Volhynian communities

---

## The Holocaust

### Destruction (1942)

Ludmir's Jewish community was annihilated:

- **German occupation**: Nazis occupied Volhynia in 1941
- **Ghetto**: Jews concentrated under brutal conditions
- **Liquidation**: The Jewish community was murdered in 1942
- **Total loss**: One of Volhynia's oldest Jewish communities was destroyed

---

## Ludmir's Place in Chabad History

The Rayatz's visit to Ludmir places this ancient city within the Chabad story of the interwar period — a time when the Rayatz worked intensively to strengthen and expand Chabad presence in Eastern Europe before the Holocaust.
