# Rostov-on-Don (ראסטאוו)

**City in southern Russia; wartime home of the Rebbe Rashab and Tomchei Temimim (1915–1920); base of Rayatz after Rashab's passing.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Country** | Russia |
| **Period as Chabad center** | 5675–5684 (1915–1924) |
| **Rebbes here** | Rebbe Rashab (fled here from Lubavitch due to WWI); Rayatz (until move to Leningrad) |
| **Rashab's death here** | 2 Nissan 5680 (1920) |

## Overview

When WWI spread to the Lubavitch region in 5675 (1914/1915), the Rebbe Rashab fled with his household and the yeshiva relocated to Rostov-on-Don in southern Russia. Rostov remained the Chabad headquarters for approximately a decade.

The Rebbe Rashab died in Rostov on 2 Nissan 5680 (1920). His son, the Rayatz, continued to live there and lead Chabad for a period. R' Yechiel Komisar (the former rebbe melamed from TT-Lubavitch) settled in Rostov, where he had a food shop.

Israel Jacobson visited Rostov for Shemini Atzeret farbrengens and for yechidus with the Rayatz. In the memoirs, he describes his encounter in yechidus where the Rayatz told him to consider emigrating to America.

## Rayatz's Move from Rostov

After the Rashab's passing, the Rayatz managed Chabad activities from Rostov until he moved to Leningrad (~5684/1924). The Soviet government's increasing pressure made Leningrad a more practical base for his national and international activities.
