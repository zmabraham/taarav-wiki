# Pskov — פסקוב

**Ancient Russian City Near Chabad Territory**

**Location**: Northwestern Russia, near the Estonian and Latvian borders  
**Historical Significance**: Capital of Pskov Governorate, which included key Chabad communities  
**Chabad Connection**: Administrative center for the region containing [[entities/places/nevel|Nevel]] and other Chabad towns

---

## Overview

Pskov (Russian: Псков; Yiddish: פסקוב / Pskov) is one of Russia's oldest cities, situated in the northwest near the borders of Estonia and Latvia. With a history stretching back over a thousand years, Pskov served as the capital of the Pskov Governorate during the Russian Empire and later the Pskov Oblast in the Soviet period. For Chabad history, Pskov's significance lies primarily in its role as the administrative center of a region that contained several important Chabad communities, most notably [[entities/places/nevel|Nevel]].

The city itself was not a major Chabad center, but the surrounding region — particularly the towns and shtetls of the Pskov Governorate — developed significant Chabad communities during the 18th and 19th centuries. The geographic position of Pskov, at the crossroads between Russia, the Baltic states, and Belarus, made the region a natural corridor for the spread of Chassidism from its origins in White Russia (Belarus) into the Russian interior.

---

## Historical Context

### Medieval Pskov

Pskov was an important medieval city and republic. The Pskov Republic, which existed from 1348 until its conquest by Moscow in 1510, was a major trading center on the northwestern frontier of the Russian world. Its location on the Velikaya River, near Lake Peipus, made it strategically important for trade and military defense. The city's ancient kremlin (fortress) and numerous churches testify to its historical significance.

### Imperial Russian Period

Under the Russian Empire, Pskov became the capital of Pskov Governorate, an administrative unit that encompassed a large territory in northwestern Russia. The governorate bordered the Baltic provinces to the west and the Belarusian provinces to the south, placing it at the intersection of multiple Jewish communities and cultural zones. During this period, the Jewish communities of the region — including those in Chabad towns like Nevel — fell under Pskov's administrative jurisdiction.

---

## Jewish and Chabad Connections

### Jewish Community

Pskov itself had a modest Jewish community, as the city was outside the Pale of Settlement for much of the imperial period. However, the broader region contained significant Jewish populations, particularly in towns closer to the Belarusian border.

### Nevel and the Chabad Towns

The Pskov Governorate's most significant Chabad community was [[entities/places/nevel|Nevel]], located in the southern part of the governorate near the Belarus border. Nevel developed into a vibrant Chabad center with a strong tradition of Torah study and Chassidic life. During the Soviet period, Nevel became particularly notable for its role in the underground Chabad network, maintaining clandestine yeshiva operations and religious activities despite intense persecution.

Other communities in the broader region also had Chabad connections, part of the network of towns and shtetls that stretched across the borderland between Belarus and Russia.

---

## Modern Pskov

Today, Pskov is a medium-sized Russian city with a population of approximately 200,000. The city preserves its historical architecture, including its medieval kremlin and numerous churches. The Jewish community of the region, once vibrant, was largely destroyed during the Holocaust and further diminished under Soviet rule. A small Jewish community has been reestablished in recent decades.
