# ווירצבורג — Würzburg

**Location**: Franconia, Bavaria, Germany  
**Chabad significance**: The Rashab's extended residence (1906–1907); location of the composition of the Hemshech Samech Vov (המשך תרס"ו)

---

## Overview

Würzburg is a city in Franconia (northern Bavaria, Germany) on the Main River. For Chabad history, it is primarily significant as the city where [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] spent an entire year (Cheshvan 5667 – after Shavuot 5667 / November 1906 – June 1907), one of the most productive periods of his life and the composition of his most celebrated chassidic masterwork.

---

## The Rashab's Year in Würzburg (1906–1907)

The Rashab arrived in Würzburg in Cheshvan 5667 (November 1906) and remained until after Shavuot 5667 (1907). He came partly for medical treatment — the German-speaking lands, with their advanced medicine, were a regular destination — but the year became far more than a medical sojourn.

**Background**: The Rashab had been forced to leave [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] when Zionist workers (_Poalei Tzion_) attacked his household. He first traveled to Germany and settled in Würzburg.

**The Kaiser's Stationery**: During his stay, the Rashab visited Kaiser Wilhelm II's writing room (through the Rayatz's bribery of a guard) and wrote a Chassidic discourse (maamar) on the Kaiser's personal stationery using the Kaiser's pen — a remarkable act of appropriating imperial space for spiritual purposes.

**The Hemshech Samech Vov**: The Rashab composed the famous **המשך תרס"ו** ("Hemshech Samech Vov"), one of the longest, most profound, and most celebrated discourses in the Chabad Chassidic canon. The discourse, begun earlier, was completed in Würzburg. It remains a cornerstone text in advanced Chabad study.

**Yud-Tes Kislev in Würzburg**: On the [[events/yud-tes-kislev|Chabad holiday of Yud-Tes Kislev]] (19 Kislev), the Rashab held a farbrengen (Chassidic gathering) in Würzburg attended by German Jews. The event made a deep impression — the Rashab's prayer style, which lasted over two hours, astonished the German Jewish attendees who were unaccustomed to such intensity.

**Orthodox Coordination**: During the Würzburg year, the Rashab worked with [[entities/people/shlomo-zalman-breuer|Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Breuer]] and [[entities/people/yaakov-rosenheim|Rabbi Yaakov Rosenheim]] to draft a detailed plan ("התאגדות היראים" / Histaadus HaYereim) for strengthening Orthodox Judaism in Germany.

**The Rashab's Anti-German Stance**: Despite his German sojourns, the Rashab's attitude toward Germany was deeply negative. He called Kaiser Wilhelm II "Amalek" and "vill-he-lem" (a Yiddish pun meaning "wants to destroy the world"), and prophesied Germany's ultimate defeat — a remarkable prescience given WWI and WWII.

---

## Historical Context

Würzburg had a historic Jewish community dating to medieval times; the city was the site of significant crusader-era persecution. By the late nineteenth century, a small but established Jewish community remained. The Rashab's presence in 1906–1907 brought an unusual visitor — the leader of the Chabad Chassidic movement — into contact with traditional Bavarian Jewish life.
