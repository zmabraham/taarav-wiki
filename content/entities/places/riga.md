# Riga, Latvia — ריגה

**The Rayatz's Headquarters in Exile (1927–1933)**

**Location**: Latvia (independent republic 1918–1940; under Soviet rule 1940–1941; under Nazi occupation 1941–1944)  
**Chabad Headquarters**: 1927–1933 — the Rayatz's base after expulsion from USSR  
**Current status**: Capital of Latvia; small Jewish community

---

## Overview

Riga (Latvian: Rīga; Russian: Рига / Riga; Yiddish: ריגע / Rige) was the first home of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] after his dramatic expulsion from the Soviet Union in 1927. As the capital of independent Latvia, Riga offered the religious freedom and safety that the Soviet Union denied, allowing the Rayatz to rebuild his movement and direct the underground Chabad network in Russia from abroad.

During six crucial years (1927–1933), Riga served as Chabad's world headquarters. From here, the Rayatz maintained contact with the thousands of Chassidim suffering under Soviet persecution, established new institutions in freedom, and laid the groundwork for the next phase of Chabad's expansion.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Riga is located on the Baltic Sea:

- **Location**: On the Gulf of Riga, at the mouth of the Daugava River
- **Region**: Historic Livonia; capital of independent Latvia (1918–1940)
- **Port City**: Major Baltic port with trade connections across Europe
- **Climate**: Maritime — cool summers, cold winters, moderate precipitation

### Historical Jewish Community

Riga had one of the most vibrant Jewish communities in Eastern Europe:

- **Population**: Approximately 40,000+ Jews (over 10% of city population) by 1930s
- **Diversity**: Mix of religious, secular, Zionist, and Bundist Jews
- **Institutions**: Synagogues, schools, yeshivos, cultural organizations
- **Economy**: Jews prominent in trade, professions, and industry
- **Culture**: Center of Hebrew and Yiddish culture, theater, and press

- **Trade**: Port facilities supported international commerce

## The Rayatz's Arrival in Riga (1927)

### From Soviet Prison to Riga

The Rayatz's journey from Soviet imprisonment to Riga was dramatic:

- **1927 Arrest**: Arrested in [[entities/places/st-petersburg|Leningrad]], imprisoned in Shpalerka prison
- **Death Sentence**: Initially sentenced to death, later commuted to exile
- **International Pressure**: World leaders and Jewish organizations campaigned for his release
- **Release**: Freed on 3 Tammuz 5687 (July 3, 1927)
- **Expulsion**: Immediately ordered to leave Soviet Union
- **Choice of Riga**: Selected Riga for its Jewish community, religious freedom, and proximity to USSR

### First Days in Riga

- **Arrival**: Arrived in Riga shortly after release
- **Warm Welcome**: Jewish community greeted him enthusiastically
- **Housing**: Established residence in Riga's Jewish quarter
- **Immediate Challenges**: Rebuilding movement after persecution

- **Coded Correspondence**: Letters sent using euphemisms and symbols
- **Emissaries**: Trusted messengers carried messages and funds
- **Strategic Planning**: Coordinated resistance to Soviet persecution
- **Fundraising**: Collected money to support underground communities

### Communication Methods

- **Couriers**: Messengers who crossed borders at great risk
- **Coded Language**: Letters written in seemingly innocent terms
- **Smuggling**: Funds and texts smuggled into USSR
- **Third-Party Channels**: Using travelers and commercial connections

### Support for Soviet Chassidim

- **Financial Aid**: Funds sent to underground communities
- **Guidance**: Halachic and spiritual direction
- **Encouragement**: Moral support for those facing persecution
- **Escape Planning**: Beginning to plan ways to bring chassidim out of USSR

## Life in Riga: 1927–1933

### The Years of Activity

The Rayatz's six years in Riga were productive:

- **1927**: Arrival and establishment
- **1928–1931**: Building institutions, directing underground
- **1932**: Preparing for potential relocation
- **1933**: Decision to move to [[entities/places/warsaw|Warsaw]]

### Decision to Move to Warsaw

The Rayatz left Riga for Warsaw in 1933 for several reasons:

- **Larger Jewish Population**: Warsaw's 350,000 Jews offered larger constituency
- **Central Location**: Warsaw was more centrally located in European Jewish world
- **Political Concerns**: Growing instability in Latvia
- **Expansion**: Opportunities for broader Chabad activities

## Post-1933 Riga

### Continuing Chabad Presence

After the Rayatz's departure:

- **Institutions**: Chabad institutions continued in Riga
- **Community**: Local Chabad community maintained presence
- **Connection**: Links to Rayatz in Warsaw and later New York

### World War II and the Holocaust

Riga's Jewish community was devastated:

- **Soviet Occupation (1940–1941)**: Annexed by USSR; religious life restricted
- **Nazi Occupation (1941–1944)**: Germany captured Riga (July 1941)
- **Riga Ghetto**: Jews forced into ghetto under horrific conditions
- **Massacres**: Most Riga Jews murdered, including many Chabad families
- **Destruction**: The vibrant Jewish community was annihilated
