# Nikolaev (Mykolaiv)

**Major port city in the Kherson gubernia of the Russian Empire (present-day southern Ukraine); home to a significant Chabad community**

## Overview

Nikolaev (Ukrainian: Mykolaiv) was a major Black Sea port city founded in 1789. It became one of the largest cities in southern Ukraine, serving as a significant naval and commercial hub. Its Jewish community grew substantially during the 19th century, and the Chabad presence there was well-established by the mid-19th century.

## Chabad Presence

Key Chabad figures associated with Nikolaev include:

- **R' Avraham David Lavot** — served as rabbi of Nikolaev, an ancestor of the Seventh Rebbe; author of the halachic work Kav Naki
- **R' Asher Grotsman** — shoichet and mashpia in Nikolaev; disciple of R' Baruch Tamrish of Kremenchug; proofreader of the 1900 Vilna edition of the Tanya

The community's character combined serious Talmudic learning with devotion to Chassidus, reflecting the dual heritage of its leaders.

## Geographic Context

Nikolaev was located far south of the traditional Chabad heartland in White Russia and the central Pale. Its position at the intersection of trade routes and its cosmopolitan port culture created a distinctive Jewish community where Chabad Chassidim lived alongside other streams of Orthodox Jewry and a growing secular intelligentsia.
