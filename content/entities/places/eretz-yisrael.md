# Eretz Yisrael — ארץ ישראל

**Chabad's Historical Presence in the Land of Israel**

---

## Overview

Chabad's connection to the Land of Israel runs from 1777 — predating the Zionist movement by a century — to the present. The story encompasses early Chassidic immigration to Safed, the establishment of the Chabad community in Hebron, sustained financial support from the Rebbes in Russia, and ultimately the modern [[entities/places/kfar-chabad|Kfar Chabad]] settlement (founded 1949) which brought Soviet-era survivors to the land.

The full history is documented in [[summaries/toldot-chabad-eretz-yisrael|תולדות חב"ד בארץ הקודש]] (43 chapters), covering two centuries of continuous Chabad presence.

### Geographic Scope

This page covers Chabad's presence across historic Palestine/Israel:

- **Northern Israel**: Safed (Tzfat), Tiberias, Haifa
- **Central Israel**: Jerusalem, Tel Aviv/Jaffa
- **Southern Israel**: Hebron, Gaza (historical)
- **Modern Settlements**: Kfar Chabad, and dozens of Chabad institutions nationwide

## Historical Periods

### First Aliyah (1777)

The 1777 aliyah of [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]] and [[entities/people/avraham-of-kalisk|Abraham of Kalisk]]:

- Approximately 300 Chassidim immigrated from Belarus
- Settled primarily in Safed and Tiberias
- Predated the Chabad movement as a distinct entity (Alter Rebbe was Mitteler Rebbe's student)
- Established the first Chassidic community in the Land of Israel

### Ottoman Period (1800s–1917)

Chabad communities developed under Ottoman rule:

- **Hebron**: Established as main Chabad center (1820s)
- **Jerusalem**: Secondary community developed
- **Safed/Tiberias**: Original Chassidic settlements continued
- **Financial Support**: _Kolel Chabad_ system organized by Rebbes in Russia

### British Mandate (1917–1948)

- **1917**: British captured Palestine from Ottomans
- **1929 Hebron Massacre**: Forced evacuation of Chabad community from Hebron to Jerusalem
- **1930s–40s**: Continued growth under British administration
- **1948**: State of Israel established

### State of Israel (1948–Present)

- **1949**: [[entities/places/kfar-chabad|Kfar Chabad]] founded by [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]
- **1950s**: Mass immigration of Soviet Chabad survivors
- **1960s–present**: Expansion of Chabad institutions across Israel
- **Today**: Hundreds of Chabad institutions serving all segments of Israeli society

## Key Cities and Communities

### Safed (Tzfat) — צפת

**First Chassidic Settlement (1777)**

#### Geographic Context

Safed is located in the Upper Galilee:

- **Elevation**: Approximately 900 meters (2,950 feet) above sea level
- **Climate**: Cool summers, cold winters (occasional snow)
- **History**: One of Judaism's Four Holy Cities (with Jerusalem, Hebron, Tiberias)

#### Chabad Presence

- **1777 Arrival**: Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk's group settled here
- **Pre-Chabad Community**: These early Chassidim preceded the formal Chabad movement
- **Kabbalistic Tradition**: Safed was the center of Kabbalah since the 16th century (Arizal)
- **Relocation**: Over time, many Chassidim relocated to Tiberias

#### Historical Events

- **Earthquakes**: Safed suffered major earthquakes (1837) that damaged the community
- **Chabad Continuity**: Some Chabad families remained throughout 19th century
- **Modern Revival**: Today, Safed has a significant Chabad presence serving residents and tourists

---

### Tiberias (Tveria) — טבריה

**Early Chassidic Center**

#### Geographic Context

Located on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee (Kinneret):

- **Low elevation**: Approximately -200 meters below sea level
- **Hot climate**: Warm winters, very hot summers
- **Holy City**: One of Judaism's Four Holy Cities

#### Chabad Presence

- **Relocation from Safed**: Many of the 1777 Chassidic immigrants moved here
- [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-of-vitebsk|Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk]] is buried here
- **Chabad Community**: Maintained presence through 19th–20th centuries

#### Today

- Chabad institutions serve the local community
- Pilgrimage site to Chassidic graves
- Yeshivos and outreach programs

---

### Hebron (Chevron) — חברון

**Main Chabad Community (1820s–1929)**

#### Geographic Context

Located in the Judean Hills:

- **Elevation**: Approximately 930 meters (3,050 feet) above sea level
- **Ancient City**: One of oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world
- **Holy Significance**: Tomb of the Patriarchs (Ma'arat HaMachpelah)

#### Chabad Community Establishment (1820s)

From approximately the 1820s, Hebron became the center of Chabad life in Israel:

**Why Hebron?**

- **Affordable Living**: Lower cost of living compared to Jerusalem
- **Existing Sephardi Community**: Welcoming infrastructure
- **Holy City Status**: Burial place of Adam, Eve, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebecca, Jacob, Leah
- **Relative Autonomy**: Less outside interference than Jerusalem

**Institutions Established**

- **Synagogues**: Multiple Chabad synagogues for the growing community
- **Yeshivot Magen Avot**: Torah study institution
- **Torat Emet Yeshiva**: Later established (1911), became major Chabad institution
- **Community Support**: Funded by _Kolel Chabad_ from Russia

**Community Life**

- **Demographics**: Primarily Chabad chassidim from Russia
- **Leadership**: Rabbis sent by the Rebbes in Russia
- **Economy**: Many worked in traditional Jewish occupations (scholars, craftsmen)
- **Relations**: Generally positive relations with local Arab population and Sephardi community

#### The 1929 Massacre and Destruction

**Events of August 1929**

- **Arab Riots**: Widespread anti-Jewish violence across Palestine
- **Hebron Massacre**: 67 Jews murdered, many injured
- **Chabad Victims**: Several Chabad families among the dead
- **Destruction**: Homes and institutions looted and burned

**Aftermath**

- **Evacuation**: British authorities evacuated surviving Jews to Jerusalem
- **Community Dispersal**: Chabad community relocated primarily to Jerusalem
- **Yeshiva Reestablished**: Torat Emet Yeshiva reopened in Jerusalem
- **Historical Trauma**: The massacre remained a defining memory for Chabad in Israel

#### Modern Hebron

- **1967 Reestablishment**: After Six-Day War, Jews returned to Hebron
- **Chabad Presence**: Chabad institutions serve the renewed Jewish community
- **Torat Emet**: Continues as major Chabad institution in Jerusalem (not Hebron)
- **Pilgrimage**: Chabad visitors come to pray at Ma'arat HaMachpelah

---

### Jerusalem (Yerushalayim) — ירושלים

**Secondary Center → Primary Community (post-1929)**

#### Geographic Context

Located in the Judean Mountains:

- **Elevation**: Approximately 750 meters (2,460 feet) above sea level
- **Holy City**: Holiest city in Judaism
- **Historic Quarter**: Old City with Jewish Quarter, Western Wall (Kotel)

#### Chabad Presence in Jerusalem

**19th Century**

- **Secondary Status**: Smaller than Hebron community initially
- **Synagogues**: Chabad houses of worship established
- **Scholars**: Torah scholars supported by Kolel Chabad

**Early 20th Century**

- **Growth**: Community expanded as Jerusalem grew
- **Institutions**: Yeshivos, schools, community organizations

**Post-1929 (After Hebron Massacre)**

- **Influx from Hebron**: Many former Hebron Chabad families relocated to Jerusalem
- **Torat Emet Yeshiva**: Reestablished in Jerusalem, became major Chabad institution
- **New Center**: Jerusalem became the primary Chabad community in Israel

#### Modern Jerusalem

Today, Jerusalem has one of the largest Chabad communities in the world:

**Institutions**

- **Torat Emet Yeshiva**: Major Chabad yeshiva in Nachlaot neighborhood
- **Beit Rivka**: Seminary for girls
- **Chabad Houses**: Dozens of outreach centers across the city
- **Kolel Chabad**: Continues its historic charitable work
- **770-style Synagogue**: Recreation of 770 Eastern Parkway in Jerusalem

**Neighborhoods**

- **Nachlaot**: Historic Chabad presence
- **Beit Yisrael**: Chabad community center
- **Old City**: Chabad activities near Kotel
- **Citywide**: Outreach in virtually all neighborhoods

### Tel Aviv / Jaffa — תל אביב-יפו

**Modern Community**

#### Geographic Context

- **Location**: Mediterranean coastal plain
- **Climate**: Mediterranean — hot, dry summers; mild, wet winters
- **Modern City**: Tel Aviv founded 1909; Jaffa is ancient port city

#### Chabad Presence

**Historical Development**

- **20th Century**: Chabad community developed in Tel Aviv
- **Yeshiva**: Established to serve the growing community
- **Outreach**: Chabad House activities serving secular Jewish population

**Modern Tel Aviv**

- **Chabad House**: Active outreach center
- **Beit Chaya Mushka**: Seminary for women
- **Community**: Growing Chabad community
- **Contrast with Jerusalem**: Tel Aviv's secular character requires different outreach approach

**Significance**

- **Cultural Bridge**: Chabad connects secular Tel Aviv with traditional Judaism
- **Business Community**: Many religious business professionals in Tel Aviv area
- **Tourism**: Chabad serves visitors to Israel's commercial capital

---

### Haifa — חיפה

**Northern Port City**

#### Geographic Context

- **Location**: Northern Israel on Mediterranean coast
- **Mount Carmel**: City built on and around the mountain
- **Mixed Population**: Jews, Arabs, other communities

#### Chabad Presence

- **Institutions**: Chabad House, yeshiva, community services
- **Bahá'í Relations**: Chabad coexists with Bahá'í World Center (also on Mount Carmel)
- **Outreach**: Activities serving diverse population

### Kfar Chabad — כפר חב"ד

**The Modern Chabad Village (1949–Present)**

See full page: [[entities/places/kfar-chabad|Kfar Chabad]]

**Overview**

- **Founded**: 1949 (5709) by [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]
- **Location**: Near Lod, in central Israel's coastal plain
- **Founders**: Chabad immigrants from Soviet Union (survivors of underground)
- **Design**: Rayatz's vision for agricultural village combining Torah study with work

**Growth and Development**

- **1956 Terror Attack**: Fedayeen attack; the 7th Rebbe's famous response: _"Venach ve'harbeh zeh"_ — "And you shall rebuild and you shall continue to build this place"
- **Expansion**: From small village to major Chabad center
- **Institutions**: Yeshivos, schools, printing press, kosher food production

**Today**

- **Population**: Thousands of residents
- **Institutions**: Dozens of educational and communal institutions
- **Visitor Center**: Museum and exhibits about Chabad history
- **Pilgrimage**: Visitors from around the world

## The Kolel Chabad System

### Structure and Function

_Kolel Chabad_ (כולל חב"ד) was the organizational framework managing Chabad's Israel communities:

**Fundraising and Distribution**

- **Chalukah System**: Collected charity from diaspora communities
- **Shadarim**: Emissaries sent to Russia and Europe to collect funds
- **Distribution**: Allocated money to scholars, families, institutions in Israel

**Institutional Management**

- **Rabbis**: Appointed and paid community rabbis
- **Yeshivos**: Funded Torah study institutions
- **Charity**: Supported widows, orphans, poor families
- **Infrastructure**: Maintained synagogues, mikvaos, schools

**Agreements and Relations**

- **Sephardi Community**: Maintained agreements with local Sephardi leadership
- **Other Kolelim**: Coordinated with other diaspora-funded organizations
- **Ottoman/British Authorities**: Navigated changing political regimes

### The Ma'amad Fund

The _ma'amad_ (מעמד) — a fund for supporting the Palestine communities — was central to Chabad-Israel connection:

**Administration**

- [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]]: Established the fund
- Subsequent Rebbes: Continued and expanded the fund
- Collectors: Special emissaries who traveled raising money

**1826 Arrest Connection**
The [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe's]] 1826 arrest was partly based on this fund:

- **Russian Suspicion**: Czarist authorities suspected the Palestine remittances were seditious
- **Accusation**: Money allegedly funded anti-government activities
- **Reality**: The fund supported Torah study and charity in the Holy Land

**Legal Battle**

- The Mitteler Rebbe was arrested for alleged misuse of the ma'amad
- After investigation, the charges were proven false
- This episode demonstrated the Rebbes' commitment to Israel communities despite personal risk
