# Old Montefiore Cemetery — בית החיים מונטיפיורי

**The Ohel and Chabad's Most Visited Pilgrimage Site**

**Location**: 121-83 Springfield Boulevard, Queens, New York  
**Significance**: Burial site of the Rebbe, the Rebbetzin, and the Rayatz  
**Chabad Importance**: Major pilgrimage site since [[events/gimmel-tammuz-1994|Gimmel Tammuz 1994]]

---

## Overview

Old Montefiore Cemetery is a Jewish cemetery in the Springfield Gardens neighborhood of Queens, New York, that has become one of the most significant pilgrimage sites in the contemporary Jewish world. It is here that the [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe]], Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson, and the Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka are buried, alongside the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] (sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn) and the Rebbetzin Nechama Dina.

The site, known simply as "the Ohel" (the tent or enclosure) in Chabad parlance, draws tens of thousands of visitors annually. It has become a focal point of [[concepts/hiskashrus|hiskashrus]] (spiritual connection) for Chabad chassidim worldwide, especially on significant dates such as the anniversary of the Rebbe's passing on [[events/gimmel-tammuz-1994|Gimmel Tammuz]], the anniversary of the Rayatz's passing on Yud Shevat, and before Rosh Hashanah and other major occasions.

---

## The Ohel

### The Structure

The Ohel is a small building erected over the graves of the Rebbe and the Rayatz. Visitors approach the Ohel along a paved pathway, passing through a preparation area where they can write _pidyonos_ (petitions) and perform the ritual of removing leather shoes, in keeping with the tradition observed at holy sites. Inside the Ohel, the graves are covered with a cloth, and visitors pray, read Tehillim (Psalms), and leave their written petitions on the gravestones.

### The Practice of Visiting

The custom of visiting the Ohel has become a defining practice of post-1994 Chabad life. Thousands of people visit annually, including not only Chabad chassidim but also Jews from all backgrounds who come to pray at the Rebbe's resting place. The practice draws on the broader Jewish tradition of visiting the graves of tzadikim (righteous individuals), particularly before Rosh Hashanah and on other significant occasions. Many visitors report experiencing a sense of spiritual connection and personal solace at the site.

### Pidyonos

Visitors typically write _pidyonos_ — personal prayers and requests — which are placed on the gravestone. This practice was common during the Rebbe's lifetime as well, when written petitions would be brought to the Rebbe during [[concepts/yechidus|yechidus]] (private audience) or delivered to his office. At the Ohel, the tradition continues in adapted form.

## Who Is Buried Here

### The Rebbe and Rebbetzin

- **Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson** (1902-1994) — the seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe
- **Rebbetzin Chaya Mushka Schneerson** (1901-1988) — the Rebbe's wife

The Rebbe was buried here on 5 Tammuz 5754 (June 14, 1994), two days after his passing on [[events/gimmel-tammuz-1994|3 Tammuz]]. The Rebbetzin had been buried at Old Montefiore since her passing in 1988.

### The Rayatz and Rebbetzin Nechama Dina

- **Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn** (1880-1950) — the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe
- **Rebbetzin Nechama Dina Schneersohn** — the Rayatz's wife

The Rayatz was originally buried at a different location after his passing in 1950 and was later reinterred at Old Montefiore Cemetery.

---

## Location and Access

Old Montefiore Cemetery is located in Queens, approximately 30 minutes from [[entities/places/crown-heights|Crown Heights]] by car. The cemetery provides dedicated parking and facilities for visitors, including a visitors' center with washing stations, a place to write pidyonos, and restrooms. The site is maintained by Agudas Chasidei Chabad and is open year-round, with extended hours during peak visiting periods such as Gimmel Tammuz and Yud Shevat.
