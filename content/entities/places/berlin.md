# ברלין — Berlin

**Country**: Germany (capital)  
**Chabad significance**: Site of multiple Rebbe visits, the Rayatz's headquarters 1928–1933, and the seventh Rebbe's student years 1928–1931; post-Holocaust Chabad renewal (1996–)

---

## Overview

Berlin occupies a paradoxical position in Chabad history. The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rebbe Rashab]] visited it multiple times, despised Kaiser Wilhelm II, and composed some of his most important writing while in the German lands. The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] used it as headquarters for five years during the Weimar Republic. The future [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe]] studied there. And ultimately, despite the Holocaust that wiped out almost all of German Jewry, Chabad established one of its most symbolic post-war institutions in the same city.

---

## The Rashab's Berlin Connections (1885–1914)

**1885 Visit**: At age 24, the Rashab called on [[entities/people/azriel-hildesheimer|Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer]], the founder of Neo-Orthodox "Adass Jisroel" and the Berlin Rabbinical Seminary. Hildesheimer received him with great honor, treating the visit as an extension of his respect for the Rashab's father, the [[entities/people/rebbe-maharash|Maharash]].

**1889 Berlin Aquarium**: The Rashab visited the Berlin Aquarium to observe a creature he thought might be the chilazon — the sea animal from which the biblical sky-blue dye (techelet) is extracted. He observed a small creature secreting black liquid; the observation was later reported in correspondence with the [[entities/people/radzyner-rebbe|Radzyner Rebbe]] who was researching techelet.

**Würzburg Year (1906–1907)**: Though based in Würzburg that year, the Rashab passed through Berlin and maintained his German connections. See [[events/wurzburg-year-1906|Rashab's Year in Würzburg]].

**Correspondence**: The Rashab maintained ongoing correspondence with Berlin rabbis, including [[entities/people/meir-hildesheimer|Rabbi Meir Hildesheimer]] (son of Azriel) and participated in coordinating the "Histaadus HaYereim" Orthodox organization.

## The Rayatz's Berlin Years (1928–1933)

After arriving in Riga in 1927 following his expulsion from the Soviet Union, the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] relocated to Berlin in 1928. He used Berlin — capital of the Weimar Republic, home to one of Europe's largest Jewish communities (approximately 170,000) — as his headquarters during the final years of German democracy.

From Berlin he:

- Continued directing the underground Chabad network inside the Soviet Union
- Maintained correspondence with communities across Europe and America
- Participated in broader European Orthodox communal affairs
- Prepared for the move to Warsaw (1933)

His years in Berlin coincided exactly with Hitler's rise to power. The Rayatz left Berlin for Warsaw in 1933 — the year the Nazi regime began.

---

## The Seventh Rebbe in Berlin (1928–1931)

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson — the future seventh Rebbe — studied at the **University of Berlin** from 1928 to 1931, living at **7 Hanse-Ufer Street**. This address was confirmed through archival research decades later. He subsequently continued his studies in Paris.

His Berlin years preceded his assumption of Chabad leadership in 1951 by two decades.

---

## The Holocaust and Destruction of Berlin's Jews

Berlin's Jewish community of approximately 170,000 in 1933 was almost entirely murdered or expelled by 1945. The Nazi regime, headquartered in Berlin, administered the Holocaust from this city. The catastrophe severed all Chabad-Berlin connections that had developed over the previous half-century.

---

## Post-Holocaust Chabad Renewal (1996–)

Against all expectations, Chabad established a major presence in Berlin beginning in 1996, when [[entities/people/yehuda-teichtel|Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel]] arrived. Together with [[entities/people/shmuel-segel|Rabbi Shmuel Segel]], he established:

- The **Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin** — funded by local Jewish donors
- A synagogue seating 250
- The **Yudishe Traditsyone Shule Ohr Avner** — in a building formerly used as a Nazi officers' club
- A Jewish restaurant, mikveh, library, and youth center
- Shabbat minyanim growing from 20 to hundreds

See [[events/chabad-berlin-renewal|Chabad Berlin Renewal]].

**The Memorial Plaque (2001)**: On 3 Kislev 5762, the Berlin city government installed a memorial plaque at 7 Hanse-Ufer Street, the seventh Rebbe's former residence. Mayor [[entities/people/joachim-zeller|Joachim Zeller]] stated: "In those days, antisemitic signs were placed in Berlin. Today we place a memorial sign for the great Jewish leader." See [[events/berlin-memorial-plaque-2001|Berlin Memorial Plaque (2001)]].
