# Hebron — חברון / Chevron

**Ancient City and Chabad Community Destroyed in 1929**

**Location**: Judean Mountains, approximately 30 km south of Jerusalem  
**Jewish Population**: Historically significant Jewish community; Chabad presence from 1820s–1929  
**Significance**: Home to Chabad community for over a century; site of 1929 massacre; Tomb of the Patriarchs  
**Current status**: Palestinian city; small Jewish settlement in Hebron; Chabad activities in nearby Jewish areas

---

## Overview

Hebron (Arabic: al-Khalil; Hebrew: חברון / Chevron) is one of the holiest cities in Judaism, home to the Tomb of the Patriarchs (Ma'arat HaMachpelah) — the burial place of Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Rebecca, Jacob, and Leah. For Chabad, Hebron was the main community in the Land of Israel from approximately the 1820s until the 1929 massacre forced the Jewish community to evacuate.

The Chabad community in Hebron maintained institutions for over a century, supported by the _Kolel Chabad_ fund from Russia. The 1929 riots, in which 67 Jews were murdered, destroyed the community and forced survivors to relocate to [[entities/places/jerusalem|Jerusalem]]. Today, Chabad has a presence in nearby Jewish areas, and the yeshiva "Torat Emet" continues in Jerusalem as a reminder of the Hebron legacy.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Hebron is located in the Judean Mountains:

- **Location**: Approximately 30 km south of [[entities/places/jerusalem|Jerusalem]]
- **Elevation**: Approximately 930 meters (3,050 feet) above sea level
- **Terrain**: Mountainous with valleys
- **Climate**: Mediterranean — hot, dry summers; cool, wet winters; occasional snow

### Historical Significance

Hebron is one of the four holy cities of Judaism (along with [[entities/places/tzfas|Tzfat]], [[entities/places/tiberias|Tiberias]], and Jerusalem):

- **Ancient City**: One of oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world
- **Biblical Significance**: Mentioned numerous times in Torah
- **Tomb of the Patriarchs**: Ma'arat HaMachpelah — burial place of the biblical patriarchs and matriarchs
- **King David**: David was anointed king in Hebron and reigned there for seven years

## Jewish History in Hebron

### Ancient Period

Hebron has ancient Jewish roots:

- **Biblical Era**: Abraham purchased the cave of Machpelah; King David's capital
- **Roman-Byzantine Period**: Jewish community continued
- **Arab Conquest (7th century)**: Muslim rule but Jewish presence continued
- **Mamluk Period**: Restrictions on Jewish access to Machpelah
- **Ottoman Period (1517–1917)**: Jewish community slowly rebuilt

### Ottoman Era (1517–1917)

- **Early Ottoman**: Small Jewish community with limited access to holy sites
- **18th Century**: Renewed Jewish settlement
- **19th Century**: Growth of Jewish community, including Chabad arrival
- **Late 19th Century**: Expanded institutions and population

---

## Chabad's Arrival in Hebron (1820s)

### Why Hebron?

Chabad chose Hebron as its main center in the Land of Israel for several reasons:

- **Holy City**: Status as one of Judaism's four holy cities
- **Affordable Living**: Lower cost of living than Jerusalem
- **Existing Community**: Welcoming Sephardi community and infrastructure
- **Relative Autonomy**: Less outside interference than Jerusalem
- **Torah Study**: Conducive environment for scholarship

### Early Chabad Settlers

- **Immigration**: Chabad chassidim began arriving in the 1820s
- **From Russia**: Many came from the Russian Empire
- **Scholars**: Torah scholars seeking to live in the Holy Land
- **Community Building**: Established institutions and communal life

---

## The Chabad Community in Hebron

### Institutions

The Chabad community established and maintained:

- **Synagogues**: Multiple Chabad congregations and prayer houses
- **Yeshivot Magen Avot**: Torah study institution
- **Torat Emet Yeshiva**: Established 1911; became major Chabad institution
- **Community Services**: _chevra kadisha_ (burial society), charity funds, communal support
- **Housing**: Community housing for scholars and families

### Community Life

- **Demographics**: Primarily Chabad chassidim from Russia and elsewhere
- **Leadership**: Rabbis sent by the Rebbes in Russia
- **Economy**: Torah study supported by _Kolel Chabad_; some trade and crafts
- **Relations**: Generally positive relations with local Arab population and Sephardi community

### Support from Russia

The Hebron community was supported by:

- **Kolel Chabad**: Fund collecting money from diaspora communities
- **Rebbes' Patronage**: The [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] and [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] were major patrons
- **Shadarim**: Emissaries sent to raise funds
- **Ma'amad Fund**: Special fund supporting the Israel communities

## The 1929 Hebron Massacre

### Background

Tensions between Jewish and Arab communities were rising in 1929:

- **Nationalist Tensions**: Rising Arab nationalism and Jewish immigration
- **Western Wall Incident**: Disputes over holy sites in Jerusalem sparked broader violence
- **Rumors**: False rumors about Jews attacking Al-Aqsa Mosque

### The Massacre (August 23–24, 1929)

The Hebron massacre was devastating:

- **Arab Mob**: Arab mob attacked Jewish neighborhoods
- **Victims**: 67 Jews murdered, including women, children, and elderly
- **Chabad Victims**: Several Chabad families among the dead
- **Injuries**: Dozens more injured
- **Destruction**: Homes, synagogues, and institutions looted and burned

### Aftermath

- **British Evacuation**: British authorities evacuated surviving Jews to Jerusalem
- **Community Dispersion**: The Jewish community of Hebron ended
- **Torat Emet Relocation**: The yeshiva reestablished in Jerusalem
- **Historical Trauma**: The massacre remained a defining memory for Chabad in Israel

---

## Post-1929 Hebron

### British Mandate Period (1929–1948)

- **No Jewish Community**: No Jewish community in Hebron after 1929
- **Jordanian Rule (1948–1967)**: After 1948 Arab-Israeli War, Hebron came under Jordanian rule; Jews prohibited from entering
- **No Access**: Jews could not visit holy sites, including Machpelah

---

## Modern Hebron (1967–Present)

### After Six-Day War (1967)

- **Israeli Capture**: Israel captured Hebron in June 1967
- **Jewish Return**: Jews returned to Hebron for first time since 1929
- **Settlement**: Jewish settlement reestablished in Hebron
- **Machpelah Access**: Jews regained access to Tomb of the Patriarchs

### Contemporary Jewish Community

Today, Hebron has a small Jewish community:

- **Population**: Approximately 500–1,000 Jews in Hebron proper
- **Adjacent Areas**: Larger Jewish community in nearby Kiryat Arba
- **Tensions**: Ongoing tensions with Palestinian population
- **Religious Significance**: Continued Jewish connection to holy sites

### Chabad Presence Today

- **Activities**: Chabad has presence in nearby Jewish areas
- **Torat Emet in Jerusalem**: The yeshiva continues in Jerusalem, not Hebron
- **Pilgrimage**: Chabad visitors come to pray at Machpelah
- **Memory**: The Hebron legacy remembered in Chabad historical consciousness
