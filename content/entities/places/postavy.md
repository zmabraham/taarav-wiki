# Postavy (Pastavy) — פּאָסטאָוו

**Eastern Polish Chabad Community; Succession of Chabad Rabbis**

**Location**: Eastern Poland (interwar period; now Pastavy, Belarus)
**Jewish Population**: Significant Jewish community through early 20th century
**Significance**: Served successively by R' Koperstock and R' Freidin as Chabad rabbis
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated during WWII

---

## Overview

Postavy (Yiddish: פּאָסטאָוו; Polish: Postawy; Belarusian: Паставы / Pastavy) was a town in the Vilna region of eastern Poland (interwar period), now in Belarus. The town had a Chabad community that was served by at least two successive Chabad rabbis — R' Koperstock (who later moved to Globoke) and R' Freidin — indicating a well-organized and continuous Chabad presence.

---

## Geography

Postavy is located in the Vilna voivodeship of interwar Poland, in the eastern borderlands of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth's former territory. The region had numerous Jewish communities, many with Chabad roots.

---

## Chabad in Postavy

### The Community

Postavy's Chabad community:

- **Established presence**: A functioning Chabad community with its own rabbi
- **Continuity**: Maintained its Chassidic character across multiple rabbinical tenures
- **Community services**: Chabad prayer, learning, and communal functions

### R' Koperstock — First Documented Chabad Rabbi

- **Service**: Served as Chabad rabbi of Postavy before moving to [[entities/places/globoke|Globoke]]
- **Later career**: Moved to Globoke, where he continued serving as Chabad rabbi
- **Survival**: R' Menachem Mendel Koperstock survived the Holocaust and settled in Haifa, Israel

### R' Freidin — Successor Chabad Rabbi

- **Service**: Served as Chabad rabbi of Postavy after Koperstock's departure
- **Period**: Active in the interwar period
- **Community continuity**: His service maintained the Chabad character of the community
- **Fate**: Shared the fate of the community in the Holocaust

---

## The Holocaust

Postavy's Jewish community was murdered during the Nazi occupation of eastern Poland/Belarus, part of the systematic destruction of this region's Jewish population.
