# קאוונע / קובנה — Kovno / Kaunas

**Country**: Lithuania (second city, former capital)  
**Yiddish name**: קאוונע (Kovne)  
**Hebrew**: קובנה  
**Chabad significance**: Location where Jan Zwartendijk issued "Curaçao visas"; Rayatz's visit (1895); escape route transit point

---

## Overview

Kovno (Lithuanian: Kaunas) was the de facto capital of interwar Lithuania and home to one of the most significant Jewish communities in Eastern Europe. In Chabad history, it is most prominently associated with two events: the Rayatz's early mission there (1895) and the critical role it played in the WWII escape route when Dutch Consul [[entities/people/jan-zwartendijk|Jan Zwartendijk]] issued "Curaçao visas" there.

---

## The Rayatz's 1895 Mission

The _Undaunted_ biography of the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] records that at age 15, the Rayatz traveled to **Kovno (1895)** and **Vilna (1896)** to represent his father the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] at conferences and meetings with Jewish leaders. These early diplomatic missions — traveling to major Jewish centers on the Rebbe's behalf — formed the Rayatz's first experience of public leadership.

---

## Jan Zwartendijk and the Curaçao Visas

Kovno's most consequential role in Chabad (and world Jewish) history came in 1940. When Jewish refugees in Lithuania were desperately seeking escape routes after the Soviet occupation, Dutch Consul **Jan Zwartendijk** — stationed in Kovno — began stamping passports with a note that "no visa is required for admission to Curaçao" (a Dutch Caribbean colony). This technically accurate but practically unusual stamp gave Jews a "destination" that allowed them to obtain transit visas.

Simultaneously, Japanese Consul [[entities/people/chiune-sugihara|Chiune Sugihara]] in Kovno began issuing transit visas to Japan. The combination — Zwartendijk's Curaçao stamps and Sugihara's Japanese transit visas — opened the remarkable escape route: **Vilna → Moscow → trans-Siberian railway → Vladivostok → [[entities/places/kobe|Kobe]], Japan → [[entities/places/shanghai|Shanghai]]**.

Chabad students were among the first groups to use this route. The Rayatz coordinated funding and logistics from Brooklyn to support their journey.

---

## Jewish Community and Holocaust

Kovno's Jewish community numbered approximately 35,000–40,000 before WWII. After the Nazi invasion in June 1941, the community was almost entirely murdered — primarily at the Ninth Fort and other killing sites around the city. The rich Jewish institutional life, including significant Orthodox and Zionist presence, was annihilated.
