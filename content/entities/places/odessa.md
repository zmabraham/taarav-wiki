# Odessa — אדעס

**Port City Gateway for Chabad Refugees and Emigrants**

**Location**: Kherson Governorate (now Odessa Oblast, Ukraine) — on the Black Sea
**Chabad Significance**: Major Ukrainian port city with significant Jewish community; key transit point for Chabad refugees and emigrants
**Current status**: Major Ukrainian city; active Jewish community with Chabad presence

---

## Overview

Odessa (Ukrainian: Одеса / Odesa; Russian: Одесса / Odessa; Yiddish: אדעס / Odes) was one of the most important Jewish cities in the Russian Empire and a significant location in Chabad history. Founded in 1794 on the Black Sea coast, Odessa rapidly grew into a cosmopolitan port city with one of the largest and most diverse Jewish communities in the world. By the late 19th century, Jews constituted a substantial portion of the city's population.

For Chabad, Odessa served multiple roles across different periods: a center of Jewish communal life, a site of both religious and secular Jewish cultural flourishing, a transit point for chassidim traveling to and from [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]], and a key departure city for those emigrating from the Russian Empire. The city's [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] sought medical treatment there, and prominent Jewish figures associated with the city included the scientist Waldemar Haffkine, with whom the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] later corresponded about combating Soviet persecution.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Odessa sits on the northwestern coast of the Black Sea:

- **Location**: Major port city in southern Ukraine, approximately 450 km south of [[entities/places/kiev|Kiev]]
- **Region**: Kherson Governorate in imperial Russia; now Odessa Oblast, Ukraine
- **Port**: Ice-free port on the Black Sea, gateway to the Mediterranean
- **Terrain**: Steppe plateau overlooking the sea; mild climate compared to the rest of the Pale of Settlement
- **Climate**: Moderate continental — warmer winters than northern Jewish centers

### Historical Jewish Community

Odessa's Jewish community was among the largest and most dynamic in the Russian Empire:

- **Population**: By the late 19th century, approximately 140,000 Jews (over 30% of the city's population)
- **Cosmopolitan Character**: Mixed population of Russians, Ukrainians, Jews, Greeks, and other nationalities
- **Economic Power**: Jews dominated commerce, banking, grain trade, and the professions
- **Cultural Flourishing**: Center of Hebrew literature, Jewish journalism, and the Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment)

## Chabad in Odessa

### Chabad Community Development

Chabad established a presence in Odessa during the 19th century:

- **19th Century Growth**: Under the [[entities/people/tzemach-tzedek|Tzemach Tzedek]] and subsequent Rebbes, Chabad chassidim settled in the growing city
- **Community Institutions**: Synagogue, beis midrash, mikvah, and educational facilities
- **Wealthy Supporters**: Prominent Chabad merchants and businessmen in Odessa supported the movement financially
- **Correspondence**: Regular communication with the Rebbes in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]]

### Relationship with Lubavitch

Odessa was part of the network of Chabad communities tied to the Lubavitch headquarters:

- **Pilgrimage**: Chassidim from Odessa traveled to Lubavitch for holidays and to receive the Rebbe's guidance
- **Financial Support**: Odessa's wealthy Chabad families contributed to the upkeep of institutions in Lubavitch
- **Emissaries**: Shluchim from Lubavitch visited Odessa to strengthen the community
- **Correspondence**: Letters from the Rebbes addressed communal questions and provided direction

### The Rashab's Visits

The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] and his family visited Odessa for health reasons:

- **Summer 1883**: The Rashab traveled to Odessa (and Yalta) seeking medical care, accompanied by his young son Yosef Yitzchak (the future Rayatz)
- **Medical Treatment**: Odessa's relatively advanced medical facilities attracted Jewish patients from across the Pale
- **Community Contact**: During visits, the Rashab and his family connected with the local Jewish community

## Odessa as Emigration Gateway

### Port of Departure

Odessa's Black Sea port made it a natural departure point for Jews leaving the Russian Empire:

- **Emigration to Eretz Yisrael**: Ships from Odessa carried Jewish immigrants to Jaffa and the growing Yishuv in Palestine
- **International Travel**: Steamer connections to Constantinople, Marseille, and other Mediterranean ports
- **Chabad Emigration**: Chabad chassidim used Odessa as a departure point when emigrating, particularly during periods of persecution
- **Post-Soviet Aliyah**: In the late 20th century, Odessa again became a gateway for Jewish emigration to Israel

### Refugee Transit

During periods of upheaval, Odessa served as a transit point:

- **Pogrom Refugees (1881-1905)**: Jews fleeing pogroms in southern Ukraine passed through Odessa
- **World War I**: Wartime displacement brought refugees to the port city
- **Transit to Central Asia**: Some wartime evacuation routes to [[entities/places/samarkand|Samarkand]] and [[entities/places/tashkent|Tashkent]] passed through or near Odessa

## Jewish Cultural and Religious Life

### A City of Contrasts

Odessa was unique in the Jewish world for its blend of tradition and modernity:

- **Religious Diversity**: Alongside Orthodox communities, Odessa had Reform synagogues, secular Jewish organizations, and Zionist groups
- **Haskalah Center**: The city was a bastion of the Jewish Enlightenment, with Hebrew publishing houses and secular Jewish schools
- **Literary Capital**: Odessa produced some of the greatest modern Jewish writers, including Hayim Nahman Bialik and Shaul Tchernichovsky
- **Zionist Activity**: A major center of early Zionist organizing and Hebrew cultural revival

### Chabad's Position

Within this diverse Jewish landscape, Chabad maintained its distinctive identity:

- **Traditional Observance**: Chabad families upheld rigorous religious standards despite the city's secular atmosphere
- **Educational Institutions**: Chabad-operated cheders and study circles
- **Communal Leadership**: Chabad rabbis provided guidance to traditional families
- **Counter-Influence**: Chabad served as a religious anchor against the pull of secularization

## The Soviet Era

### Bolshevik Revolution (1917)

The revolution transformed Odessa's Jewish community:

- **Violent Upheaval**: The city changed hands multiple times during the Russian Civil War (1918-1920)
- **Pogroms**: Devastating pogroms killed thousands of Odessa's Jews during the civil war
- **Sovietization**: Religious institutions closed or repurposed under communist rule
- **Underground**: Chabad maintained clandestine religious activity, including secret minyanim and Torah education

### World War II and the Holocaust

The Holocaust devastated Odessa's Jewish community:

- **Romanian Occupation (1941-1944)**: Unlike most of Ukraine, Odessa was occupied by Romania rather than Germany directly
- **Massacres**: Tens of thousands of Odessa's Jews were murdered, including the infamous Dalnik massacre (October 1941)
- **Deportations**: Surviving Jews were deported to Transnistria, where many perished
- **Liberation (1944)**: The Red Army recaptured Odessa; only a fraction of the pre-war Jewish community survived

---

## Modern Odessa

### Post-Soviet Revival

Since Ukrainian independence (1991):

- **Jewish Community Revival**: Significant rebuilding of Jewish communal life
- **Chabad Presence**: Chabad has established a synagogue, school, and community services in Odessa
- **Emigration**: Large-scale emigration to Israel reduced the Jewish population significantly
- **Cultural Memory**: Odessa's Jewish heritage is remembered through museums, memorials, and cultural events

---

## Odessa's Place in Chabad History

### Multiple Roles

Odessa occupied several roles in the Chabad story:

- **Community Center**: A significant Chabad community thrived amid the city's Jewish majority culture
- **Transit Gateway**: Port city connecting Russian Jews to the wider world
- **Medical Destination**: Where Rebbes and their families sought treatment
- **Cultural Encounter**: Where Chabad chassidim navigated the challenges of modernity and secularism
