# Otwock — אוטבוצק

**The Rayatz's Summer Residence and Escape Point (September 1939)**

**Location**: Poland (approximately 25 km southeast of Warsaw)  
**Chabad Significance**: The Rayatz's primary residence 1936–1939; site of the dramatic September 1939 escape  
**Current status**: Suburban city in Poland; former Chabad sites commemorated

---

## Overview

Otwock (pronounced: OHT-votsk; Polish: Otwock; Yiddish: אָטוואָצק / Otvotsk) is a Warsaw suburb that served as the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] primary residence from 1936 to 1939. A quiet health resort known for its forests and fresh air, Otwock provided the Rayatz with a peaceful environment for work and study during the tumultuous years leading up to World War II.

Most significantly, Otwock was the site of the dramatic September 1939 escape. As Nazi Germany invaded Poland, the Rayatz fled from Otwock — a journey that would eventually bring him to safety in the United States and allow him to rebuild Chabad after the Holocaust.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Otwock is located near Warsaw:

- **Location**: Approximately 25 km southeast of Warsaw
- **Terrain**: Forested area with sandy soil
- **Climate**: Slightly cooler than Warsaw due to forests; fresh air
- **Transportation**: Connected to Warsaw by train

### Why Otwock? A Health Resort

Otwock was a popular Warsaw suburb for several reasons:

- **Health Resort**: Known for fresh air and pine forests
- **Sanatoriums**: Many health facilities for respiratory illnesses
- **Summer Destination**: Wealthy Warsaw residents spent summers there
- **Quiet Environment**: Provided respite from crowded city

### Jewish Community

Otwock had a significant Jewish population:

- **Population**: Approximately 2,000–3,000 Jews (significant proportion of town)
- **Summer Residents**: Many Warsaw Jews summered in Otwock
- **Institutions**: Synagogues, schools, sanatoriums
- **Religious Diversity**: Mix of religious and secular Jews

## The Rayatz in Otwock (1936–1939)

### Establishing Residence

The Rayatz began spending time in Otwock around 1936:

- **Health Reasons**: The fresh air benefited the Rayatz's health
- **Quiet Environment**: Better for work and study than crowded Warsaw
- **Summer Residence**: Initially summers, eventually became primary residence
- **Proximity to Warsaw**: Close enough to maintain Warsaw activities

### Daily Life in Otwock

The Rayatz's routine in Otwock included:

- **Teaching**: Delivered _maamarim_ (Chassidic discourses) to followers who visited
- **Writing**: Composed correspondence and works
- **Receiving Visitors**: Chassidim traveled from Warsaw and elsewhere
- **Health**: Benefited from fresh air and quiet environment
- **Administration**: Directed Chabad affairs from Otwock residence

### The Otwock Residence

- **Location**: Villa or house in Otwock's Jewish area
- **Court**: Small court established with staff and facilities
- **Synagogue**: Arranged space for prayer services
- **Study**: Used Otwock's quiet for study and writing

## The September 1939 Escape

### Nazi Invasion of Poland

- **September 1, 1939**: Germany invaded Poland, starting World War II
- **Rapid Advance**: German forces advanced quickly toward Warsaw
- **Bombing**: Luftwaffe bombed Polish cities, including Warsaw area
- **Chaos**: Panic as civilians fled the advancing army

### The Decision to Flee

The Rayatz made the difficult decision to flee:

- **Assessment**: Realized Poland would fall to Nazis
- **Danger**: The Rayatz, as a prominent Jewish leader, was in grave danger
- **Planning**: Arranged escape route through still-unconquered eastern Poland
- **Timing**: Had to flee before Germans captured Otwock area

### The Escape Route

The dramatic journey from Otwock:

1. **Departure from Otwock**: Left Otwock as German forces approached
2. **Journey East**: Traveled eastward toward still-unoccupied Poland
3. **Border Crossing**: Attempted to cross into territory not yet occupied
4. **Soviet Encounter**: Encountered advancing Soviet forces (Germany and USSR had partitioned Poland)
5. **Temporary Detention**: Briefly held by Soviet authorities
6. **Release**: After intervention, allowed to continue journey
7. **Vilna**: Reached [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]] (then under Lithuanian control)
8. **Further Journey**: From Vilna to [[entities/places/riga|Riga]], Sweden, and eventually America

### The Escape's Significance

The escape from Otwock was crucial:

- **Saving the Rebbe**: The Rayatz survived to lead Chabad through the Holocaust and beyond
- **Leadership Continuity**: Allowed Chabad to rebuild after the war
- **Symbolism**: Represents divine providence and the Rebbe's mission

## The Holocaust in Otwock

### Nazi Occupation

Otwock under Nazi rule:

- **Capture**: German forces captured Otwock September 1939
- **Terror**: Immediate persecution of Jews
- **Forced Labor**: Jews conscripted for labor
- **Ghetto**: Otwock's Jews were likely forced into ghettos or deported

### Fate of Otwock's Chabad Community

- **Murder**: Most Otwock Chabad families were murdered in the Holocaust
- **Yeshiva Students**: Many yeshiva students killed
- **Destruction**: All Chabad institutions destroyed
- **Survivors**: Very few survived; those who did escaped early

### Treblinka

Many Otwock Jews were likely deported to Treblinka death camp:

- **Deportations**: 1942 saw mass deportations from Warsaw region
- **Treblinka**: Extermination camp approximately 80 km northeast of Warsaw
- **Murder**: Nearly all deportees were murdered upon arrival

## Postwar Otwock

### Communist Era (1945–1989)

Under communist rule:

- **Tiny Community**: Very few Jews returned to Otwock
- **Suppression**: Religious life restricted
- **Historical Memory**: Communist regime minimized Jewish history
- **Development**: Otwock expanded as Warsaw suburb

### Post-Communist Poland (1989–present)

Since fall of communism:

- **Democracy**: Poland became democratic
- **Historical Recognition**: Otwock's Jewish history acknowledged
- **Memorials**: commemorations of Jewish community and Holocaust
- **Chabad Memory**: The Rayatz's residence remembered

### Contemporary Otwock

Today's Otwock:

- **Population**: Approximately 40,000 (small Jewish minority)

- **Health Resort**: Still known for sanatoriums and fresh air
- **Jewish Sites**: Locations of former Jewish institutions marked

### Historical Memory

- **Former Sites**: Locations of synagogues and Jewish buildings
- **Memorials**: Holocaust memorials commemorate the destroyed community

Otwock represents:

- **Peace Before Storm**: Last years of normal Chabad life in Europe
- **Productivity**: The Rayatz's fruitful work period
- **Education**: Yeshiva students who studied there
