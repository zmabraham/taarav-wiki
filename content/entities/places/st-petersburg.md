# סנקט פטרבורג — St. Petersburg / Petrograd / Leningrad

**Country**: Russia  
**Names over history**: St. Petersburg (until 1914), Petrograd (1914–1924), Leningrad (1924–1991), St. Petersburg (1991–present)  
**Chabad significance**: Site of the Alter Rebbe's two arrests (1798, 1800); site of the Rayatz's arrest (1927); administrative center of the imperial and Soviet systems that persecuted Chabad

---

## Overview

No single Russian city is more consequential for Chabad history than St. Petersburg / Leningrad. The imperial capital in the Alter Rebbe's era and the Soviet administrative center under Stalin, the city witnessed or administered the major legal persecutions of Chabad leadership across three generations.

Note: A separate page [[entities/places/leningrad|Leningrad]] exists focusing on the Soviet-era period. This page covers the full historical scope.

---

## Imperial St. Petersburg and the Alter Rebbe's Arrests

**First Arrest (1798)**: The [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] was arrested and brought to the **Petropavlovskaya Fortress** (Peter and Paul Fortress) in St. Petersburg in **Tishrei 5559 (1798)**. After investigation by imperial authorities, he was released on **19 Kislev 5559 (1798)** — the date celebrated annually as [[events/yud-tes-kislev-1798|Yud-Tes Kislev]], the "Festival of Liberation" and Chabad's major annual holiday.

**Second Arrest (1800)**: The Alter Rebbe was arrested again in **1800** and brought once more to St. Petersburg. He was again released after investigation. See [[events/alter-rebbe-arrest-1800|Alter Rebbe's Second Arrest (1800)]].

The Czarist Senate and imperial courts in St. Petersburg also played a role in the later liberation of the [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] — the final imperial confirmation of his acquittal came from the **Russian Imperial Senate in Petersburg** in **21 Kislev 5588 (1827)**, though this arrived posthumously.

---

## Soviet Leningrad and the Rayatz's Arrest (1927)

After the 1917 Revolution, the city was renamed Petrograd (1914) and then Leningrad (1924). It was in **Leningrad**, on **15 Sivan 5687 (June 1927)**, that the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] was arrested by Soviet GPU agents at midnight at his residence. He was imprisoned at Shpalerka Prison (see [[events/rayatz-arrest-1927|Rayatz's Arrest (1927)]]).

Thousands of chassidim gathered at the **Leningrad train station** to see the Rayatz off after his liberation — as he departed Soviet Russia forever.

---

## The Dual Role

St. Petersburg/Leningrad represents the administrative machinery of both the Czarist and Soviet systems that attempted to suppress Chabad. In each era, the city's courts, prisons, and bureaucracies were engaged in persecuting Chabad leadership — and in each case, the Rebbe emerged (or was posthumously vindicated).
