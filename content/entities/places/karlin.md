# Karlin (קארלין)

**Town adjacent to Pinsk in the Minsk gubernia; birthplace of the Karlin Chassidic dynasty; center of Mitnagdic-Chassidic conflict in the 1770s**

## Overview

Karlin (Belarusian: Карлін) is a suburb or adjacent settlement to the city of Pinsk, located in the Minsk gubernia of the Russian Empire (present-day Belarus, near the Ukrainian border in the Polesie region). The town gave its name to one of the earliest and most distinctive Chassidic dynasties: the Karlin-Stolin school.

## The Karlin Chassidic Dynasty

The Karlin chassidic tradition was founded by Rabbi Aaron the Great of Karlin (Reb Ahrele), who was a major disciple of the Maggid of Mezeritch. Reb Ahrele attracted a significant following with his distinctive approach: passionate, intensely fervent prayer — physically and vocally expressive in ways that scandalized traditional observers — combined with deep personal holiness and concern for the spiritual welfare of simple Jews.

Reb Ahrele died young in 1772, and his primary disciple Rabbi Shlomo of Karlin continued the dynasty. However, Rabbi Shlomo's leadership was immediately challenged by the wave of Mitnagdic persecution following the Vilna Gaon's 1772 herem. The Mitnagdim of Pinsk and the surrounding region, alarmed by the Chassidic presence and particularly by the distinctive Karlin prayer style, organized opposition that culminated in the expulsion of Rabbi Shlomo from Karlin around תקל"ד–תקל"ה (1774–75).

## The Expulsion and Its Aftermath

The expulsion of Rabbi Shlomo from Karlin was one of the most dramatic episodes of Mitnagdic persecution during the 1770s. Rabbi Shlomo relocated to Ludmir (Vladimir-Volynsky in Ukraine), where he continued his leadership until his martyrdom in 1792. The Karlin community was scattered but not destroyed.

After Rabbi Shlomo's death, the Karlin tradition was carried on by disciples who eventually settled in Stolin, where Rabbi Asher of Stolin — who had studied under Rabbi Shlomo's successor Rabbi Mordechai of Lechovitch — established what became the Stolin (Karlin-Stolin) branch of the dynasty.

## Significance

Karlin illustrates the geographic concentration of Chassidic-Mitnagdic conflict in the Pinsk-Polesie region during the 1770s and the power that organized Mitnagdic communities could exercise through coordinated communal pressure and expulsion. The survival of the Karlin tradition through persecution, martyrdom, relocation, and eventual re-establishment in Stolin is one of the more resilient stories in eighteenth-century Chassidic history.
