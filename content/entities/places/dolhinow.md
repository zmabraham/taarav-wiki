# Dolhinow (Dolhinów) — דאָלגינאָוו

**Chabad Community in Eastern Poland/Belarus; Home of R' Gedalia Vilenskin**

**Location**: Eastern Poland (interwar period; now Daŭhinava, Belarus)
**Jewish Population**: Significant Jewish community through early 20th century
**Significance**: Served by R' Gedalia Vilenskin as Chabad rabbi
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated 1942

---

## Overview

Dolhinow (Yiddish: דאָלגינאָוו; Polish: Dolhinów; Belarusian: Даўгінава / Daŭhinava) was a small town in the Vilna region of eastern Poland (interwar period), now part of Belarus. The town had a Chabad community served by [[entities/people/gedalia-vilenskin|R' Gedalia Vilenskin]], who was among the Chabad rabbis active in this eastern borderland region.

---

## Geography

Dolhinow is located in what was the Vilna voivodeship (province) of interwar Poland — the eastern borderlands known in Yiddish as "Lite" (Lithuania) even though administratively Polish. The region had a dense concentration of small Jewish communities, many with Chabad sympathies.

---

## Chabad in Dolhinow

### The Community

Dolhinow's Chabad community was one of many in the eastern Polish/Lithuanian borderlands:

- **Chabad tradition**: The community followed Chabad customs and prayer
- **Part of the network**: Connected to the Chabad structure in the Vilna region

### R' Gedalia Vilenskin

The Chabad rabbi of Dolhinow:

- **Service**: Served as the dedicated Chabad rabbi of the community
- **Period**: Active in the interwar period
- **Background**: His family name "Vilenskin" (of Vilna) suggests family origins in the Vilna region
- **Role**: Provided rabbinical leadership according to Chabad tradition
- **Fate**: Details of his fate in the Holocaust are recorded in the Toldos Chabad source

---

## The Holocaust

Dolhinow's Jewish community was murdered during the Nazi occupation of eastern Poland/Belarus in 1942, part of the systematic destruction of this densely Jewish region.
