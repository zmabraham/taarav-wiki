# Shumyatz

A small town in the **Mogilev region of Belarus** (now Shumyachi, in Russia's Smolensk Oblast, near the Belarus border), home to a Chabad community during the Soviet era. It is the hometown of [[zusman-pradkin|R. Zusman Pradkin]], father-in-law of the narrator of *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu*.

## Geographic Context

Shumyatz (Shumyachi/Шумячи) sits in the region where Russia, Belarus, and Ukraine converge — the historical Pale of Settlement. The town was part of the Mogilev gubernia in the Russian Empire and later the Smolensk Oblast of the RSFSR in the Soviet period.

## Jewish and Chabad Community

The memoir's narrator describes R. Zusman Pradkin as the **shochet** (ritual slaughterer) and **de facto rabbi** of Shumyatz. This was typical of small Jewish towns in the region: a learned shochet with Torah knowledge would serve the entire community's religious needs — performing ritual slaughter, answering halachic questions, and providing religious leadership.

## Historical Context

By the Soviet period (1920s–1940s), Shumyatz's Jewish community was under increasing pressure. Religious practice was technically illegal, and rabbis/shochtim operated covertly. R. Zusman Pradkin's role in this environment required both religious competence and practical discretion.

## Connection to the Memoir

The narrator traveled to Moscow partly to find work and community in the larger Soviet Jewish underground. Through the matchmaking of [[zusha-paz|R. Zusha Paz]] in Moscow, he met and married R. Zusman Pradkin's daughter, linking him permanently to Shumyatz's Chabad community.
