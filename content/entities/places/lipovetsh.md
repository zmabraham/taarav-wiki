# Lipovetsh (Lypovets)

A town in Vinnytsia oblast, Ukraine, larger than the nearby shtetlach of Zhivotov, Titov, and Dashov. Lipovetsh became the Levin family's home after they fled the pogroms in 1920, and the memoir's account of early Soviet-era Jewish life centers here.

## Description
Lipovetsh was a regional center, larger than the surrounding shtetlach. When the Levin family arrived just before Passover 1920, they were hosted by R. Moshe Litvak, a respected elderly member of the community. The town had a large beis midrash where Rabbi Shlomo Sternberg prayed, along with the shochet R. Levi.

After the pogroms, Lipovetsh received hundreds of orphans from nearby towns that had been devastated (especially Titov). The Soviet authorities set up two orphanages ("Priyutin") managed by the Yevsektsia (Jewish Section of the Communist Party), which denied the orphaned children — many from rabbinical and pious homes — any connection to Judaism. Children begged to say kaddish for their murdered parents and were refused.

The Yevsektsia also operated a "Talmud Torah" building in town that actually taught Yiddish secular curricula and served as a center for anti-religious activity. At Chanukah 1921 (5682), they organized a public anti-religious parade mocking R. Efraim Shmuel Levin's cheder and Judaism generally.

The Levin family was declared "lishentsy" (persons deprived of civil rights) due to their religious observance and teaching Torah. Most religious families eventually left Lipovetsh for larger cities or emigrated. The town became increasingly desolate, with houses sold for firewood.

By 1925 (5685), the local rabbi had relocated to Kyiv, leaving the town without rabbinic leadership at a critical moment.
