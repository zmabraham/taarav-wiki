# Haditch (Gadyach) — האַדיטש

**Burial Place of the Alter Rebbe and Major Chabad Pilgrimage Site**

**Location**: Chernigov Governorate (now Poltava Oblast, Ukraine) — on the Psel River
**Chabad Significance**: Site of the Alter Rebbe's burial; one of the holiest sites in Chabad
**Current status**: Village in central Ukraine; Chabad ohel maintained as pilgrimage site

---

## Overview

Haditch, also known as Gadyach or Hadyach (Ukrainian: Гадяч / Hadyach; Russian: Гадяч / Gadyach; Yiddish: האַדיטש / Haditch), is a small Ukrainian town that holds profound significance for Chabad Chassidim worldwide. It is here that the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] — Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the founder of Chabad Chassidism — is buried. The Alter Rebbe died in Haditch on 24 Tevet 5573 (December 1812) while fleeing Napoleon's invading army, and his _ohel_ (tomb) has been a site of pilgrimage for over two centuries.

The Alter Rebbe's flight from [[entities/places/liadi|Liadi]] during Napoleon's 1812 invasion of Russia is one of the most dramatic episodes in Chabad history. The elderly Rebbe, approximately 62 years old, fled eastward with his family through harsh winter conditions. He reached the town then known as Piena (in the Chernigov Governorate), where his health failed and he passed away on the eve of Shabbos, 24 Tevet 5573. His dying wish to live until Shabbos was reportedly granted.

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Haditch is located in central Ukraine:

- **Location**: On the Psel River, in what was the Chernigov Governorate of the Russian Empire; now Poltava Oblast, Ukraine
- **Region**: Approximately 200 km east of [[entities/places/kiev|Kiev]]
- **Terrain**: Rolling plains along the Psel River valley; agricultural countryside
- **Climate**: Continental — cold winters, moderate summers

### Historical Context

At the time of the Alter Rebbe's death (1812), Haditch (Piena) was:

- A small town within the Pale of Settlement
- Located far enough east to be beyond Napoleon's immediate reach
- Home to an established Jewish community that could receive the fleeing party
- In the Chernigov Governorate, separate from the Vitebsk Governorate where [[entities/places/liadi|Liadi]] was located

## The Alter Rebbe's Final Journey

### Flight from Napoleon (1812)

The events leading to the Alter Rebbe's burial in Haditch are central to Chabad history:

- **Napoleon's Invasion (June 1812)**: Napoleon Bonaparte invaded Russia with his Grande Armee, sweeping through the western territories of the Pale of Settlement
- **Alter Rebbe's Position**: The Alter Rebbe opposed Napoleon, viewing him as a spiritual danger to Judaism despite the Czar's own persecution of Jews
- **Prophetic Words**: The Alter Rebbe reportedly stated: "If Napoleon wins, the Jews will have material prosperity but lose their identity; if the Czar wins, they will suffer materially but maintain their Jewishness"
- **Urgent Flight**: As French forces approached [[entities/places/liadi|Liadi]], the Alter Rebbe made the difficult decision to flee eastward

### The Journey

The flight from Liadi was arduous and dangerous:

- **Winter Conditions**: The journey took place in harsh winter weather
- **Elderly Rebbe**: The Alter Rebbe was approximately 62 years old and in declining health
- **Split Routes**: Family members traveled on different routes to reduce the risk of capture
- **French Pursuit**: There are accounts of French forces attempting to capture the influential rabbi

### Death in Piena (Haditch)

The Alter Rebbe reached Haditch in a weakened state:

- **Arrival**: The Alter Rebbe and his party arrived in the town
- **24 Tevet 5573 (December 1812)**: The Alter Rebbe passed away on the eve of Shabbos
- **Final Wish**: Tradition records that the Alter Rebbe had requested to live until Shabbos, and he died just as Shabbos was about to begin
- **Burial**: He was buried in the local Jewish cemetery in Haditch

## The Ohel (Tomb)

### The Alter Rebbe's Resting Place

The Alter Rebbe's _ohel_ in Haditch is one of the most sacred sites in Chabad:

- **Location**: In the Jewish cemetery of Haditch
- **Structure**: A simple structure (_ohel_ means "tent") marking the grave
- **Inscriptions**: Hebrew inscriptions identifying the burial place of the founder of Chabad Chassidism
- **Significance**: The ohel is second in Chabad pilgrimage importance only to the ohel in [[entities/places/770|Queens, New York]] where the seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe is buried

### Pilgrimage Traditions

Visiting the Alter Rebbe's ohel has been a Chabad practice for over two centuries:

- **Writing _Kvittlach_**: Visitors write petitions (_kvittlach_) containing prayers and requests, which are placed at the ohel
- **Tehillim**: Pilgrims recite Psalms at the site
- **24 Tevet**: The anniversary of the Alter Rebbe's passing (_yom hillula_) is a particularly significant day for pilgrimage
- **Historical Visits**: Throughout the generations, Chabad Rebbes and prominent chassidim visited the ohel

---

## History of the Ohel

### 19th Century

During the 19th century, the ohel was accessible and regularly visited:

- **Chassidim from Lubavitch**: Pilgrims from [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] and across the Chabad world traveled to Haditch
- **Mitteler Rebbe**: The [[entities/people/mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]], the Alter Rebbe's son and successor, presumably visited his father's grave
- **Maintenance**: The local Jewish community and visiting chassidim maintained the site
- **Tradition**: Pilgrimage practices and customs became established during this period

### Soviet Era (1917-1991)

The Soviet period presented enormous challenges:

- **Restricted Access**: Soviet authorities made pilgrimage nearly impossible
- **Neglect**: The ohel fell into disrepair without regular maintenance
- **Danger**: Visiting carried political risk; the KGB monitored religious activity
- **Persistence**: Despite the dangers, individual chassidim occasionally made the dangerous journey to pray at the Alter Rebbe's grave

### Post-Soviet Revival (1991-present)

Since the collapse of the Soviet Union:

- **Restoration**: The ohel has been repaired and maintained by Chabad
- **Regular Pilgrimages**: Organized groups from Israel, America, and elsewhere visit regularly
- **24 Tevet Observance**: The anniversary of the Alter Rebbe's passing is marked with large gatherings
- **Accessibility**: The site is now accessible to pilgrims from around the world

## Haditch in Chabad Thought

### Spiritual Significance

The Alter Rebbe's burial in Haditch carries deep spiritual meaning:

- **Founder's Resting Place**: The grave of the founder of Chabad Chassidism connects chassidim to the movement's origins
- **Tanya Connection**: The Alter Rebbe composed the [[concepts/tanya|Tanya]], Chabad's foundational text; visiting his ohel is seen as connecting to the source of Chabad philosophy
- **Mesirus Nefesh**: The Alter Rebbe's death while fleeing for his faith exemplifies [[concepts/mesirus-nefesh|mesirus nefesh]] (self-sacrifice)
- **Eternal Presence**: Chabad tradition teaches that a tzaddik's influence is more powerful after passing than during life

### Connection to Other Holy Sites

The ohel in Haditch is part of a network of Chabad holy places:

- [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] — where the Tzemach Tzedek, Maharash, and Rashab are buried
- [[entities/places/rostov|Rostov-on-Don]] — original burial place of the Rashab (later reinterred in Lubavitch)
- [[entities/places/770|770 Eastern Parkway / Queens]] — burial place of the seventh Rebbe
