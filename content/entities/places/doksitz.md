# Doksitz (Dokshitsy) — דאָקשיץ

**Chabad Community with Four Synagogues in Historic Lithuania/Belarus**

**Location**: Historic eastern Lithuania / Belarus (now Dokshitsy, Belarus)
**Jewish Population**: Significant Jewish community through early 20th century
**Significance**: Four Chabad synagogues; served by R' Arye Leib Sheinin; community martyred in Holocaust
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated 1942

---

## Overview

Doksitz (Yiddish: דאָקשיץ; Russian: Докшицы / Dokshitsy; Belarusian: Докшыцы) was a Jewish town in the historic Lithuania/Belarus border region, notable in Chabad history for having four separate Chabad synagogues. Like nearby [[entities/places/globoke|Globoke]], Doksitz represented a deep penetration of Chassidism in this region.

The town was served in the later period by [[entities/people/arye-leib-sheinin|R' Arye Leib Sheinin]], a Chabad rabbi who was known not only for his local service but also as an itinerant teacher and mashpia who visited Jewish communities throughout the region. He was martyred in the Holocaust.

---

## Geography

Doksitz is located in the historically contested eastern borderlands between Lithuania and Belarus, in the region historically known as "Litvishe Rusland" — the Lithuanian-influenced part of what is now western Belarus. The town is situated near the town of Dokshitsy in modern Belarus.

## Chabad in Doksitz

### Four Chabad Synagogues

The community supported four distinct Chabad synagogues:

- **Deep roots**: The community had been Chassidic for generations
- **Community organization**: Four separate congregations suggests a well-organized Chabad presence
- **Parallel with Globoke**: The same characteristic as nearby Globoke — four synagogues
- **Regional pattern**: The eastern borderlands had particularly strong Chabad penetration

### R' Arye Leib Sheinin — Rabbi and Itinerant Mashpia

The most notable Chabad figure associated with Doksitz:

- **Role**: Served as Chabad rabbi of Doksitz
- **Itinerant activity**: In addition to local service, traveled widely to other communities as a mashpia
- **Regional influence**: His travels connected Doksitz to a broader network of Chabad communities
- **Teaching style**: Brought Chassidic teachings and encouragement to isolated communities
- **Martyrdom**: Murdered in the Holocaust (1942) along with the Doksitz Jewish community
- **Legacy**: Remembered as one of the martyred traveling mashpiim of Polish-Lithuanian Chabad

## Community Life

### Religious Practice

In Doksitz Chabad:

- **Nusach Ari**: Prayer according to the Chabad liturgical rite in all four synagogues
- **Shechita**: Kosher slaughter according to Chabad standards
- **Learning**: Regular Torah study in the Chassidic tradition
- **Farbrengens**: Gatherings on Shabbos and holidays

---

## The Holocaust

### 1942 Destruction

The Doksitz Jewish community was murdered during the Nazi occupation of Belarus:

- **German occupation**: Eastern Belarus was occupied by the Nazis in 1941
- **Ghetto**: Jews concentrated under terrible conditions
- **Liquidation**: The community was murdered in 1942
- **R' Sheinin's death**: The Chabad rabbi was among those killed
- **Four synagogues silenced**: The four Chabad synagogues that had functioned for generations were destroyed

## Doksitz in Chabad History

### A Regional Chabad Center

Doksitz illustrates:

- **Eastern borderland Chassidism**: The strong Chabad presence in the historic Lithuania/Belarus frontier
- **Four-synagogue model**: Like Globoke, a community where Chassidism had deeply taken root
- **Martyred leadership**: The loss of R' Sheinin represented the destruction of a unique teaching tradition
