# Montreal — מונטריאל

**Significant Early Chabad Community in North America**

**Location**: Quebec, Canada (approximately 50 km from the U.S. border)  
**Chabad Significance**: One of the first and most important early North American Chabad communities  
**Population**: Approximately 1.8 million city; approximately 90,000+ Jewish in metropolitan Montreal  
**Chabad Community**: Established community with yeshivos, schools, and institutions

---

## Overview

Montreal is home to one of the earliest and most significant Chabad communities in North America. Established in the early 20th century, Montreal's Chabad community predated the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] arrival in America (1940) and provided crucial support to the fledgling American Chabad movement.

Located in the French-speaking province of Quebec, Montreal's Jewish community has historically been one of the largest and most vibrant in Canada. The Chabad community here has maintained institutions, educated generations of students, and served as a model for other North American Chabad communities.

---

## Geography and Demographics

### Physical Geography

Montreal is located in southwestern Quebec:

- **Location**: On the Island of Montreal, at the confluence of the Saint Lawrence and Ottawa rivers
- **Region**: Southern Quebec, approximately 50 km from the U.S. border
- **Terrain**: Island city with Mount Royal (the "mountain" in Montreal)
- **Climate**: Humid continental — cold, snowy winters; warm, humid summers

### Linguistic Context

Montreal is unique:

- **French-Speaking Province**: Quebec is predominantly French-speaking
- **Bilingual City**: Montreal is functionally bilingual (French and English)
- **Jewish Community**: Historically English-speaking but increasingly bilingual
- **Quebec Politics**: Language politics have affected all communities, including Jews

## Jewish History in Montreal

### Early Jewish Settlement

Jewish settlement in Montreal began in the 18th century:

- **First Arrivals**: Jewish traders and soldiers in the 1700s
- **19th Century**: Increased immigration from Europe
- **Late 19th–Early 20th Century**: Mass immigration from Eastern Europe
- **Golden Age**: Early-to-mid 20th century was peak of Jewish Montreal

### Jewish Population

Montreal has historically had one of Canada's largest Jewish communities:

- **Peak**: Approximately 120,000+ Jews in mid-20th century
- **Today**: Approximately 90,000+ Jews in metropolitan Montreal
- **Institutions**: Synagogues, schools, community centers, kosher businesses
- **Neighborhoods**: Historically concentrated in Plateau Mile End; now more dispersed

## Chabad's Arrival in Montreal

### Early 20th Century

Chabad established itself in Montreal early:

- **Pre-1940**: Chabad families settled in Montreal before the Rayatz's arrival in America
- **Early Institutions**: Synagogue and community organizations established
- **Immigration**: Chabad families from Europe immigrated to Montreal

### Support for the Rayatz

Montreal's Chabad community supported the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]]:

- **Financial Support**: Montreal provided crucial funding to the Rayatz's efforts
- **Correspondence**: Active correspondence with the Rebbe
- **Visits**: Community members traveled to see the Rebbe in [[entities/places/crown-heights|New York]]
- **Influence**: Montreal's support helped establish Chabad in America

## Montreal's Chabad Community

### Institutions

Montreal has a complete Chabad infrastructure:

- **Yeshivos**: Tomchei Temimim yeshiva and other educational institutions
- **Schools**: Jewish day schools with Chabad affiliation
- **Synagogues**: Multiple Chabad congregations
- **Community Services**: Mikvah, kosher facilities, charity organizations
- **Publishing**: Chabad texts published in Montreal

### Community Life

Montreal's Chabad community includes:

- **Families**: Multi-generational Chabad families
- **Students**: Yeshiva students from Montreal and abroad
- **Shluchim**: Emissaries serving other Canadian communities
- **Integration**: Balaging insular community with broader society

## Montreal vs. New York

### Early Advantage

Montreal had advantages over New York in early days:

- **Earlier Establishment**: Montreal Chabad predated major New York expansion
- **Size**: Large, cohesive Jewish community
- **Stability**: Canadian society provided stable environment
- **Distance**: Safe from European disruptions

### Relationship with Crown Heights

- **Subordinate to 770**: [[entities/places/crown-heights|Crown Heights]] ultimately became world headquarters
- **Collaboration**: Montreal collaborated with New York leadership
- **Autonomy**: Some autonomy in local matters
- **Pilgrimage**: Montreal chassidim travel to Crown Heights

## Modern Montreal Chabad

### Contemporary Community

Today's Montreal Chabad:

- **Population**: Several thousand Chabad chassidim
- **Institutions**: Dozens of institutions serving community and broader public
- **Shlichus**: Emissaries serving across Canada
- **Education**: Major educational institutions

### Challenges

- **Quebec Politics**: Language laws and Quebec nationalism affect all communities
- **Emigration**: Some families leave for larger communities (New York, Israel)
- **Assimilation**: Pressures of modern society
- **Economic Challenges**: Maintaining religious lifestyle in contemporary economy

### Outreach

- **Shluchim**: Emissaries serving Quebec and other Canadian provinces
- **Campuses**: Chabad activities at McGill University and other institutions
- **Public Programs**: Educational programs for broader Jewish community
- **Integration**: Balaging outreach with maintaining community standards
