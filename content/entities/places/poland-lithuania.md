# Poland, Lithuania, Latvia

**Chabad's Second Diaspora — Between Russia and the West**

---

## Overview

The Polish-Lithuanian-Latvian region played a crucial role in Chabad history across three distinct periods: as the territory of the original ban against Chassidism (1798), as the Rayatz's exile home after leaving the Soviet Union (1927–1940), and as the arena of the Holocaust's destruction of Chabad's European community.

Full documentation in [[summaries/toldot-chabad-poland|תולדות חב"ד בפולין, ליטא ולטביא]].

---

## Key Periods

### The Vilna Gaon's Ban (1798)

On Hoshana Raba 5559 (1798), the Vilna Gaon issued his most severe ban (_cherem_) against the Chassidic movement, with particular force in Lithuania. This shaped the adversarial relationship between Mitnagdim and Chasidim for generations and explains why Chabad's strongholds were in Russia/Belarus rather than Lithuania proper.

### Tomchei Temimim Branches (1897 onward)

After founding the mother yeshiva in Lubavitch, the Rashab and Rayatz established Tomchei Temimim branches throughout the region, including Zembin, Doksycy, Koblitz, Vitebsk, and ultimately Warsaw and Vilna.

### The Rayatz's Exile Headquarters

**Riga, Latvia (1927–1933)**

- Rayatz arrives October 21, 1927 — thousands come to the station
- Word "temporary" on his stationery: "I fled the bad, but have not found the good"
- Launched 70 letters to Soviet chassidim within first month
- Key ally: Rabbi Mordechai Dubin (Latvian MP, devoted chassid)
- 1929 matzah campaign for Soviet Jews coordinated from Riga
- Rayatz diagnosed with multiple sclerosis during this period

**Warsaw/Poland (1933–1939)**

- Relocated 1933 to be near Tomchei Temimim Polish branches
- Daughter Sheina married Rabbi Menachem Mendel Wolff (wedding in Landwarow)
- Network of yeshivos expanded across Poland and Lithuania
- September 1, 1939: Germany invades; Rayatz evacuated with American diplomatic help

### Holocaust (1939–1945)

The Polish-Lithuanian Jewish community — including Chabad communities in Warsaw, Vilna, Lublin, and hundreds of smaller towns — was annihilated. The Rayatz lost his daughter Sheina and son-in-law in the Holocaust.
