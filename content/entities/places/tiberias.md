# טבריה — Tiberias

**Country**: Israel; formerly Ottoman Palestine, British Mandate  
**Character**: One of Judaism's four holy cities; on the Sea of Galilee (Kinneret)  
**Chabad significance**: Early Chabad settlement; part of the four-city presence in Eretz Yisrael

---

## Overview

Tiberias (Hebrew: טבריה / Tveria) is situated on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee (the Kinneret) in the Galilee region of Israel. It is one of the four holy cities of Judaism alongside Jerusalem, Hebron, and Tzfas (Safed). Tiberias contains the tomb of Rabbi Akiva and is near the grave of Maimonides (Rambam), making it a major pilgrimage destination.

---

## Chabad in Tiberias

The _Toldot Chabad BeAretz HaKodesh_ (Chabad History in the Holy Land) documents Chabad's presence in Tiberias as part of the four-city structure of Jewish settlement in Eretz Yisrael. The book traces Chabad communities in all four holy cities — Hebron, Jerusalem, Tzfas, and Tiberias — as well as the later emergence of Tel Aviv.

While [[entities/places/hebron|Hebron]] and [[entities/places/jerusalem|Jerusalem]] were the larger Chabad centers, Tiberias maintained a Chabad community as part of the broader **Yishuv Eretz Yisrael** (settlement of the Land of Israel). The community was supported by the **Kol Chabad** fundraising network that sent emissaries (_shdarus_) from Eretz Yisrael to Russia and Europe to collect funds.

---

## Historical Context

Tiberias was founded in approximately 20 CE by Herod Antipas and served as a major Jewish center in late antiquity — the Sanhedrin moved there after Jerusalem's destruction and the Talmud Yerushalmi (Jerusalem Talmud) was compiled in the region. In the medieval and early modern periods, the city had significant Jewish presence.

The early Chassidic aliyot of the eighteenth century included settlement in Tiberias and Tzfas — the mystically-oriented northern cities that attracted Chassidic immigrants more than the politically complex south.
