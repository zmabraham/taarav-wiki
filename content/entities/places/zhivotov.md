# Zhivotov (Zhyvotiv)

A small shtetl in the Vinnytsia (Vinnitsa) region of Ukraine (Podolia), near the towns of Titov and Dashov. This was the birthplace of Rabbi Yisrael Yehuda Levin (born 7 Tammuz 5672 / 1912) and the home of the Levin family before the pogroms.

## Description
Zhivotov was a traditional Jewish shtetl where the Levin family lived before being forced to flee in the winter of 1920. The family had a small shop there, and R. Efraim Shmuel ran a cheder for somewhat older students.

The town had a large, tall synagogue with a hidden loft space under the roof — called the "Makif" by locals — where townsfolk hid from pogromists. Non-Jews did not know the entrance to this space. During the height of the pogrom violence, all the townspeople hid there, sending young children out to the streets to scout and report back, since the bandits generally did not harm small children.

During the civil war period (1919-1920), the town suffered repeated raids by various armed bands: Denikin forces, Petliura forces, local gangs (Tyutunik, Sokolov, and others). Each band extorted money, food, and labor from the Jewish community. One notorious bandit named Sokolov arrived unannounced and warned that ten men from the town would be killed — and indeed ten people were murdered, including the wife of the local rav R. Chaim. When Sokolov reached the neighboring town of Titov, a group of young men resisted him and he massacred nearly the entire population.

The family fled Zhivotov just after Purim 1920, arriving in Lipovetsh before Passover.
