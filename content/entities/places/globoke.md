# Globoke (Glubokoe) — גלובאָקע

**Chabad Town with Four Synagogues and a Dedicated Chabad Rabbi**

**Location**: Historic Lithuania / Belarus border region (now Hlybokaye, Belarus)
**Jewish Population**: Significant Jewish community in 19th–20th century
**Significance**: Four Chabad synagogues; dedicated Chabad rabbi; visited by the Rayatz in 1934
**Holocaust**: Jewish community annihilated 1942

---

## Overview

Globoke (Yiddish: גלובאָקע; Russian: Глубокое / Glubokoye; Belarusian: Глыбокае / Hlybokaye) was a notable Chabad center in the historic Lithuania/Belarus border region. The town had four separate Chabad synagogues and its own dedicated Chabad rabbi — an unusual distinction that reflects the depth of Chassidic penetration in this community.

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] visited Globoke in the summer of 1934 (Sivan 5694), during a broader trip through the Vilna region. This visit, which followed a stay in [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]], energized the local Chabad community and served as one of the Rayatz's last visits to the region before WWII.

---

## Geography

Globoke is located in what was historically the eastern edge of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, near the modern Belarus-Lithuania border. In the interwar period, it fell within Polish-governed territory (eastern Poland). The region had a mixed Jewish, Polish, Belarusian, and Lithuanian population.

## Chabad in Globoke

### Four Chabad Synagogues

The presence of four distinct Chabad synagogues is remarkable for a provincial town:

- **Indication of size**: A large and diverse Chabad community supporting four congregations
- **Different groups**: Possibly representing different neighborhoods, occupational groups, or shades of Chabad practice
- **Organizational capacity**: Required significant community organization to maintain four institutions
- **Comparison**: Even larger Jewish cities sometimes had fewer dedicated Chabad synagogues

### Dedicated Chabad Rabbi

Unlike many small towns where Chassidim prayed in their own minyan but were served by the general community rabbi, Globoke had its own Chabad rabbi:

- **Separate authority**: A rabbi who specifically served and represented the Chabad community
- **Halachic guidance**: Provided rulings according to Chabad tradition
- **Community leadership**: Led the community in both religious and communal matters

### R' Menachem Mendel Koperstock

One of the Chabad rabbis of Globoke:

- **Service**: Served as Chabad rabbi of Globoke in the interwar period
- **Later history**: Survived the Holocaust and eventually settled in Haifa, Israel
- **Legacy**: One of the few Globoke Chabad leaders to survive and continue teaching

### R' Yitzchak Leivi Mandelboim

Another Chabad rabbi of Globoke:

- **Service**: Served the community, possibly as successor or concurrent with Koperstock
- **Martyrdom**: Murdered in the Holocaust in 1942/1943 along with the rest of Globoke's Jews
- **Memorial**: Remembered as one of the martyred Chabad rabbis of Polish-Lithuanian Jewry

## The Rayatz's Visit to Globoke (1934)

### The Visit

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] visited Globoke during the summer of 1934:

- **Date**: Sivan 5694 (June 1934)
- **Context**: Part of a broader trip through the Vilna region after a visit to [[entities/places/vilna|Vilna]]
- **Reception**: The community received the Rayatz with great enthusiasm
- **Activities**: The Rayatz delivered Torah discourses, met with community members, and strengthened the local Chabad institutions

### Significance

This visit:

- **Energized the community**: Gave renewed vitality to the Chabad institutions
- **Last major visit**: One of the Rayatz's last visits to the Vilna/Belarus region before WWII
- **Connection**: Strengthened the bond between the community and Chabad leadership

---

## The Holocaust

### 1942 Destruction

The Globoke Jewish community was murdered during the Nazi occupation:

- **Ghetto**: Jews concentrated in a ghetto under the Nazis
- **Liquidation**: The ghetto was liquidated and the Jewish population murdered in 1942
- **R' Mandelboim's death**: The Chabad rabbi was among those murdered
- **Loss**: Four synagogues, centuries of Jewish life, and thousands of people destroyed

## Globoke's Place in Chabad History

### Unusual Depth

Globoke stands out because:

- **Four synagogues**: Extraordinary for a town of its size
- **Own rabbi**: More than a minyan — a fully organized community with dedicated leadership
- **Rayatz visit**: Received personal attention from the Rebbe himself
