# Russia / Soviet Union — רוסיה / ברית המועצות

**Chabad's Homeland and Greatest Arena of Struggle**

---

## Overview

For nearly two centuries (1772–1927), Russia was the physical home of the Chabad movement. From the [[entities/people/alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe's]] founding of the movement in [[entities/places/liadi|Liadi]] through the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab's]] final years in [[entities/places/rostov|Rostov-on-Don]], every Chabad Rebbe lived in Russian territory. After the [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz's]] expulsion in 1927, Russia (as the Soviet Union) became the site of the most dramatic underground religious resistance in Chabad history.

The Russian experience fundamentally shaped Chabad:

- **Geography**: The vastness of the Russian Empire enabled Chabad's spread across diverse Jewish communities
- **Persecution**: Czarist and Soviet oppression forged a culture of _mesirus nefesh_ (self-sacrifice)
- **Adaptation**: Underground resistance under communism created uniquely resilient communal structures
- **Diaspora**: Soviet refugees seeded Chabad communities worldwide (Israel, America, Europe)

## Czarist Russia (1772–1917)

### The Pale of Settlement

Chabad developed primarily within the _Pale of Settlement_ (_Cherta osedlosti_) — the western region of Imperial Russia where Jews were permitted to live. Established by Catherine the Great in 1791, the Pale included:

- **Modern territories**: Belarus, Lithuania, Ukraine, Moldova, eastern Poland
- **Jewish population**: Approximately 5 million Jews by late 19th century
- **Legal restrictions**: Jews could not live outside the Pale without special permission
- **Chabad spread**: The movement grew throughout these Jewish communities

### Key Chabad Centers in Czarist Russia

#### Belarus (White Russia)

The heartland of early Chabad:

- [[entities/places/liadi|Liadi]] — Alter Rebbe's base (1800–1812)
- [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] — World headquarters (1813–1915)
- [[entities/places/vitebsk|Vitebsk]] — Major early center, Mitteler Rebbe arrested here (1825)
- [[entities/places/nevel|Nevel]] — Important Chabad community
- [[entities/places/homel|Homel/Gomel]] — Strong Chabad presence, R' Yitzchak Aizik Epstein's base
- [[entities/places/mogilev|Mogilev]] — Regional capital, significant Jewish community
- [[entities/places/minsk|Minsk]] — Major Jewish population, underground Chabad activities

#### Ukraine

Southern Chabad communities:

- [[entities/places/kremenchug|Kremenchug]] — Major Chabad community, yeshiva center
- [[entities/places/berdichev|Berdichev]] — Famous Chassidic town, Chabad presence
- [[entities/places/uman|Uman]] — Breslov center, mixed Chassidic community
- [[entities/places/odessa|Odessa]] — Port city, Chabad activities
- [[entities/places/kiev|Kiev]] — Major Jewish center, later underground

#### Russia Proper

Areas under direct Russian administration:

- [[entities/places/st-petersburg|St. Petersburg (Leningrad)]] — Capital city, site of Alter Rebbe's arrests (1798, 1800) and Rayatz's arrest (1927); Shpalerka prison
- [[entities/places/moscow|Moscow]] — Underground network hub; railway departure point for forged passport escapes (1946–47)
- [[entities/places/rostov|Rostov-on-Don]] — Rashab's final base (1915–1920), site of his burial

#### Central Asia (Wartime Refuge)

WWII destinations for fleeing Chabad families:

- [[entities/places/samarkand|Samarkand]] — Major wartime Chabad refugee community
- [[entities/places/tashkent|Tashkent]] — Uzbekistan capital, significant Chabad presence
- [[entities/places/bukhara|Bukhara]] — Ancient Jewish community, Chabad refugees settled here
- [[entities/places/ferghana|Ferghana Valley]] — Scattered Chabad families

## Revolutionary Period (1917–1927)

### The Bolshevik Revolution (1917)

The collapse of the Czarist regime and rise of the Bolsheviks transformed Jewish life overnight:

- **Religious Freedom Lost**: New regime persecuted all religious institutions
- **Yevsektsiya**: Jewish Section of Communist Party specifically targeted Jewish religious life
- **Property Seizure**: Synagogues, yeshivos, and communal buildings confiscated
- ** arrests**: Rabbis and religious leaders arrested as "counter-revolutionaries"

### Chabad Under Early Soviet Rule

#### Initial Confusion (1917–1920)

- **Civil War**: Multiple factions controlled different territories
- **Temporary Anarchy**: Some religious activity continued in chaos
- **Rashab's Death (1920)**: The Rashab died in Rostov, leadership passed to Rayatz

#### Rayatz's Leadership (1920–1927)

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] assumed leadership at age 18:

- **Rebuilding**: Attempted to rebuild Chabad institutions
- **Underground Begins**: Created clandestine network of yeshivos
- **Secret Correspondence**: Maintained contact with chassidim across USSR
- **Financing**: Arranged funding from abroad despite currency restrictions

#### The 1927 Arrest and Expulsion

**Arrest in Leningrad** (15 Sivan 5687 / June 15, 1927):

- GPU (secret police) arrested the Rayatz in [[entities/places/st-petersburg|Leningrad]]
- Imprisoned in the Shpalerka prison
- Sentenced to death, later commuted to exile
- International pressure led to release on 3 Tammuz 5687 (July 3, 1927)

**Expulsion from USSR** (1927):

- Rayatz was forced to leave Soviet Union
- Settled initially in [[entities/places/riga|Riga, Latvia]]
- Marked end of any official Chabad presence in Russia
- Beginning of leadership in exile

## Soviet Underground (1927–1946)

### The Network Structure

From exile, the Rayatz directed a vast underground network:

#### Geographic Spread

Chabad operated clandestinely in dozens of Soviet cities:

- **Major Centers**: Moscow, Leningrad, Kiev, Odessa, Minsk
- **Regional Hubs**: Kremenchug, Nevel, Homel, Samarkand
- **Small Communities**: Virtually every city with significant Jewish population

#### Institutional Structure

- **Tomchei Temimim**: Underground yeshivas disguised as technical schools
- **Meshuchrarim**: Former students who became teachers/mashpiim
- **Secret Minyanim**: Private prayer services in apartments
- **Communication**: Couriers carried messages between cities
- **Financing**: Cash sent through emissaries using coded language

### Key Figures in the Underground

#### The Shluchim (Emissaries)

- [[entities/people/shmaryahu-gurary|Shmaryahu (Shmaryahu Gurary)]] — Rayatz's son-in-law, organized underground
- [[entities/people/menachem-mendel-schneerson|Menachem Mendel (the Rebbe)]] — Later 7th Rebbe, helped coordinate before leaving USSR
- [[entities/people/isaac-schneersohn|Isaac Schneersohn]] — Rayatz's brother, coordinated Moscow operations
- [[entities/people/binyamin-gorodetsky|Binyamin Gorodetsky]] — Key fundraiser and organizer

#### Local Heroes

- **The Mashpiim**: Spiritual mentors who risked arrest teaching Torah
- **The Mohelim**: Circumcisers who performed _bris milah_ in secret
- **The Shochtim**: Ritual slaughterers who provided kosher meat clandestinely
- **The Families**: Host families who hid yeshiva students from authorities

### The Wartime Exodus (1941–1945)

#### Operation Barbarossa (June 1941)

Nazi Germany invaded the Soviet Union, transforming Jewish life:

- **Holocaust Unfolds**: Nazis murdered Jews in western USSR (Belarus, Ukraine, Baltic states)
- **Soviet Evacuation**: Stalin authorized relocation of industries and populations eastward
- **Chabad Flight**: Thousands of Chabad families fled to Central Asia (Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Kazakhstan)

#### Samarkand: The Wartime Haven

[[entities/places/samarkand|Samarkand]], Uzbekistan became the largest Chabad refugee community:

- **Population**: Thousands of Chabad chassidim gathered here
- **Community Life**: Maintained Jewish observance despite Soviet restrictions
- **Underground Network**: Continued correspondence with Rayatz via coded letters
- **Postwar Dilemma**: Stay in USSR or attempt emigration?

## The Great Escape (1946–1947)

### The Polish Passport Scheme

After WWII, Chabad organized one of the most remarkable escapes in Jewish history:

#### Background

- **Polish Citizenship**: Many Jews in western USSR had been Polish citizens before WWII
- **Repatriation Agreement**: Stalin's Poland allowed former citizens to "return"
- **Forged Documents**: Chabad obtained Polish passports for thousands who weren't actually Polish citizens

#### The Operation

- **Organization**: Coordinated by [[entities/people/binyamin-gorodetsky|Binyamin Gorodetsky]] and others
- **Moscow Hub**: Refugees gathered in Moscow, boarded Trans-Siberian Railway
- **Poland Transit**: Traveled to Poland, then to displaced persons camps
- **Final Destinations**: Most reached [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Israel]] or America

#### Numbers

- Approximately 1,000 Chabad families escaped (estimates vary)
- Included virtually the entire Chabad underground leadership
- Depleted Soviet Chabad but seeded diaspora communities

## Postwar Soviet Era (1946–1991)

### The Dark Period (1946–1953)

- **KGB Crackdown**: intensified surveillance of remaining religious Jews
- **Stalin's Anti-Jewish Campaign**: The "Doctors' Plot" and broader repression
- **Further Emigration**: Limited but possible departures continued

### The Thaw and Stagnation (1953–1980s)

- **Post-Stalin**: Some relaxation but religious persecution continued
- **Refusenik Era**: Jews applying for emigration became "refuseniks"
- **Underground Survival**: Small Chabad communities maintained observance

### Perestroika and Collapse (1980s–1991)

- **Gorbachev's Reforms**: _Glasnost_ (openness) and _perestroika_ (restructuring)
- **Religious Revival**: Synagogues reopened, Jewish life became possible
- **Emigration**: Mass exodus of Soviet Jews to Israel and America
- **Chabad Return**: Chabad shluchim re-entered Russia to serve Jewish communities

---

## Post-Soviet Russia (1991–Present)

### Chabad's Renaissance

Since the Soviet collapse, Chabad has rebuilt across the former USSR:

#### Major Centers Today

- **Moscow**: Chabad synagogue, Jewish institutions led by [[entities/people/berel-lazar|Berel Lazar]]
- **St. Petersburg**: Rebuilt Jewish community
- **Kiev, Odessa, Dnipro**: Major Ukrainian centers
- **Minsk, Belarus**: Rebuilt community

#### The Ohel in Lubavitch

- The [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab's]] burial site in [[entities/places/lubavitch|Lubavitch]] remains a pilgrimage destination
- Museum opened in 2001 commemorating Chabad's history there

#### Contemporary Challenges

- **Ukraine War (2014–present)**: Ongoing conflict affects Chabad communities in eastern Ukraine
- **Russia-Ukraine Relations**: Political tensions impact cross-border Jewish life
- **Jewish Revival**: Despite challenges, Jewish life continues to rebuild
