# Zuravichy (זוראוויץ)

**Small town in Belarus; hometown of Israel Jacobson's family, with four generations of shochtim.**

## Key Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Country** | Russian Empire (now Belarus) |
| **Governorate** | Mogilev (Homel region) |
| **Jewish community** | Mixed Chasidic/Mitnagdic; two shuls (Nusach Ari and Nusach Ashkenaz) |
| **Notable families** | Jacobson (shochtim); Malkav family |

## Overview

Zuravichy was a small town in the Mogilev governorate of the Russian Empire. It had a Jewish community with two houses of prayer — one in Nusach Ari (Chasidic rite) and one in Nusach Ashkenaz — indicating a mixed community of Chasidim and Mitnagdim. The town suffered from ongoing tensions between these groups.

There was a weekly market day (four times a year), and Jews engaged in trade, livestock, and small commerce. The Jacobson family had been shochtim (ritual slaughterers) in the town for four generations.

## Near Zuravichy

- **Kanove** (קאנאווע) — nearby village; home of maternal grandfather R' Avraham Nachum's estate (with water mill, wool processing factory, fishpond)
- **Davsk** (דאווסק) — small town on the railroad crossroads to Homel, Mogilev, Rogatchev, and Smolensk; where Israel Jacobson studied Hebrew with his uncle R' Hillel Alkin

## Jacobson Family in Zuravichy

The Jacobson family served as shochtim for four generations:
1. R' Yeshaya — first shochet (traced lineage to the Shelah HaKadosh)
2. R' Yaakov Leib — shochet during the Tzemach Tzedek's time; died young
3. R' Avraham Shlomo — shochet; follower of Tzemach Tzedek then Maharash; died 25 Elul 5661; buried here
4. R' Yaakov Leib (father of Israel) — shochet, mohel, communal leader; emigrated to USA ~1923

R' Yaakov Leib (the father) built the new synagogue, the mikveh, and bathhouse in Zuravichy — the only stone structures in town.
