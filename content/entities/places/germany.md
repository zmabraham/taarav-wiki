# Germany

**From the Rashab's Medical Visits to Post-Holocaust Renewal**

Full documentation: [[summaries/chabad-germany|אדמו"רי חב"ד ויהדות גרמניה]]

---

## Overview

Germany represents one of the most complex and irony-laden chapters in Chabad history. The Rashab, who despised Kaiser Wilhelm II and called Germany "Amalek," nonetheless made multiple visits to Germany for medical treatment and communal work. His grandson, the future seventh Rebbe, studied at the University of Berlin. And after the Holocaust that destroyed German Jewry, Chabad established one of its most vibrant modern centers in Berlin.

---

## Key Periods and Events

### The Rashab's Germany Visits (1885–1914)

**1885 (5645):** Rashab visits Berlin; meets Rabbi Azriel Hildesheimer (founder of Adass Jisroel and Berlin Rabbinical Seminary). First contact between Chabad and German Neo-Orthodox leadership.

**1889:** Rashab visits Berlin Aquarium specifically to observe a creature potentially identified as the chilazon (source of biblical sky-blue techelet dye). He reports a black-ink-secreting creature but does not recognize it. Reports this observation to the Radzyner Rebbe twenty years later.

**1906–1907 (Cheshvan 5667 – after Shavuot 5667):** Rashab spends an entire year in Würzburg, Bavaria — his longest sojourn outside Russia. The Rayatz joins him in Kislev (December 1906). Key events:

- Rashab accesses Kaiser Wilhelm's writing room (through a bribed guard) and writes a Chassidic discourse on the Kaiser's personal stationery
- Composes the _Hemshech Samech-Vav_ — one of the deepest Chassidic philosophical discourses in Chabad history
- Collaborates with Rabbis Shlomo Zalman Breuer and Yaakov Rosenheim on a plan ("Histaadus HaYereim") to strengthen German Orthodoxy
- Leads a farbrengen on Yud-Tet Kislev attended by German Jews impressed by his two-hour prayer

**WWI Period:** Rashab watches with satisfaction as German overconfidence is checked by England. Calls Wilhelm II "Amalek" and predicts: "The German will conquer Russia, he will swallow it, and it will choke him." Composes prayers for Russian victory.

### The Rayatz in Berlin (1928–1933)

During his Riga exile years, the Rayatz made extended visits to Berlin and conducted significant communal and diplomatic activity there. This coincided with the Weimar Republic's final years and Hitler's rise to power.

### The Future Seventh Rebbe in Berlin (1928–1931)

Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson studied at the University of Berlin from 1928 to 1931, living at 7 Hanse-Ufer Street. He later continued studies in Paris before accepting Chabad leadership in 1951.

**2001:** Berlin city government installs a memorial plaque at 7 Hanse-Ufer Street — the first official Berlin honor for a Jewish personality. Mayor Joachim Zeller: "In those days, antisemitic signs were placed in Berlin. Today we place a memorial sign for the great Jewish leader."

### Holocaust

German Jewry was annihilated in the Holocaust. Chabad communities in Germany — never large — were destroyed along with the rest of German Jewish civilization.

### Post-Holocaust Renewal

**1996:** Rabbi Yehuda Teichtel arrives in Berlin to establish Chabad presence.

Chabad Berlin (Lubavitch Zentrum Berlin) grew to include:

- A major Chabad center in central Berlin
- A synagogue seating 250
- A Jewish school ("Yudishe Traditsyone Shule Ohr Avner") in a building that had served as a Nazi officers' club
- A Jewish restaurant, mikveh, library, and youth center
- Regular Shabbat minyanim growing from 20 to hundreds of participants

Chancellor Angela Merkel acknowledged the community as evidence of genuine Jewish confidence in Germany's future.

---

## Irony and Meaning

The Rashab's prophecy that Germany would "choke" on what it swallowed proved accurate in the Holocaust's aftermath. That his grandson studied in Berlin, and that Chabad now flourishes there, gives the Germany story a remarkable arc from condemnation to renewal.
