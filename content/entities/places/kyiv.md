# Kyiv (Kiev)

The capital of Ukraine and one of the major cities where the author studied in underground yeshiva settings. Kyiv appears repeatedly throughout the memoir as both a destination and a transit point.

## Description
The author first traveled to Kyiv in summer 5687 (1927) to study at a small yeshiva there, where the maggid shiur was "the Rav from Konotop." It was in Kyiv that he first learned of the Tomchei Temimim yeshiva network and became inspired to join it.

Kyiv had a significant Chabad presence and was a transit hub for students going to yeshivos in Vitebsk, Polotsk, and Nevel. The author's letters to the Rayatz (preserved in the Chabad Library archives) were written in part from Kyiv.

A significant episode occurred when students studying in the women's gallery of a synagogue in Kyiv were raided by the NKVD. The author managed to escape without his luggage and identity papers. The students were taken for interrogation; a lawyer they consulted warned them they each deserved five years of imprisonment. The author subsequently fled Kyiv by boat (Parakhod) down the Dnieper River to Chernigov.

The Dnieper River (Dnieper) served as an escape route, with a boat traveling from Kyiv to Chernigov passing through various village stops.
