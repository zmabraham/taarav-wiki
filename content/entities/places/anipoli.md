# Anipoli (Annopil / Anopol)

**Small town in Volhynia; where the Maggid of Mezeritch died on 19 Kislev 1772; home of Rabbi Zusia of Anipoli**

## Overview

Anipoli (Polish: Annopol; Ukrainian: Аннопіль) was a small town in the Volhynia region of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, located in the vicinity of Rovna (Rivne). The area was known for its concentrations of chassidic communities during the late eighteenth century.

## The Death of the Maggid

Anipoli's primary significance in Chabad history is as the place where Rabbi Dov Ber of Mezeritch — the Maggid, the Baal Shem Tov's primary successor and the teacher of the Alter Rebbe — passed away on **19 Kislev תקל"ג (1772)**. This date, which would later become famous as the date of the Alter Rebbe's liberation from Petersburg in 1798, was first marked in Chabad consciousness as the day the Maggid died.

The Maggid had spent his final years in the Rovna-Anipoli area after Mezeritch, and he died there surrounded by his leading disciples. His death precipitated the dispersal of the Maggid's school: each major disciple took responsibility for a different region, and it was in the years immediately following the Maggid's death that the Alter Rebbe's independent leadership of White Russian Chassidism began to crystallize.

The coincidence that 19 Kislev — the day of the Maggid's death — later became the day of the Alter Rebbe's miraculous liberation from prison in 1798 gave the date a layered spiritual significance within Chabad tradition: a day of both mourning and joy, death and redemption, ending and new beginning.

## Rabbi Zusia of Anipoli

Anipoli is also associated with Rabbi Zusia of Anipoli (Reb Zusia), one of the most beloved figures in all of Chassidic literature. Reb Zusia was a disciple of the Maggid and brother of Rabbi Elimelech of Lizhensk. He lived in or near Anipoli and became identified with the town. His grave in Anipoli became a site of chassidic pilgrimage after his death in 1800.
