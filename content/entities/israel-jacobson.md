# Israel Jacobson (American Chabad Representative)

**American Chabad representative in Washington D.C.; liaised with the U.S. State Department on behalf of the Rayatz; instrumental in arranging diplomatic rescue that brought the Rebbe to America in 1940.**

---

## Overview

Israel Jacobson served as the American representative of Chabad in Washington D.C. during the critical decades of the Rayatz's leadership, playing a central role in two major diplomatic rescue efforts: the 1927 campaign to free the Rayatz from Soviet imprisonment, and the 1940 mission to extract the Rayatz from Nazi-occupied Warsaw.

In 1927, Jacobson worked with the U.S. State Department and American diplomatic contacts to apply pressure on the Soviet government to release and expel the Rayatz. His Washington connections allowed Chabad to leverage American diplomatic resources in a way that would not have been possible without a skilled and well-connected advocate in the capital. The success of this effort — securing the Rayatz's expulsion from the Soviet Union rather than a longer internal exile — owed significantly to Jacobson's work.

In 1940, when the Rayatz was trapped in Nazi-occupied Warsaw and the world was at war, Jacobson again mobilized American diplomatic resources. The effort to extract the Rebbe required navigating wartime bureaucracy, Nazi-occupied territories, and the complex politics of American neutrality. The eventual success — the Rayatz arriving in New York on March 9, 1940 — was a tribute to Jacobson's persistence and his connections in Washington.

## Life and Role

Jacobson's position as a Washington-based Jewish communal figure with connections to the State Department made him uniquely valuable to Chabad at moments when the survival of the movement's leader depended on American diplomatic intervention. His work required understanding both the needs of the Chabad community and the language and priorities of American diplomatic and political life.

The 1927 rescue was his first major intervention. The Rayatz had been arrested in June, sentenced to death, and then to exile — and the international campaign to free him needed American muscle. Jacobson coordinated with the State Department, obtained official inquiries and protests, and contributed to the pressure that led to the Rebbe's expulsion from the USSR in October 1927.

The 1940 rescue was far more complex. Nazi-occupied Warsaw was not the Soviet Union — the political dynamics were different, the dangers more immediate, and the channels narrower. Jacobson worked tirelessly through American channels, leveraging personal relationships and official representations to secure the documents and permissions that allowed the Rayatz and his family to leave. The Rebbe's arrival in New York in March 1940 was a culmination of these efforts and the beginning of Chabad's American chapter.
