# Alexander Lavut (Underground Emissary)

**Chassidic emissary in the underground Jewish network in Soviet Russia; participant in maintaining clandestine yeshivos; arrested by Soviet authorities.**

---

## Overview

Alexander Lavut was a devoted Chassid and emissary who operated within the underground Chabad network in Soviet Russia. During the period when the Soviet regime systematically dismantled Jewish religious life — closing synagogues, arresting rabbis, and shutting down schools — Lavut was among those who risked their lives to maintain Jewish education and community. His participation in the underground network of clandestine yeshivos (Torah schools) operated under constant danger of GPU surveillance and arrest.

He was eventually arrested by Soviet authorities — the fate that befell many of the most active members of the underground network. His story is representative of the dozens or hundreds of Chabad activists who sacrificed their freedom, and in some cases their lives, to keep the flame of Chassidic Judaism alive under Communism.

## Life and Role

The underground Chabad network in Soviet Russia during the 1920s–1940s was sustained by a network of committed individuals who took on specific roles: some ran clandestine schools, others transported materials, others maintained communication between scattered communities. Lavut was part of this network, operating in the dangerous intersection of religious defiance and Soviet political surveillance.

The courage required for this work cannot be overstated. The GPU (later NKVD) actively recruited informers within Jewish communities, and the penalty for organizing religious education or maintaining unauthorized religious institutions ranged from exile to the labor camps to execution. Yet Lavut and his colleagues continued — motivated by the Rayatz's teaching that the survival of Torah education was worth any personal risk.

His arrest placed him among the community of Chabad martyrs whose suffering was simultaneously a personal tragedy and a testament to the movement's depth of commitment. The Rayatz, from his position in Riga and later Warsaw, was informed of these arrests and used diplomatic and financial resources to attempt rescue — with varying success.
