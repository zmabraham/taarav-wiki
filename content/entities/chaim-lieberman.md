# Chaim Lieberman (Librarian and Aide)

**Librarian and close aide to the Rayatz; central figure in preserving the Chabad library and literary heritage under Soviet persecution.**

---

## Overview

Chaim Lieberman served as librarian and aide to Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz) during the Soviet period and the subsequent exile years. His responsibilities centered on the Rayatz's extensive library and literary holdings — collections of Chassidic manuscripts, responsa, and books that represented the accumulated intellectual heritage of Chabad. The preservation of this library under conditions of Soviet persecution, when Jewish books were confiscated and their owners arrested, was an act of ongoing courage.

Beyond preservation, Lieberman was involved in the active effort to smuggle books out of the Soviet Union — bringing Chabad's literary heritage to safety in the West. This work was part of the broader effort to ensure that Chabad's intellectual and spiritual legacy survived the Soviet attempt to destroy Jewish religious life. His expertise as librarian gave him unique knowledge of the collections and their significance.

Lieberman's scholarly knowledge extended beyond mere cataloguing; he was a man of genuine learning who could appreciate the significance of the materials he helped preserve. His service to the Rayatz represents one of the vital supporting roles that enabled the Rebbe's leadership to function: without the preservation of documents and the transmission of the written tradition, the broader work of maintaining Chabad would have been severely impaired.

## Life and Role

The Chabad library was not merely a collection of books; it represented the written legacy of generations of Rebbes and their courts — manuscripts of Chassidic discourses, correspondence, halachic works, and rare editions. During the Soviet period, the regime's systematic confiscation of Jewish religious materials and destruction of Jewish institutions made the physical survival of these collections an urgent concern. Libraries were raided, books burned or confiscated, and owners arrested.

Lieberman's work in this environment required discretion, ingenuity, and courage. The task of identifying which materials were most at risk, organizing their concealment or transfer, and ultimately facilitating their physical removal from the Soviet Union was complex and dangerous. The success of these efforts meant that significant portions of Chabad's literary heritage survived to reach safety in the West, where they eventually became part of the public collections maintained by Agudas Chassidei Chabad.

The question of the Chabad library's ownership became, in later decades, the subject of legal proceedings in American courts, reflecting the library's significance to the movement's identity and the ongoing importance of the preservation work that Lieberman and others undertook in the darkest years of Soviet persecution.
