# The Seven Lubavitcher Rebbes

**Complete lineage of the seven Rebbes of Chabad-Lubavitch, from the movement's founding in the 18th century through the 20th century.**

---

## Overview

Chabad-Lubavitch is led by a succession of seven Rebbes, each of whom both continued and developed the movement's spiritual heritage. The lineage spans from the founding of Chabad in the 1750s–1770s through the modern era of global expansion. Each Rebbe gave the movement a distinctive character while transmitting the essential Chabad inheritance to the next generation.

The first three Rebbes lived in the era of the movement's founding and consolidation; the fourth and fifth led during the period of Czarist persecution and institutional development; the sixth and seventh led through the catastrophes of Soviet persecution and the Holocaust and rebuilt Chabad into a worldwide movement.

---

## The Seven Rebbes

### 1. Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi — Alter Rebbe (1745–1812)

**[[entities/alter-rebbe|Full page: Alter Rebbe]]**

The founder of Chabad. Born in Liozna, Belarus, in 1745; died in Pyena, Russia, during flight from Napoleon's invasion, on 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812). Disciple of Rabbi DovBer of Mezeritch, the Maggid, and spiritual heir of the Baal Shem Tov.

- Founded Chabad as an intellectually-oriented branch of Chassidism
- Author of the **Tanya** (Likkutei Amarim) — foundational text of Chabad
- Author of the **Shulchan Aruch HaRav** — comprehensive halachic code
- Twice imprisoned by Czarist government; first release (19 Kislev) celebrated as "New Year of Chassidism"
- Established court in Liadi; opposed Napoleon's invasion of Russia

---

### 2. Rabbi DovBer Schneuri — Mitteler Rebbe (1773–1827)

**[[entities/mitteler-rebbe|Full page: Mitteler Rebbe]]**

Second Rebbe; only son of the Alter Rebbe. Born in Liozna, 1773; died in Nizhyn, Ukraine, on 9 Kislev 5588 (1827). Yahrzeit observed as part of the 19 Kislev celebrations.

- Relocated Chabad's center to **Lubavitch** — giving the movement its second name
- Known for lengthy, elaborate Chassidic discourses
- Brief imprisonment by Czarist government (1826)
- Authored numerous works of Chassidic thought

---

### 3. Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn — Tzemach Tzedek (1789–1866)

**[[entities/tzemach-tzedek|Full page: Tzemach Tzedek]]**

Third Rebbe; grandson of Alter Rebbe (maternal) and son-in-law of Mitteler Rebbe. Born in Liozna, 1789; died in Lubavitch, 13 Nissan 5626 (1866).

- Named for his responsa work *Tzemach Tzedek* ("Righteous Sprout")
- Led during Czar Nicholas I's harsh decrees (cantonist system, forced conscription of Jewish boys)
- Championed traditional Jewish education against the Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment)
- Father of seven sons, each of whom led a separate Chassidic court
- Prodigiously prolific in halacha and Chassidic discourse

---

### 4. Rabbi Shmuel Schneersohn — Maharash (1834–1882)

**[[entities/maharash|Full page: Maharash]]**

Fourth Rebbe; youngest son of the Tzemach Tzedek who remained in Lubavitch. Born in Lubavitch, 2 Iyar 5594 (1834); died in Lubavitch, 13 Tishrei 5643 (1882).

- Known for "**Lechatchila Ariber**" teaching — "from the outset, go over the obstacle"
- Father of the Rashab (5th Rebbe)
- Maintained Lubavitch as Chabad's primary center while his brothers led separate courts

---

### 5. Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn — Rashab (1860–1920)

**[[entities/rashab|Full page: Rashab]]**

Fifth Rebbe; son of the Maharash. Born in Lubavitch, 20 Cheshvan 5621 (1860); died in Rostov-on-Don, 2 Nissan 5680 (March 21, 1920).

- Founded **Tomchei Temimim** (1897) — first structured Chabad yeshiva with Chassidus curriculum
- Author of the **Hemshech Taarav** — the longest Chassidic discourse ever written (see [[index]])
- Led Chabad through World War I; forced to flee Lubavitch in 1915
- Died in Rostov-on-Don; his son Yosef Yitzchak became sixth Rebbe

---

### 6. Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn — Rayatz / Frierdiker Rebbe (1880–1950)

**[[entities/rayatz|Full page: Rayatz]]**

Sixth Rebbe; only son of the Rashab. Born in Lubavitch, 12 Tammuz 5640 (1880); died in Brooklyn, NY, 10 Shvat 5710 (January 28, 1950).

- Led Chabad underground through Soviet persecution; arrested and sentenced to death in 1927
- Death sentence commuted through international campaign; exiled from USSR
- Led from **Riga** (1927–1933) and **Warsaw** (1933–1940)
- Escaped Nazi-occupied Warsaw through American diplomatic rescue; arrived in New York, March 9, 1940
- Established **770 Eastern Parkway** as Chabad world headquarters
- Directed the global postwar Chabad rebuilding from Brooklyn until his passing

---

### 7. Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn — Ramash / The Rebbe (1902–1994)

**[[entities/menachem-mendel-schneersohn|Full page: 7th Rebbe]]**

Seventh Rebbe; son-in-law of the Rayatz (married Chaya Mushka in 1928). Born in Nikolaev, Ukraine, 11 Nissan 5662 (April 18, 1902); died in New York, 3 Tammuz 5754 (June 12, 1994).

- Accepted Rebbiship on 10 Shvat 5711 (one year after Rayatz's passing)
- Led Chabad for 44 years; transformed it into a global movement
- Established worldwide **shlichus** (emissary) network: thousands of Chabad families in 100+ countries
- Author of thousands of letters (Igrot Kodesh); delivered weekly public discourses
- Father of the Chabad outreach model now maintained by thousands of emissary families worldwide
