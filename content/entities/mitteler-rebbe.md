# Rabbi DovBer Schneuri (Mitteler Rebbe)

**Second Lubavitcher Rebbe; son of the Alter Rebbe; established Lubavitch as the permanent center of the Chabad movement.**

---

## Overview

Rabbi DovBer Schneuri (1773–1827), known as the Mitteler Rebbe (the "Middle Rebbe"), was the second Rebbe of Chabad-Lubavitch and the only son of the Alter Rebbe. He was born in Liozna in 1773 and grew up in his father's court, receiving from him the full tradition of Chabad Chassidism. Upon the Alter Rebbe's passing during the flight from Napoleon in 1812, Rabbi DovBer assumed leadership of the movement.

His most consequential practical decision was the relocation of the Chabad court from Liadi to the small town of Lubavitch (in present-day Smolensk Oblast, Russia), which he did shortly after assuming leadership. This move gave the movement its permanent name — Chabad-Lubavitch — and Lubavitch remained the seat of Chabad leadership for over a century, until the displacements of World War I. The Mitteler Rebbe is also known for his distinctive style of Chassidic discourse — elaborate, lengthy, and deeply analytical — which could sometimes last for hours. He authored numerous works of Chassidic thought. He passed away on 9 Kislev 5588 (1827) in Nizhyn. His yahrzeit, 9 Kislev, is observed together with the 10th of Kislev (yahrzeit of the Alter Rebbe's first release) as "Yud-Tes Kislev."

## Life and Role

The Mitteler Rebbe's leadership spanned the difficult post-Napoleonic period of Russian history. The Czarist government under Alexander I and later Nicholas I regarded Chassidism with suspicion, and the Mitteler Rebbe navigated the same tensions between religious leadership and state authority that his father had faced. Like his father, he was briefly arrested (1826) on accusations from opponents; he was released but the experience affected his health.

His scholarly output was extraordinary in both depth and volume. He wrote systematic explanations of Kabbalistic and Chassidic concepts that extended his father's methods, producing works that remain foundational in Chabad study. His discourses were renowned for their length and elaborateness — a style sometimes contrasted with the Alter Rebbe's more concise approach. This led to the famous saying that the Alter Rebbe "condensed worlds into words" while the Mitteler Rebbe "expanded words into worlds."

By establishing Lubavitch as the movement's permanent home, the Mitteler Rebbe gave Chabad a geographical identity that defined the movement for over a century. The name "Lubavitch" — meaning "city of brotherly love" in Russian — became synonymous with Chabad itself, a usage that persists to this day in the movement's full name: Chabad-Lubavitch.
