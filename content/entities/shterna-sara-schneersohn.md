# Rebbetzin Shterna Sara Schneersohn

**Wife of the Rashab (5th Rebbe); mother of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz); woman of great piety and profound influence on her son's upbringing.**

---

## Overview

Rebbetzin Shterna Sara Schneersohn was born in 1860 and became the wife of Rabbi Shalom DovBer Schneersohn, the fifth Lubavitcher Rebbe (the Rashab). As the Rebbetzin of the fifth Rebbe and the mother of the sixth, she occupies a pivotal position in the Chabad dynastical lineage. She is remembered as a woman of exceptional piety and learning — herself deeply versed in Chassidic thought — whose influence on her only son Yosef Yitzchak was profound.

The Rayatz was the Rashab and Shterna Sara's only surviving child. This made his upbringing particularly intense in terms of parental attention and spiritual investment. The Rashab's teachings, combined with Shterna Sara's own piety and the environment of the Lubavitch court, formed the crucible in which the Rayatz developed the extraordinary character he would display throughout his lifetime of persecution and leadership. She was widowed in 1920 when the Rashab passed away in Rostov-on-Don, and she lived on until 1942, when she perished — at the advanced age of approximately 82 — in the Holocaust.

## Life and Role

Shterna Sara lived her entire life within the world of Chabad leadership. Born in 1860, she came of age during the leadership of the Tzemach Tzedek's sons and the early leadership of the Maharash. Her marriage to Shalom DovBer placed her at the very center of the movement as it entered its most intellectually rigorous period — the era of the Hemshech Taarav and the founding of Tomchei Temimim.

Her influence on her son Yosef Yitzchak is frequently referenced in Chabad biographical literature. The Rayatz attributed much of his inner strength and commitment to his parents' example. Growing up in Lubavitch during the apex of the Chabad court's influence — before World War I forced the family's departure in 1915 — Shterna Sara shaped the future sixth Rebbe's character in ways that sustained him through the most extreme trials.

She witnessed the Rashab's passing in Rostov in 1920, the beginning of her son's leadership as sixth Rebbe, the Soviet persecutions, his 1927 arrest and miraculous release, and his eventual exile from Russia. She remained in Soviet-occupied territory while her son built Chabad from Riga and Warsaw. Her fate in 1942 — murdered in the Holocaust at age approximately 82 — represents one of the countless elderly Jewish victims swept away in the Nazi genocide.
