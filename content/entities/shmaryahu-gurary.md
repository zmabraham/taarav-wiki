# Rabbi Shmaryahu Gurary (Rashag)

**Son-in-law of the Rayatz; married the eldest daughter Chana in 1926; a senior figure in Chabad throughout the interwar years and the American period.**

---

## Overview

Rabbi Shmaryahu Gurary, known by the acronym Rashag (Resh-Shin-Gimel, from Rav SHmaryahu Gurary), married Chana Schneersohn, the eldest daughter of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz), in 1926. This marriage made him one of the two sons-in-law of the Rayatz — the other being Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (the future seventh Rebbe), who married Chaya Mushka in 1928. As son-in-law of the sixth Rebbe, the Rashag occupied a position of significant honor and responsibility within the Chabad leadership structure.

During the interwar years — the Rayatz's periods in Riga (1927–1933) and Warsaw (1933–1939) — the Rashag was a central figure in the Chabad court, involved in the movement's organizational activities and educational work. He participated in the escape from Nazi-occupied Warsaw in 1940 and was part of the small group that accompanied the Rayatz to America.

In Brooklyn, the Rashag served in various senior roles within the Chabad institutions that the Rayatz established at 770 Eastern Parkway. After the Rayatz's passing and the Ramash's acceptance of the Rebbiship, the Rashag continued to play a role in Chabad life. His position as eldest son-in-law placed him in a delicate position relative to the Ramash, and the relationship between the two sons-in-law was a significant dynamic in the post-1950 Chabad community.

## Life and Role

The Rashag's marriage to Chana Schneersohn in 1926 was a celebrated event in the Chabad world, coming during the Rayatz's Soviet-era leadership when such occasions were rare moments of joy amid persecution. The following year, 1927, was the watershed year of the Rayatz's arrest and eventual exile from the Soviet Union — events that would define the next two decades of Chabad leadership.

Throughout the Riga and Warsaw years, the Rashag was part of the Rebbe's immediate household and institutional inner circle. When the Rayatz escaped Nazi-occupied Warsaw through the intervention of the American State Department and diplomatic pressure in early 1940, the Rashag and Chana were among the family members who made the perilous journey to America.

In the American period, the Rashag contributed to Chabad's institutional establishment and educational work. The dynamic between the two sons-in-law — the Rashag (elder son-in-law, husband of the eldest daughter) and the Ramash (husband of the second daughter but by far the more prominent scholar and eventual successor) — was one of the defining interpersonal realities of post-war Chabad.
