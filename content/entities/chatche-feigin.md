# Reb Chatche Feigin (Chaim Lieberman Feigin)

**Close aide and personal secretary to the Rayatz during the Soviet years and beyond; maintained critical communication networks under extreme danger.**

---

## Overview

Reb Chatche Feigin (full name Chaim Lieberman Feigin), known affectionately in Chabad circles as Reb Chatche, was one of the closest aides and personal secretaries to Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz) during the most dangerous years of Soviet persecution. He served the Rayatz with extraordinary personal devotion — managing correspondence, protecting sensitive documents, and maintaining the communication networks that kept the underground Chabad network functioning — at great personal risk.

During the Soviet period, when even possessing religious books could lead to arrest, Feigin's role as keeper of the Rayatz's correspondence and records was crucial and dangerous. He was intimately involved in the logistics of the Rebbe's leadership during the years of underground activity. His closeness to the Rayatz gave him unusual insight into the inner workings of the movement's secret operations, and he was trusted with information that, if discovered by Soviet authorities, could have led to execution.

He continued to serve the Rayatz during the exile years in Riga and Warsaw and was part of the broader circle of devoted assistants who enabled the Rayatz's leadership to function across international distances. His role exemplifies the category of the Rebbe's "right-hand men" — those who translated the Rebbe's vision into operational reality.

## Life and Role

The position of personal secretary to a Chassidic Rebbe involves extraordinary intimacy with the leader's daily life and thinking — managing the flow of correspondence, filtering access, and sometimes composing or transmitting communications on the Rebbe's behalf. For Reb Chatche during the Soviet period, these functions were carried out under the constant shadow of GPU surveillance and the ever-present danger of arrest.

Feigin's loyalty to the Rayatz was of the kind that Chabad calls mesirat nefesh — self-sacrifice, the willingness to endanger one's life for a higher purpose. The preservation of the Rayatz's correspondence and the maintenance of communication with scattered Chabad communities across the Soviet Union required constant ingenuity and courage. The underground network of Chabad activists depended on trusted intermediaries like Feigin to keep lines of communication open.

After the Rayatz's exile from the Soviet Union in 1927 and his eventual relocation to Riga and Warsaw, Feigin continued in roles that supported the Rebbe's international leadership. His intimate knowledge of the Soviet Chabad network made him an invaluable resource for understanding the situation of Jews still inside the USSR, and his experience navigating Soviet surveillance and secrecy was a unique form of expertise.
