# Rabbi Leibel Groner (Personal Secretary to 7th Rebbe)

**Personal secretary to Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn (the 7th Rebbe); as a young man in Brooklyn, part of the early Chabad community around the Frierdiker Rebbe.**

---

## Overview

Rabbi Leibel Groner (Rabbi Yehuda Leib Groner) served as personal secretary to Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn, the seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe, for decades. As a young man growing up in Brooklyn, he was part of the early Chabad community that formed around the Frierdiker Rebbe (the Rayatz) after the Rebbe's arrival in America in 1940. His proximity to the Rebbe's household from these early years gave him unique insight into both the Rayatz and his successor.

Rabbi Groner's role as personal secretary to the seventh Rebbe placed him at the center of the movement's administration for much of the post-1950 period. The Rebbe's daily schedule, correspondence, private audiences (yechidus), and organizational decisions all passed through Groner to some degree. His decades of service made him one of the most informed living sources on the Rebbe's personality, methods, and decisions.

## Life and Role

Groner's childhood and youth in Brooklyn during the 1940s placed him in the formative environment of American Chabad — the period when the Rayatz was establishing 770 Eastern Parkway as the movement's world headquarters and when a small but devoted community of Chassidim was being built in Crown Heights. Growing up in this environment gave him fluency in the culture, language, and priorities of Chabad leadership.

When he became personal secretary to the seventh Rebbe, Rabbi Groner occupied a position of enormous trust and delicacy. The personal secretary to a Rebbe manages the flow of access to the Rebbe — deciding who can approach, when, and under what circumstances. He also manages the Rebbe's time, correspondence, and schedule in ways that shape the texture of the Rebbe's leadership. Groner performed these functions for decades, becoming an institution within the institution.

His testimony and recollections, shared in various contexts, have become important primary sources for understanding the seventh Rebbe's personality and daily life.
