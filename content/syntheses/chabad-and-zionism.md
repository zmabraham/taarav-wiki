# Chabad and Zionism

**A Complex, Evolving Relationship**

---

## Overview

Chabad's relationship with Zionism is one of the most nuanced in Jewish history — neither the wholehearted opposition of the Satmar Rebbe nor the wholehearted embrace of Religious Zionism, but a position that shifted over generations while maintaining certain constants.

---

## The Rashab's Strong Opposition (1896–1920)

When Theodor Herzl published _Der Judenstaat_ in 1896 and convened the First Zionist Congress in 1897, the [[entities/people/rebbe-rashab|Rashab]] was one of the most articulate Orthodox opponents.

His objections were theological and practical:

- **Theological**: The Land of Israel must be redeemed by divine will and the Messiah, not by political organizing
- **Practical**: Political Zionism would create a secular Jewish state that abandoned Torah — a worse outcome than Exile
- **Social**: Zionist youth movements in Russia were pulling young Jews away from religious life (the Poalei Tzion confrontation at Tomchei Temimim in 1906 was a live example)

The Rashab issued public statements against Zionism and worked with Agudas Yisrael as the Orthodox alternative political framework.

He did not oppose settlement in Eretz Yisrael per se — Chabad had maintained communities in Hebron and Safed since 1777 and 1823 respectively, and the Rashab himself founded the Toras Emes yeshiva in Hebron (1911). His opposition was to _political_ Zionism as the vehicle of Jewish redemption.

---

## The Rayatz: Practical Adaptation (1920–1950)

The [[entities/people/rayatz|Rayatz]] inherited his father's anti-Zionist theology but faced a radically different world:

- The Holocaust made the need for Jewish refuge viscerally clear
- The British Mandate created facts on the ground
- The Soviet Union had made Eastern Europe untenable for Jewish life

The Rayatz's position evolved to be:

- Maintain opposition to _secular_ Zionism as a religious ideology
- Support Jewish settlement in Eretz Yisrael as a physical refuge
- Build Chabad infrastructure in Israel (Toras Emes in Jerusalem; ultimately Kfar Chabad in 1949)
- Never endorse the Zionist movement as the fulfillment of messianic prophecy

When the State of Israel was declared (1948), the Rayatz did not celebrate it as a religious milestone but pragmatically acknowledged the reality.

---

## The Seventh Rebbe: Complex Engagement (1951–1994)

The [[entities/people/rebbe-7th|seventh Rebbe's]] position on Israel was nuanced and often misunderstood:

**Support for Jewish life in Israel:**

- Maintained strong support for the Israeli army and the safety of Jews in Israel
- During the Six Day War (1967), insisted that not one inch of liberated territory should be surrendered — for _security_ reasons, not religious Zionism
- His "territories" position (no territorial concessions) became famous and controversial

**Opposition to Oslo and land concessions:**

- In his final years (early 1990s), the Rebbe was one of the most prominent voices against the Oslo Accords, arguing that territorial concessions endangered Jewish lives

**Not Religious Zionism:**

- The Rebbe never endorsed the _Religious Zionist_ ideology that the State of Israel represents the "beginning of the redemption" (_reshit tzmihat geulateinu_)
- He had significant theological disagreements with the Religious Zionist rabbinic establishment

**Practical Chabad Israel presence:**

- Built a massive Chabad network throughout Israel
- Sent hundreds of shluchim to every city and region of Israel
- Founded Tzeirei Agudat Chabad in Israel

## The Shared Territory

Despite theological disagreements, Chabad and Israel have enormous practical common ground:

- Israel has the world's second-largest Chabad community (after the US)
- Chabad is one of the largest Jewish education providers in Israel
- Chabad's political influence in Israel is substantial
- [[entities/places/eretz-yisrael|Kfar Chabad]] and dozens of other Israeli Chabad communities flourish
- The seventh Rebbe's picture hangs in many Israeli homes that have no other connection to Chabad

---

## Conclusion

Chabad's position on Zionism is best described as: _deep love of the Land of Israel and commitment to Jews living there safely, combined with rejection of secular Zionism as a theological framework for Jewish redemption_. This nuanced position has sometimes been misread as either anti-Israel (by Zionists) or too supportive of Israel (by extreme anti-Zionist Haredim).
