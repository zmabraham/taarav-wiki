# The Mitteler Rebbe's Trial (1825–1828)

The legal proceedings against **Rabbi DovBer Schneuri**, the [[mitteler-rebbe|Mitteler Rebbe]] (second Rebbe of Chabad-Lubavitch), constitute one of the most significant events in Chabad's Czarist-era history. The case ran from 5585 (1825) through 5588 (1828), ultimately ending in acquittal — though the Rebbe died twelve days before the final Senate ruling.

The Rebbe's release from Vitebsk on **10 Kislev 5586** became the Chassidic holiday of **Yud Kislev**, celebrated annually.

---

## Background: Ma'amad Money

The Rebbe's Chassidim contributed voluntary support payments ("ma'amad" — מעמד) to sustain the Rebbe and his extended family in [[lubavitch|Lubavitch]]. This practice, common across Chassidic courts, formed the basis of the charges against him.

After the death of the [[alter-rebbe|Alter Rebbe]] (24 Tevet 5573 / 1812), the Mitteler Rebbe assumed leadership. A major ma'amad collection — 35,000 rubles — was distributed to the extended Schneuri family. The document recording this distribution eventually fell into the hands of [[simcha-kissin|Simcha Kissin]], who used it as evidence of alleged fraud.

---

## Three Stages of the Proceedings

### Stage 1: Summer 5585 (1825)

**[[simcha-kissin|Simcha Kissin]]** of [[vitebsk|Vitebsk]] filed three denunciations with **Governor [[khovansky|Khovansky]]** of Vitebsk. Kissin was a relative of the Rebbe's family (through the Kissin family of Vitebsk, connected to the Rebbetzin Sterna's family) who had turned against Chassidus.

**The three charges:**
1. **Illegal tax collection** — Ma'amad money characterized as an unlawful levy on Jewish communities
2. **Financial fraud** — Kissin attached a translation of the Rebbe's distribution letter, alleging (via deliberate mistranslation) that 140,000 rubles had been collected but only 25% distributed
3. **Subversive organization** — Chassidism characterized as a sect undermining authority and corrupting youth

### Stage 2: Tishrei–Kislev 5586 (October–November 1825)

Governor Khovansky ordered interrogations:
- **[[korbitsky|Korbitsky]]** (Vitebsk police official) traveled to Lubavitch in September 1825 to deliver the summons
- **[[zalman-roizes-slonim|Zalman Roizes]]** of Slonim and **[[shlomo-freydes-shklov|Shlomo Freydes]]** of Shklov interrogated on 5 October 1825
- Special investigator **[[loginov|Colonel Loginov]]** and court advisor **[[markov-investigator|Markov]]** conducted the inquiry
- **[[aaron-halevi-staroselye|Rabbi Aharon of Staroselye]]** summoned from [[staroselye|Staroselye]] — interrogated 21–28 October 1825
- The Mitteler Rebbe himself interrogated (three sessions, October 1825)
- Inspector sent to Lubavitch to examine conditions on the ground
- **The Rebbe released to his home on 10 Kislev 5586** — this date became **Yud Kislev**

### Stage 3: 5586–5588 (1825–1828)

The case moved through the courts:
1. **[[babinowitz|Babinowitz]] District Court** — first instance
2. **Mogilev Gubernia Supreme Court** — acquitted the Rebbe **11 Kislev 5587** (29 November 1826)
3. **Governor Khovansky's Senate appeal** — Khovansky appealed to the Senate in St. Petersburg
4. **Senate ruling** — Final acquittal on **21 Kislev 5588** (28 November 1827)

The Mitteler Rebbe died on **9 Kislev 5588** — twelve days before the Senate's final acquittal was issued.

## The Rebbe's Defense

The Rebbe's legal defense rested on two arguments:
1. **Ma'amad was entirely voluntary** — no one was compelled to contribute; it was a free-will donation
2. **He was not a "rabbi" in the legal sense** — the Jewish Statute of 1805 prohibited rabbis from collecting beyond their fixed salary, but the Rebbe argued he was not a formally elected rabbi subject to that statute

These arguments proved persuasive to the Mogilev Supreme Court, which acquitted him.

---

## The Simcha Kissin Affair

The case is notable for the involvement of a **family member as informant**. The Kissin family of [[vitebsk|Vitebsk]] was connected to the Rebbe's family through the Alter Rebbe's wife [[rebbetzin-sterna|Rebbetzin Sterna]] (whose family the Kissins were related to). 

Simcha Kissin had actually sent supportive letters to the Rebbe in 5586 (the same year he filed denunciations in 5585), suggesting his motives were complex — possibly financial grievances within the family regarding the ma'amad distribution.

---

## The Holiday of Yud Kislev

The **10th of Kislev** (Yud Kislev) became a Chassidic holiday celebrating the Rebbe's release from Vitebsk in Stage 2 of the proceedings. The Maasar book discusses some uncertainty about whether the holiday commemorates the release in 5586 or 5587, but the consensus is 5586.

This holiday parallels the Alter Rebbe's liberation on **19 Kislev** (Yud Tes Kislev) from his Petersburg arrest in 5559 (1798), making the month of Kislev particularly significant in Chabad tradition.
