# Rayatz Timeline

**A comprehensive chronological timeline of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn (the Rayatz, 6th Rebbe) and the key events of his era — from the founding of Chabad through the establishment of the movement in America.**

---

## Overview

The biography of Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, known as the "Undaunted" narrative, spans a full century of Jewish history — from the founding of Chabad in the 18th century through the rebuilding of the movement in America in the mid-20th century. This timeline tracks the key events chronologically, with Hebrew year dates alongside secular dates.

## Narrative Overview: The Major Eras

### Era 1: The Founding Period (1745–1812)
Chabad was founded by the Alter Rebbe (Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi) as an intellectually rigorous branch of Chassidism. The movement grew in the Pale of Settlement, faced Czarist suspicion, and was tested by the twice-imprisonment of its founder. The Alter Rebbe's passing in flight from Napoleon's armies in 1812 ended the first era.

### Era 2: Consolidation in Lubavitch (1812–1882)
The Mitteler Rebbe established Lubavitch as the movement's permanent home. Under the Tzemach Tzedek (3rd Rebbe), Chabad navigated Czar Nicholas I's brutal anti-Jewish decrees and established itself as a major force in Eastern European Jewish life. The Maharash (4th Rebbe) continued this work with his characteristic boldness.

### Era 3: Institutional Development (1882–1915)
The Rashab (5th Rebbe) founded Tomchei Temimim in 1897 — the movement's first structured yeshiva — and began the Hemshech Taarav. The movement reached its highest institutional development in Lubavitch before WWI forced the family's displacement in 1915.

### Era 4: The Soviet Crucible (1917–1927)
The Bolshevik Revolution ended the Pale's relative framework of Jewish life and replaced it with systematic assault on Jewish religion. The Rayatz, who became 6th Rebbe in 1920 upon the Rashab's passing, built an underground network of Chassidic education that defied Soviet policy at the cost of constant persecution. His arrest in 1927 brought the crisis to a head — and the international rescue campaign that freed him demonstrated the movement's global connections and moral authority.

### Era 5: The Exile Years (1927–1939)
From Riga (1927–1933) and Warsaw (1933–1939), the Rayatz led Chabad internationally — organizing underground activities still ongoing in the USSR, building communities across Europe, and creating the celebrated marriage union (Chaya Mushka and the Ramash, 1928) that would shape Chabad's next generation. The relative freedom of these years allowed enormous organizational and literary productivity.

### Era 6: The War Years and Rescue (1939–1945)
Germany's invasion of Poland on September 1, 1939 ended the Warsaw period catastrophically. The harrowing rescue of the Rayatz from Nazi-occupied Poland — through American diplomatic intervention — brought him to New York in March 1940. The establishment of 770 Eastern Parkway as Chabad's headquarters began the American chapter. The Holocaust consumed the European Jewish world the Rayatz had led — including his youngest daughter Sheina and son-in-law, and his own mother — while he rebuilt from Brooklyn.

### Era 7: American Rebuilding and Transition (1940–1950)
The decade 1940–1950 saw the Rayatz establish Chabad's American institutions, direct the postwar escape of Anash families from the Soviet Union, and begin the process of creating a new Jewish community in America that maintained Chassidic standards. His declaration that "America is not different" set the tone for the American Chabad project. His passing on 10 Shvat 5710 (January 28, 1950) ended his own tenure and began the transition to the seventh Rebbe — who would take the movement to worldwide dimensions.
