# Chabad Underground in Soviet Russia (1920s–1940s)

A synthesis of the clandestine Chabad network in Soviet Russia, drawn primarily from the memoir *Lemaan Yede'u Banim Yivaledu* by an unnamed Chabad Chassid (born [[zlobin|Zlobin]], 5674/1914).

---

## Background: The Bolshevik Anti-Religious Campaign

After the Bolshevik Revolution (1917), the Soviet government progressively suppressed all religious institutions. For Jews, this was implemented especially through the **[[yevsektsia|Yevsektsia]]** (Jewish Section of the Communist Party), which:
- Closed synagogues and yeshivos
- Arrested rabbis and religious functionaries
- Promoted secular Yiddish culture while attacking Hebrew and religious life
- Infiltrated underground religious networks

By the late 1920s, all official Chabad educational institutions had been closed.

---

## The Underground Yeshiva Network

Rather than ceasing operations, **[[tomchei-temimim-underground|Tomchei Temimim]]** went underground. The memoir's narrator describes his own experience as a student in this network (c. 5689–5698 / 1929–1938):

### Cities and Branches

| City | Period | Status |
|------|--------|--------|
| **[[vitebsk|Vitebsk]]** | Winter 5689 (1929) | Operating secretly |
| **Mogilev** | 1929 | In hiding |
| **[[kremenchug|Kremenchug]]** | 1929–1930 | Active branch |
| **Odessa** | 1929–1930 | Active branch |
| **[[kherson|Kherson]]** | Winter 5690 (1930) | Active, then raided |

### The Kherson Raid (Winter 5690 / 1930)

The Kherson branch was raided by Soviet authorities. The head teacher **[[yechezkel-himelshtein|R. Yechezkel Himelshtein]]** and the *mashgiach* were arrested along with the students. The narrator, arriving from [[zlobin|Zlobin]] on the same day, found the branch gone — Mrs. Himelshtein met them at the door in tears. They had to retreat to [[kremenchug|Kremenchug]].

### Survival Tactics

- **Cover occupations**: Students and teachers maintained visible secular work (bookbinding, typesetting) to explain their presence without appearing to be involved in religious activities
- **Constant relocation**: When one city became dangerous, the network moved to another
- **Private homes**: Learning took place in homes, not identifiable institutions
- **No paper trails**: Communications were in person; written records were avoided
- **Covert travel**: Journeys were presented as ordinary movement, not yeshiva attendance

## The Samarkand Years (1941–1947)

When Germany invaded the USSR in 1941, thousands of Jews fled eastward. Chabad Chassidim gathered in **[[samarkand|Samarkand]]** in Uzbekistan, which became a wartime center of Chabad life.

| Name | Role |

### The Emigration Decision (c. 5706 / 1946)

When Polish Jews were allowed to return to Poland (and thus escape westward), a community debate arose about whether Chabad Chassidim should use this route. After discussion among the elders (sitting in R. Yisrael Noach Belenetsky's courtyard), the ruling was: **those already traveling should continue; new travelers should not begin.** Many eventually left anyway.

---

## The Flight West

The narrator used the **[[bricha|Bricha]]** underground escape network to flee westward:
1. **Samarkand → [[lemberg|Lemberg (Lvov)]]** — crossing from Soviet territory
2. **Lemberg → Poland → Germany/Austria**
3. **[[paris|Paris]]** — arrived before Shavuot 5707 (1947)
4. **Brooklyn, New York** — arrived 5708 (1948)

---

## Psychological and Spiritual Dimensions

The memoir's narrator describes a dimension more painful than physical persecution: the **spiritual losses**. Many fellow yeshiva students "turned" under Soviet pressure — abandoning religious observance, some joining the Communist Party. The narrator names this as more devastating than arrests.

> "The Yevsektsia's worst weapon was not the prison cell but the ideological corruption of Jewish youth."

---

## Legacy in Brooklyn

After arriving in America, the narrator worked at the **[[geertz-brothers|Geertz Brothers printing house]]** near 770 Eastern Parkway, setting type for Chabad publications under the direct supervision of the **[[menachem-mendel-schneerson|Rebbe]]** (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneersohn). The skills developed in the Soviet underground — covert printing, typesetting — became tools for Jewish revival in America.
