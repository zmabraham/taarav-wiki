# The Mitteler Rebbe Era (1773–1827)

**Rabbi DovBer Schneursohn — Establishing Lubavitch as Chabad's Center**

---

## Overview

The Mitteler Rebbe era represents the critical consolidation of Chabad as an organized movement with a permanent center. Where the Alter Rebbe era was one of founding and scattered growth, the Mitteler Rebbe transformed Chabad into a structured institution with a geographical center: the small town of Lubavitch, which would remain Chabad's world headquarters for over a century.

Rabbi DovBer Schneursohn was born on 9 Kislev 5534 (1773) in Liadi — the same day that would become his yahrzeit 54 years later. This extraordinary coincidence of birth and death date, combined with the nearby date 10 Kislev (the beginning of his liberation from Czarist imprisonment), gives Kislev an almost mystical significance in Chabad: the month of the Mitteler Rebbe.

The defining external crisis of his era was his arrest by Czarist authorities in 1825, on charges initiated by an informant named Simcha Kissin of Vitebsk. The case wound through Russian imperial bureaucracy for years, and the Mitteler Rebbe was fully exonerated — but he died on 9 Kislev 1827, the day marking both his birth and his first stage of liberation. Full exoneration came posthumously in 1830.

This era is documented through an extraordinary primary source: the actual Russian imperial court documents, translated and published in *Masar veGeulat HaAmemutzai* (Vol. 1 of the Mesibbv L'Chassidus series), which preserves the bureaucratic machinery of the Czarist state's encounter with a Chassidic leader.

Internally, the Mitteler Rebbe's era was marked by a significant theological controversy: after the Alter Rebbe's death in 1812, his top disciple, Aaron HaLevi of Staroselye, refused to accept the Mitteler Rebbe's leadership. The disagreement was philosophical — Aaron HaLevi favored ecstatic self-nullification in prayer, while the Mitteler Rebbe emphasized sustained intellectual meditation. This split created the Staroselye branch of Chabad, which eventually died out, leaving the Mitteler Rebbe's approach as normative Chabad.

## The Rebbe

**Full name:** Rabbi DovBer Schneursohn  
**Born:** 9 Kislev 5534 (November 17, 1773), Liadi, Belarus  
**Died:** 9 Kislev 5588 (November 17, 1827), Lubavitch  
**Age at death:** 54 (exactly; same birth and death date)  
**Buried:** Lubavitch, Belarus  
**Rebbe years:** 1812–1827  
**Acronym:** "Mitteler Rebbe" (Middle Rebbe); "Admur HaEmtzo'i"  
**In Russian documents:** "Berkeh Shniur" (Ber, son of Shneur)  
**Chabad-wiki:** [[entities/mitteler-rebbe|mitteler-rebbe.md]], [CW] E028-mitteler-rebbe

The Mitteler Rebbe received his education directly from his father the Alter Rebbe and was regarded as the most capable of explaining Chassidic concepts to others. He was the Alter Rebbe's second son but clearly the chosen successor, possessing both the intellectual depth and communal capacity for leadership. His tenure was brief (15 years) but transformative: he established Lubavitch as the movement's center, organized Chabad communities across White Russia and Ukraine, and left a substantial body of Chassidic teaching.

## Theological Legacy

### The Intellectual Approach to Ecstasy
The Mitteler Rebbe's distinctive contribution was his emphasis on *haskalah* — sustained, intellectual meditation on Chassidic concepts — as the path to spiritual elevation. Unlike Aaron HaLevi, who favored dissolving the self in divine contemplation, the Mitteler Rebbe taught that the self should be *refined* through understanding rather than *dissolved* through ecstasy.

This approach was expressed in his prolific writing. He wrote so rapidly and in such depth that disciples reportedly had to plunge his pen hand into cold water to slow him down — otherwise he could dictate for hours without pause, the words flowing from a state of sustained contemplative trance.

### The Staroselye Controversy
The theological dispute with Aaron HaLevi of Staroselye was not merely personal but represented a genuine tension within the Alter Rebbe's own teachings, which contained both intellectual and ecstatic elements. The Mitteler Rebbe's resolution — that intellectualism and emotion are not opposed but sequential — became normative Chabad doctrine.

### Establishing Communal Structures
Beyond theology, the Mitteler Rebbe created the organizational infrastructure of Chabad: networks of communities across White Russia and Ukraine, with the court in Lubavitch as the central hub. His 15 years established patterns that would persist for over a century.

### The Arrest as Historical Document
The Mitteler Rebbe's arrest case is historically significant beyond Chabad history. The Russian imperial documents, translated in Vol. 1 of the Mesibbv L'Chassidus series, show how the Czarist bureaucracy categorized, investigated, and ultimately acquitted a Chassidic leader. The Rebbe appears in these documents as "Berkeh Shniur" (Ber son of Shneur) — stripped of all religious titles, reduced to a bureaucratic subject. His liberation thus has dual significance: release from prison and restoration of his true identity.
