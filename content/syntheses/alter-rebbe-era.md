# The Alter Rebbe Era (1745–1812)

**Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi — Founder of Chabad Hasidism**

---

## Overview

The Alter Rebbe era marks the birth of Chabad-Lubavitch as a distinct intellectual and spiritual movement within Hasidism. Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi transformed the ecstatic emotionalism of early Chassidism into a rigorous system in which the intellect serves as the gateway to divine connection. His foundational text, the Tanya (1796), remains Chabad's cornerstone to this day.

Born on 18 Elul 5505 (1745) in Liozna, Belarus, Schneur Zalman was identified early as an exceptional scholar. He mastered the Talmud and Kabbalistic texts by his early teens, married at 16, and at 19 made a fateful journey to Mezeritch to study under the Maggid of Mezeritch — the Baal Shem Tov's principal successor. What began as a skeptical visit became an eight-year immersion that transformed him completely.

After the Maggid's death in 1772, the Alter Rebbe returned to Liozna and for sixteen years taught and guided disciples without formally establishing a court. Only in 1788 did he publicly assume the title of Rebbe, establishing in Liadi what would become a world-changing movement. He survived two Czarist imprisonments (1798 and 1800), each time emerging more respected than before. His liberation on 19 Kislev 1798 is celebrated annually as "Yud-Tet Kislev" — the New Year of Chassidism.

The Alter Rebbe's era ended not with a peaceful succession but with catastrophe. When Napoleon invaded Russia in 1812, the Alter Rebbe — who saw Napoleon's secularizing influence as more dangerous to Jewish spiritual life than Czarist oppression — fled eastward. He died on 24 Tevet 5573 in the village of Pyena while in flight, far from his home. His body was interred at Haditch, Ukraine, where his grave remains a pilgrimage site.

The Alter Rebbe left behind not just disciples and a community, but a complete intellectual system. Chabad (acronym for Chochmah-Binah-Daas: Wisdom-Understanding-Knowledge) was the name he chose for the approach — emphasizing that divine service must begin in the mind, with deep contemplative understanding of divine truths that then permeates the heart and action.

## The Rebbe

**Full name:** Rabbi Schneur Zalman ben Baruch Schneersohn  
**Born:** 18 Elul 5505 (September 4, 1745), Liozna, Belarus  
**Died:** 24 Tevet 5573 (December 27, 1812), Pyena (Piena), Russia  
**Buried:** Haditch, Ukraine  
**Rebbe years:** 1788–1812 (publicly; teaching from 1772)  
**Acronym:** "Baal HaTanya" (author of the Tanya); "Alter Rebbe" (the Old Rebbe)  
**Chabad-wiki:** [[entities/alter-rebbe|alter-rebbe.md]], [CW] P0566-alter-rebbe

The Alter Rebbe was genealogically connected to the Maharal of Prague. He studied first in Vitebsk and Liozna, then made the decisive journey to Mezeritch in 1764. He spent approximately eight years under the Maggid and formed close relationships with other great disciples, including Avraham of Kalisk and Menachem Mendel of Vitebsk, who would lead the first Chassidic aliyah to Eretz Yisrael in 1777.

## Theological Legacy

### Chabad Philosophy
The Alter Rebbe's core innovation was **Chabad** — the intellectual path to divine service. Where the Baal Shem Tov emphasized joy, story, and emotional ecstasy, the Alter Rebbe argued that true transformation requires intellectual understanding. The three Sefirot of Chochmah (Wisdom), Binah (Understanding), and Daas (Knowledge) are the pathway: first understand divine truths intellectually, then allow that understanding to permeate the emotions (Middot), and only then act.

### The Tanya (1796)
The Alter Rebbe's masterwork, formally titled *Likkutei Amarim* but universally known as the *Tanya*, is one of the most important Chassidic texts ever written. It introduces the concept of the **Beinoni** (intermediate person) — not a saint, not a sinner, but someone who controls his actions, speech, and thought even when his impulses pull otherwise. This psychologically realistic model of human spiritual development became Chabad's guiding framework for personal growth.

The Tanya also elaborates the **two souls** doctrine: every Jew has an animal soul (seeking physical pleasure and survival) and a divine soul (seeking connection to God). Life is the battle between these two souls, and the divine soul can and should prevail — not by suppressing the animal soul but by elevating it.

### Response to Mitnagdim
The Alter Rebbe faced fierce opposition from the Vilna Gaon's followers, who banned Chassidism and arrested its leaders. His 1798 imprisonment was triggered by a Mitnagdic informer. Rather than responding with hostility, the Alter Rebbe articulated a vision in which Chassidism deepened rather than contradicted traditional Torah study.

### The Napoleon Question
The Alter Rebbe's decision to flee Napoleon — despite the Maggid's tradition of supporting anti-Russian attitudes — reflected a profound theological judgment: Napoleon's Enlightenment values, which promised Jews emancipation in exchange for assimilation, were more spiritually dangerous than Czarist oppression, which at least left Jews' inner life intact.
